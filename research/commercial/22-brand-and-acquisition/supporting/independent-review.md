## Audit requirements applied to the new deliverable

| Question | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Does the benchmark qualify? | Identify an exact finished liquid can/bottle and a dated 2025–2026 launch, reformulation or meaningful traction event. Older products may be context only. Exclude preparation formats, liquid pouches and THC-led products from launch recommendations. |
| Does commercial evidence support the claimed mechanism? | A campaign, retailer listing, follower count or creator deal proves observable activity only. Sales growth does not identify the campaign's causal contribution. Keep line, flavor, company, channel, period and geography distinct. |
| What is being measured before inventory? | Comprehension, occasion relevance, stated trade-offs and qualified contact permission can be learned. Paid conversion, product effect, sensory acceptance and retention cannot be inferred from clicks or signups. A transparent concept page must not imply stock or proven effects. |
| Is the experiment interpretable? | Fix one main comparison, keep price/pack/offer and audience exposure comparable, randomize where practical, include the current alternative and no-purchase choice, and prespecify timing and exclusions. Different creative territories are bundles; an initial concept screen cannot identify which visual or word caused the response. |
| Are numbers described honestly? | Spend figures are analyst caps with inclusions/exclusions, not quotations. Sample targets and decision thresholds are management heuristics. When a valid denominator is not reached before the cap, report inconclusive rather than treating an arbitrary rate as a pass. |
| Are channel economics complete? | Include media, production/creative attributable to the channel, creator fees, free stock at landed cost, discounts, event fees, commissions, variable fulfilment and returns consistently. Show founder hours separately. Do not imply a sampling cap pays for a compliant production run. |
| Is acquisition causal? | A code or link measures attribution, not incrementality. Dedupe buyers across channels; separate attributed CAC from incremental CAC, which needs a credible holdout or experimental comparison. Store/venue clusters and creator audiences may overlap. |
| Is repeat real? | Distinguish intended repeat, subscription selection, automatic recurring shipments, deliberate repeat paid orders, employer restocking and individual consumption. Track non-promotional repeat separately and do not count a replacement/refund correction as another purchase. |
| Are cohorts comparable? | Use first-order date and a fixed follow-up window. Report buyers eligible for full follow-up, refunds, stockout exposure and lost contact. Do not silently remove nonrepeaters who encountered stockouts. Different pack sizes change depletion time. |
| Are claims and formula dependencies controlled? | A factual caffeine/ingredient/sugar statement still needs a verified final specification. Marketing creative, landing pages, creators and packaging all require the relevant claims review. An ingredient study in another form does not substantiate the finished drink. |
| Is CBD independent? | Keep CBD-specific demand, effects, legal use, source quality, platform eligibility and partner acceptance separate. A CBD-free parent brand does not automatically make a CBD landing page eligible for advertising. No evasion through neutral-looking creatives. |
| Could a channel cannibalize another? | A referral discount may subsidize a person who would have purchased anyway. Retail promotion can shift sales in time or between nearby stores. Track prior buyers and normal-price periods; show uncertainty when there is no control. |

## Measurement and economics rules

These are proposed analytical definitions, not observed company results or industry benchmarks:

- **Qualified priced-offer conversion:** unique new eligible adults making a first valid paid purchase divided by unique eligible adults demonstrably shown the same priced, available offer during the test. Use neither impressions nor footfall as a substitute. When exposure cannot be measured reliably, label conversion unavailable and report the observable denominator instead.
- **Attributed acquisition cost:** clearly defined channel acquisition spend divided by deduplicated first paying customers attributed under a rule fixed before the test. Report the rule and attribution window. This is not automatically incremental CAC.
- **Non-promotional 60-day repeat:** first buyers placing at least one further valid paid order without a repeat-only discount within 60 days, divided by all first buyers with a complete 60-day observation opportunity. Keep subscription auto-renewal separate; retain original cohort counts, cancellations and refunds. Report stockout-affected cohorts and sensitivity rather than selecting only continuously served successes.
- **60-day realized contribution per initial buyer:** cohort net sales excluding VAT, refundable deposits and refunded value, less product/packaging landed cost, payment, fulfilment, shipping subsidy, retailer/marketplace commissions and other applicable variable costs, divided by the initial-buyer cohort. Count every cost once. Acquisition spend is excluded from this contribution figure so it can be subtracted explicitly; fixed costs and desired profit require additional headroom.
- **Acquisition stop rule:** a channel with attributed acquisition cost above realized cohort contribution cannot fund itself within that window under the observed attribution. A cost below contribution is only a necessary screen: attribution may overstate incremental buyers, the mature sample may be small, and fixed costs are still unpaid. No unobserved future retention should be used to rescue a failing pilot.
- **Retail/office units:** report delivered stock, actual consumer sell-through, waste and account reorder separately. One office or shop is one account; unidentified consumers do not become a verified consumer-repeat cohort. Customer-level inference from store-level sales needs additional data.

The prior topic's proposed 30–35 repeat buyers per 100 is a chosen learning hurdle, not an industry benchmark or statistically established target. At 35 out of 100, the approximate 95% Wilson interval is 26–45%; selection and attribution bias can matter more than this sampling interval. Small qualitative samples cannot estimate population prevalence, and concept-screen fractions cannot substitute for paid conversion.
