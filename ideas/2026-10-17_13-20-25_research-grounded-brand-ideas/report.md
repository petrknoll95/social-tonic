# 20 beverage brand ideas, grounded in the research

**Created 2026-10-17T13:20:25+02:00 · Europe/Prague**

**My strongest directions are Along, Dayward and Ovelo.** They answer three different briefs: a drink that belongs in company; a drink for a daytime break; and a distinctive identity whose flavour and descriptor explain the product. They should be compared within the relevant product concept, not treated as three interchangeable wrappers.

The previous set relied on whimsical combinations and stories added afterwards. This set starts with the actual choice the customer is making. The names use English words, English constructions or abstract constructions; no borrowed foreign-language meaning or invented local heritage is part of the pitch. These are new proposals for this project, not a claim that every string has never been used elsewhere. Existing uses are recorded in [screening.md](screening.md).

## What the research changes

| Evidence in our research | Decision for this naming round |
|---|---|
| **R1 — Naming:** a distinctive identity can sit beside a clear local beverage descriptor. The sample does not establish a winning length or naming formula. | The name does one job. The descriptor says what the drink is. Do not cram flavour, ingredient, mood and occasion into a compound. |
| **R2 — Customers:** work-break, everyday refreshment, private evening and social participation are different jobs. Taste, value and convenience remain decisive. | Give each candidate a primary occasion. A work-break name does not automatically become a sleep or social brand. |
| **R3 — Commercial:** test a specific work-break proposition against ordinary energy; retain social/evening as a separate challenger. Personality: clear, warm, adult, curious. | Explore daytime names without promising better concentration. Avoid cute character names and productivity guilt. |
| **R4 — Ingredient work:** sensory-led social soda has the strongest immediate development fit; a reliable stimulant-free caffeine replacement was not established. | Keep a serious social route. Do not imply a buzz, disinhibition, stress treatment or a guaranteed mental effect. |
| **R5 — Sensory:** citrus refreshment and peach/tea are proposed workday tests, not winning recipes. Aperitif bitterness is not right for every occasion. | Show names on recognisable drinks. Do not build the masterbrand around an unselected botanical, flavour or carbonation level. |
| **R6 — Competition:** Czech/EU functional offers already exist; ordinary coffee, water and soda are real alternatives. | “Functional,” “clean,” “calm” or “focus” is not a distinctive brand idea by itself. |

**The apparent tension matters:** R3 prioritises the work-break occasion for commercial learning; R4 prioritises sensory-led social soda for near-term product development. Neither establishes a launch winner. This round keeps those two routes separate and includes neutral identities as a third route. It does not propose launching a range spanning all of them.

All pronunciations and likely associations below are editorial hypotheses. They have not been tested with Czech customers. All pack examples are concept illustrations, conditional on the eventual recipe; they are not approved labels or ingredient claims.

## The five directions I would develop first

### 1. Along

**English word · uh-LONG · Social / meal accompaniment · R2, R4**

The central idea is accompaniment: the drink goes along with dinner, conversation and the people already there. It gives the social proposition a positive identity without defining the customer by abstinence or asking the drink to change their personality.

**On a can:** `ALONG / Grapefruit / Sparkling soft drink`

**Creative line:** “Good company.”

**Visual idea:** two simple shapes sharing one continuous line; flavour remains prominent.

**Why advance it:** one familiar word, a coherent reason to exist and a natural request: “Bring an Along.” **Main weakness:** common-word search noise and English spelling recovery. The company association may not survive unaided Czech reading. No claim of local sourcing is intended.

### 2. Dayward

**English construction: day + -ward · DAY-werd · Daytime / work break · R2, R3**

Names the part of life the drink belongs to. The directional ending gives it movement without using energy-drink aggression or promising cognitive improvement. The execution should celebrate taking a break, not squeezing more work out of someone.

**On a can:** `DAYWARD / Peach / Tea drink`

**Creative line:** “A break in your day.”

**Visual idea:** a clear horizontal band that shifts with the flavour; straightforward typography.

**Why advance it:** a more specific daytime identity than a generic wellness name, with room for ingredient reformulation. **Main weakness:** an existing planning app uses Dayward; the name and context could also make people expect stimulation. Keep the actual caffeine content unmistakable. It is a poor fit for a bedtime brand.

### 3. Ovelo

**Abstract construction · oh-VEH-loh · Neutral masterbrand · R1, R5**

A name-led route: the identity becomes familiar through the drink, rather than through an explanatory story. No translation, botanical origin or sound-induced feeling is claimed. The research supports testing an opaque name with a concrete descriptor; it does not establish that this particular sound will work.

**On a can:** `OVELO / Lemon / Sparkling soft drink`

**Creative line:** “Pick your flavour.”

**Visual idea:** a confident wordmark, one large flavour colour and very little decorative symbolism.

**Why advance it:** tests whether a clean verbal identity works better than an English occasion metaphor for Czech buyers. **Main weakness:** Ovelo already appears in other categories, including bicycles and mugs. Stress may shift to the first syllable in Czech. Do not portray it as an unused word or cleared mark.

### 4. Wellmet

**English construction from “well met” · WELL-met · Hosting / welcome · R2, R4**

The social idea is the greeting: being pleased someone has arrived. That offers a host-friendly brand for a drink chosen to share. The benefit belongs to the gathering, rather than to a promised change in mood.

**On a can:** `WELLMET / Citrus / Sparkling soft drink`

**Creative line:** “Glad you came.”

**Visual idea:** paired forms that meet at the centre; warm colours without wellness gradients.

**Why advance it:** a compact, relevant English construction with an identifiable social meaning. **Main weakness:** the greeting is old-fashioned and may be unfamiliar; “well” may suggest a health product. There is also an existing wellness-services app. Test those inferences before developing the identity.

### 5. Unfold

**English word · un-FOHLD · Transition / pause · R1, R2**

Makes room for what comes next: a break, the end of work or an evening taking shape. This is an occasion metaphor, not a promise that the drink will relax the body. A folded-paper motif gives the word a concrete visual expression without an elaborate backstory.

**On a can:** `UNFOLD / Peach / Tea drink`

**Creative line:** “Let the afternoon unfold.”

**Why develop cautiously:** more evocative than a literal break label, with an ordinary English meaning. **Main weakness:** the final consonants may hinder spoken recovery, and a coffee shop named Unfold surfaced during screening. Keep it as a creative challenger pending closer category-conflict review, below the first three.

## What I would actually take forward

**For the sensory-led social soda: Along, then Wellmet.** Along is accompaniment; Wellmet is welcome. They are different enough to test a meaningful choice, and neither needs a pharmacological story.

**For the work-break drink: Dayward, with Between as the plain-language challenger.** Dayward is more brand-like; Between is more explicit about the pause. Both need caffeine-expectation testing.

**For a neutral identity: Ovelo.** It should earn its place through recognition, spoken recovery and the product description. Do not rescue an unmemorable name with a fabricated etymology.

Unfold remains a promising creative direction with a category-conflict flag. The other names broaden the exploration; they are not an invitation to produce twenty elaborate visual identities.

## How the name would work on the product

Use **name → flavour → local beverage descriptor → verified composition and serving information**. For example, the hypothetical work-break stimulus could read `DAYWARD / Broskev / Čajový nápoj`, if the recipe really is a peach tea drink. The brand remains English-based; the practical explanation is localised.

Two flavours can share a name without becoming separate brands. Reformulating an ingredient need not change these masterbrands, provided the descriptor stays accurate. A later social or functional family must earn its own positioning; especially for Dayward, Offclock and Afterward, do not force an incompatible occasion under the same identity. Removing a functional statement should leave an understandable drink, not an empty promise.

First compare names in the same plain type, on the same specified drink and at the same price. Ask buyers to say the name, spell it after hearing it, identify what they think is inside, and explain when they would choose it. Then test recall and likely caffeine/alcohol/effect expectations. Do not explain the story before measuring comprehension. Use the existing [evaluation protocol](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/supporting/evaluation-and-testing.md); none of those customer tests has been run here.
