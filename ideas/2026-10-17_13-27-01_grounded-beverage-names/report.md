# 20 grounded but creative beverage names

**Created 2026-10-17T13:27:01+02:00 · Europe/Prague**

**My strongest options are Nice Pour, Good Spell and Little Break.** They use familiar English, have a connection to choosing or enjoying a drink, and leave room for the product to establish the brand. At Leisure is a quieter, more premium direction. Leaf & Peel is a more concrete option if the recipe follows the citrus-and-tea route.

This round responds to the request for less eccentric naming. It uses ordinary words and recognisable phrases, with enough personality to support a brand. There are no invented syllables, animals or imaginary heritage stories. None of these twenty names repeats a previous final candidate.

## The research reference

The useful reference is now **Recess's clear occasion, innocent's conversational personality and TÖST's drinking ritual**. Our [naming report](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/report.md) records established evidence for innocent and emerging traction for Recess and TÖST; those observations do not prove their names caused success. This round borrows those naming approaches without near-spellings, translations or copied brand imagery.

The [customer research](../../research/customers/05-segments-and-occasions/report.md) supports exploring work breaks, meals and social participation as separate occasions. The [sensory work](../../research/product/13-flavour-and-sensory/report.md) proposes citrus and peach/tea tests, without choosing a formula. Those findings give the names a practical setting. They do not justify promising a buzz, better focus or physiological relaxation.

## What I would develop first

**Nice Pour** is the most directly beverage-related. It is easy to put into a natural request: “I'll have a Nice Pour.” A restrained wordmark, a clear flavour and a recognisable drink description would be enough to start testing it.

**Good Spell** has the most personality while staying understandable. The intended meaning is a pleasant period of time. Avoid potion imagery and therapeutic language.

**Little Break** is the clearest match to the work-break hypothesis. It should be assessed with that product concept, rather than assumed to work equally well for an evening drink.

**At Leisure** is the understated social alternative. **Leaf & Peel** is the most concrete taste-led alternative, conditional on the formula. These two should not be treated as interchangeable names for an unspecified drink.

For a first pack exercise, use the same layout and clearly mark all proposed ingredients as hypothetical. Examples: `NICE POUR / Grapefruit / Sparkling soft drink`, `LITTLE BREAK / Peach / Tea drink`, and `LEAF & PEEL / Lemon / Tea drink`. Localise the drink description for Czech buyers. The name does not need to carry the ingredient list or a functional promise.

## What remains untested

These are creative candidates, not validated consumer preferences. Familiar English is not automatically familiar Czech: especially test pour, spell, leisure, fare and serve. Ask people to say the name, write it after hearing it, identify the expected drink, and recall it later without hearing the brand story first. Follow the project's [evaluation protocol](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/supporting/evaluation-and-testing.md).

[Initial screening](screening.md) records known overlaps and the domain checks. No candidate is presented as trademark-cleared or globally unused.
