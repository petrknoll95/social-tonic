# Demand and country selection for a functional RTD launch

**Research cutoff: 17 October 2026 · Geography: Czechia, Austria, Germany, Poland, Netherlands · Status: completed desk research; commercial validation pending.** Finished ready-to-drink liquids in cans or bottles only. Primary concept CBD-free. All market-entry choices below are conditional recommendations, not evidence of product-market fit.

## Recommendation and its limits

**Test Czechia first, with a CBD-free drink for workday alertness/focus occasions.** The first benefit should be modest, transparent caffeine-led alertness; “focus” is an internal research occasion label, not cleared front-of-pack wording; even a name can imply a health claim. It is not an established promise of greater productivity or an advantage over equivalent caffeine. Test a pleasant 250–330 ml canned drink against existing focus/energy cans, including a caffeine-free contrast concept. Select size, sweetness, carbonation and dose through testing and co-packer feasibility; neither a complex botanical stack nor CBD is required. A canned/bottled tea base is an eligible option, not a selected recipe.

This is the best **learning priority** for a Czech-based founder under the explicit weights below. Czech focus scores **73/100**, versus **71** for general energy. That two-point difference is weak: reducing the assumed focus switching advantage by one point reverses the category choice. Czechia's local operating advantage is more persuasive than the category winner. Current local competitors invalidate an “empty market” narrative. A test that produces no convincing switch reason should stop the custom launch, not lead automatically to generic energy.

**Provisional expansion sequence: Czechia → one German region → Austria → Poland → Netherlands.** Before choosing the second market, run a small equal-spend German/Austrian comparison; a verified Austrian partner could reverse their order. German and Austrian operating scores differ by only one weighted point, and no actual buyer access has been secured. Dutch premium capacity is attractive, but distance, tax and packaging administration add friction. Poland adds population scale but lower observed focus pricing and tax/channel constraints. Expand only on paid repeat and landed contribution, not population or retailer logos.

The Czech base scenario yields approximately **672 first buyers, 235 repeat buyers, 9,811 cans and €10,792 net brand sales** over the first 12 months after a compliant pilot is sellable. The low/high sales scenarios are **€277/€160,992**. About **86.3% of base units depend on unobserved repeat orders**. These intentionally span a failed pilot to a demanding upside plan; they are not probabilities. At the illustrative base €0.25 unit contribution, only **€2,453** remains before acquisition and fixed costs. **The research does not justify committing to a national launch or a large manufacturing run.**

## Scope, shortlist and existing work

Czechia is the founder's home learning option; Austria and Germany test neighboring higher-spending markets; Poland tests regional scale and different price/channel economics; the Netherlands adds a compact premium-beverage context with more distant logistics. These four alternatives are plausible, not a claim that the rest of the EU is inferior. Slovakia is a sensible later language-adjacent test; France requires an additional language/route before this founder has validated neighboring entry.

We reviewed and reused [US liquid benchmarks](../01-us-product-landscape/README.md), [EU/Czech competition](../04-eu-czech-competition/README.md), [recent industry signals](../26-trends-and-industry-intelligence/README.md) and [customer occasions](../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/README.md). Three agents researched complementary country and benefit/legal streams in parallel; independent reviews challenged the integrated model and recommendation. [Method and delegation](supporting/research-plan-and-method.md), [source register](sources.csv), [data/model guide](data/README.md).

No benchmark is a powder, sachet, pouch, capsule, gummy, gel, drop or dilution product. Prepared coffee is discussed only as a competing habit. Ingredient studies can use other delivery forms, but do not establish finished-can effects. Non-alcoholic-beer category totals sometimes include draft; they inform moderation behavior only and never enter the qualifying-product model. No people were contacted, no quotes requested and no tests/purchases completed. The concurrently published [commercial-traction study](../02-commercial-traction/README.md) was reviewed before publication: its stronger energy/soda evidence reinforces this report’s caution. Its Neutonic reorder claim lacks a defined cohort/channel denominator and is not imported into the 35% repeat assumption.

## What current demand evidence establishes

The evidence hierarchy is **paid product/category behavior → comparable observed offers → stated interest → analyst hypothesis**. Science is a separate axis. The most important gap is shared by all five countries: comparable adult awareness, first-purchase conversion and paid repeat for the proposed product are **unknown**. A drink's claim, retailer listing or general energy-market growth cannot fill that gap.

## Category–country comparison

Category fit supplies the remaining **55 points**: demand evidence **15**, repeat plausibility **10**, credible switching reason **20**, ingredient/effect/claim simplicity **10**. Switching reason has high weight because this is a low-budget entrant facing incumbents; it is also the weakest assumption and must be falsified early. Demand 1 = no meaningful qualifying demand evidence; 3 = direct eligible offers plus relevant adjacent behavior; 5 = established paid category purchases. Repeat 1/3/5 spans uncertain occasion/recurring plausible/strong observed routine; a 4 for caffeine occasions is an inference from incumbent habits, not measured new-product retention. Switch reason 1 = commodity, 3 = specific but unvalidated occasion/experience difference, 5 = demonstrated paid preference (none). Legal/effect simplicity 1 = difficult unresolved formulation, 3 = conditional familiar functional route, 5 = straightforward and fully cleared (none).

The focus row assumes a modest caffeine-led proposition; **caffeine-free cognitive enhancement or a novel nootropic stack does not inherit its score**. Relaxation combines two distinct jobs for initial comparison; calm effects require more evidence than an adult social ritual. A purely sensory social drink could score better on claims simplicity and should be tested separately if it wins interviews. Hydration/recovery likewise excludes blanket recovery promises.

## Bottom-up demand: visible assumptions, not a disguised market-share forecast

The horizon is **12 months after the first compliant saleable pilot**, one country/category at a time. Population is a ceiling, not the reachable pool. No contracted channels or known audience were supplied. The assumed unique adults must first be delivered through specific venue/digital plans. Country scenarios are not equal-budget experiments; larger reach requires more execution/capital and acquisition costs remain unknown.

Base Czech reach: **12 venues × 500 distinct adults + 10,000 distinct digital adults − 2,000 overlapping adults = 14,000 unique adults**. Venue adult counts already exclude duplicates across venues; digital counts exclude repeat impressions. Qualification by a relevant workday occasion is assumed **60%**, yielding **8,400**. All these are hypotheses, including 500/venue; an office's staff count or a café's total footfall cannot be substituted without screening and deduplication.

**Units = reachable qualified adults × paid trial rate × [first-order cans + repeat rate × repeat orders × cans per repeat order].** Repeat purchasers are a subset of first buyers. Frequency is average orders actually occurring within the remaining launch horizon, not a full year assigned to late-acquired buyers. These are blended consumer orders; wholesale stock and consumer sell-through are never counted twice.

The midpoint cost €0.85 produces €2,453, only **€3.65 per first buyer** available for acquisition and fixed-cost coverage. Weight sensitivity is deliberately unfavorable to a simplistic winner: a **demand-first** weighting selects Czech general energy; a **premium/scale** weighting selects German general energy. The default selects Czech workday focus/alertness. A one-point downgrade of focus switching reason costs four points, enough to remove its two-point lead. Reducing Czech founder access from 5 to 2 removes nine points and can put Germany ahead. [All weights and alternative ranks](data/weight-sensitivity.csv). If Czech founder access falls 5→3 and channel access 4→3, its operating score ties Germany; the model separates convenient local iteration from assumed customer access. ## Practical entry and separate legal gates

For all countries, exact ingredient identity, concentration, processing, food/supplement/medicine classification and final claims must be checked before a sellable pilot. “Available in the US” or “listed by an EU retailer” is not permission. Permitted nutrient claims concern specified normal functions and conditions; they do not promise acute productivity. Caffeine has a better-supported acute alertness rationale, but it does not establish a finished drink's added nootropic benefit. The main recipe remains undecided. [Scientific and regulatory review](supporting/benefits-benchmarks-and-regulatory-gates.md).

|---|---|

Ingredient effects require matching dose, identity, population, duration and delivery. Caffeine may improve acute alertness, but ingredient-presence claims cannot establish sustained real-world productivity. Chronic botanical capsule studies do not demonstrate an immediate social sensation. ## Cheapest next decisions

1. **€0–150 proposed cap:** audit 20 genuinely accessible venues and current can/bottle controls; interview 15–20 adults about recent paid choices, dissatisfaction and no-purchase occasions. Test whether local access and a specific switch reason actually exist.
2. **€150–350:** compare workday, general-energy and caffeine-free social concepts at one fixed price first, then test the finalist’s prices separately, always with a no-purchase choice. Clicks/signups measure interest only. No efficacy promises.
3. **€150–300 for commercial controls:** blinded taste/price comparison with 30–40 adults; professionally produced prototypes and legal/process review are separately scoped. Keep caffeine-free demand distinct from desired stimulation.
4. **Only after those gates:** obtain formula/label/process/packaging review and competing outsourced production/landed-cost options; one flavor, smallest feasible compliant batch. No owned production plant recommended.
5. **Paid pilot:** log all qualified unique adults shown a priced, in-stock offer, including nonbuyers and acquisition spend; target 100 distinct first buyers and follow mature cohorts for 8–12 weeks. A provisional 30–35% second-purchase gate and positive contribution are useful learning hurdles, not proof of annual repeat or frequency. Reject paid acquisition that consumes the already small contribution.
6. **Expansion:** equal-spend German/Austrian concept tests, then one compliant regional route; add Poland or the Netherlands only when country-specific contribution and repeat justify setup costs.
