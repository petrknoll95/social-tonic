# Czech and EU sensory evidence and local repertoire

Research/access date: **17 October 2026**. Status: completed desk-research workstream; no products tasted and no consumer fieldwork conducted. Product anchors are finished liquids in cans or bottles. Laboratory model solutions are evidence about sensory response, not commercial product benchmarks. Source metadata: [czech-eu-sources.csv](czech-eu-sources.csv).

## What the evidence can support

There is **no established Czech national optimum** for sweetness, acidity, bitterness, aroma intensity, body, carbonation or aftertaste in the proposed functional drink. None of the located studies represents Czech premium functional-RTD buyers across all four occasions. The useful conclusion is to test competing sensory preferences within recruited occasion users, not to assume Czech consumers uniformly prefer dry, bitter or botanical drinks.

The existing [competition research](../../../market/04-eu-czech-competition/report.md) supplies direct local workday and calm controls. The [customer research](../../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/report.md) separates work, activity, evening and social occasions and contains selected taste objections. The [country-selection research](../../../market/06-demand-and-country-selection/report.md) recommends local learning conditionally; its demand scores do not predict a winning flavour. Current exact product pages were reopened in this workstream. Reviews remain self-selected observations, not representative prevalence.

|---|---|---|

[ČZU / Theses.cz](https://theses.cz/id/2qezrj/) | The small sensory panel ranked sucrose most favourably on average and steviol glycosides least favourably. | Local reason to measure aftertaste and blind liking; not proof that Czech consumers reject all stevia materials. Sweetener identity, dose, acid, aroma and beverage matrix matter. Non-peer-reviewed thesis; 14 assessors cannot estimate population shares. These solutions are not product benchmarks. |
| **CZ02 — peer-reviewed experiment**, 8 December 2020; **60 adults in Warsaw/suburbs**, Poland; pasteurized fruit-herbal drinks with 80% juice. Antioxidant measurements are not evidence of a finished drink's clinical benefit. |
| **CZ03 — peer-reviewed experiment**, online 2 March / issue July 2024; **350 Italian adults from Florence/Prato** and **143 US adults**; citric acid in pear juice or water. [Food Quality and Preference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11014420/) | Increasing sourness produced distinct liking patterns, including a minority whose liking rose. An overall mean concealed heterogeneity. | Strong justification for reporting individual paired responses and screening for current drink habits. It does not provide Czech segment sizes or an acid target. The Italian pear-juice matrix differs from a clear functional drink. |
| **CZ04 — PhD thesis reporting a randomized whole-diet trial**, 13 June 2025; **180 healthy Dutch adults**, six months plus follow-up. [Wageningen University](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/sweet-tooth-nature-or-nurture-assessing-the-role-of-dietary-sweet/) | Altered dietary sweetness exposure did not significantly change sweet-taste preference in this trial. | Do not base a business case on consumers learning to love a deliberately disliked low-sweetness recipe. This is whole-diet evidence, not a test of any proposed can or Czech demand. |
| **CZ05 — peer-reviewed water experiment**, online 24 September 2016 / issue 1 January 2017; Spain-based research, French/Spanish waters; **69 volunteers**, 25 bottled/tap waters. [Talanta abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27837803/) | Liking varied with mineral composition; in this sample, calcium-bicarbonate/sulfate-rich waters were preferred over sodium-chloride-rich waters. | Minerals cannot be represented by one generic “electrolyte taste.” It is water evidence, not evidence that a magnesium-fortified fruit drink will be liked. Participant residence and complete sampling detail are not established by the accessible abstract. |

These studies supply experimental questions, not sales projections or a transferable recipe. Older research remains useful for sensory mechanisms; it does not substitute for 2026 Czech customer validation.

## What to investigate by sensory dimension

All proposed tests below are **formulation/research hypotheses**, not observed preferences.

| Dimension | What is presently known or unknown for the target | Practical comparison |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweetness** | Current local offers span unsweetened CANS+, steviol-sweetened Vilgain and sugar-containing Magnesia Plus. Market variety proves alternatives exist, not which is most liked. | Within one flavour/base, compare unsweetened, lightly sweet and moderately sweet prototypes. Separate perceived sweetness from sugar grams and from attitudes to a label. Do not compare different flavours and call the result a sweetener effect. |
| **Acidity/sourness** | No Czech optimum established; Italian evidence supports heterogeneity. Perceived sourness is not interchangeable with pH. | Vary acid level in a matched base, record titratable acidity and pH, and measure sour intensity plus “too little / about right / too much.” The final safe pH/process is a technologist's decision. |
| **Bitterness** | A tonic control establishes an available bitter botanical reference. It does not mean people want tonic bitterness during work or exercise. | Compare restrained vs clearer bitter finish in social prototypes only initially; in focus/hydration prioritize whether bitterness is acceptable at the intended ingredient dose. Separate bitterness from drying astringency. |
| **Aroma** | Raspberry, citrus, mango, apricot, lemon balm, mint, linden and basil appear in exact Czech liquid offers. Familiarity and personal associations remain unmeasured. | Ask what the drink reminds the person of, before prompting with fruit/herb names. Measure fruity, tea-like, floral, herbal and perfume/medicinal associations without labeling any intrinsically good/bad. |
| **Body/texture** | No target-population optimum identified. Water-like, juice-like and fuller textures can imply different uses. | Record thin/watery, full-bodied, syrupy, coating, chalky and sediment/grit separately. Re-test after processing and storage; a formula's mineral/fibre/emulsion system may change texture. |
| **Carbonation** | Local products include lightly sparkling mineral water, sparkling cans and still mineral-water options. No evidence establishes a universal preference for sparkling functional drinks. | Compare still and low-carbonation versions within category, with equal temperature and fresh-opening times. An energy-can comparison alone cannot choose a bedtime format. |
| **Aftertaste** | Existing public customer material raises aftertaste as a hypothesis; it does not quantify incidence. | Record sweetness, bitter, metallic/mineral, herbal and drying persistence at a fixed time after swallowing; ask whether anything makes another sip less appealing. Capture first sip, end of full serving and next occasion separately. |

## Repertoire without national stereotypes

“Local” should mean a documented offer or a participant's own experience. Fruit/herb combinations can be recognizable to some people without being universally liked. The repertoire above suggests three **testable bridges**: raspberry/citrus for a clear workday drink; apricot/citrus or tea-fruit for a softer alternative; linden/basil or restrained bitter citrus for social/food use. Lemon balm is an aroma/association option, not a demonstrated calming effect. Sea buckthorn's regional story cannot substitute for acceptance: the Polish experiment makes its strong sour/aroma character a reason for an exploratory arm rather than the default launch flavour.

Benchmark flavour names should not dictate the recipe. An imported tropical-fruit or sweet energy profile may win locally; an elderflower or sour-cherry concept may lose. Ask participants to identify familiar notes, choose freely and explain the occasion before attaching national/traditional messaging.

## First research choices and neutral Czech prompts

Begin with recruited adults who already buy/use eligible workday drinks, including unsweetened and sweetened users. Compare a small set of current local controls to map dissatisfaction. Then commission safe matched prototypes for a **sweetness × acidity screen in one workday flavour**, followed by still vs low-carbonation testing in the finalist base. Compare caffeine status/dose in a separate labeled-choice exercise; blind sensory liking cannot establish focus efficacy.

Run social/food-pairing and bedtime arms separately. For social use, contrast mild fruit-botanical vs more bitter citrus; for bedtime test a still, restrained-acid, caffeine-free fruit/herbal direction against an appropriate eligible control. These are hypotheses. No appropriate locally available dedicated bedtime RTD was verified in this workstream. Do not substitute prepared herbal tea as a product benchmark, although interviewees may discuss it as a habit.

Use ordinary Czech, piloted for comprehension. Ask overall liking before detailed descriptors, then:

- **„Jak vám tento nápoj celkově chutná?“** (overall liking; not “How refreshing/healthy is it?”)
- **„Jak intenzivní je sladkost / kyselost / hořkost?“** (intensity only)
- **„Je pro vás sladkost příliš nízká, tak akorát, nebo příliš vysoká?“** (repeat independently for acidity and bubbles; do not combine them)
- **„Co vám vůně nebo chuť připomíná?“** (open response before a vocabulary list)
- **„Co případně zůstává v ústech po napití?“** (then time-specific intensity measures)
- **„Kdy, pokud vůbec, byste si takový nápoj dal/a?“** (allow no occasion)
- **„Chtěl/a byste teď pokračovat v pití? Proč ano, nebo proč ne?“**
- After a complete, permitted serving: **„Dal/a byste si tento nápoj znovu při podobné příležitosti?“** Ask separately whether they want another serving now; offer water/no drink and comply with ingredient and label limits.

Record current drink habits rather than assigning tastes from nationality, gender or age. The small first panel can identify issues and competing preferences; it cannot estimate Czech market shares. Food, exercise, work and bedtime context may change ratings, so re-test the shortlisted packaged drink at its relevant real occasion and realistic full volume before a launch choice.
