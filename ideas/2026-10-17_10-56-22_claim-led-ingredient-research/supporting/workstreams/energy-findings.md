# Energy, alertness and caffeine alternatives: specialist findings

Research date 17 October 2026. This is a targeted evidence review, not a systematic review, approved label, recipe or exposure recommendation. All amounts below are **study exposures**. Primary abstracts are explicitly distinguished from full-text extraction in `energy-studies.csv`. Lead integration owns legal clearance, commercial language and finished-beverage feasibility.

## Decision findings

**A reliable, noticeable, sustained, stimulant-free substitute for caffeine in a casual EU soda is not established by this evidence.** That is an analytical conclusion about the combined evidence and transfer requirements, not proof that no future ingredient can work. Genuine caffeine-free compounds can produce selected cognitive or fatigue effects; those effects are not interchangeable with feeling energetic. Paraxanthine is the clearest stimulant-substitution lead, but has small sponsored studies, selective results, incomplete chronic/sleep-risk data and an unresolved ordinary-beverage authorization gate. Theobromine's structural similarity and longer residence do not rescue directly negative human mood/vigilance evidence. [EN02](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23764688/); [EN03](https://palmerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/Baggott-et-al-Psychopharm-2013.pdf); [EN04](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8622427/); [EN05](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8708375/); [EN06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11089923/)

There are narrower, scientifically credible **research questions** around ginseng and demanding-task fatigue, tyrosine and resource-demanding performance, and creatine during sleep deprivation. None establishes that one palatable shelf-stable can noticeably and safely improves everyday energy in rested, well-nourished adults. “Natural sustained energy” must not be inferred from an adaptogen category, an ATP mechanism or the time over which a study happened to collect data.

## Caffeine reference and fair comparison

Caffeine is a central stimulant with clearer evidence for alertness, reduced sleepiness and vigilance than for universal improvements in intelligence, memory, motivation or productivity. A randomized trial in 102 light/nondependent users found subjective arousal and vigilance improvements, while one memory task worsened. Its effects therefore cannot all be dismissed as withdrawal reversal, although withdrawal reversal materially confounds studies in habitual consumers asked to abstain. [EN01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16541243/)

An active comparator must match outcome, test conditions, time of day, habitual intake and abstinence; equal milligram doses are not evidence of pharmacological equivalence. Caffeine can raise blood pressure and cause unpleasant stimulation; tolerance and withdrawal complicate repeated use. EFSA's 200 mg single and 400 mg/day adult reference levels concern healthy nonpregnant adults across sources and are not recommended doses, guarantees of tolerability or per-can limits. Even 100 mg near bedtime can disturb some adults' sleep. No ingredient in this review earns an unqualified “no jitters,” “no crash,” “non-drowsy,” or driving-compatible promise. [EN20](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine)

## E02 Theobromine

**Identity/provenance.** 3,7-dimethylxanthine, a compound present in cacao and related to caffeine. Purified material and a cocoa extract are not interchangeable: cocoa includes other constituents and may contain caffeine. Supplier source, purity and caffeine assay are required before calling a drink caffeine-free. “Found in nature” does not specify industrial extraction or synthesis.

**Evidence and experience.** Two direct comparator studies are unusually useful. The beverage study found no consistent mood/vigilance benefit while its caffeine control did. The capsule study found little desirable subjective action and more unpleasant effects at higher exposure. Both defeat the inference that adenosine-receptor activity necessarily produces a good caffeine replacement. Felt onset, consistent beneficial duration and a commercially meaningful effect are **unknown/unestablished**, not “slow-release energy.” See EN-ST02/03 for extraction. [EN02](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23764688/), [EN03](https://palmerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/Baggott-et-al-Psychopharm-2013.pdf)

**Safety/application judgment.** Heart-rate increases, nausea, headache and dysphoria at higher study exposures undermine an escalation strategy. Data do not set a safe casual multi-can maximum or demonstrate that insomnia-sensitive people can substitute it freely. Its priority should be low for an energy proposition. A cocoa taste/ritual rationale can stand separately from stimulation, subject to caffeine content and formulation. No reviewed clinical effect authorizes health wording or ordinary-beverage use.

## E03 Paraxanthine

**Identity/provenance.** 1,7-dimethylxanthine; a major metabolite formed after caffeine ingestion, and itself a central stimulant. Administering paraxanthine is chemically different from administering caffeine, but does not meet an ordinary consumer's likely expectation of avoiding stimulant effects merely because a product is chemically caffeine-free. The EU application examined is specifically for **synthetic** paraxanthine; proposed uses and applicant safety analogies are not authorizations. [EN27](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/330ca889-27cc-4db5-835b-a0670180b85a_en?filename=novel-food_sum_ongoing-app_2022-90305.pdf)

**Evidence and experience.** Three small studies of ENFINITY were extracted. The first two describe positive changes but their key omnibus treatment-by-time tests are often null; selected timepoint comparisons must not be converted into a six-hour continuously noticeable effect. The head-to-head study is promising only for specified outcomes after exercise; its title does not establish superiority for a rested office consumer. All three come from substantially the same sponsored network. Independent replication, a clearly specified primary outcome and adequate control of multiplicity are needed. [EN04](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8622427/), [EN05](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8708375/), [EN06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11089923/)

**Safety/application judgment.** Seven-day tolerability and no adverse events in tiny selected samples cannot establish rare risks, chronic use, tolerance, withdrawal, cardiovascular susceptibility or sleep effects. The applicant's analogy to caffeine's intake levels must not be presented as EFSA's paraxanthine safe limit. It is a research watchlist candidate with a high regulatory gate, not a primary EU launch recommendation. Branded-material purity and contaminants need matching before transferring trials to generic material. No caffeine or paraxanthine stacking formula is proposed.

## E04 L-tyrosine

**Identity/provenance.** The L-amino acid is a precursor in catecholamine synthesis and occurs in protein. This mechanism is not proof that extra substrate raises desired motivation or stimulation in an adequately nourished person. The studied free L-tyrosine must not be substituted by N-acetyl-L-tyrosine on a simple milligram-for-milligram assumption; equivalent absorption/evidence was not established. Source/process and purity require a material specification; “amino acid” does not demonstrate fermentation versus extraction versus synthesis.

**Evidence and experience.** Overnight-work evidence concerns preservation under sleep loss. The 2 g study in orange juice is a useful direct liquid-delivery precedent, but showed demanding-task accuracy rather than a mood/physiological stimulation response. This is a plausible **task-performance research route**, not established caffeine-free energy. Onset/duration in a rested general population, independent repeatability and perceptibility after a can remain unknown. [EN07](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7794222/), [EN08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3863934/)

**Safety/application judgment.** The clinical studies screened healthy volunteers and do not establish everyday repeated beverage safety across medication users or vulnerable groups. Medication/medical-history review and cumulative exposures are unresolved. The sleep-loss study's 150 mg/kg equals 10.5 g at 70 kg (arithmetic only), far from a decorative label amount. Fresh orange-juice delivery does not establish acid/heat/shelf stability or palatability. Ordinary-beverage use and claim status need exact-material review. No motivation, ADHD, mood-disorder or sleep-replacement claim follows from these experiments.

## E05 Creatine monohydrate

**Identity/provenance.** Use evidence for creatine monohydrate, not another salt/ester marketed as superior. Its involvement in cellular energy buffering and presence in meat/fish do not establish that supplemental material is animal-derived or “natural.” Supply process and impurities require documentation. Shelf-stable acidic liquid delivery is a separate feasibility question.

**Evidence and experience.** A larger preregistered six-week trial found small, uncertain cognitive effects; it did not substantiate broad enhancement. Two sleep-deprivation experiments provide a distinct acute research signal, including a 2026 follow-up, but use much larger single exposures than a typical lightly fortified soda. The 2026 paper still observed increasing fatigue/sleepiness and memory deterioration. Reporting “up to 12%” as a product-wide energy improvement would be misleading. These studies do not show that habitual users need less sleep. [EN09](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10647179/), [EN10](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54249-9), [EN11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13119191/)

**Safety/application judgment.** Gastrointestinal/other reported side effects were more common than placebo in the six-week study; the small 2026 acute study reported none. Neither sets an unrestricted repeated-can exposure. Health conditions, renal assessment context and other supplements require competent safety review rather than a blanket “safe” statement. A 70 kg arithmetic illustration is 14 g or 24.5 g in the acute studies; it is not a formulation recommendation.

**Claims gate.** EFSA in 2024 did not establish cause and effect for improved cognitive function. Commission Regulation (EU) 2026/1118 subsequently refused the proposed claim. The 2026 trial does not itself reverse that legal decision, and existing physical-performance territory cannot be relabeled as felt mental energy. [EN21](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2024.9100), [EN24](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?qid=1791117117902&uri=OJ%3AL_202601118)

## E06 Taurine

**Identity/provenance.** 2-aminoethanesulfonic acid, a naturally occurring human/food constituent; being endogenous is not proof of benefits from extra intake. Beverage-grade source/process, purity and suitability must be documented. It is not caffeine and should not automatically be described as a stimulant.

**Evidence and experience.** Component studies are more informative than complete energy-drink trials. Extracted studies show mixed/null independent cognitive outcomes and do not support a reliable felt-energy boost. A trend toward attenuating caffeine on one task is not established synergistic calm-alert energy; neither is a combination better merely because it contains two familiar ingredients. The small flavored-drink study confirms test delivery in 250 ml, not efficacy or shelf stability. [EN12](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0091305712001980), [EN13](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0195666313000044), [EN14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4226910/)

**Safety/application judgment.** EFSA's taurine assessment evaluated particular exposure scenarios and ingredient safety, not all energy-drink mixtures. Its toxicological NOAEL is not a human serving recommendation. Multi-can exposure and other sources still need review. Consumer recognition may be strong in the energy-drink context, but that is a separate hypothesis for the perception workstream. For caffeine-free alertness, taurine presently supplies a stronger category association than a demonstrated acute sensation. [EN26](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2009.935)

## E07 Rhodiola rosea root/rhizome extracts

**Identity/provenance.** Specify *Rhodiola rosea* L., root/rhizome, extraction solvent, drug-extract ratio and marker profile. SHR-5 is not interchangeable with unspecified “rhodiola,” another species or isolated salidroside. EMA characterizes the studied SHR-5 as DER 2.5–5:1, first ethanol 70% extraction and second water extraction. Marker percentages alone do not establish matching full composition. [EN22](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf)

**Evidence and experience.** The better relevant comparisons concern fatigue syndrome and night-duty physicians, not a casual rested drinker. Changes after two to four weeks cannot promise one-can onset. EMA considers clinical studies limited and does not establish well-established medicinal efficacy; traditional medicinal recognition is not equivalent to a positive modern RCT evidence rating. The headline “adaptogen” does not specify a measurable effect or reliable lasting energy. [EN15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19016404/), [EN16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11081987/)

**Safety/application judgment.** Short selected trials reporting few adverse events do not establish safety in pregnancy, children, medication users or repeated social-drinking exposure. Exact interaction and safety appraisal should use the current EMA monograph/assessment and food-law review. This route may merit preparation-specific fatigue research after clearance; it is low priority for a noticeable one-can energy promise. An herbal medicine's traditional-use conditions are not authorization to make the corresponding claim on soda. Unspecified “3% rosavins/1% salidroside” suppliers do not automatically inherit SHR-5 findings.

## E08 Panax ginseng G115

**Identity/provenance.** Distinguish *Panax ginseng* C.A.Mey. root and a standardized G115 extract from *P. quinquefolius*, *Eleutherococcus senticosus* (“Siberian ginseng”), leaves, red-ginseng processing or isolated ginsenosides. G115 is commonly characterized by total ginsenosides; exact extraction and compositional match need verification in a proposed material. Different source/process/plant part can change the constituents even when a shared name appears on labels. [EN28](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-panax-ginseng-camey-radix-revision-1_en.pdf)

**Evidence and experience.** This is one of the more plausible non-caffeine acute **task-fatigue** research leads, but the dose-response is not straightforward and attention/mood outcomes are mixed. The memory study included reduced late alertness and slower attention at some doses. “Effects measured up to six hours” therefore cannot become “six hours of energy.” Generic ginseng evidence and branded extract evidence should remain separate. No extracted study establishes equivalence with caffeine. [EN17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15982990/), [EN18](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1028415X.2001.11747370)

**Safety/application judgment.** NCCIH identifies insomnia, potential glucose lowering, clotting and autoimmune concerns and medication interactions; pregnancy/childhood require particular care. Insomnia is especially relevant to a non-overstimulating/no-crash promise. No appropriate casual multi-can safe maximum was derived. The formulation and legal route must be reviewed before prioritizing an exact extract for an ordinary beverage. Food use, medicinal boundaries and permitted communication remain different decisions. [EN23](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng)

## N03 Carbohydrate/glucose route

Glucose supplies metabolic energy: it can be factually quantified in grams and kilocalories. That is different from proving stimulation or a persistent improvement in experienced energy. In a small fasted, demanding-task factorial experiment, 25 g glucose and G115 each improved selected outcomes; their combination had no demonstrated cognitive synergy. One cannot infer that a small sugar amount in a low-calorie drink reproduces the study, or that a glucose effect persists through a whole afternoon. [EN19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16401645/)

This amount is 100 kcal by ordinary 4 kcal/g carbohydrate arithmetic, illustrating the conflict with low-sugar positioning and repeated soda consumption. “No crash” requires a defined subjective/rebound endpoint and comparator; blood-glucose dynamics alone do not prove a felt crash. Lead's separate glucose study and nutritional/claims review should govern synthesis. A fuel/hydration product for exercise is a different occasion from a social or office alertness drink.

## What a comparison programme would have to establish

After ingredient legality, safety and stability gates, a credible investigation would compare the actual end-of-shelf-life product with a sensory-matched placebo and a prespecified caffeine reference. It would preregister one clear primary experience/outcome, sample enough participants for a **consumer-meaningful** effect, control caffeine dependence/withdrawal, assess both rested and specifically fatigued strata, and collect subjective energy/alertness separately from objective vigilance or accuracy. Onset, duration, later fatigue, jitteriness, sleep, cardiovascular responses and repeated-use tolerability need planned measurement. A noninferiority/equivalence claim needs a justified margin and adequate power; failure to detect a difference is not equivalence. Product evidence still does not automatically authorize an EU health claim.

No direct social-disinhibition, social-confidence or interaction evidence was established for these energy candidates. Their inclusion in a social tonic should not import a physiological social-ease claim.

## Existing-repository evidence map contribution

The repository already recognizes caffeine's distinct role, warns that caffeine-plus-theanine evidence does not validate theanine alone, and identifies paraxanthine's EU application as unresolved. The market and sensory reports preserve product facts and formulation hypotheses rather than demonstrated founder-product effects. This review adds direct negative theobromine controls, ingredient-separated taurine trials, nuanced ginseng/tyrosine findings, and current creatine evidence/claim refusal. It also strengthens a recurring caveat: a positive abstract/title or a selected within-group change is not a robust common effect across a study. No prior market “focus,” “natural energy,” or no-crash language was treated as scientific evidence.
