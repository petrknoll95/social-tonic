# Independent challenge: functional opportunity and commercial feasibility

Research/check date: **17 October 2026, Europe/Prague (CEST, UTC+02:00)**. Read the complete functional memo and source ledger, commercial memo, and the evolving integrated report. Reopened decisive CANS, office-channel, manufacturer and EFSA sources. This is a challenge to inference and project fit, not a legal opinion or supplier audit. No companies were contacted and no products, testing or production were commissioned.

## Conclusion

**No development or production spend is justified by the present evidence.** F1 merits, at most, a cheap attempt to falsify a specific customer/venue access problem. It is not presently an underserved functional category. A credible decision could be to distribute or recommend an existing product, or to do nothing. S1/F1 as two research tracks rather than two launch recommendations is defensible, provided the executive summary carries the same dose-specific caution as the revised F1 card.

The lead's F1 card has already been corrected after the early challenge: it now selects no dose and explicitly distinguishes a hypothetical 40–60 mg preference concept from evidence at 75 mg. The executive wording “stronger ingredient science” still needs this practical qualification; otherwise a reader may remember an efficacy advantage the actual proposed low-dose product does not yet possess.

## Material challenge 1: F1 must not borrow the wrong caffeine evidence

**Verified finding:** EFSA specifically evaluated an application proposing an alertness claim at 40 mg. Its 2014 opinion found the relationship unsubstantiated under those conditions, noting that most reaction-time outcomes at 40–less than 75 mg did not show an effect. It reiterated the prior favourable scientific assessment at at least 75 mg. Adoption was 5 February 2014; publication 20 February 2014; checked today. [EFSA opinion](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/3574).

**Implication:** familiar caffeine pharmacology does not establish the effect of F1 at the initially suggested 40–60 mg serving. This does not prove nobody feels a lower dose, nor does a favourable EFSA opinion alone authorise a commercial claim. The regulator workstream controls current claims status. Do not increase the dose merely to pursue an alertness narrative: that would change the alleged modest-dose customer job.

**Required decision rule:** if research only wins interest by promising stronger focus, no crash, freedom from overstimulation, or sleep safety, F1 has not cleared its premise. If customers already buy a measured modest portion because they like its taste and convenience, this can support a preference proposition without asserting a validated cognitive advantage. Their willingness to pay over tea/free coffee remains unknown.

## Material challenge 2: a close product and the channel already exist

**Verified finding:** dm Czechia lists CANS+ Performance at **39.50 CZK including VAT for 330 ml**, shipping extra, online in stock. Its stated 12.8 mg caffeine per 100 ml yields **42.24 mg per can**. That is directly inside the earlier F1 concept range. The same page describes green-tea extract, fruit, magnesium and vitamins; this is availability and label evidence, not proof of efficacy, legality, sales or repeat. [Exact dm listing](https://www.dm.cz/p/d/3149633/cans-performance).

**Verified finding:** CANS' own office page offers wholesale prices and free fridge loan for subscriptions above ten cartons. The reviewed office page discusses original CANS and does not establish that Performance is included or actually supplied to offices. [CANS office offer](https://cz.cans.com/pages/cans-do-kancelare).

**Inference:** neither “modest caffeine” nor “sell to offices” is meaningful differentiation by itself. An outlet-by-outlet access gap could exist, but online supply and an incumbent office offer make that something to demonstrate, not assume. Free coffee, smaller coffee, tea and declining a purchase are essential comparators. We have no secured route, exclusive customer access, purchaser/payor definition or price premium. A channel may be unattractive because employees will not pay and employers already subsidise coffee; this is as plausible as an overlooked opportunity.

## Economics and decision gates

The commercial memo's arithmetic is correct: 6,000 / (12 assumed outlets × 26 assumed weeks) = **19.2 cans per outlet per week**; 4,000 gives **12.8**. It is a stock-clearance illustration, not break-even, sell-through forecast or expected repeat. Twelve outlets and 26 usable selling weeks are unestablished. Samples/rejects, late listings, reorders and retailer remaining-life requirements alter the viable plan. A line's declared shelf life does not imply that all of it is commercially usable.

The contribution ledger correctly excludes VAT/refundable deposits and distinguishes retailer markup from net brand receipts. Keep fixed formulation/design/setup costs and working capital visible in any later launch cash plan even when not included in per-order contribution. The current report does not invent margins, CAC or carrier prices. Its shipping-mass calculations are correct, but mass alone does not prove a DTC channel unviable.

**Before development:** establish a repeated recent purchase occasion with an identifiable dissatisfaction that existing CANS/tea/coffee do not solve, distinguish employee from employer payment, check exact lawful presentation, and define the one pack/process that matches the proposed occasion. The cheapest initial exercise is desk/menu/office-offer comparison; interviews and sensory work remain future separately authorised work under this assignment's boundaries.

**Before production:** obtain a complete project-specific accepted specification and written all-in quotation, demonstrate actual paid preference and reordering in an appropriately authorised later validation, and build a cash/inventory plan from those observations. Neither a manufacturer MOQ nor a subscription checkbox is evidence that these gates have been met.

**No forced alternative:** F2 nutrient-routine substitution has no demonstrated tablet inconvenience; F3 sleep burden is not sleep-shot demand; F4 generic senior protein deficiency is contradicted by the supplied Dutch evidence. Easier claims or a measurable health burden do not compensate for absent product-specific paid demand. These remain subordinate or rejected hypotheses, as the functional memo concludes.
