# Calm, stress, attention and social-interaction evidence workstream

Research/access date: **17 October 2026, Europe/Prague (+02:00)**. Lead integration input. No formula, serving, safe maximum, claim, driving assurance or ingredient approval is proposed. Quantities below identify experiments; they are not consumption instructions. Companion files: `calm-studies.csv` (22 primary study records) and `calm-sources.csv` (32 sources).

## Decision-relevant conclusions

1. **L-theanine is not established as a noticeable caffeine-free energy replacement.** Some controlled attention-task findings are positive; energy, fatigue, alertness and attention must remain separate. A July 2026 meta-analysis reports a choice-reaction-time signal but no significant fatigue benefit. The authors' favourable overall interpretation should not be converted into a guaranteed felt effect, equivalence to caffeine or a safety guarantee. [CA24](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-026-03727-9)
2. **The direct evidence for calm is mixed and often context-dependent.** Biomarker or EEG change is not the same as feeling relaxed. Several studies described positively in abstracts/conclusions have null placebo-controlled subjective outcomes. See extraction records CA-ST04–07 and CA-ST13–14.
3. **Lemon balm has unusually relevant beverage-format evidence**, but preparation, sweetener and food matrix materially change results. One drink experiment is useful for deciding what to investigate, not for declaring any lemon-balm soda effective. Calmness can coexist with reduced alertness. [CA11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4245564/), [CA12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15272110/)
4. **Repeated-use evidence is a poor match to “feel it after one social drink.”** Ashwagandha and saffron have selected stress/mood findings after weeks. Lion's mane is preliminary, preparation-dependent and often overinterpreted. These do not establish a first-can effect or alcohol substitution.
5. **Do not make a blanket statement that no ingredient has any social evidence.** Tryptophan has direct daily-interaction self-report trials, including conflicting results in different populations. Saffron has a secondary social-relationships questionnaire finding after 56 days. Neither establishes acute social confidence, disinhibition or alcohol-equivalent effects. [CA14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7882499/), [CA22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1488902/), [CA23](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4571634/)

## Repository evidence map

Reviewed the root README/research index, existing ideas opportunity report, topic 13 ingredient/sensory/CBD work and topic 09 botanical/Czech enforcement work. Existing material correctly places taste/occasion before an unapproved physiological promise, distinguishes product marketing from efficacy, and requires exact botanical preparation and claim-transition evidence. These are useful working principles, not independent scientific substantiation.

The earlier sensory assessment contains plausible risk hypotheses but no finished-product sensory results. This work adds preparation-matched human trials and important nulls. The existing topic 09 work already treats the 2025 C-386/23 judgment and Article 28 routes separately; retain that nuance. No old recommendation should become a “cleared ingredient” by repetition.

Evidence gaps remain: Czech representative recognition and willingness-to-pay data for these ingredients; observed interaction outcomes in healthy social drinkers using the exact proposed drink; reliable noticeability thresholds; repeated-can exposure; shelf-life potency and impurity data; ingredient process/plant-part proof; and current exact-material beverage permission. These gaps cannot be filled by copying a competitor's label.

## E01 — L-theanine

**Identity and evidence transfer.** L-theanine is the L enantiomer of a non-protein amino acid associated with tea. Tea-derived isolates, enzymatic synthesis and fermentation-related production must be distinguished on specifications. The Hidese trial explicitly used an enzymatically synthesized Suntheanine material. A tea image does not show that a supplier's theanine was extracted from tea. Evidence transfer between preparations is more plausible for verified identical high-purity L-theanine than for chemically different botanical extracts, but purity, enantiomer profile, residual caffeine and other constituents still require review. [CA06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6836118/)

**Perception versus experience.** “Calm focus,” tea-associated naturalness and “cancels jitters” are familiar marketing narratives; this work does not establish representative consumer familiarity. The theanine–caffeine combination cannot substantiate a caffeine-free benefit. Rogers' factorial study found a blood-pressure interaction without a significant jitteriness benefit; vascular response and felt calm are distinct endpoints. [CA02](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17891480/)

**Human findings.** The older Haskell and Dodd factorial studies give no reliable standalone energy story and conflicting combination profiles. White's commercially formulated drink is a blend and analysed a selected stress-responder subset. Hidese's repeated-use total cognition result was null against placebo in the whole sample. Evans' 2021 Results section is more cautious than its plain-language summary; Moulin 2024 shows why within-group improvements cannot substitute for a placebo comparison. See CA-ST01–07 for exact exposures and findings. [CA01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18006208/), [CA03](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4480845/), [CA05](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4728665/), [CA07](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8475422/), [CA08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11263523/)

**Current synthesis.** Gerolymos et al. pooled 31 trials (1,168 participants). Their publisher abstract reports single-dose choice reaction time SMD 0.51 (95% CI 0.25–0.77), acute stress SMD 0.31 heavily influenced by high-risk studies, and no significant fatigue effect. Full review access was unavailable; subgroup composition and all analytic decisions were not independently verified. This supports a qualified attention-research lead, not broad “energy” or “no crash.” [CA24](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-026-03727-9)

**Expected experience and timing.** A person might notice less stress in a particular demanding context; many may notice nothing. General rested-state calm, sustained energy and an onset/duration perceptible in daily life remain unknown. A significant endpoint at 1 or 3 hours does not prove continuous benefit between those points. No selected trial establishes outgoing behaviour or alcohol-like disinhibition.

**Safety and repeated use.** Short trials provide some tolerability observations, including headache, metallic taste and dry mouth; they cannot define an unrestricted multi-can limit. Pregnancy, childhood, co-medication and chronic repeated-can use are not cleared here. Absence of a cognitive deficit on a small laboratory battery is not evidence of driving compatibility. Do not infer absence of tolerance or withdrawal from studies that did not test them.

**EU communication and beverage relevance.** The historical EFSA opinion did not substantiate the proposed cognition/stress/sleep claims. New positive trials do not themselves authorise copy. Ingredient identity/process/category and current official claim status require the separate regulatory assessment. Actual beverage studies show liquid administration is feasible; they do not establish carbonated acidic shelf-life stability. Ingredient cost: **quote required**. [CA25](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2011.2238)

**Analytical judgment:** retain for exact-material calm/attention research only after beverage legality is established; reject it as the scientific anchor of a promised energy boost.

## C01 — Withania somnifera (ashwagandha)

**Identity.** Root-only KSM-66 with a specified withanolide assay is different from root-plus-leaf extracts, purified withanolides or another solvent/marker system. The 2019 Shoden paper itself calls its material root extract and reports 35% withanolide glycosides; match that trial specification rather than silently borrowing specifications from newer commercial products. Marker percentages measured by different analytical methods are not automatically potency-equivalent. [CA09](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3573577/), [CA10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6750292/)

**Perception.** The adaptogen/stress-resilience story may create recognition, but recognition, purchase influence and expected felt effect require target-market research. “Adaptogen” is not a human outcome measure. Neither traditional use nor plant origin guarantees safety.

**Demonstration and experience.** Selected stressed adults have improved on some stress/anxiety measures after repeated administration. A small root-extract trial was positive; another showed HAM-A benefit with a nonsignificant DASS comparison. Neither measured acute convivial conversation. Onset after one drink is unknown; chronic questionnaire improvement is not proof of a prolonged first-dose effect. [CA09](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3573577/), [CA10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6750292/)

**Safety.** BfR flags gastrointestinal, sedative, endocrine and liver concerns, advises vulnerable groups to avoid supplementation, and could not derive a health-based safe intake. NCCIH notes insufficient long-term evidence and possible interactions with sedatives, thyroid medication, immunosuppressants, antihypertensives and diabetes medicines. A short RCT without serious events cannot rule out rare liver injury. [CA27](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/349/ashwagandha-food-supplements-with-potential-health-risks.pdf), [CA28](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha)

**Beverage/EU gate.** Ordinary soda use cannot be inferred from supplements. Exact root/leaf/process and national status need clearance. Possible drowsiness and several-can exposure conflict with a non-drowsy social promise. Bitter/extract sensory burden and aqueous stability are formulation questions here, not measured results. Beverage-grade availability and cost require supplier documentation and **quote required**; none contacted.

**Analytical judgment:** scientifically interesting repeated-use route but weak first-line fit for casual social soda because efficacy timescale, safety and market-specific legality all need resolution.

## C02 — Melissa officinalis leaf (lemon balm)

**Identity and perception.** Culinary herb, essential oil, powdered leaf and concentrated leaf extracts are different preparations. Rosmarinic acid is a useful marker, not proof that two extracts have equivalent pharmacology. The herb can contribute a familiar evening ritual and aroma; consumer recognition and purchase-driver status are unmeasured.

**Evidence and expected experience.** The beverage-format experiment is the strongest transfer lead in this set but has missing-data and sweetener-comparator limitations. A separate capsule study illustrates the central trade-off: increased calmness accompanied reduced alertness at the higher tested exposure. Therefore “natural calm without drowsiness” remains a compound proposition requiring its own evidence. Noticeability and consistent real-world duration are unknown. [CA11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4245564/), [CA12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15272110/)

**Safety and regulatory interpretation.** EMA's medicinal assessment rests on traditional use for mild stress/sleep indications, which is different from proof of efficacy and different again from food-claim authorisation. Concentrated use in vulnerable groups or with sedating medicines needs assessment; no casual multi-can exposure maximum is established. Do not transfer medicinal instructions into a soda formulation. [CA31](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/melissae-folium)

**Liquid fit.** The tested aqueous extract was water-soluble and a palatable drink was produced. Those findings establish a feasible experimental matrix, not a verified industrial shelf life or carbonated-can process. Botanical aroma/colour, sensory effects, marker retention, sediment and processing should be measured using the intended extract. Cost: **quote required**. Herbal flavouring use at ordinary food exposures does not substantiate physiological calm.

**Analytical judgment:** promising candidate for a sensory-led evening drink and controlled follow-up research; weak basis for an immediate relaxation guarantee.

## C03 — Crocus sativus stigma extracts (saffron)

**Identity.** Stigma, petals, crocin, safranal and branded multi-constituent extracts must remain separate. affron and Safr'Inside have different material specifications; a generic “saffron extract” cannot inherit both dossiers. Natural saffron and synthetic safranal are distinct research preparations, despite safranal occurring in saffron.

**Perception and evidence.** Premium spice, mood support and colour can provide a consumer story, but recognition as a functional-drink ingredient is not established here. A positive low-mood study of affron is tempered by EFSA's assessment that the submitted overall evidence was insufficient. Jackson's primary mood endpoint was null; its secondary social-relationships measure is not acute observed sociability. [CA13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28735826/), [CA26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8251646/), [CA14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7882499/)

**Acute interpretation trap.** The 2023 extract/safranal experiment used sublingual administration. Its significant subjective pairwise signal belonged to isolated synthetic safranal; this cannot be advertised as a swallowed saffron soda working within minutes. [CA15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10346743/)

**Expected experience and safety.** Repeated-use mood improvement in selected low-mood adults is more plausible than a first-can social buzz; general healthy/rested enhancement, noticeable magnitude and lasting acute effects remain unknown. Gastrointestinal upset was more frequent with saffron in Jackson. Pregnancy and concurrent medicines were commonly excluded, leaving exposure gaps; no unlimited consumption or safe social-use maximum follows.

**Beverage/EU gate.** Food-spice history does not clear every concentrated extract or claim. A specific favourable trial does not override the claims framework. Colour/aroma may be valuable sensory components but potency, heat/light/storage behaviour and exact can compatibility require testing. Low material mass alone does not prove economical feasibility; **quote required**.

**Analytical judgment:** a possible premium sensory route; a chronic mood research proposition would be a different product hypothesis from immediate social ease.

## C04 — Oral gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)

**Identity and transfer.** GABA is 4-aminobutanoic acid. Purified synthetic/fermentation-derived GABA, GABA-enriched fermented foods and rice-germ extracts are not automatically interchangeable. Fermentation can supply a consumer-naturalness story; actual strain/process, impurities, residual matrix and food status still matter. Do not confuse oral GABA with phenibut, gabapentin or receptor-modulating medicines.

**Evidence.** Oral administration does not establish delivery to the brain in the way marketing diagrams imply. Human stress/sleep evidence is limited and the central versus peripheral mechanism remains uncertain. Yoto provides a context-specific vigour finding alongside null subjective VAS measures; Byun provides a repeated-use insomnia-population latency finding rather than general daytime social calm. [CA16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22203366/), [CA17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6031986/), [CA30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7527439/)

**Experience and safety.** Reliable felt relaxation, onset, duration, non-sedation and repeated-can effects in healthy rested adults remain unknown. USP found no dedicated healthy-person safety trials in its review; some data indicate lower blood pressure and a potential antihypertensive interaction. Pregnancy/lactation data were absent. Its admission for monograph development is not an EU authorisation or safe daily intake. [CA29](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399837/)

**Liquid/claims fit.** Water solubility is favourable, but the finished acidic/carbonated shelf-life and sensory profile remain unverified. An exact source/process/category permission is necessary; no reviewed data support a claim that this ingredient reproduces alcohol's social action. Cost: **quote required**.

**Analytical judgment:** low-priority experimental route; weak evidence-to-expectation match and unresolved beverage/claim status.

## C05 — Hericium erinaceus (lion's mane)

**Identity.** Fruiting body, mycelium, mycelium-plus-growth-substrate biomass, aqueous extracts and erinacine-enriched preparations are not equivalent. Hericenone/erinacine or beta-glucan labels do not establish human efficacy equivalence. The 2023 pilot used a proprietary mushroom complex, not a verified fruiting-body-only extract. The 2026 preprint used combined fruiting-body and mycelial biomass. [CA19](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10675414/), [CA20](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.13.26350781v1.full)

**Perception versus demonstration.** “Nootropic,” nerve-growth and brain-fog narratives are mechanistic/marketing associations. Human trials do not show that a soda regrows nerves or restores clarity. The small cookie trial's depression comparison was null between groups. The young-adult pilot's isolated task improvement came with negative and null results. Emerging chronic evidence is worth following but is not first-can energy. [CA18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20834180/), [CA19](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10675414/)

**Expected experience and safety.** A consistent noticeable acute effect in healthy rested adults is unestablished. Limited chronic signals come from heterogeneous preparations/populations. Sparse adverse-event reporting does not prove universal safety; mushroom allergy, contamination/identity, co-medication and vulnerable-group data require review. No tolerance/withdrawal or repeated-social-serving conclusion was established.

**Beverage/EU gate.** Gram-scale powders raise an obvious dispersion/sediment/taste development question; that is a testable formulation concern, not a measured failure of this project. A clear liquid extract may differ from the studied preparation. Food history for an edible fruiting body does not automatically cover mycelium or selectively concentrated extracts. Exact material legality, end-of-life assay and cost remain unresolved; **quote required**.

**Analytical judgment:** high story appeal relative to evidence for this brief; do not anchor immediate focus or sociability on it.

## Social outcomes, expectation and ritual

**What was actually measured?** A stress scale, HRV change, clinical anxiety score and social-behaviour record occupy different evidentiary levels. Even questionnaires with “social” in their name may measure functioning across weeks, rather than confidence at a party. The best direct social evidence found in this workstream concerns repeated tryptophan use; it is selected-population, self-reported and inconsistent. Hogenelst's counterpoint prevents a universal serotonin-to-friendliness inference. [CA22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1488902/), [CA23](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4571634/)

The large alcohol-reference study illustrates suitable direct endpoints: unfamiliar groups, observed conversational behaviour/facial affect and self-reported bonding, with both placebo and acknowledged alcohol-free controls. The alcohol result cannot be transferred to any calm ingredient. A related experiment shows expectation can alter group risk choices; that makes expectation scientifically relevant, not automatically beneficial. Both reports derive from the same project and are not independent replications. [CA21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5462438/), [CA32](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21639596/)

**Analytical judgment for honest development:** investigate adult flavour, glassware/serve ritual, pacing, inclusion and an appealing shared choice as product benefits in their own right. Test the accurate label and realistic social setting. A participant enjoying a social occasion after a pleasant drink cannot by itself establish a pharmacological ingredient effect; a placebo response does not make an unsupported physiological claim truthful.

A useful future finished-product design would prespecify the intended outcome, distinguish taste/brand expectation from composition, measure perceived benefit and observable interaction separately, assess alertness/impairment and repeated consumption, and include a credible control. This is a design recommendation, not approval to recruit or dose people. A caffeine-equivalence claim would additionally need a prospectively justified noninferiority/equivalence margin on matched outcomes and timepoints; neither overlapping marketing words nor separate positive placebo trials can supply that comparison.

## Extraction cautions for integration

- Do not label all “healthy” participants healthy/rested/general-population: many had elevated stress, low mood, insomnia, cognitive concerns or selected personality features.
- Keep **within-group**, **between-group**, **omnibus**, **post-hoc** and **subgroup** results distinct. A treatment arm improving while placebo fails its own significance test is not itself evidence of a treatment difference.
- Haskell completed **24**, White's responder analysis used **26**, Jackson analysed **56 per protocol**, Nagano analysed **26**, and Hogenelst analysed **40 after 42 started**. Do not inflate the effective sample by citing recruited totals alone.
- White is a blend; Pouchieu is sublingual; Docherty is a proprietary fungal complex. These preparation/route mismatches are decisive.
- A significant brainwave, hormone or reaction-time result can be real while the consumer notices nothing. Magnitude, minimum perceptible change and acceptable trade-offs are largely unknown.
- Search was targeted and broad across the designated candidates, not a registered systematic review. Some older full papers/funding details could not be fully verified; the CSV records the level and explicit unknowns. No brands' efficacy assertions were treated as trial findings. No supplier, participant or regulator was contacted.
