# Safety, claims and ingredient gates for a small RTD range

**Evidence cut-off: 17 October 2026. Geography: EU and Czechia, with study populations identified below.** Research conclusions, not a cleared formula or label. This workstream read the root README, research index, [topic 01 evidence memo](../../../market/01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md), [topic 02 transfer assessment](../../../market/02-commercial-traction/supporting/eu-czech-transfer.md) and [topic 04 update](../../../market/04-eu-czech-competition/supporting/regulatory-and-evidence-update.md), then rechecked primary sources. Source IDs resolve in [safety-sources.csv](safety-sources.csv).

The consumer product must be a finished liquid in a can or bottle. Ingredient studies using capsules are evidence about an intervention, not eligible product benchmarks. Powdered manufacturing ingredients are compatible with an eventual liquid product, subject to their own food-use status.

## Implication for launch architecture

**Recommendation/inference:** one CBD-free formula for one clearly understood occasion is the least demanding starting architecture. A second flavour retains the same exposure and claim logic, although its finished recipe, processing and shelf life still need validation. A second daypart formula creates a separate safety and claims problem and requires evidence that customers want a second job done. Caffeine-free does not mean risk-free or proven relaxing.

Focus and energy can be two descriptions of the same daytime buying occasion; do not create separate stimulating SKUs simply to fill both labels. An evening extension could belong under an adult non-alcoholic brand if it has distinct sensory cues, unmistakable caffeine information and an independently valuable occasion. Do not promise that an evening drink reverses daytime caffeine or that buying a morning/evening bundle improves health. A daily system has not been assessed or validated here.

## Dose and pack size are coupled

**Verified EU rule:** covered beverages above **150 mg caffeine/litre** require the Annex III high-caffeine warning and caffeine content in mg/100 ml. A specific exception applies to coffee/tea-based beverages whose name includes coffee/tea. Being below the threshold does not establish suitability for every consumer. [SR01, Regulation 1169/2011, 25 October 2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN-IT/TXT/?fromTab=ALL&uri=CELEX%3A32011R1169).

**Calculated examples, not proposed doses:** concentration = caffeine mg ÷ volume litres.

| Fixed caffeine per container | 250 ml | 330 ml | 500 ml |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 mg | 200 mg/l | 151.5 mg/l | 100 mg/l |
| 60 mg | 240 mg/l | 181.8 mg/l | 120 mg/l |
| 80 mg | 320 mg/l | 242.4 mg/l | 160 mg/l |

At fixed **concentration**, increasing volume increases the container dose. At fixed **dose**, increasing volume changes concentration, flavour intensity and claim eligibility calculations. Consequently, serving size should not be selected independently of the eventual formula. A 330 ml can with 50 mg is already above the caffeine-warning threshold. Manufacturing tolerances must be considered rather than designing exactly at a legal threshold.

**Verified safety reference:** EFSA describes up to 200 mg in one dose and 400 mg over a day from all sources as not raising safety concerns for generally healthy non-pregnant adults; these are reference points, not targets or guarantees. Even 100 mg close to bedtime can affect some adults' sleep. The opinion does not cover interactions with medicines or affected clinical populations. [SR02, EFSA assessment, 27 May 2015](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine).

**Planning consequence:** state the eventual amount per whole container prominently in addition to legally required units; identify the intended adult group; test whether users understand one container and recognize caffeine-containing versus caffeine-free options. For multi-serve bottles, assess realistic pours, a whole-bottle misuse scenario and open-bottle stability. Resealability helps convenience but does not control dose. Single-serve containers simplify explanation; they do not prevent multiple-can consumption.

## Combined use must be assessed before subscription or mixed daypart packs

Use a cumulative exposure worksheet with rows for every ingredient and columns for one unit, two units of the same product, each cross-SKU combination, and reasonable background food/drink/supplement intake. Check exact chemical form, ingredient interactions, intended population, timing and any maximum use/label conditions. This is a **proposed development protocol**, not a performed toxicological assessment.

- A lower-caffeine daytime drink can still be added to coffee and other energy products. An evening product must not be presented as cancelling that exposure.
- Repeating a shared vitamin/mineral premix across three dayparts can multiply exposure without adding a separate benefit. EFSA's August 2025 summary lists a 12 mg/day adult vitamin B6 UL and a 250 mg/day magnesium UL for specified readily dissociable added salts/compounds, excluding naturally occurring food magnesium. These are chronic intake references with different scopes, not statutory per-can caps. Absence of a UL is not evidence that unrestricted addition is safe. [SR03](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ul-summary-report.pdf).
- A relaxation blend needs its own tolerability and interaction assessment. BfR's September 2024 ashwagandha communication identifies unresolved safety concerns and does not derive a safe intake. A plant's traditional use does not clear every extract or every use pattern. [SR04](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/349/ashwagandha-food-supplements-with-potential-health-risks.pdf).
- Mixed subscriptions should remain an optional convenience, with flexible cadence, until separate repeat-use demand and combined-use safety are established. A recurring shipment must not imply an obligation to consume every product every day.

## Scientific rationale does not establish finished-drink efficacy

| Proposition | Evidence and transfer limitation | Portfolio consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Acute alertness | Caffeine has a stronger established ingredient rationale than an unspecified botanical blend [SR02]. This does not prove superior work output, no crash or no anxiety. | Keep a daytime proposition intelligible without a long list of speculative actives. Exact dose remains open. |
| Caffeine plus L-theanine | A 2025 Sri Lankan crossover trial tested 37 overnight sleep-deprived adults with 200 mg theanine + 160 mg caffeine in capsules versus placebo, with selected task/brain-response improvements. There was no caffeine-only arm in this experiment; it cannot isolate theanine's added benefit. Capsule delivery, dose, population and task are different from a processed stored beverage and an ordinary workday. [SR05, 12 August 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114525104169). | Do not select theanine solely from a US can or advertise a proven superior ratio. Exact ingredient route and beverage status also need clearance. |
| Caffeine-free cognition/calm | Earlier small, mixed theanine/mushroom findings remain insufficient to guarantee a one-can effect. The earlier review correctly notes that a 2025 lion's-mane experiment used a freshly prepared drink; the missing transfer is extract equivalence, industrial processing, storage and reproducible meaningful outcomes, not liquid delivery itself. See [topic 01](../../../market/01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md). | An evening ritual may be commercially attractive without a demonstrated acute calming effect; test the sensory occasion and permissible proposition separately. |
| Nutrient-backed normal functions | Authorized nutrient claims concern normal functions, conditional on qualifying composition; they do not demonstrate that additional intake enhances already adequate performance or induces relaxation. [SR06](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/432/2025-08-20/eng). | A possible claims route, not a reason to create duplicate daytime and evening formulas from the same premix. |
| CBD calm/sleep | A 2024 Australian pilot tested 30 people with insomnia using 150 mg sublingual CBD nightly for two weeks: most sleep outcomes did not differ from placebo, with some secondary benefits. This cannot validate a low-dose canned drink, an acute social sensation or an effective EU beverage dose. [SR07, 1 May 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38174873/). | No evidence basis here to make CBD necessary for the main proposition. |

## Claim wording and nutrition targets

**Verified EU conditions, not finished-label approval:** the nutrition-claim rules for liquids use limits per 100 ml. [SR08, Regulation 1924/2006, consolidated 13 December 2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?qid=1787446686193&uri=CELEX%3A02006R1924-20141213).

| Claim | Legal compositional condition | 250 ml arithmetic | 330 ml arithmetic |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Low sugars | At most 2.5 g sugars/100 ml | 6.25 g/container | 8.25 g/container |
| Sugars-free | At most 0.5 g sugars/100 ml | 1.25 g/container | 1.65 g/container |
| Low energy | At most 20 kcal/100 ml | 50 kcal/container | 66 kcal/container |
| Energy-free | At most 4 kcal/100 ml | 10 kcal/container | 13.2 kcal/container |

These maxima are not recommended sensory targets. Sweetness, sugar and total energy are separate measurements. “No added sugars” has a different condition: no added mono/disaccharides or other foods used for sweetening, with required naturally occurring sugar wording where applicable. Juice cannot automatically be used to sweeten a “no added sugars” product. Sweetener permission, dosage and label qualifiers require exact-recipe review; brand descriptions such as “natural” do not settle these issues.

Pantothenic acid's authorized wording concerns normal mental performance; magnesium's concerns include normal psychological function. Both require qualification as a nutrient source and the other applicable conditions. Melatonin's authorized sleep-onset claim involves 1 mg near bedtime, but it does not resolve food-versus-medicine classification nationally or authorize a particular beverage. None of these claims validates “instant focus,” “stress relief” or insomnia treatment. [SR06](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/432/2025-08-20/eng).

For source-level vitamins/minerals, Annex XIII generally considers 7.5% NRV per 100 ml for beverages, or 15% NRV per portion where the package contains only one portion. Final calculation must use the applicable category, analytical tolerances and retained shelf-life amount. Do not design the formula from a US supplement panel. [SR01](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN-IT/TXT/?fromTab=ALL&uri=CELEX%3A32011R1169).

The April 2025 CJEU judgment confirms that botanical health claims are generally prohibited unless authorized or covered by a valid transitional regime; being “on hold” is not a blanket permission. This affects names and implied benefit language as well as detailed advertising. [SR09, CJEU, 30 April 2025](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2025-04/cp250057en.pdf). A separate caffeine claim for alertness at 40–75 mg per portion was refused in Regulation 2016/1411; do not confuse a favourable scientific opinion with permission. Final caffeine wording and any relevant transitional route remain **unresolved**. [SR10, 24 August 2016](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/CS/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R1411).

The supplier must identify species/part, manufacturing route, chemical form, purity, standardization and intended food category. Obtain the actual applicable status decision/authorization and its specification; an ingredient name, US GRAS status or supplement listing is insufficient. For novel foods the Union list and implementing acts determine permissions. [SR11](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/authorisations/union-list-novel-foods_en).

## CBD alternatives: independent commercial and legal assessment

**Demand:** a US survey of 2,010 adults published in November 2024 found 58% would or might try CBD beverages. This is combined tentative/firm stated interest, not purchases or repeat; its willingness-to-pay estimate is conditional on the interested subset. No Czech CBD-only RTD demand uplift was established. [SR18](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-wine-economics/article/canning-cannabis-consumer-preferences-for-cbd-and-thcinfused-beverages/4343A6856F99D7C186836D32ADBD810A). THC market growth is outside the intended scope and cannot fill this gap.

**Safety versus permission:** February 2026 EFSA guidance gave a provisional 0.0275 mg/kg/day, about 2 mg for a 70 kg adult, solely for qualifying ≥98% CBD supplements without nanoparticles and with specified safety prerequisites. Safety was not established for under-25s, pregnancy/lactation or medication users. [SR19](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/news/provisional-safe-level-cannabidiol-novel-food). The September 2026 positive opinion concerns a particular synthetic ≥98% CBD in MCT oil, exclusively for supplements at 2 mg/day and specified adults; it is neither an aqueous RTD assessment nor Commission authorization nor efficacy proof. [SR20](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10154). A separate May 2026 isolate opinion could not establish safety at proposed 21 mg/day, including unresolved formulation/particle questions. [SR21](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10124).

**EU/Czech market route:** no authorization applicable to the intended CBD can/bottle was established from the Commission list and authorization search. This is a documented unresolved gate, not a claim that all CBD uses have the same legal status. Czech SZPI confirmed fines, withdrawals/recalls and marketing prohibitions for CBD food violations since 2021; “no current proceedings” in its 29 May 2025 response described that date only. [SR11](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/authorisations/union-list-novel-foods_en), [SR22](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/soubor/27-2025-redigovano.aspx). No applicable authorized CBD calm/focus/sleep claim was established; ingredient and claim permission are separate.

| Architecture | Incremental demand hypothesis | Operating and brand implications | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| No CBD extension | Daytime clarity or evening refreshment may meet the buying occasion without cannabinoid preference. | Avoids CBD-specific sourcing, analytical, emulsion, compliance and communication work; loses any genuinely incremental CBD buyers. | **Preferred baseline.** Validate the occasion and repeat independently. |
| Later CBD version of a successful CBD-free drink | Existing buyers might pay more for CBD, but substitution could replace rather than add sales. | Adds another functional formula, batch/testing protocol, label, inventory position and possible confusion with the original. Familiar flavour does not establish formulation equivalence or combined-use safety. | Retain as a conditional option only after a lawful drink route, finished-form safety and incremental demand are demonstrated. |
| Separately positioned CBD SKU | A narrow CBD-seeking adult segment may be distinct enough to warrant its own offer. | Clearer identity reduces accidental confusion but introduces a second proposition/channel strategy. Same ingredient/claims/safety gates remain; separate branding cannot solve them. Lowest likely sharing of marketing and inventory assumptions. | Defer. Consider only if the segment and permitted channel economics support separate acquisition and repeat. |

**Sourcing protocol, not supplier clearance:** require a route-specific lawful food specification, traceable food-grade manufacture, independent CBD/THC and other-cannabinoid assays with detection/quantification limits, solvent/pesticide/metals/microbiological results, authorized emulsifiers, dose uniformity and potency in the actual can/bottle through shelf life. A “water soluble,” “isolate” or “nano” description does not prove this. The prior regional CBDepot application lead has not been converted into a current qualified supplier or quotation.

**Costs unknown:** request ingredient minimum orders, emulsion development, analytical testing frequency, stability/pack compatibility, changeovers/cleaning, regulatory work and possible channel/insurance requirements as separate quote lines. Do not invent a CBD cost per can or assume the tiny active mass makes the extension cheap. No supplier contact, purchases, clinical testing or customer fieldwork occurred.

**Release gate for any first range:** review the complete recipe and use scenarios; confirm ingredient and additive status in the selected destination; approve exact Czech label and advertising copy; assay compositional targets and shelf-life retention; validate process/pack safety; obtain actual manufacturing and quality costs; then authorize production. Until those gates close, every dose, benefit claim and final nutrition specification remains provisional.
