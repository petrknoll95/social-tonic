# Name evaluation and neutral testing protocol

**Design only · 17 October 2026.** No participants recruited, stimuli tested, results simulated or names cleared. Applies to a later defined Czech/EU beverage proposition. See [report](../report.md), [naming brief](naming-brief.md) and [legal/language memo](workstreams/language-legal.md).

## Decision structure

Use a gate sheet followed by an evidence profile. Do not compute a weighted “name score” and treat it as probability of sales. Legal distinctiveness and perceptual distinctiveness are separate. A name can score well with buyers yet fail legal review; a registrable name can be hard to order aloud.

| Hard gate | Hold or exclude when | Who resolves it |
|---|---|---|
| Trademark/confusion | Qualified review finds material unacceptable infringement/registration risk in chosen markets and goods, or the identity is practically confused with an incumbent. | Trademark professional; document scope and unresolved objections. Initial desk searching is a triage, not clearance. |
| Truth and claims | Name/full presentation makes a material false or unsupported ingredient, origin, alcohol or health promise; no viable lawful route for the proposed product. | Food-law/claims reviewer and product team. A disclaimer or descriptor may not cure it. |
| Language harm | Credible native-language review establishes an unacceptable insulting, obscene, medicinal or other misleading meaning for the audience. | Native speakers plus qualified local review; distinguish isolated personal dislike from repeatable meaning. |
| Product fit | Name inherently promises an ingredient/benefit inconsistent with the actual product or core CBD-free constraint. | Founder/product team using settled positioning; do not reject solely because every possible future extension is not covered. |
| Operational identity | No usable, non-confusing route to the brand across necessary purchase channels after reasonable fixes. | Commercial team; exact .com or identical handle is not universally mandatory. |

Unknown legal status is **pending**, not pass. Do not set arbitrary thresholds such as “80% pronunciation = legally safe”. Hard gates should be applied before costly design, then repeated on the final name/pack combination.

## Evidence profile (no combined sales score)

For each dimension record **weak / workable / strong / unknown**, plus evidence, language group, denominator, uncertainty, remedy and owner. These labels are structured discussion aids, not validated intervals. Agree which dimensions are essential for the actual channel before seeing results; do not adjust weights to rescue the founder's favourite.

| Dimension | Observable question and measure | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Distinctiveness | Confusion and false recognition against close beverage/adjacent names; verbatim explanations of difference. | Unfamiliarity can produce novelty without an identifiable brand. |
| Memorability | Correct unaided delayed recall and approximate recall, separately; recognition with plausible foils and false positives. | Recognition is easier than recall; category word recalled without the brand is not a success. |
| Pronunciation | First unassisted reading, plausible variants, rhythm/stress, hesitations and confidence. | Multiple harmless local pronunciations may be acceptable; one prescribed accent is not the goal. |
| Spelling/spoken search | Exact and near spelling after audio; successful retrieval from a realistic result set; wrong-brand choices. | Deliberate misspellings may aid visual memory but raise audio friction. |
| Associations | First spontaneous associations and undesirable inferences, coded before product reveal. | Relevant suggestion versus premature ingredient/health commitment. |
| Category comprehension | Unprompted expected product/category/contents before and after descriptor; “don't know” retained. | Abstract masterbrands can rely on clear descriptors; they need not explain everything alone. |
| Positioning fit | Fit with a predefined occasion/price/taste brief and unintended effect strength/onset. | Liking a word does not mean it suits the proposition. |
| Range flexibility | Credibility of same name across explicitly proposed families and a plausible reformulation. | Broadness can dilute relevance; current focus may outweigh speculative flexibility. |
| Cross-language usability | Pronunciation, spelling and meaning results separately by language/proficiency. | An overall average can hide a serious Czech or expansion-market failure. |
| Trademark/claims risk | Dated legal assessment, scope and open issues; not participant opinion. | Distinctive spelling does not necessarily avoid similar sound/meaning or implied claims. |

Optional preference is collected last: liking, perceived tone, premium/accessibility and reasons. Report disagreement rather than erasing it with an average. This profile does not predict revenue, repeat purchase or product efficacy.

## Study design

1. **Freeze the decision brief.** Select the audience/occasion and factual product card. Test social and functional concepts separately if both remain open; do not pool them into one winning name. Fix the candidate list, planned contrasts, exclusion rules, acceptable pronunciation variants and coding manual before fieldwork. A small qualitative pilot may refine unclear instructions, then use a fresh main sample.
2. **Recruit relevant adults, not only enthusiasts.** Include actual buyers/users of substitutes, occasional alcohol avoiders where relevant, and less-engaged people. Record age band, location, dominant language, English proficiency, category familiarity and existing-name familiarity. Start with Czech; add Slovak for nearby expansion and German/Polish for plausible neighbouring markets. French is a useful different pronunciation/semantic stress test if a western-European route is credible. These are diagnostic groups, not proxies for all EU consumers. Do not combine UK with EU legal conclusions.
3. **Choose size from the decision.** For exploratory language interviews, 8–12 people per priority language can reveal obvious issues but cannot certify population acceptance. For an optional quantitative comparison, plan sample size around a prespecified difference and uncertainty. Example planning calculation only: around 96 independent observations per cell gives roughly ±10 percentage points for a proportion near 50% at 95% confidence under simple sampling; it is not a power calculation, and non-probability recruitment, multiple comparisons and attrition weaken inference. Avoid 20 names × 5 languages × 2 designs with tiny cells. A researcher should finalise sample/power design before recruitment.
4. **Use monadic or balanced incomplete blocks.** Each person first sees one name, or a small randomly assigned set with balanced positions. Avoid putting close lookalike names side by side before first exposure. Randomise name, presentation order and foil order. Only the first exposure qualifies as unprimed. Repeated names or later tasks are labelled as learned/exposed responses. Keep moderator tone/instructions identical; ideally blind moderators and coders to founder preference.
5. **Separate audio and visual routes.** Use fresh participants or counterbalanced first-exposure arms. Hearing an intended pronunciation before reading destroys the unaided pronunciation measure; seeing spelling before audio destroys spelling-from-hearing. Use the same speaker, volume, pacing, number of exposures and sentence structure for all audio. Test a locally natural variant only as a predefined additional condition.

## Tasks and neutral prompts

| Stage | Exact task idea | Record and avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Visual first exposure | “Please read this aloud as you normally would. What, if anything, comes to mind?” | Audio/phonetic coding with consent; no correction, founder story or adjective prompts. |
| Audio first exposure | Play the isolated name once, with the same neutral context as the visual arm. Then: “Write what you heard. How would you look for it?” | Exact spelling, near match, alternative segmentation, intended query; do not show wordmark. |
| Category before reveal | “What sort of product, service or organisation, if any, would you expect this to be? What might be in it?” | Open response first; don't supply “calming drink” or “healthy soda” options. |
| Expectations | “What would you expect it to do, if anything? When would someone choose it?” | Benefits, onset, strength, alcohol/caffeine/CBD/medicine assumptions; non-response is data. |
| Delayed memory | After an unrelated filler task (e.g. 15–20 minutes, fixed for everyone): “What names do you remember from earlier?” Optional next-day unaided follow-up before reminders. | Exact and approximate recall separated; report completed/eligible denominators and dropout. For a clean brief-exposure memory measure, use a separate arm that sees/hears the name and then only the filler task. If associations/category questions occurred first, label recall as following elaborated exposure; do not compare it to brief exposure as if equal. No quizzes that teach the answer beforehand. |
| Recognition | After unaided recall, mix target with matched plausible foils: “Which, if any, appeared earlier?” | Correct recognition and false alarms, including none-of-these controls. No famous foil that is obviously wrong. |
| Consistent pack reveal | Show the same can/bottle shape, colour allocation, typography, descriptor, flavour, price and exposure duration with only the name changed: “What is this? What would you expect?” | Comprehension change and inferred promises; avoid giving one name a bespoke appealing identity. Record necessary font-size changes for long names. |
| Shelf and listing task | Find the named product in a matched shelf scene/listing or menu, then identify variant/flavour. | Time, errors and competing-brand picks; prototypes do not measure real conversion. Counterbalance shelf position. |
| Confusion check | “Does this remind you of an existing name? Which one?” Later show predefined close marks/brands in randomized order. | Spontaneous versus prompted confusion separately; legal conclusions require expert analysis. |
| Extension task | Show two prospective families with the same parent and lawful factual descriptors; ask “What connects these? What seems inconsistent?” | Fit and unwanted carryover (e.g. stimulant expected in evening drink); repeat with a benefit statement removed or localised for another country. Not purchase demand. |
| Final preference | “Which would you choose for this stated use, if any, and why?” | Preference plus reasons and no-choice option; never the sole selection measure. |

Test name alone, name-plus-descriptor and name-on-pack as distinct conditions. To estimate the descriptor's contribution, assign independent randomized groups or acknowledge learning/order effects in within-person reveals. A neutral pack is the fairness baseline; a later design study can explore identity synergy without calling it a pure naming test.

## Search and channel simulation

Real search results personalise and change. Capture date, country, language, device, query and result position; test brand-only, name+drink, near spellings and accent-free variants. A prelaunch name may have no real result, so use a clearly labelled mock listing task for comparable discovery, then conduct a separate live collision audit. Do not fabricate a successful live search. Record social-handle alternatives and how a creator would say the URL without requiring spelling rescue.

Reserve sentences such as “Yesterday I tried [name]” for a separate realistic recommendation task after the unprimed measures. For café/bar speech, use a quiet and a controlled noisy condition with the same audio setup; ask the order taker to repeat the requested item. In subscription scenarios ask the buyer to identify the next delivery's family, flavour and quantity; name preference is not retention. On small packs check readable descriptor and variant hierarchy at real scale, including diacritics and colour-independent identification. No consumer need is established by passing these tasks.

## Analysis and decision record

Code open associations before seeing preference totals. Use two independent coders for a meaningful subset and reconcile disputes; report agreement method and disagreement rather than inventing precision. Compare exact and approximate spellings separately, and predefine which plausible pronunciations count as successful. Report raw numerator/denominator, exposure route, language group and uncertainty for every rate; no significance shopping across dozens of outcomes.

Keep a table of evidence against each hard gate, unresolved issues, strongest/weakest dimension and proposed fix. Retest material spelling/descriptor changes with fresh participants. Select only after product/positioning and legal reviews agree; retain a runner-up and document the trade-offs. Name tests cannot substitute for taste, pricing, distribution or paid-repeat validation.
