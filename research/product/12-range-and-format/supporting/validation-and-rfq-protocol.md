# Low-cost validation and supplier-quote protocol

**Proposed work only, 17 October 2026. No interviews, tastings, supplier contacts, orders or purchases have occurred for this topic.** Samples must be safe and lawful before people consume them. Start with concept and packaging work that does not depend on a finished formula.

## 1. Test a reason to choose the product

Recruit roughly 12–18 adults by recent behaviour, split between packaged daytime-drink purchasers and caffeine-free evening/social purchasers, including satisfied incumbent buyers. These numbers are practical qualitative starting points, not statistical sample-size claims. Reuse the [existing interview guide](../../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/interview-guide.md). Ask about the last real occasion, current drink/no-drink choice, price actually paid, caffeine preferences, leftovers and packaging needs before showing concepts.

Compare A (one daytime formula), B (one caffeine-free evening formula), C (two distinct occasions) and the existing choice/no-purchase option at the same stated price basis. Counterbalance concept order. Record unaided comprehension: benefit expected, timing, caffeine amount, portion and whether two drinks are understood as optional or necessary. Discard a concept whose appeal requires an unsupported instant effect or medical promise. Do not infer market size from interview enthusiasm.

## 2. Separate taste, package and claim effects

After preliminary ingredient/process clearance, use outsourced small safe batches for blind tests of two flavours on the same core. Compare no/very low sweetness versus lightly sweet; then light carbonation versus still. Avoid a full combinatorial menu: use sequential elimination to keep cost down. Test comparable temperatures, randomized codes and both plain and branded presentation. Use a qualified participant-suitability and tasting-safety protocol; record each sample volume and cumulative caffeine/other actives across all arms, including background intake. Multiple prototypes do not imply drinking multiple full cans. Record overall liking, bitterness/aftertaste, completion, preferred volume and substitution at a displayed price. Ask whether 250 or 330 ml changes value or comfort; do not tell participants which is preferred.

A small directional test (for example 24–36 adults, not a powered efficacy trial) can identify obvious rejection and choose the next prototype. Functional benefit efficacy needs an appropriate separate study; taste acceptance does not prove it. Check aged samples as well as fresh samples before commercial release.

## 3. Measure paid repeat only once a compliant pilot is sellable

Offer single cans in one reachable local route, a pickup trial pack, and a replenishment offer. Include a no-purchase choice. Record SKU, price, promotion, channel, stock availability and first/repeat order date. An illustrative first cohort of 50–100 paid buyers can reveal operating problems but cannot establish national demand. Do not treat complimentary sampling or automatically billed subscriptions as spontaneous repeat.

Track (a) trial-to-paid conversion, (b) eligible paid first buyers with a second paid purchase within 30/60 days, (c) cans per retained customer, (d) unpromoted units per in-stock store-week, (e) contribution after delivery/promotion, (f) complaints/leftovers, and (g) cannibalisation between variants. State denominators, censor recent buyers not yet eligible, and report confidence limits where numbers support them. Never project subscriber lifetime value from the first order.

Before ordering, use the actual MOQ and saleable window to set the hurdle: minimum weekly units **for each SKU** = SKU run / available saleable weeks, with a buffer for uncertainty and the channel's required remaining life. The model's 6,000/26 =231 units per SKU per week is arithmetic, not a sales target proven feasible. For a 30%-share second SKU, total demand would need about 769/week at those inputs. Do not rescue a weak SKU with permanent discounting or compulsory mixed packs.

Launch the second flavour only if it wins incremental buyers/repeat and the combined plan remains economical. Launch a second occasion only if demand is distinct, its own contribution works, legal/safety review passes and users understand the range without a prescribed daily stack. A tie or weak evidence favours one SKU or no launch.

## 4. Request comparable outsourced quotes later

Send the same specification to a small number of qualified fillers; no requests are being sent in this research. Ask for separate options at feasible published minimums and higher volumes, never an assumed universal MOQ. Request:

- Formula ownership, permitted ingredient provenance and country/category assessment; whether the flavour change is counted as a new recipe; ingredient and packaging minima independent of fill-run minimum.
- Exact can/bottle sizes, decoration choices and minimums per artwork; available stock components, closure/liner specifications, food-contact compliance, migration and thermal compatibility.
- Preservation method matched to pH/water activity/ingredients; ability to produce still and sparkling liquids; microbial validation, stability plan and third-party assays; allergens and cross-contact controls.
- Per-SKU run size, over/underrun, yield/waste ownership, sample and line-trial costs, development/setup/cleaning/release fees; mixed-flavour filling/packing possibilities and whether total volume can genuinely be shared.
- Unit cost by run, payment deposits/balance, lead times, transport terms, pallet configuration, storage, expiry at dispatch, rejection/recall responsibilities and product liability.
- Singles, 4/6/12/24-can packs assembled from existing cans; mixed-pack labour, case labels/barcodes, packing minimums, e-commerce damage specification and delivery weight bands.
- Final all-in landed cost and channel contribution including taxes, deposits/EPR, warehousing, promotions and returns. Do not choose on ex-factory unit price alone.

For a later CBD project add exact authorization/use conditions, independent batch cannabinoid/contaminant results, quantitative THC specification and assay detection limits, aqueous formulation stability/dose uniformity, excipient and nano-status, medication/age restrictions and insurability. A supplier certificate cannot replace lawful use or product safety review.
