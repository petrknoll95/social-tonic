# Packaging and outsourced production

**Research date: 17 October 2026. Geography: EU production, with Czech and nearby suppliers. Status: desk research; no supplier contact, quotation, certification audit or product test.**

The operational starting point is **one CBD-free formula, one can size and one lead flavour**, with a second flavour conditional on sensory preference and enough demand to support its own minimum production run. Shortlist **250 ml and 330 ml aluminium cans**, select one after consumer and production testing, and avoid launching both. A bottle is a credible alternative when resealability or a short shot occasion proves more valuable than a drink-length refreshment occasion. These are strategy judgments, not findings of customer research.

This workstream read the root README, research index and topics [01](../../../market/01-us-product-landscape/report.md), [04](../../../market/04-eu-czech-competition/report.md) and [26](../../../market/26-trends-and-industry-intelligence/report.md). Topic 26's Hardtank example was rechecked; current public terms remain consistent. Newly found 2026 Madison pilot capability broadens the size options but contains conflicting minimums. The commercial beverage comparisons remain restricted to finished liquids in cans/bottles. Other formats appearing on supplier websites are excluded.

**Evidence notation:** **Fact** means an identified source supports the statement, not that a supplier promise has been independently audited. **Supplier claim** means advertised capability or terms. **Judgment** means a proposed decision or engineering implication. **Unknown** means not established. `[T]` requires testing; `[Q]` requires a project-specific quote; `[R]` requires legal/regulatory review. A capability page is neither approval of a formula nor a contracted MOQ.

## 1. Compare the material and closure, not just the silhouette

| Material / closure | Customer and occasion hypothesis | Operational implications and constraints | First-launch decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aluminium can, conventional stay-on tab | Familiar energy/soft-drink format; portable single occasion; no resealing after opening | Opaque metal walls protect against light; package barrier depends on intact seams and liner. Validate formula/liner compatibility, seams, pressure and transport. No need for bottle/cap stock. Dent damage remains possible. `[T,Q,R]` | Preferred candidate for one measured serving; compare 250 with 330 ml before selecting one |
| Glass bottle, screw cap | Premium tea/adult soft drink; can be resealed; beverage visible in clear glass | Bottle, cap and label components; breakage protection and delivered shipping weight matter. Clear glass needs light-stability assessment; coloured glass or labels may help but require tests. Returnable glass needs a working collection/washing arrangement, not merely a reusable-looking bottle. `[T,Q,R]` | Viable local hospitality alternative if premium sensory presentation wins; do not add alongside cans at launch |
| Glass bottle, crown cap | Recognisable café/social ritual, but generally not resealable | Opening convenience must match use; no benefit for carrying a partially drunk bottle. Returns possible only with an actual scheme. `[T,Q]` | Defer unless hospitality is the validated channel |
| PET/rPET bottle, screw cap | Useful for desk, commuting or hydration when consumers sip and reseal | Closure, preform/bottle, process and barrier specification must fit the formula. Validate oxygen/light/carbonation performance over shelf life; hot-fill and aseptic bottles are not interchangeable. rPET requires suitable food-contact compliance and supplier documentation. `[T,Q,R]` | Strong candidate for a distinct hydration occasion later; weak reason to duplicate a single-occasion focus can |
| Aluminium bottle, screw closure | Can combine opaque package and resealability | Additional closure/line requirements; public RHODIUS sizes exist but this project's MOQ/cost are unknown. `[T,Q,R]` | Defer; no demonstrated need to pay for a less standard startup format |

Metal barrier attributes are supported by the packaging manufacturer [Crown, undated, global](https://www.crowncork.com/sustainability/environment/sustainable-packaging) [PO10]. RHODIUS advertises 250/400 ml aluminium bottles [PO06]. Other comparisons are engineering/strategy judgments to verify on the actual package. Do **not** repeat a supplier's blanket assertion that cans have lower carbon footprints than every glass/PET alternative: no product-specific life-cycle assessment, recycled content, actual weights, route or return rate has been established here. The pack winner for cost, environmental impact and customer preference may differ.

## 2. Serving-size architecture

| Finished-liquid format | Rationale to test | Dose and repeat-use implications | Trade-off / provisional decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60–100 ml bottled RTD shot | Rapid morning/work ritual; little fluid; portable | One bottle can define a dose, but concentrated flavour, sediment, caffeine and ingredient uniformity need testing; small volume is not permission for a higher dose. Packaging does not settle beverage versus supplement classification. `[T,R]` | Qualifying format, but lower refreshment value and an additional bottle line; defer unless interviews show strong shot preference. Madison advertises 80/100 ml manufacturing; 60 ml is a design comparison, not a confirmed supplier capability here |
| 200–250 ml can or bottle | Short coffee-break replacement; manageable before a meeting; possible evening small serving | Whole-container dosing is easy to explain. Concentration is higher than in a larger pack for the same dose. `[T,R]` | 250 ml lead challenger: lower liquid mass and short occasion; perceived value must be tested against 330 ml |
| 330–355 ml can or bottle | Familiar soda-length daytime refreshment or adult evening drink | More room to balance bitterness and aroma, but full consumption takes longer. Do not silently scale actives up with volume. `[T,R]` | 330 ml lead challenger: local co-pack options; 355 ml US benchmark does not require copying that size. Crown lists both in its European region, but availability on a chosen line remains unknown [PO11] |
| 400–500 ml can or resealable bottle | Longer active/commuting session or substantial hydration | A large pack may invite partial consumption; show whole-pack caffeine and intended serving clearly. More fluid is not a stronger efficacy proposition. `[T,R]` | Defer for focus MVP; evaluate only if longer hydration/sipping is a distinct customer job |
| 750 ml–1 L resealable bottle, **finished RTD** | At-home sharing or repeated pours; no dilution/preparation required | Must establish dose per pour and per bottle, mixture uniformity, opened-pack microbiological stability, storage period, carbonation loss and foreseeable overconsumption. Sharing makes individual exposure harder to track. `[T,R]` | Defer for caffeinated/active-led MVP; more plausible later for low-active adult refreshment. Not a concentrate or syrup |

**Size arithmetic, not a freight quote:** at 12 units, liquid volume alone is 3.00 L for 250 ml, 3.96 L for 330 ml and 6.00 L for 500 ml. At approximately 1 kg/L these are roughly 3.00/3.96/6.00 kg of liquid, before packaging. Actual carrier weight bands, dimensions, protective packaging and damages determine delivered cost. A 330 ml unit contains 32% more liquid than 250 ml; that does not imply 32% higher unit cost because filling, active dose and packaging costs do not all scale with volume.

**A crucial Czech operational detail:** BOFILLEX's terms effective **15 April 2024** require can-producer compatibility approval before an order, including sending 20 L with ingredient details at the customer's expense. This is one supplier's contractual process, not a universal requirement, and makes clear why a laboratory recipe is not yet a sellable packaged product.

## 4. Product/process requirements before selecting a co-packer

These are proposed development checks, not validated process instructions or promised shelf life:

1. **Start with an ambient-storage target `[T,Q]`.** Compare the formula under the manufacturer's validated thermal/aseptic process. Heat can change aroma, colour, active retention and texture; quantify these instead of relying on “functional” ingredient inclusion at mixing. Coffee/tea cold extraction does not automatically mean cold-chain distribution.
2. **Do not assume a chilled route is cheap `[Q]`.** Add chilled warehousing, transport, returned/damaged stock and expiry exposure if required. Non-thermal HPP is not automatically ambient shelf stability: EFSA's 2022 assessment notes that spores may survive non-thermal HPP and stresses validated controls [PO12]. The exact acid profile, microbial hazards and package determine the safe route.
3. **Choose still versus sparkling through an occasion test `[T]`.** Light carbonation is a plausible daytime refreshment/social cue; still tea or a shot may suit a short work ritual. Compare pleasantness, perceived bitterness/acidity and full-container completion. Do not launch both gas systems as separate SKUs before preference is known. Confirm line suitability, pressure, foaming, seam/closure performance and CO₂ retention.
4. **Treat common actives as a platform, not identical finished recipes `[T,Q]`.** Citrus and berry variants can change pH, colour, aroma oils, sediment and compatibility. Each finished flavour needs an approved specification and a justified stability/quality plan. A common active premix may simplify sourcing but does not erase changeovers or per-flavour MOQ.
5. **Start with a narrow sweetness experiment `[T,R]`.** Compare a zero/near-zero-sugar prototype against approximately **2 g sugar/100 ml** with matched flavour and acidity. At 250/330 ml the latter supplies **5.0/6.6 g sugar**, equivalent to **20/26.4 kcal from sugar alone**; total calories also depend on other ingredients. These are formulation hypotheses, not nutrition claims or final specifications. Ask about aftertaste, sweetness liking, fullness and willingness to repurchase before choosing the sweetener system.
6. **Request a complete release plan `[T,Q,R]`:** ingredient identity/contaminants; batch homogeneity; measured caffeine and other relevant actives; pH/acidity; microbial testing and challenge work where appropriate; thermal/aseptic validation; oxygen; package migration/compatibility; nutrient/active retention; sensory drift; seam or closure integrity; transport and opened-pack studies where relevant. Exact tests and sampling depend on the qualified food technologist's hazard analysis.

## 5. Flavours, formulas, multipacks and subscriptions

**One formula in two flavours** shares active procurement, dose communication and positioning, but still creates two finished-liquid stocks, labels, forecasts, minimum runs and potential expiry problems. **Two formulas with one flavour each** can serve different occasions, yet also adds distinct ingredient control, safety/claim review, dosing instructions, stability work and customer education. **Two formulas × two flavours × two sizes = eight liquid SKUs** before pack variants. Avoid that multiplication during validation. These are operational deductions, not observed cost percentages.

Use singles for low-commitment physical trial. For direct online orders, test a **6-can introductory box** and **12-can refill** `[T,Q]`, with the same sellable cans and plain shippers where practical. A mixed box can be assembled from the same two flavours; it is not a third liquid formula, but creates a packing item and can be blocked by the slower-selling flavour. Two flavour-specific 12-packs plus a mixed 12-pack require three fulfilment configurations even though only two liquid SKUs exist. A large 24-pack reduces shipping cost per unit only if its actual shipping/handling economics and customer commitment are acceptable.

Multipack feasibility is evidenced by BOFILLEX's 4/6/12/24 formats [PO05]; whether small mixed boxes are machine-packed, repacked by hand, or handled by a fulfilment partner is unknown. Public Hardtank carton formats are 12 or 40 [PO01]; a branded 6-can e-commerce trial would therefore need separate confirmation or repacking. A shrink sleeve/label can reduce early printed-can commitment, but requires legibility, condensation, recycling and final appearance checks; Madison's **8 October 2026** sleeve discussion supports the flexibility trade-off [PO13], not a universal legal interpretation.

Do not infer repeat purchase from a subscription button. Offer a subscription only after paid repeat orders reveal consumption cadence, at a cadence the customer can skip/pause. A weekday focus occasion may repeat more regularly than social evenings, but that is an interview hypothesis. Check **order contribution after discount, VAT, payment fees, product cost, pick/pack, delivery subsidy, damage/returns and customer acquisition**, plus inventory availability. A subscription discount that turns repeat orders unprofitable is not retention evidence. Do not imply consumers should finish a multipack on a fixed schedule or use morning/day/evening formulas together.

## 6. Packaging compliance and Czech launch implications

These issues require product-specific review before ordering packaging; they are not a complete legal opinion.

- The Commission identifies **12 August 2026** as the PPWR application date [PO14]. EKO-KOM's Czech guidance updated in August/September 2026 addresses declarations of conformity and changed producer responsibilities [PO15]. Establish who prepares the packaging documentation and who is the producer for each relevant material/country; purchasing filling services does not itself settle these responsibilities.
- PET design needs a compliant closure and recycled-content specification. The Commission's SUP overview identifies tethered-cap design requirements and the 25% recycled-plastic PET-bottle target from 2025, with further requirements from 2030 [PO16]. Do not interpret a high-level target as proof that a particular bottle complies; request the applicable supplier and national documentation.
- For can liners and closures, obtain current food-contact conformity/migration evidence. The EU's **19 December 2024** BPA-ban announcement expressly includes metal-can coatings and has transition provisions [PO17]; legal review must establish the applicable dates and exemptions for actual stock. “BPA-free” marketing alone is not a complete package dossier.
- EKO-KOM's current client guidance describes packaging accounting, quarterly reporting/payment and annual registration fees [PO18]. Include primary, grouped and transport packaging in the responsibility assessment and cost model. Country expansion can add obligations; do not default to EU-wide direct shipping from day one.
- **Czech compulsory deposit-system start date and project-specific charges are unresolved in this workstream.** Older government proposals are not evidence of current implementation. Do not add a guessed mandatory deposit to the price brief or assume that existing reusable-glass deposits apply to a new bottle. Confirm enacted national rules immediately before a launch, while designing stock/labels to accommodate future changes.

## 7. Minimum viable production decision

Choose the **same can size for both candidate flavours** and initially validate **one formula**. Reserve the second flavour only if it improves acceptance without pushing expected sell-through below the minimum economic run. A reasonable working plan is laboratory sensory screening, a single commercially representative pilot, then conditional scale-up; public pilot availability is evidence that this sequence may be feasible, not a supplier selection or purchase recommendation.

Defer a separate high-caffeine energy formula, evening/sleep formula, CBD SKU, shot, hydration bottle and multi-serve bottle until each has a demonstrated incremental occasion, a safe/legal formula and a viable independent reorder case. Any later daypart pair should share visual recognition and pack infrastructure where possible, while distinguishing caffeine and use instructions clearly. Shared branding does not establish safe combined use.

**Questions to put into a future written quote brief (not sent):** minimum per flavour and formula; common-base batch splitting; labelled versus printed-can minimum; actual package/closure and process; pilot versus repeat-production equivalence; ingredient overage, waste and changeover charges; development and ownership/licensing terms; deposits/payment timing; release testing and shelf-life responsibilities; carton/mixed-box options; warehousing; delivered Czech pallet cost; available remaining shelf life; lead times; complaints/recalls; compliance documentation. Unknowns should remain blank in a model until a supplier answers.
