# Brand and acquisition strategy for a functional RTD beverage

**17 October 2026 · US 2025–2026 commercial benchmarks; Czech/EU launch hypotheses · Desk research complete; no customer validation or campaign results.**

## Recommendation and confidence

**Start by testing a specific work-break drink occasion against ordinary energy positioning. Keep a caffeine-free social/evening concept as a separate challenger.** Sell an understandable drink—taste, occasion, convenient portion and transparent composition—before asking customers to believe a complex cognitive or botanical story. Focus is the first learning priority, not a chosen formula or proof of a large Czech market. A conventional energy message is the commercial control; a social ritual is a different customer job. Dedicated sleep positioning should remain a research arm until both demand and the formula/claim route justify it.

The provisional brand proposition is **“an enjoyable, clearly described drink for a particular moment in your day.”** It deliberately has room for a defensible future functional benefit, but the first SKU must earn one occasion. It is too broad to use as the eventual advertising headline. Do not launch a productivity-to-sleep umbrella range on this research: it would multiply education, formulas, claims, inventory and customer expectations before any paid repeat is known. The main product is CBD-free. CBD gets a separate gate and no assumed launch or advertising budget.

The [brand brief](#brand-brief), [message hierarchy](#provisional-message-hierarchy), [three creative territories](#three-distinct-creative-territories) and [ranked tests](#ranked-acquisition-test-plan) are ready to brief research and design. They are **proposals**, not approved pack copy. All consumer products and proposed formats are finished liquids in cans/bottles. Prepared coffee/tea, supplements and other habits can inform interviews but are not benchmarks.

**What is known:** eligible US energy lines have materially stronger liquid-line purchasing evidence than specialist cognitive and sleep examples; brands use product clarity, familiar flavours, retail presence, partnerships and replenishment offers. **What remains unknown:** Czech understanding of the requested terms, category-specific willingness to pay, incrementality of the observed channels, a startup's CAC, repeat, safe/effective finished formula, production cost and profitable launch country. No public engagement or shelf-access metric is substituted for those missing outcomes.

### Commercial mechanisms worth testing

1. **Lead with a recognisable drink and moment.** Tea, fruit, carbonation and serving size reduce the burden of decoding a stack of actives. This is a design hypothesis, not measured Czech conversion lift. Stronger cognitive language may attract clicks while raising an effect expectation the formula cannot meet.
2. **Give trial a convenient place and a clear price.** A cold single can at the relevant venue answers taste and access questions. Festivals and celebrity partnerships demonstrate visibility; they do not disclose incremental trial economics. A local staffed tasting must still earn subsequent full-price purchases.
3. **Use variety and flavour as repeat hypotheses.** Limited flavours and mixed packs create observable reasons to revisit; they can also create waste, complexity or discount-driven buying. Begin with one production SKU and use mock concepts/commercial controls to explore flavours before multiplying inventory.
4. **Make replenishment easy after repeat appears.** Hiyo's current site describes points/referrals and a 15% subscription discount with free shipping. This is an observed offer, with no disclosed causal retention improvement; discounts and shipping can consume contribution. Do not copy the economics or force a subscription. [Hiyo guide, undated; accessed 17 Oct 2026; US](https://drinkhiyo.com/blogs/all/hiyo-rewards-program-guide).

No verified channel-specific CAC, payback or causal sales lift was found for the benchmark tactics. Founder-led creator audiences are not an asset a new brand can assume it owns. Acquisition announcements, funding, distribution doors and whole-brand revenue do not validate an individual RTD extension.

## Czech/EU language: exposure is not understanding

Czech offers and trade sources establish that these words circulate; they do not measure what a representative audience thinks they mean. No suitable current, representative Czech study comparing all eight terms specifically for canned/bottled RTDs was found. English terms can signal category familiarity to some buyers and obscure the product for others. There is no single “EU audience”: localise and re-test before extending into another country.

The [Czech/EU memo](supporting/czech-eu-language-and-constraints.md) records local-language sources and the legal distinctions. The table below is a **provisional interpretation map to test**, not measured consumer sentiment.

| Word / territory | What a buyer might infer; what is unknown | Provisional communication decision |
|---|---|---|
| Functional / funkční | A drink with something added or a health purpose; may be too broad to explain when to choose it. Familiarity and trust are unknown. | Use a precise beverage descriptor and occasion first; “functional” is optional category education, not the main promise. |
| Focus / soustředění | Immediate attention, fewer distractions or better work; may be confused with energy. Exact expected onset and outcome unknown. | Privately test “pro pracovní pauzu” against focus language. Any advertised concentration benefit/name needs a formula-specific claims route. |
| Productivity / produktivita | More completed work or better performance; stronger implied output than simply feeling awake. | Keep as a research question, not an unqualified consumer efficacy headline. Ask what task outcome the person means. |
| Nootropic / nootropní | Scientific sophistication, a supplement or something drug-like; meaning may be unknown altogether. | Avoid front-of-pack jargon in the first execution; test unaided paraphrase before showing definitions. Ingredient education cannot substitute for lawful evidence. |
| Energy / energie | Familiar stimulant category, sugar or caffeine; ingredient and crash expectations vary. | Use as the workday control. Distinguish stated caffeine content from an authorized nutrient claim about normal energy metabolism; they are not the same promise. |
| Adaptogens / adaptogeny | Stress resilience, naturalness or medicinal potency; not necessarily a recognised ingredient class to buyers. | Do not lead with it or promise stress relief. Disclose actual ingredient identities when relevant and permitted; botanical claim review required. |
| Sleep / spánek | Falling asleep faster, sedation, staying asleep, or treating insomnia; a bedtime ritual is a narrower proposition. | Separate “evening occasion” from physiological sleep claims. Test cold liquid, portion and carbonation fit before a dedicated sleep formula. |
| Social tonic / společenský nápoj | An alcohol alternative, mixers/quinine, a mood change or a “buzz”; the English phrase may need explanation. | Test “nealkoholický nápoj ke společnému stolu” rather than requiring category literacy. State caffeine/CBD absence only if substantiated; do not imply intoxication or guaranteed sociability. |

**Research procedure:** ask for an unaided explanation, likely contents, caffeine/alcohol expectations, expected effect/onset, when they would drink it and what they would replace. Then show the actual planned facts and ask what changed. Include “I don't know” and no purchase. Record misunderstandings before teaching; a taught answer is not comprehension. Use [T01–T03](supporting/acquisition-test-protocol.md) and the existing [Czech interview guide](../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/interview-guide.md).

**Local channel reality:** Czech NIQ data reported for the 52 weeks to 5 April 2026 put energy at 21% of non-alcoholic retail sales, with value growth of 21.5%. That supports a familiar commercial control, not a premium focus niche. Retailers describe chilled impulse placement and clearer category grouping. CANS already advertises office wholesale and a fridge offer, so office access is an occupied channel, not white space. [Zboží & Prodej, 15 Jun 2026; CZ](https://www.zboziaprodej.cz/2026/06/15/zboziprodej-5-2026-nealko-prodava-prehlednost/), [CANS office offer, undated; accessed 17 Oct 2026; CZ](https://cz.cans.com/pages/cans-do-kancelare).

## Compare the messaging territories before choosing a brand architecture

| Territory | Customer job and useful distinction | Credibility burden | Provisional priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Productivity / cognition | Accompany a defined work block; distinguish task completion from alertness or a pleasant break. | Highest expectation risk if promising better cognition without finished-product evidence and permitted claims. Ingredient mechanisms alone are insufficient. | First research priority for a use-case message, with an ordinary-energy control. |
| Energy / alertness | A known drink choice for feeling awake, with clear caffeine and taste expectations. | Crowded category; caffeine science does not automatically authorize any pack wording, guarantee “no crash,” or establish better work. | Essential control and credible candidate if taste/price/access win. |
| Relaxation / social | Enjoyable adult refreshment during an evening transition or gathering. | A social ritual can stand on taste and occasion; stress/anxiety/cortisol/“buzz” claims add a separate evidence and legal burden. | Distinct challenger; do not assume the workday buyer or identity carries over. |
| Sleep / wind-down | Establish a bedtime ritual; clarify whether any physiological sleep benefit is expected. | Ingredient/dose/classification and claim conditions; cold volume/carbonation acceptance; weaker recent line-specific sales evidence. | Research arm/watchlist. Do not launch because social evening messaging performed well. |
| Hydration / recovery | Convenient refreshment at a defined exertion/heat/travel occasion. | Desk workers' need for additional electrolytes is unproven; recovery/hydration claims need composition/evidence review. | Secondary only with a distinct reachable occasion. |
| Gut / everyday nutrition | A familiar soda or tea with a measurable compositional difference. | Fibre type/dose/tolerability, permitted wording and shelf-life compatibility; fibre presence is not generic gut-health proof. | Secondary taste/nutrition option; avoid bundling it with cognition merely to add benefits. |

An endorsed multi-line architecture is an option only after repeat demand is observed in each occasion. Different caffeine states need unmistakable separation; a bedtime pack must not look like an energy SKU. If the evening concept wins but the workday identity confuses buyers, choose a separate identity or one territory; do not insist on an umbrella for manufacturing convenience.

## Brand brief

| Brief element | Provisional instruction |
|---|---|

## Provisional message hierarchy

This is a pack/landing-page brief with **conditional placeholders**, not a completed label. Do not print factual claims until verified or use a proposed ingredient as if it were in a finished recipe.

| Order | Customer question | Provisional answer / placement | Evidence and review dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is it? | Front: a plain Czech beverage descriptor, for example “Jemně perlivý čajový nápoj” **only if true**. Flavour immediately visible. | Actual formula, carbonation, category classification and final label review. |
| 2 | When would I choose it? | Front/hero: “Pro pracovní pauzu.” Alternative social route: “Ke společnému stolu.” | Occasion wording is lower-dependency than an effect claim, but full context and implied meaning still need review. |
| 3 | What's in this serving? | Clear pack volume; caffeine in mg per container (plus mandatory declarations where applicable), sugar/nutrition information and factual ingredients. | Assay/specification and labeling rules. “Bez kofeinu,” “bez cukru,” “bez přidaného cukru” and “0,0 %” each need their own basis; they are not interchangeable. |
| 4 | Why believe any functional benefit? | One legally eligible, formula-matched claim if available; otherwise omit this layer. No invented benefit to fill the space. | Exact permitted wording/conditions, nutrient level through shelf life and destination-country review. No “clinically proven” from an ingredient paper. |
| 5 | How does it fit my routine? | Serving suggestion, flavour notes, storage and purchase options. Make a one-time trial easy. | No universal daily dose or evening caffeine suitability assumption. Product safety/formula determines instructions. |
| 6 | Where can I learn more? | Ingredient facts, evidence explainer and limitations; separate evidence summary from marketing headline. | Branded educational pages and influencer scripts can themselves be commercial health claims. Linking a study does not make a prohibited claim lawful. |
| 7 | What can I do now? | Before stock: “Připravujeme nový nápoj. Dejte nám vědět, zda vás zajímá ochutnávka.” After clearance/stock: transparent priced purchase. | Do not imply availability, take fake orders or treat research sign-ups as purchases. Optional marketing consent separately. |

### Language requiring regulatory review

**High priority:** “focus,” “mental clarity,” “lepší soustředění,” “produktivita,” “nootropní,” “adaptogenní,” “zklidňuje,” “snižuje stres/kortizol,” “pomáhá spát,” “bez úzkosti,” “no crash,” “bez nervozity,” “brain boost,” “gut health,” “lepší hydratace,” “recovery,” and product/brand names or imagery implying these outcomes. “Treats anxiety/ADHD/insomnia” and similar disease claims are outside the proposed food-brand approach. “Natural” is neither a safety guarantee nor proof of a physiological effect.

The review must distinguish: (a) ingredient/use legality; (b) safety and finished-product evidence; (c) nutrition/health claim eligibility and exact conditions; (d) label warnings; (e) advertising/platform rules. Passing one is not passing all. The current Czech/EU source memo explains why older botanical “on-hold” guidance cannot be treated as blanket permission after the 2025 Court judgment. It also separates caffeine scientific opinions from usable authorized claims. [EU claims regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32006R1924), [CJEU judgment, 30 Apr 2025](https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/redirect/juris/documents.jsf?critereEcli=ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2025%3A304), [official-source analysis and dates](supporting/czech-eu-language-and-constraints.md).

## Three distinct creative territories

These are original briefing directions. Proposed Czech lines are stimuli pending review. The full territory is a **bundle**; a bundle preference cannot identify which colour, word or photograph caused it. After selecting an occasion, use matched one-variable message tests.

### 1. The work pause — precise, calm, legible

**Audience/occasion:** adults choosing a drink between tasks, especially those dissatisfied with another familiar workday drink. **Provisional line:** “Další úkol začíná pauzou.” Support: “Chuť, složení a kofein přehledně.” The last line requires an actually clear label; it promises transparency, not cognitive enhancement.

**Visual direction:** warm paper/ink palette with one clear flavour colour, a time/desk motif and generous spacing. Show an ordinary desk, a real break and the unopened/opened can; avoid brain scans, lab coats, glowing neural graphics or completed-work counters. Front hierarchy: drink type → flavour → serving/caffeine → occasion. A bottled tea alternative should preserve that hierarchy rather than becoming a medicine-like shot by default.

**Creative experiment:** a short scene moves from closing a laptop briefly to pouring/opening the drink, with no before/after productivity result. Compare “Pro pracovní pauzu” with a neutral beverage/flavour headline at the same stated price. **Hypothesis:** the occasion increases correct recognition and qualified interest without implying guaranteed focus. **Risk:** “task” imagery still implies performance; T02 measures expected effects and T04 requires approved context.

### 2. Everyday momentum — lively, familiar, taste first

**Audience/occasion:** existing energy or RTD tea buyers making an on-the-go daytime purchase. **Provisional line:** “Otevři. Ochutnej. Pokračuj.” Support: beverage type, flavour and verified caffeine amount; “energy” terminology only after claims/label review.

**Visual direction:** crisp, high-contrast flavour blocks, a recognisable fruit/tea cue and a large factual caffeine badge. Photography shows a commute or daytime break, not extreme sports or all-night studying. Front hierarchy: category → flavour → caffeine/portion → optional eligible claim. Test against the work-pause route using the same formula facts; otherwise it is a different product proposition.

**Creative experiment:** a flavour-led close-up/cold-can creative against an occasion-led version, with equal paid placement. **Hypothesis:** familiar category and flavour lower explanation cost and improve paid choice once available. **Risk:** generic category competition; “clean energy,” “no crash” and “healthy boost” must not fill a differentiation gap without evidence and clearance. Strong engagement with low paid conversion fails the commercial test.

### 3. At the table — adult flavour, social participation

**Audience/occasion:** adults hosting or joining an evening gathering who want an enjoyable alcohol-free option. Private unwinding is researched separately; this concept does not promise sleep. **Provisional line:** “Dobrá chuť. Společný večer.” Support: “Nealkoholický nápoj ke společnému stolu,” plus factual caffeine absence if verified.

**Visual direction:** warm evening photography, tactile geometric illustration and a restrained flavour-led palette; a can or bottle looks natural beside food. Avoid medicine, cannabis leaves, cocktail intoxication cues and imitation of Hiyo/Recess. Front hierarchy: alcohol-free beverage type → flavour → caffeine state/portion → occasion. Verify any alcohol-free/zero statement for the final formula.

**Creative experiment:** a host choosing drinks for all guests versus an individual evening ritual, initially in separate qualified cohorts. **Hypothesis:** hosting gives a clearer purchase reason and larger appropriate basket than vague “calm.” **Risk:** participants may expect a buzz, sedation or therapeutic stress relief. Reject that dependence rather than adding CBD to rescue it. A bedtime research card may test “K večernímu rituálu,” but it is not a fourth launch territory or a validated sleep claim.

### Derive affordable acquisition from contribution

The [editable model](data/acquisition-economics.csv) and [worked explanation](data/README.md) use an entirely hypothetical six-can order: CZK240 net receipts less CZK190 variable costs leaves **CZK50 before acquisition**. At a hypothetical 35% of buyers placing one further order within 60 days, with CZK60 repeat-order contribution, expected contribution is **CZK71**. A chosen 30% cash buffer leaves **CZK49.70** for acquisition. Before observing repeat, the equivalent first-order-only ceiling is **CZK35**. These are not observed CAC/LTV or a recommendation to spend those amounts.

The model's 20–50% repeat scenarios give ceilings of CZK43.40–56.00. Increasing landed cost by CZK5/can cuts the middle ceiling to CZK21.35. The lesson is sensitivity to fulfilment and product cost; actual margins may be worse or negative. A CZK12,000 sampling test yielding 60 buyers has CZK200 attributed cost and fails scale under these assumptions, even if it teaches useful things. Do not hide ongoing sampling, creator fees or discounts as “research.” Incremental CAC is unknown without a defensible holdout.

## What can be learned before finished inventory exists

**Now:** comprehension/misunderstandings; real occasions and substitutes; comparative concept appeal at an explicit price; whether caffeine choice matters; pack hierarchy; likely sensory requirements from eligible commercial controls; qualified recruitment interest; indicative manufacturing and claims feasibility through documentary review. None proves actual product taste, physiological effect, safety, paid conversion, repeat, price elasticity or CAC.

**After formula/process/label clearance and compliant stock:** blind candidate preference, real paid choice, refund/complaint rates, channel service burden, stock-adjusted sell-through, mature repeat and realized contribution. Outsourcing still needs evidence of process fit, stability, permissible ingredients, MOQ and commercial terms.
