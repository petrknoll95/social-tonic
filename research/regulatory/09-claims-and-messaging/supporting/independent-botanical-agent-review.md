## Caffeine: authorisation and science remain separate

**No material error identified in the checked conclusions.** A fresh request to the [Commission’s public register-data endpoint](https://ec.europa.eu/food/food-feed-portal/backend/api/policy-items?foodDomain=nut&authorisationType=nut_auth) returned **2,337 published policy-item records** on the access date. These are records, not necessarily a count of unique legal claims. Four records with the exact substance label “Caffeine” were returned, all marked non-authorised: POL-HC-6840, POL-HC-6841, POL-HC-6842 and POL-HC-8450. POL-HC-8450 concerns the **40–75 mg/serving** alertness application. No authorised caffeine alertness/concentration entry was located. Register omissions do not resolve every transitional route: the author correctly keeps the pending route and its historical conditions separate from authorisation.

[Regulation 2016/1411](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R1411) independently supports the low-dose refusal. Its discussion of a positive scientific assessment at **at least 75 mg** does not grant that separate claim an authorisation. The [Parliament’s adopted objection of 7 July 2016](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/TA-8-2016-0319_FR.html) supports the distinction between the Commission draft and adopted law. The memo correctly avoids turning the vetoed draft, a positive EFSA opinion or the old pending-ID document into a present permission threshold.

The memo also correctly limits its cited German surveillance interpretation to that source and jurisdiction; it is not presented as a Czech ruling. Final Czech review still needs the exact claim, Article 13 category, Article 28 transition basis and any historical filing/use evidence. The botanical judgment is relevant to legal reasoning about psychological/behavioural claims, but it is not described as an individual caffeine ruling.

## Nutrient claims and arithmetic

The same fresh Commission data independently confirms **POL-HC-6433**, pantothenic acid and normal mental performance, and **POL-HC-6533**, zinc and normal cognitive function, as authorised with their respective “source of” condition. These normal-function claims do not establish increased work output, acute mental clarity, no crash or a noticeable effect in a nutrient-replete customer.

The beverage threshold and genuine single-portion-package alternative were checked against [Regulation 1169/2011, Annex XIII](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?qid=1771601123549&uri=CELEX%3A32011R1169): **7.5% NRV per 100 ml** or **15% NRV per portion where the package contains only one portion**. A freely chosen serving in a multi-serving bottle does not acquire the single-portion alternative. Values below are arithmetic checks, not formulation recommendations; permitted nutrient forms, bioavailability, safety, measured amounts and shelf-life remain separate.

All eight author-table rows matched independent recalculation. The energy-metabolism, fatigue, psychological-function and cognition nutrient groups were also checked for inadvertent interchange: no material mismatch was identified in the listed groups. In particular, “normal psychological function” is not a relaxation or sleep claim, and “normal energy-yielding metabolism” is not a promise of perceived energy or alertness.

Caffeine conversions also pass: **40 mg/250 ml = 160 mg/L; 40 mg/330 ml ≈ 121.21 mg/L; 75 mg/250 ml = 300 mg/L; 75 mg/330 ml ≈ 227.27 mg/L; 80 mg/330 ml ≈ 242.42 mg/L**. The warning threshold is **more than 150 mg/L**, not 150 mg per can; the corresponding totals are **more than 37.5 mg/250 ml** or **more than 49.5 mg/330 ml**. The integrated matrix’s additional **60 mg/250 ml = 24 mg/100 ml = 240 mg/L** is correct. The coffee/tea exception correctly requires both the relevant drink basis and coffee/tea in its name; a caffeine ingredient extracted from tea is insufficient by itself. ## CBD and Czech sleep gates

The [EFSA February 2026 statement](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/news/provisional-safe-level-cannabidiol-novel-food) independently confirms the narrow provisional **0.0275 mg/kg/day**, approximately **2 mg/day for 70 kg** context. Exact arithmetic is **1.925 mg/day**. This is not a universal safe RTD dose, claim authorisation or novel-food permission. The [September 2026 synthetic-CBD opinion](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10154), independently reopened, concerns a specified high-purity material and MCT-oil supplement use, with adult/population restrictions; it does not approve an aqueous canned beverage. The workstream preserves the distinction between the February provisional assessment, the applicant-specific later assessment, a Commission authorisation and a finished-drink claim.

[SÚKL’s 14 March 2023 statement](https://sukl.gov.cz/dulezite-informace/zmena-rozhodovaci-praxe-v-pripade-vyrobku-obsahujici-latku-melatonin/) was independently checked through its current official page. Czech practice treats oral products supplying **0.5 mg/day or more** melatonin as medicines by function. The authorised **1 mg sleep-onset** claim therefore does not clear an ordinary Czech beverage; dropping below 0.5 mg is neither proof of lawful food status nor a workaround for that claim. [Current SÚKL page](https://sukl.gov.cz/dulezite-informace/zmena-rozhodovaci-praxe-v-pripade-vyrobku-obsahujici-latku-melatonin/)

## Integrated matrix correction and remaining limits

**M24 requires unambiguous conjunction.** Its draft condition “No added mono/disaccharides OR foods used for sweetening” could be read as an either/or test. The [Regulation 1924/2006 Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02006R1924-20141213) requires **no added mono- or disaccharides AND no other food used for its sweetening properties**. If sugars occur naturally, the prescribed naturally occurring sugars statement is additionally required. This correction was sent to the lead for the Markdown and CSV together.

The separate CBD reviewer’s corrections to exact claim wording, lactose “of the product,” protein’s energy-share gate and carbohydrate-versus-sugars distinction remain valid. A sugar-free product could contain qualifying glucose polymers; “electrolyte presence alone is insufficient” is the correct rule rather than a blanket sugar-free exclusion. No new substantive error was identified in the focus/caffeine/nutrient rows checked.

**Remaining uncertainty:** current national transitional practice, exact product legality, nutrient analytical tolerances and stability, an individual advertising layout and the transferability of ingredient studies to a proposed beverage have not been resolved by these arithmetic and source checks. Those are expressly preserved as formula- and market-specific review gates. No supplier, consumer, regulator or clinician was contacted, and no purchase or product test was performed.
