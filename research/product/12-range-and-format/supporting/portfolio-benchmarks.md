# Portfolio benchmarks and customer logic

**Date:** 17 October 2026. **Scope:** US finished RTD cans and bottles with 2025–2026 launches, range changes or recent traction; Czech/EU transfer is a hypothesis. **Status:** completed commercial workstream, with source and version gaps preserved. Read the [11-record dataset](../data/rtd-benchmarks.csv) and [dated source register](portfolio-sources.csv). Benefits described below are brand positioning, not established physiological effects.

## What the evidence supports

**Start with one customer occasion, one functional specification and one hero flavour; earn a second flavour through preference and paid-repeat evidence.** This is an analyst recommendation for an early-stage founder, not a finding that one flavour maximizes sales universally. Pure Leaf's two-flavour Mental Focus launch is the clearest recent small-range reference. Bloom supplies stronger liquid-line commercial evidence, but its mass distribution and price are not startup economics. Hiyo demonstrates a social occasion; it should not determine the daytime product's formula or identity.

The existing [landscape](../../../market/01-us-product-landscape/report.md), [commercial evidence](../../../market/02-commercial-traction/report.md), [Czech/EU map](../../../market/04-eu-czech-competition/report.md), [occasion research](../../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/report.md) and [trend review](../../../market/26-trends-and-industry-intelligence/report.md) were reviewed. Useful corrections retained: Bloom's energy-line sales are separable from supplements and soda; Magic Mind subscriber counts are not allocated to bottles; Hiyo's case size/window are unknown; Neutonic formulas differ across current pages and new retail announcements; VYBES' current 12oz bottle differs from its earlier 14oz pack. No interviews, purchases, tests or supplier contacts were performed.

## Three architectures worth testing

These are alternatives or sequential options, not a request to develop all three. All start CBD-free. Functional doses, claims, cost and launch market remain unresolved.

| Architecture | Customer rationale | Provisional range | Main trade-off and operating consequence | Evidence that would earn progression |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. One daytime occasion** — preferred starting hypothesis | A recognizable chilled tea/fruit drink for a specific work break; caffeine amount visible, no promise of effortless productivity | One core formula, one hero flavour, one can size. Test two flavour candidates before selecting; second flavour only if it wins incremental buyers | Clear message and concentrated stock; may underserve caffeine-sensitive buyers. A flavour change still requires its own recipe/label/stability checks and minimum run | Paid preference versus the actual coffee/tea/energy substitute, acceptable taste through shelf life, repeat at a viable delivered price |
| **B. One caffeine-free evening/social occasion** — viable alternative if customer evidence wins | A grown-up drink to mark the end of work or join a social occasion without alcohol/caffeine | One shared formula, one hero flavour initially; no sleep or intoxication promise | Accessible occasion and potentially repeated sipping, but premium value must survive without a promised acute sensation. Botanical complexity is optional, not inherently valuable | Repeated choice versus non-alcoholic beer, tonic, adult soda or existing canned/bottled tea; distinct reason to pay |
| **C. Sequential two-occasion range** — later option | The same brand offers a daytime choice and an evening choice, with clear purpose and caffeine distinction | Launch A or B first; later add one separate formula in one flavour after independent validation | Broader relevance but separate development, quality specifications, forecasts and minimum runs. Two formulas may cost more to validate than two flavours of one core formula | Meaningful same-customer overlap, independent repeat for both, combined-use review and enough volume to fund each SKU without excess stock |

Focus and energy can plausibly share one daytime product because buyers may seek alertness during work. Do not create separate “focus” and “energy” formulas unless customers reliably distinguish the job and pay for both. Relaxation can sit under the same broader brand if language and visual identification are clear; this is a brand-test hypothesis. Do not package a daytime and evening pair as a necessary daily system or claim one cancels the other. Sleep, hydration, recovery and gut-health SKUs should each justify a distinct demand and channel case before they use scarce inventory budget.

## Trial, repeat and subscriptions

A mixed-flavour box can reduce flavour uncertainty, but it cannot solve a high minimum run for each flavour. Use existing single-can stocks to assemble a trial box only when fulfilment costs make sense; do not multiply printed pack formats at launch. A proposed four-can trial and a 12-can repeat case are hypotheses, not pack-size recommendations validated by US success.

Subscriptions appear on Hiyo, VYBES and Magic Mind pages, which establishes an offered replenishment mechanism only. Daily-use copy, reviews, guarantees and discounted first orders do not establish retained paid users or contribution margin. Offer easy reorder first, then optional subscription only after observing actual consumption intervals. Evening occasions may be less regular than work breaks, while some buyers use both only intermittently: ask and measure rather than assume.

Keep one-time and subscription cohorts separate. Record units consumed, second paid order, elapsed reorder time, skipped/cancelled orders, effective revenue after discount/refunds, pick-and-pack/freight and unsold flavour stock. A starter discount can conceal low willingness to pay. Do not require a subscription to reveal the real trial price.

## Validation and uncertainty

Use the existing [Czech interview guide](../../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/interview-guide.md) and [diary study](../../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/diary-study.md). Compare occasion messages before paying to develop separate formulas. Then blind-test candidate liquid flavours and sweetness/carbonation, show actual mock packaging and a realistic total price, and measure paid repeat after regulatory/formula clearance. Neither interview enthusiasm nor a small taste test demonstrates physiological efficacy.

The strongest commercial evidence here is for an energy line; the strongest small-range design example is a new tea launch. These are different strengths. Calm evidence is narrower and often brand-reported; sleep and caffeine-free cognition have little public SKU-level repeat evidence. US shelf prices, store counts and ingredient availability do not establish EU legality, Czech recognition, landed cost or local willingness to pay. Packaging material is explicitly unknown where current evidence did not verify it; a can-shaped image does not establish alloy or lining specifications.
