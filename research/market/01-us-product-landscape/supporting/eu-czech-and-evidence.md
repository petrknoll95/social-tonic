# EU/Czech transfer and evidence assessment

As of **17 October 2026**. Source IDs resolve in [sources.csv](../sources.csv). This is a scientific and regulatory evidence assessment; no specific formula has been evaluated for sale. US demand, efficacy, ingredient legality and claims legality are separate questions.

## What the science supports—and what it does not

| Ingredient/proposition | Evidence reviewed | Transfer to a finished drink | Confidence and limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caffeine for acute alertness | EFSA recognizes stimulation/greater alertness; its safety assessment distinguishes single doses from total daily exposure. [L01](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine) | Dose can be measured per can, but tolerance, other caffeine and time of day matter. This does not establish an all-day productivity improvement or a no-crash guarantee. | Relatively strong ingredient rationale; exact-product benefit and tolerability are not established here. |
| Caffeine plus L-theanine | Owen et al. (2008), 27 healthy volunteers, 50 mg caffeine with/without 100 mg theanine: selected attention outcomes at 60–90 minutes improved. Industry-affiliated study. [L09](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18681988/) | Small task-based experiment; does not validate a commercial can, a universal ratio, work output, or hours of effect. The abstract alone does not establish equivalence of delivery to a shelf-stable beverage. | Limited evidence on selected tasks; weak basis for broad product promises. |
| Caffeine-free theanine focus/calm | EFSA's 2011 assessment did not establish the proposed cause-and-effect relationships for cognition, stress or sleep. This is an older claim evaluation, not a review of every subsequent study. [L10](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2903/j.efsa.2011.2238) | Caffeine-combination findings do not establish the effects of a caffeine-free drink. Extract/source and food-use status are separate questions. | Uncertain; proven acute focus from theanine alone is not established by the cited assessment. |
| Lion's mane for cognition | A 2023 pilot used 1.8 g/day in 41 adults for 28 days, with selective rather than broad benefits. A 2025 acute crossover in 18 adults used 3 g of 10:1 fruiting-body extract and found no overall cognitive/mood improvement, despite a task-specific signal. [L11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38004235/), [L12](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12018234/) | Milligrams of different mushrooms/extracts are not equivalent. The 2025 study used a freshly prepared 250 ml water/lemon-squash drink, so liquid delivery itself is not a missing link. Its exact extract/dose, small sample and multiple endpoints still do not validate a processed, stored commercial mixed drink. The study includes commercial affiliations. | Low confidence for a perceptible one-can productivity benefit. |
| Ashwagandha for stress | A 64-person Indian trial used 300 mg root-extract capsules twice daily for 60 days and improved stress measures. [L13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23439798/) | Chronic dosing in stressed adults does not prove a first-can social sensation. BfR flags endocrine/liver concerns and insufficient data to derive a safe intake level. [L14](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/349/ashwagandha-food-supplements-with-potential-health-risks.pdf) | Transfer depends on exact product identity; national rules and safety remain formulation-specific. |
| CBD for sleep/calm | In a 2024 pilot, 30 people with insomnia received 150 mg CBD or placebo sublingually nightly for two weeks; most sleep outcomes did not differ, with some secondary improvements. [L15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38174873/) | Neither the dose, sublingual delivery, clinical population nor duration matches typical low-dose social cans. No inference that 10–25 mg in a can produces reliable calm/sleep. | Low confidence for a reliable effect from the low-dose RTD formats reviewed. |
| Magnesium/B vitamins | EU-authorized nutrient claims can support normal functions where qualifying conditions are met. They are not evidence that extra vitamins improve performance in already replete adults. [L03](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32012R0432) | **Elemental magnesium** differs from compound weight; no end-of-shelf-life assays were reviewed. EFSA/SCF lists a **250 mg/day UL** for readily dissociable added magnesium salts/compounds in supplements, water and fortified foods, excluding naturally occurring food magnesium. This is not a blanket statutory per-drink maximum; its applicability depends on exact form and cumulative exposure. VYBES' disclosed 300 mg bottle is a US product fact, not an EU dose authorization. Normal psychological function is different from treating anxiety or inducing sleep. [L27](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ul-summary-report.pdf) | Authorized normal-function claims exist under specified conditions; support for a noticeable acute sensation is limited. |
| Fiber/gut health | The EU native chicory inulin bowel-function claim requires at least 12 g daily and a defined ingredient specification. [L16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2015/2314/oj/eng) | A soda with a few grams of mixed fiber does not automatically qualify. Exact-product tolerability and sensory outcomes are not established here; generic prebiotic language does not establish compliance in a particular jurisdiction. | Fiber quantity can be verified; exact gut-health efficacy remains ingredient/product specific. |
| Hydration and protein recovery | EU claims distinguish carbohydrate-electrolyte solutions under defined conditions from generic electrolyte water, and protein's contribution to muscle maintenance/growth from instant recovery. [L03](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32012R0432) | Evidence depends on exertion context and composition. Electrolytes alone do not prove superiority to water for desk work; protein grams do not prove performance benefit from the complete product. | Defined nutrient functions; no general advantage across all consumption occasions is established. |

These are selected primary/official evidence anchors, not a meta-analysis. No founder product or formula is assessed. Sponsorship, registration, comparator, attrition and full results affect interpretation of commercial brand studies.

## EU and Czech regulatory context

**Ingredient-use authorization.** The Commission's novel-food framework requires prior authorization where applicable. The Union list specifies identity, use category, maximum level and labeling; a supplement permission does not automatically permit an ordinary canned beverage. The nonbinding status catalogue is an aid, not an authorization. [L05](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food_en), [L06](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/authorisations/union-list-novel-foods_en)

- Citicoline's original EU authorization covered supplements and foods for special medical purposes; it is not evidence of a general soft-drink permission. The reviewed authorization does not establish permission for the same ingredient in Neutonic/C4-style ordinary beverages. [L07](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32014D0423)
- Synthetic paraxanthine has an application, NF-2022-9030, explicitly proposing functional drinks. An applicant's safety argument and intended use are not an authorization. No applicable beverage authorization was established in this review. UPDATE is an informative US positioning benchmark, not a cleared EU recipe. [L08](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/330ca889-27cc-4db5-835b-a0670180b85a_en?filename=novel-food_sum_ongoing-app_2022-90305.pdf)
- The food-use status of L-theanine, rhodiola, ginseng, mushroom extracts and other botanicals depends on exact species/part, extraction process, history and intended food category. A traditional tea or culinary mushroom does not settle the status of a concentrated extract. National rules can also differ for botanicals. [L05](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food_en)

**Claims have separate legal conditions.** The EU register and relevant legal acts specify claim status, conditions and warnings. An EFSA opinion is not itself permission to advertise a claim. The Commission also identifies claims awaiting decisions; neither a missing entry nor a pending claim alone establishes a universally permitted or forbidden status. The applicable status of caffeine alertness/concentration wording and national transitional rules was not resolved for a specific label in this report. An authorized pantothenic-acid claim concerns normal mental performance under specified conditions. No proposed label copy is approved here. [L02](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en), [L03](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32012R0432)

**Caffeine labeling is concentration based.** Regulation 1169/2011 Annex III requires a warning and caffeine mg/100 ml above 150 mg/l for covered beverages, with the specified coffee/tea-name exception. A hypothetical 60 mg in 250 ml is **240 mg/l**, hence above that threshold; a modest amount per can does not remove the requirement. EFSA's 200 mg single-dose and 400 mg/day adult levels are safety reference points across sources, not recommended targets; even 100 mg near bedtime can affect sleep. [L04](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32011R1169), [L01](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine)

**Sleep-related claims and classification.** An EU melatonin claim exists under specific conditions, including 1 mg near bedtime for sleep-onset wording, but that does not settle national food/medicine classification or authorize any formula. That claim does not establish permission to describe a beverage as a treatment for a sleep disorder. [L03](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32012R0432)

This report does not resolve formula-specific Czech food classification, ingredient forms, claims, label language, warnings, operator duties or packaging obligations. Neither a UK product listing nor a US GRAS assertion establishes EU permission.

## CBD: demand, effects, sourcing and regulatory status

**Demand:** US listing availability, funding and cannabinoid category growth do not establish Czech demand for a CBD-only drink. THC beverage growth is not evidence of demand for CBD-only drinks. The reviewed evidence does not isolate Czech preference for the CBD ingredient from interest in relaxation more generally. The current evidence is insufficient to estimate a Czech CBD-drink addressable market.

**Legality:** CBD isolates/extracts are treated as novel foods in the Commission's status decisions. No authorization applicable to the intended EU canned/bottled drink was established. The Commission lists numerous 2026 CBD application terminations, including one dated 16 October; these are decisions about individual applications, not proof that all applications have failed. [L17](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/consultation-process-novel-food-status_en), [L18](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/decisions-terminating-procedure_en)

The Czech SZPI's 29 May 2025 response confirms sanctions, withdrawals/recalls and marketing prohibitions for CBD foods since 2021. Its statement that no proceedings were ongoing on that response date is not a permission to sell. A July 2026 Ministry of Agriculture report continues to identify unauthorized CBD foods in enforcement reporting; that report concerns mainly confectionery, not drink demand. [L19](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/soubor/27-2025-redigovano.aspx), [L20](https://mze.gov.cz/public/portal/mze/tiskovy-servis/aktuality/system-rychleho-varovani-pro-potraviny-a-krmiva-loni-v-eu-zaznamenal-pres-pet-tisic-hlaseni)

**Safety:** EFSA's 9 February 2026 provisional level is 0.0275 mg/kg/day (1.925 mg for a 70 kg adult, approximately 2 mg). It applies only to qualifying ≥98% CBD food-supplement formulations without nanoparticles and with the stipulated production/genotoxicity conditions. It is **not a beverage safe dose or market authorization**. Safety could not be established for under-25s, pregnancy/lactation or people taking medicines; significant organ-system data gaps remain. The framework does not establish safety or permission for a US-style 25 mg can or for nano-emulsion formulations. [L21](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/news/provisional-safe-level-cannabidiol-novel-food)

**Sourcing:** CBDepot, historically a Czech novel-food applicant, publicly describes validated applications for isolated and synthesized CBD; its current site lists a Slovak group contact. This documents a regional industry participant; it does not establish approved supply for a drink or current Czech production. No quotation, batch certificate, audit or beverage suitability was obtained. [L22](https://www.cbdepot.eu/pages/blog/all-cbdepots-novel-food-applications-are-now-declared-valid-by-both-europea) Unverified sourcing dimensions include exact ingredient authorization/specification, food-grade traceability, independent batch cannabinoid assays and detection limits, residual solvents/pesticides/metals/microbiology, stability and dose uniformity in the finished container, emulsifier compatibility and end-of-life potency. Full-spectrum and isolate descriptions do not establish a measured per-can cannabinoid profile or food-use authorization. No present supplier compliance or finished-drink suitability is inferred.

**Claims:** No applicable authorized CBD calm/focus/sleep claim was established. Neither medicinal CBD evidence nor US retail presence supplies one. FDA itself distinguishes observed sales from lawful CBD food marketing. [L23](https://www.fda.gov/food/conversations-experts-food-topics/what-fda-doing-protect-consumers-cannabidiol-cbd-foods) CBD is assessed here as a separate research segment. THC-led and intoxicating formulas are outside the intended launch scope specified in the project brief.
