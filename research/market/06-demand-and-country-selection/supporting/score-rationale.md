# Score rationale and confidence

**Analyst decision preferences, 17 October 2026.** Scores are not measured shares or statistically estimated parameters. The exact numbers are in [inputs](../data/model-inputs.json); this note explains the judgments that matter to the decision. Use [score uncertainty](../data/score-sensitivity.csv) as well as weight sensitivity.

| Factor | Rationale | Evidence / uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Czech founder access5; adjacent AT/PL3; DE/NL2 | Faster local iteration is the stated Czech-based founder advantage; unfamiliar language/service burden may be higher abroad. DE's geography does not force its lower score: it reflects an unverified larger/localized execution task. | Founder location is supplied; actual fluency/travel/network unknown. Raising DE access by one point or lowering CZ access materially changes ranking. Residence is not a customer list. |
| Spend CZ/PL2, AT4, DE/NL5 | AIC83/88/113/120/119 provides broad capacity context. | Official Eurostat2025; no inferred premium-demand conversion. AIC differences within western countries are too small for a precise consumer preference ranking. |
| Channels CZ4, DE/PL3, AT/NL2 | Local venue recruitment presumed cheaper; German/Polish scale offers niche-route possibilities; concentrated Austrian/Dutch chain buying adds friction. | CP15–16/WG09–11 plus observed exact offers; no contracted access. This separate factor concerns sell-through access, not language convenience. Czech4 must fall if Gate1 cannot identify real entry venues. |
| Logistics CZ5, neighboring markets4, NL2 | Domestic versus nearby road distribution versus more distant replenishment. | Geographic operating hypothesis, no freight/parcel quote. Compare landed costs and damage before relying on it. |
| Packaging CZ4, others2 | Foreign DRS/representation/setup creates extra initial work; Czech EPR still applies and is not scored5. | CP17–20/WG12–15/L02; exact Czech deposit commencement unresolved, no historical exemption assumed. These broad scores do not rank real compliance fees. |
| Focus demand3 CZ/DE/NL;2 AT/PL | Exact offers combined with reviewed work/caffeine motive evidence are stronger in the first group; others have offers but thinner directly relevant motive evidence. | CP06–08/WG19–25; a purposive source sample, not true prevalence ranking. One-point evidence uncertainty is reasonable. Do not interpret missing studies as absent customers. |
| General energy demand5 CZ/DE/PL;4 AT/NL | Established category buying and caffeine habits stronger than novel-proposition evidence. | CZ/PL direct category data; German5 is partly an inference from established availability/habits and should be stress-tested to4. AT/NL comparable adult energy frequencies missing. |
| Focus/energy repeat4; social/hydration/gut3; sleep2 | Existing caffeine occasions appear recurrent; other jobs may recur but frequency is less clear; sleep RTD format uptake especially unverified. | Habit/occasion inference, not exact new-product retention. All forecasts deliberately retain a common unvalidated repeat baseline until fieldwork. |
| Focus/social switch3; energy/hydration1; sleep/gut2 | A narrow workday or adult-social experience offers a plausible question to test; broad energy/hydration face obvious incumbents; gut/sleep need stronger reasons than a label. | Hypotheses only. No proposed formula is demonstrated superior. Focus3→2 reverses its2point Czech lead. Taste/portion/clarity are potential reasons, not proven needs. |
| Evidence/legal focus/energy/hydration4; gut3; social2;sleep1 | Familiar caffeine or defined hydration approach has a clearer evidence path than an effect-led botanical/sleep stack. | BR18–40: ingredient status, claims and national classification separate. Not a statement that every evening drink is illegal. A non-effect sensory ritual could improve social score after its own analysis. |

The weights sum to100 but the information does not deserve 100-point precision. Scores are intentionally simple ordinal judgments; differences of a few points are unresolved. Reported default rankings prioritize learning and reversibility, while demand-first/premium-scale variants show how a different founder strategy changes the answer.
