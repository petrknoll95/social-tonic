# Regulatory and evidence update

**17 October 2026 · EU and Czechia unless specified.** This updates the [earlier evidence memo](../../01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md). No exact launch formula, label or supplier has been cleared. Ingredient permission, permissible claims, safety, actual effects and consumer demand are independent questions.

## CBD: separate assessment, separate decision

| Dimension | What is established | What remains unresolved / consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Customer demand | Exact CBD beverage listings exist in the country research; some are unavailable or internally inconsistent. | No reliable Czech CBD-only drink penetration, repeat-purchase, velocity or willingness-to-pay estimate was established. THC beverage growth, CBD-oil demand and UK brand success cannot fill this gap. Test CBD naming separately from a desire for relaxation. |
| Commercial access | Retail offers demonstrate visibility in specific channels. | A retailer's ability to display an add-to-cart button neither establishes a lawful product nor shows paid repeat. | Effects | The previously reviewed 2024 insomnia pilot involved 30 participants and 150 mg nightly sublingual CBD, with mostly null sleep outcomes and some secondary signals. [Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38174873/) and [prior independent review](../../01-us-product-landscape/supporting/independent-review.md). | This does not demonstrate calm or sleep effects from a 10–25 mg mixed RTD, nor support an effective beverage dose. Different route, dose, population and exposure duration; industrial processing and storage also need assessment. Full-text re-access in this pass hit a browser challenge; the previous review is reused explicitly. |
| Safety | EFSA's February 2026 provisional level is **0.0275 mg/kg/day**, approximately 2 mg/day for a 70 kg adult. Its scope is qualifying ≥98% CBD **food supplements**, without nanoparticles and subject to production/genotoxicity conditions. Safety was not established for under-25s, pregnancy/lactation or medicine users. [EFSA opinion](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10154). | This refines the earlier topic: do not imply EFSA has never reached a positive CBD safety conclusion. It is **not** a beverage assessment, an efficacy finding or a Commission marketing authorization. The exact synthetic ingredient and oil matrix cannot be generalized to botanical extracts or aqueous canned emulsions. |

Before any CBD development, obtain the actual implementing authorization and confirm ingredient identity, authorized food category, conditions, data protection and intended country. This pass does not establish a procurement-ready supplier. | Require exact authorized specification/use, food-grade traceability, independent CBD/THC and other-cannabinoid assays with detection limits, contaminant/residual-solvent tests, emulsion compatibility, dose uniformity and end-of-shelf-life stability in the actual can/bottle. Supplier validation is not authorization. No supplier is recommended as cleared. |

**Decision:** keep CBD as a research-only extension. The core concept and commercial validation can proceed independently as CBD-free. Reconsider a CBD variant only after a documented lawful beverage route, ingredient/formulation safety assessment, permissible communication and evidence that customers prefer it enough to justify complexity. This is an authorization/evidence gate, not a claim of zero demand. THC-led and intoxicating products are excluded.

National sources for Germany, Austria and Poland are in [Central EU research](central-eu-research.md); France, the Netherlands, UK and Switzerland are in [Western Europe research](western-europe-research.md). The UK and Switzerland are separate jurisdictions. A UK public-list status or Swiss offer cannot establish EU permission.

## What can support a CBD-free proposition?

**Focus/alertness:** caffeine has a stronger acute ingredient rationale than a generic multi-botanical productivity blend. EFSA describes increased alertness and reduced sleepiness, while noting that even 100 mg near bedtime can disturb sleep in some adults. Its 200 mg single-dose and 400 mg/day adult safety reference points are across sources, not formulation targets. [EFSA caffeine assessment](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine). Less caffeine is a composition choice, not automatically a differentiated proposition or a guarantee against anxiety/crash. Local CANS+ and other rows already occupy this territory.

**Non-stimulant clarity, calm and sleep:** the prior memo's small/mixed theanine and lion's-mane evidence and chronic ashwagandha trials do not validate an acute commercial can. The 2025 lion's-mane study did use a freshly prepared liquid; the missing transfer is exact extract/dose, industrial processing, stability and replicated meaningful outcomes, not liquid delivery itself. Nutrient claims about normal psychological function or mental performance are different from an immediately perceptible calm/focus effect. See [ingredient-by-ingredient evidence](../../01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md).

**Gut health, hydration and recovery:** fermentation, fiber, electrolytes or protein on a label do not establish the complete product's effect. Ingredient identity, dose, population and use conditions remain material. A daily office hydration story needs a comparison with water; gut positioning needs tolerance and applicable claims, not extrapolation from US soda sales. These are secondary tests until repeat purchase and technical feasibility are shown.

Claims review must distinguish authorized, non-authorized and pending claims, including botanical transitional questions. The [Commission register](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en) is a starting point; the current legal act and destination-country interpretation control the final wording. Existing competitor copy is not a compliance precedent.

## Packaging and alcohol-description boundary

All proposed consumer formats are finished cans/bottles. Supplement classification does not make powders or drops eligible benchmarks; a qualifying bottled shot still needs its own classification and ingredient-use check. Coffee/tea and energy caffeine-warning requirements should be assessed from the exact formula and name; the prior memo details the concentration-based rule.

Czech SZPI's **12 March 2025** guidance distinguishes the statutory non-alcoholic beverage/beer category (up to 0.5% ABV) from voluntary zero-alcohol wording, which creates stricter expectations. This matters for beer and kombucha substitutes and any own strict-zero promise. [SZPI guidance](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/docDetail.aspx?docid=3750373&doctype=ART). Do not imply all alcohol-free competitors are chemically identical at zero alcohol, or that fermentation remains stable without verification.
