# Global product and commercial evidence workstream

Research snapshot: 17 October 2026, Europe/Prague (UTC+02:00). This workstream contributes to the integrated report; it is not an exhaustive global census. Exact SKU rows are in [products-workstream.csv](../data/products-workstream.csv), commercial observations in [commercial-workstream.csv](../data/commercial-workstream.csv), and reference metadata in [product-sources.csv](product-sources.csv). Source IDs beginning P identify this workstream. EU-specific Humble+ and Clean MONO rows are owned by the EU workstream. FeelGo remains an unverified/excluded discovery lead and is not counted as a confirmed product.

## What the evidence establishes

Creatine-led soda is a real, internationally dispersed product format. The strongest evidence of current US offers is Creia, Konsoom and Joyburst. VitClear sells a UK soda; Denmark's ION18 has marketed finished cans but is currently sold out and offered for sale as a business. Additional EU products appear in the EU workstream. FITAID is a longer-lived carbonated sports-recovery comparator. Still dairy drinks and a finished liquid shot broaden the adjacent landscape. A current listing establishes an offer, not nationwide stock, repeat purchase, efficacy or shelf-life retention.

Commercial success remains incompletely observable. No independently audited, product-specific revenue, retention or retail velocity was established for these sodas. FITAID has the strongest documented operating continuity and a company-reported portfolio share. Joyburst has verified club-retail listings, but the much-repeated 15-million-can story is promotional prelaunch demand/supply language. ION18 offers unusually candid small-batch figures and manufacturing problems. None of these findings warrants labelling private brands unsuccessful merely because financial data are absent.

## Creia in detail

[Creia Drinks](https://creiadrinks.com/products/variety-pack) markets a daily, lightly sparkling can for people wanting a pleasant creatine routine. It is distinct from the UK powder-sachet company at creia.com; the latter's customers, reviews and founders must not be attributed to this drink. Three flavors are Peach Ginger, Yuzu and Watermelon Lime. Current Watermelon Lime copy says sold out, returning in November; the other flavors have active offers. An original launch date was not established. [P001–P002, P007–P008]

The [official nutrition artwork](https://creiadrinks.com/cdn/shop/t/10/assets/creia-nutrition-all.png?v=116422104381408979331785627586) was visually inspected. Each can is 355 mL, 40 kcal, 10 g sugar including 9 g added sugar, with 75 mg sodium, 50 mg potassium and 25 mg magnesium. Full flavor-specific ingredient lists are transcribed in the product CSV. All include CreaBev-A, described as creatine monohydrate, soluble pea fiber and sunflower lecithin. The marketed 5 g CreaBev amount needs a supplier or manufacturer specification to resolve blend-versus-active interpretation; retailer wording of “5g creatine monohydrate” does not resolve this independently. [P003, P006]

The accessible public catalogue currently lists $39.99 per 12-pack and $119.97 per 36-pack. It exposes 10% subscription prices, while the page also contains a first-order promotion and contradictory language that subscriptions are not yet available. Accordingly the base comparison is $3.33 per can, before any unresolved promotion. Free contiguous-US delivery is stated. A brand list contains 62 stockists; Airgoods has a wholesale listing. These are distribution leads, not confirmed inventory or sell-through. [P002, P004–P006]

Creia is unusually explicit about technical uncertainty: its displayed stability curve is an estimate from Glanbia testing at the target pH, while independent tests of its own cans are underway. It states that it raises pH as far as palatability allows, uses smaller runs and fills above the declared amount. The actual pH, overage, complete retention data and validated shelf life were not disclosed in the accessed material. This is evidence of work in progress, not independent confirmation of a stable finished product. Revenue, units, cohorts and retailer reorders remain unknown. [P002]

Cost scope is unclear and the figure is not a transferable manufacturing quotation. The sale explanation is lack of founder focus and capital, not an admission that consumers reject the category. Its claim to have solved stability has no accessible assay attached. [P017]

## Adjacent formats and continuity

**FITAID RX / FITAID Creatine:** Original RX launched in summer 2017, RX Zero in May 2019, and a CreaBev reformulation in April 2024. This is a carbonated sports-recovery drink with creatine prominent, rather than a pure soda ritual proposition. Current US listings include Sour Grape plus zero-sugar Sour Grape, Blue Raspberry and Juicy Apple. Historic RX Zero had 1 g creatine and 45 mg caffeine. Current pages say caffeine-free yet retain a 40 mg FAQ, so historic dose/caffeine must not be transferred to today's formula. Direct US-site access sometimes redirected to the EU site; indexed current product text and a HEB listing were used, and current full active labels remain unverified. [P030–P035]

A 2024 company release says Sour Grape accounted for 23% of portfolio sales and nearly 30% of Recovery volume, without an absolute denominator, reporting window or geography. The 2019 figure exceeding $100,000 concerned combined preorders for two launches, FITAID Zero and RX Zero; it is not creatine-product revenue. A permanent 2018 price reduction does not establish failure. Multi-year availability supports persistence, while profitability and repeat-purchase rates remain unknown. [P032–P034]

**PRIME Shake + Creatine:** Chocolate, Cookies & Cream and Vanilla are still dairy-based shakes declaring 32 g protein, 3 g creatine and 3 g sugar. Official pages exist; Chocolate and Vanilla offer notification rather than an order. Trade reporting dates the launch to late March 2026. The CreaShield stability story is not an independent finished-bottle assay. Whole-company PRIME performance is excluded from this line's traction. [P036, P042–P044]

**Lifeway Muscle Mates:** Three still cultured-dairy flavors were announced on 8 September 2025 with planned Q4 shipment. Current official pages and Target/Walmart listings move the line beyond announcement-only status. Two Target listings are $3.79 per 8 oz bottle; Walmart's $3.82 Mixed Berry listing was locally unavailable. Mixed Berry/Strawberry Banana nutrition lists 20 g protein and 5 g creatine in a 240 mL serving. Website flavor/mineral transcription errors are flagged rather than silently repaired. Lifeway's overall public-company revenue does not measure this new line. [P045–P050]

**Ultramade CR5:** A finished 77 mL liquid shot is sold through UAE channels. The current offer says 5 g Creapure monohydrate; Rightdose shows AED9.60 per bottle, while the brand 12-pack and another retailer show sold out. An October 2025 brand article instead says 10 g per bottle and two half-servings; a February 2026 article says 5 g and one bottle daily. The older two-year expiry assertion is not supported by accessible retention assays. This is a genuine adjacent liquid-shot lead, with unresolved version/dose information. [P051–P055]

**Body Armour Australia Hydrate+:** A 330 mL sparkling recovery drink with 1,485 mg creatine marketing appears at Nutrition Warehouse and in a supplier datasheet. It is unrelated to the US Bodyarmor brand. A complete ingredient list is recorded, but no product-specific commercial figures were found. [P057–P058]

## Historical evidence and exclusions

Bio-Synergy's Sub Zero existed by June 2008, corroborated by a contemporaneous trade archive and UKIPO proceedings. A June 2026 retrospective reports its discontinuation after creatine degraded over six months. That is useful adverse historical evidence, but no original finished-product assay or exact withdrawal date was retrieved. Republishing the same retrospective does not create independent corroboration. [P036–P038]

Musashi Creatine Cola appears as a 500 mL plastic-bottle product in South Australia's 20 May 1999 Gazette. A 24 October 2019 entry revokes its container approval after a waste-management arrangement was cancelled. The latter is not a 2019 launch announcement, nor sufficient proof of when or why commercial sales ended. It challenges broad “first ever” promotional claims without establishing efficacy or success. [P040–P041]

Bang is historical marketing context, not a valid successful-creatine delivery benchmark. The US appellate memorandum of 15 April 2025 affirms the false-advertising judgment and injunction relating to Super Creatine; it explains that the challenged Bang drink did not contain creatine. More than $270 million in damages is a litigation figure, not revenue or a measure of ordinary creatine-formulation failure. [P039]

KA-EX's 2026 creatine booster stores 3 g of dry creatine in an activation cap. Its “ready-to-drink” headline does not make it a finished creatine-containing liquid before activation, so it is excluded from the primary register and retained only as a technical alternative. Create's verified offer is a powder mix/gummy business, not a qualifying drink. Supplement powders sold under energy-drink brands likewise do not establish creatine in those brands' cans. Concentrate/serum drops and dry-cap products were excluded. [P059–P060]

## Search method, limits and audit priorities

Discovery combined official-brand pages, product catalogues, retailer searches, trade reporting, legal records and historic product searches. Queries covered “creatine soda”, “sparkling creatine”, “creatine ready to drink”, “creatine shot”, “creatine cola”, individual brands with revenue/launch/discontinued/stability terms, and Danish “kreatin på dåse”. US and UK/Canada/Australia/UAE leads received follow-up; the EU teammate separately searched local languages and retailers. Current Shopify product JSON was inspected where the public page was ambiguous. Creia and Joyburst official label images were viewed directly; no laboratory work or purchases were performed.

Repository 01/02/26 market material supplied general evidence-quality and category context, but yielded no decisive current creatine-soda sales or stability proof. The new register should not inherit general functional-drink growth figures as demand for this format. Exact launch dates remain unknown for several small brands. No paywalled market reports, private retail panels or company interviews were obtained. Some retailer pages require delivery addresses; listings are recorded without assuming nationwide fulfillment.

Priority validation requests are (1) finished-product batch assays through shelf life; (2) exact active-creatine versus preparation amounts; (3) current formula/label versions for contradictory pages; (4) dated product-specific sales and retailer reorders; (5) subscription cohorts and repeat behavior; and (6) independent confirmation of EU stock. These require evidence, not inference from ingredient popularity, stockouts, press, reviews, or shelf counts. The current workstream does not imply that these validations have been completed.
