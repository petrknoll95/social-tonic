# Seven-day routines diary

**Proposed, not conducted · 17 October 2026.** Follow eight consenting interview participants for seven consecutive days including a weekend: four with work/study needs and one from each other provisional segment. For the private-evening diarist, seek naturally occurring after-work and bedtime routines and code them separately; if one never occurs, mark that coverage gap rather than asking the person to create it. Replace dropouts transparently; do not silently fill missing days. This small panel is for recurrence and context, not efficacy or population estimates.

## Participant instructions

„Sedm dní si krátce zaznamenejte situace, kdy se rozhodujete, co budete pít, nebo kdy se snažíte zvládnout práci, únavu, pohyb, odpočinek či setkání s lidmi. Neměňte své běžné návyky. Nemusíte nic kupovat ani zkoušet. Zajímá nás i situace, kdy jste nepil/a nic nebo nic neřešil/a. Vynechte osobní nebo pracovní údaje, které nechcete sdílet.“

At onboarding, agree one personally relevant recurring occasion without requiring dissatisfaction. Make a short entry at that occasion (including no-drink/no-need cases), plus up to two other noteworthy decisions daily. Complete one 60-second evening check even on uneventful days. Expected burden: about 3–5 minutes/day; this is a planning estimate. Use a simple private form or message template, not this public repository. Compensation must not depend on reporting a problem, buying a drink or completing every entry.

## Event form — approximately one minute

1. Participant code, date/time and broad setting (home/work/study/outdoors/social; no precise address).
2. What were you trying to do? What, if anything, did you want to feel or change?
3. What was happening just before? Optional prior-night rest rating; recent food, movement and interruptions in their own words.
4. What did you choose: exact beverage and can/bottle size if applicable, prepared beverage, food/break/other action, or nothing? Optional package photo without people/identifiers.
5. How much did you consume? Caffeine amount **only if known from a label**; otherwise “unknown.” Record time and other caffeinated items, without pretending a coffee's dose is known.
6. Where did it come from? Actual spend, currency, who paid, promotion, portion/pack basis. If already at home, original cost or unknown; distinguish free from unknown.
7. What alternatives did you consider, and why this one? What almost changed your choice?
8. Optional later note after the occasion: what happened, what you noticed, what else changed? No fixed expectation that an effect should occur.

For work/study occasions only: record an optional planned task category and whether it was completed/partly completed/not completed/changed. Separately note felt wakefulness and ability to stay with the task on a simple 0–4 scale with “not relevant.” Capture interruptions and errors/rework only if noticed; do not demand confidential work or infer objective error reduction. Do not combine ratings and task completion into one productivity score.

## Evening check — one minute

- Did the agreed occasion happen? Yes/no; if no, why (e.g. day off)? Missing response is not “no.”
- Were any relevant decisions missed? Describe briefly or mark unknown.
- Any drinks or actions you chose not to take, and why?
- What mattered most today? Anything unexpected?

On mornings following a private wind-down entry, optionally ask bedtime/wake time in broad bands and perceived rest. Do not diagnose sleep or infer that the prior drink caused the result. Participants keep ordinary habits; there is no caffeine, CBD or alcohol intervention.

## Follow-up and analysis

Conduct a 15-minute closing interview using two actual entries: one ordinary and one contrasting. Ask what the researcher misunderstood. Compare stated frequency with recorded opportunities, purchases and no-purchase decisions. Code the same five productivity meanings used in the interview guide. Track whether a new hypothetical drink would replace an existing paid purchase, a free option, preparation at home, or no drink.

Report completion as person-days received / person-days expected, and occasion entries separately. With eight people over seven days, the maximum is **56 person-days**, not 56 independent participants. Do not count multiple entries from one person as independent demand confirmations. Keep a negative-case column for satisfied routines and no need. Diary prompting, self-report, novelty, recruitment bias, variable work tasks, sleep and concurrent intake prevent causal efficacy conclusions.

Use the blank [diary template](../data/diary-template.csv) and [interview coding template](../data/interview-coding-template.csv). They contain headers only; no fabricated responses. Store completed participant data privately. Publish only de-identified summaries with consent and appropriate disclosure controls.
