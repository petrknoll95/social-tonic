# Caffeine and micronutrients: claims workstream

Research cut-off and access date: **17 October 2026**. Geographic scope: EU rules; Czech enforcement guidance; one German enforcement-research counterpoint identified separately. Status: source-backed research for integration, not approval of a formula or label. Finished consumer formats are **ready-to-drink liquids in cans or bottles only**. The calculations below are hypothetical can/bottle examples, not a chosen formulation. Ingredients used in manufacturing may be powders or concentrates.

Repository inputs: [topic 01 evidence](../../../market/01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md) and [topic 06 evidence/gates](../../../market/06-demand-and-country-selection/supporting/benefits-benchmarks-and-regulatory-gates.md). Those reports correctly separated caffeine science from legal permission but left the claims status open. This memo resolves the authorisation question and identifies the narrower remaining transition question. Sources CN01–CN21 are described in [the workstream source ledger](caffeine-and-nutrients-sources.csv).

## Findings that change the decision

**Do not build a launch message around a supposedly EU-authorised caffeine alertness/concentration claim.** On the research date the live Commission register contained no authorised caffeine alertness/concentration claim. It did contain the explicit non-authorisation of the lower-dose alertness application, [POL-HC-8450](https://ec.europa.eu/food/food-feed-portal/screen/health-claims/eu-register/details/POL-HC-8450). The live register was queried through its public data endpoint (2,337 published records), cross-checked against legal acts and the Commission's linked pending-claims document. An old register PDF generated in 2013 is not the live register. [CN01–CN03]

**B5, iodine, iron and zinc provide narrower, authorised routes**, subject to nutrient and other conditions. B5 can support *normal mental performance*; iodine/iron/zinc can support *normal cognitive function*. None authorises an upgraded promise of acute focus, more completed tasks, all-day concentration, clearer thinking or no crash. Legal claim eligibility does not establish a perceptible benefit from an extra dose in an already adequately nourished customer. [CN04–CN07, CN15]

**The endpoints differ.** Alertness concerns wakefulness; concentration/attention concerns attention tasks; cognitive function and mental performance encompass different normal functions; productivity is useful output in real life; energy-yielding metabolism is a normal biochemical function; perceived energy is a subjective experience; sleep onset/duration/quality are separate outcomes. Do not bridge these endpoints merely because they sound adjacent. This is an analytical distinction used to prevent claim inflation, not a claim that every phrase has only one possible legal classification. [CN04–CN07, CN13, CN15]

## Caffeine: 75 mg science, rejected 40 mg application and the transition issue

1. EFSA's 2011 opinion supported increased alertness and attention at **at least 75 mg per serving** in the relevant adult population. That scientific opinion is not an authorising regulation. The associated IDs include 736, 1485, 1491 and 2375 for attention, with additional alertness IDs. [CN13]
2. A 2016 draft proposed wording for alertness and concentration with **at least 75 mg per quantified portion**, **no more than 200 mg in a single intake**, information about **400 mg/day from all sources (200 mg for pregnant/breastfeeding women)**, and exclusion of foods targeting children including adolescents. **These are conditions of a draft, not enacted claim conditions.** Parliament vetoed the proposal on 7 July 2016. [CN10–CN11]
3. Separately, Regulation 2016/1411 refused the Article 13(5) application for increased alertness in the **40–75 mg-per-serving range**. Its recital reiterates EFSA's positive assessment at at least 75 mg. Read the decision in that context: do not say it rejected caffeine's alertness science at every dose, and do not treat the upper boundary as a new authorisation. The current register reproduces the range in its rejected claim. [CN03, CN12]
4. The Commission's current register information page still links a **12 June 2013** working list of claims awaiting finalisation, including the relevant caffeine IDs. Regulation 432/2012 recital 11 refers such pending claims to Article 28(5) and (6). **A listed ID alone does not prove that a particular 2026 Czech label has a valid transition.** The linked list is old; some IDs also concern different endpoints. [CN01–CN02, CN04]
5. Article 28(5) is limited to Article 13(1)(a) claims; claims about psychological or behavioural functions fall under Article 13(1)(b), for which the materially different Article 28(6) route needs examination. Article 28(6) concerns claims used under national provisions before entry into force and the relevant evaluation/application history (including the 19 January 2008 application deadline in paragraph (b)). A new entrant cannot assume that “pending” plus 75 mg satisfies this history. Czech SZPI's 2016 on-hold guidance itself says Article 28 conditions and all other food-law requirements must be met. [CN09, CN14]
6. **Counterevidence:** a 2024 primary surveillance paper by CVUA Karlsruhe food-control researchers says most caffeine transitions no longer apply and records the absence of authorisation. This is German enforcement research, not a Czech ruling or a new EU authorising/refusing act. Its disagreement with a casual reading of the old pending list is a reason for targeted legal verification, not for silently choosing the most favourable interpretation. [CN20]

**Decision:** exclude caffeine effect claims from the provisional ready-to-use message hierarchy. A lawyer should verify the precise endpoint/claim ID, Article 13 category, applicable Article 28 route and historical evidence, current Commission status and Czech interpretation before any exception is used. Do not describe all caffeine claims as universally prohibited at every dose in every transitional circumstance; equally, do not describe 75 mg as an EU permission threshold. Neutral verified caffeine quantity can be considered separately, with the full presentation reviewed. No caffeine/productivity or no-crash authorisation was identified.

## Micronutrient matrix for an ordinary RTD beverage

All positive rows below are **specific authorised health claims**, subject to the food being at least a **source of the named nutrient**, compliance with general claim rules and use of the authorised meaning. Exact English wording is shown for research; final Czech text should use the official Czech version and be reviewed in its complete layout. These are not interchangeable nutrient promises. [CN04–CN07]

| Proposed wording | Named eligible nutrients verified in current register | Conditions and safer use | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Pantothenic acid contributes to normal mental performance” | Pantothenic acid/B5; live register POL-HC-6433 | Source of B5; attribute the function to B5 and retain “normal”. | Immediate focus, higher intelligence, faster work or finished-drink productivity. |
| “[Iodine / iron / zinc] contributes to normal cognitive function” | Iodine POL-HC-6385; iron POL-HC-6390; zinc POL-HC-6533 | Source of the chosen named nutrient. Folate and zinc do **not** gain this claim merely because other B vitamins/minerals do. | Feeling energised, alertness, calorie burning/weight loss or all-day energy. |
| “[Nutrient] contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue” | B2/riboflavin, niacin, B5, B6, folate, B12, vitamin C, iron | Source of named nutrient. | A guaranteed immediate lift, eliminating tiredness, treating chronic fatigue, preventing a caffeine crash. |

Safer provisional alternative: truthful occasion/flavour copy plus a clearly attributed eligible nutrient claim. | Broad entitlement from any nutrient, caffeine or combination. |

The current register also contains a **water** claim on maintaining normal physical and cognitive functions, subject to instructions concerning at least 2 litres daily from all sources and use only on water complying with the relevant water legislation. It is not an automatic route for a flavoured functional soft drink. [CN04, CN07]

## Amounts, portions and the finished drink

The source-of-vitamins/minerals nutrition claim refers to a significant amount; Regulation 1169/2011 Annex XIII Part A gives, as a rule, **7.5% NRV per 100 ml for beverages**, **or 15% NRV per portion where the package contains only a single portion**. Do not use the 15% rule for an arbitrarily defined portion from a multi-serving bottle. “High in” requires twice the source-of amount. These are claim-eligibility calculations, not recommended doses or upper safety limits. [CN08, CN16]

SZPI's 2022 enriched-food guidance specifically gives 7.5% NRV/100 ml for beverages. Its older general claims guidance includes a 15%/100 g or 100 ml summary that should not displace the beverage-specific Annex XIII rule. The single-portion alternative comes from the binding EU text, not the simplified SZPI summary. For a robust initial brief, quantify both bases and ask final review to confirm pack/portion treatment rather than hiding it. [CN08, CN15, CN17]

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B5 (6 mg) | 0.45 mg | 1.125 mg | 1.485 mg | 0.9 mg |
| B6 (1.4 mg) | 0.105 mg | 0.2625 mg | 0.3465 mg | 0.21 mg |
| B12 (2.5 µg) | 0.1875 µg | 0.46875 µg | 0.61875 µg | 0.375 µg |
| Niacin (16 mg) | 1.2 mg | 3 mg | 3.96 mg | 2.4 mg |
| Magnesium (375 mg) | 28.125 mg | 70.3125 mg | 92.8125 mg | 56.25 mg |
| Iodine (150 µg) | 11.25 µg | 28.125 µg | 37.125 µg | 22.5 µg |

Calculations: NRV × 0.075; multiply the concentration by 2.5 or 3.3; single-portion alternative NRV × 0.15. Values are intentionally unrounded arithmetic, not proposed label declarations or manufacturing targets. Example: 0.9 mg B5 in a genuine one-portion 330 ml can is 15% NRV per can but only 0.273 mg/100 ml, below the per-100-ml route. Thus pack architecture matters. Formulation allowances for degradation and analytical variability must be scientifically justified; do not plan to land exactly on the legal floor after shelf life. [CN08, CN18]

Eligibility requires the **finished drink** to contain the relevant amount in available form and the quantity reasonably expected to be consumed to deliver the claimed benefit. For magnesium use elemental magnesium, not the mass of magnesium citrate; analogous distinctions apply to mineral salts and vitamin premixes. Measure/justify final concentration after processing and through the intended shelf life. A supplier certificate for the raw blend alone does not verify a can. [CN09, CN18]

For added nutrients, check **Regulation 1925/2006 Annex I (nutrients) and Annex II (forms)** for ordinary foods and any additional novel-food conditions. Examples listed in the ordinary-food annex include calcium D-pantothenate, sodium D-pantothenate and dexpanthenol for B5; nicotinic acid and nicotinamide for niacin; and magnesium salts of citric acid. A substance allowed in supplements is not necessarily allowed in an ordinary drink: do not borrow the separate supplement list in Directive 2002/46. The register authorisation of a nutrient claim is not an ingredient-use authorisation or a safety clearance. The EUR-Lex act identifies **26 November 2025** as its current consolidation; exact selected form must be checked again against that text and associated food-use permissions. [CN19]

## Caffeine concentration, warnings and safety are different tests

For covered beverages containing **more than 150 mg caffeine/litre from any source**, Regulation 1169/2011 Annex III requires the high-caffeine warning (not recommended for children or pregnant/breastfeeding women), in the same field of vision as the beverage name, followed by caffeine quantity in **mg/100 ml**. The exception is for beverages based on coffee, tea or their extracts **where the name includes “coffee” or “tea”**. Merely adding green-tea-derived caffeine to a citrus soda does not automatically qualify. A fact about milligrams per can does not replace the mg/100 ml statement. [CN08]

| Hypothetical finished can/bottle | mg/100 ml | mg/litre | Above 150 mg/l threshold? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 40 mg / 250 ml | 16 | 160 | Yes, unless the precise coffee/tea exception applies |
| 40 mg / 330 ml | 12.12 | 121.21 | No; other labelling/claims obligations remain |
| 75 mg / 250 ml | 30 | 300 | Yes, subject to exception |
| 75 mg / 330 ml | 22.73 | 227.27 | Yes, subject to exception |
| 80 mg / 330 ml | 24.24 | 242.42 | Yes, subject to exception |

The threshold is exceeded above 37.5 mg in 250 ml or 49.5 mg in 330 ml. Exactly 150 mg/l is not “in excess of” the threshold; manufacturing variation still needs consideration. These computations are not an instruction to formulate just below a warning boundary.

EFSA's **up to 200 mg in a single dose** and **up to 400 mg/day** for healthy non-pregnant adults are all-source safety reference levels, not efficacy targets, mandatory portion recommendations or health-claim permission. Pregnancy/lactation considerations differ. EFSA also notes that **100 mg near bedtime can affect sleep in some adults**. This defeats a simplistic inference that “normal metabolism” or “no crash” can promise restful sleep later. Individual response and total intake matter. [CN21]

## Focused legal-review questions

- Which exact statement is a nutrient claim, health claim, name/implied claim, or factual/sensory/occasion statement in the complete pack, website and advertisement?
- Does the ingredient, form, dose, food category and shelf-life amount support the precise claim, with reasonable serving instructions and the required dietary/lifestyle information?
- Does a “Focus” masterbrand or a productivity subline convert otherwise narrow B5 wording into an unsupported broader promise?
- For any caffeine effect exception: which exact claim ID and Article 28 paragraph apply, with what documented history and Czech basis, despite the 2016 veto and counterevidence? Do not approve solely from 75 mg or an EFSA opinion.
- Does the can/bottle genuinely contain one portion, and which threshold route is being used? Is each vitamin/mineral named in a combined claim independently eligible?
- Are the caffeine source, total amount, warning threshold, coffee/tea exception and same-field-of-vision requirement correct?
- Have claims been kept separate from actual acute efficacy, willingness to pay and whether the desired drink experience is noticeable? These remain product/consumer research questions.

No supplier contact, purchase, clinical trial or consumer test was conducted. The primary concept remains CBD-free; adding CBD would not expand any caffeine/nutrient claim and requires a separate ingredient-legality and claims assessment.
