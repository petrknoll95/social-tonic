# Blind comparison and repeated-serving protocol

**Proposed, not executed · 17 October 2026 · adult Czech pilot, transferable only after local replication.** This is a taste/occasion study, not an efficacy or safety trial. All products and prototypes are finished liquids supplied in cans or bottles; controlled aliquots in neutral tasting cups do not change that product-format requirement. No preparation-dependent consumer products enter the benchmark set.

## Why several stages

ISO 11136 covers controlled consumer liking tests; its public scope explicitly excludes ad-libitum intake measurement. The proposed full-serving observations below therefore extend beyond that standard, and this plan does not claim ISO certification. The 2014 edition was confirmed in 2025. [ME01, ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/50125.html).

Attribute questions can change liking judgments; Popper et al. found this particularly for just-about-right questions in dairy desserts. That supports collecting preference before diagnostic prompts, without claiming the same effect size in these drinks. [ME02, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodqual.2003.12.004). Methven et al. observed mouthdrying/coating and metallic build-up in nutritional drinks, with older adults/patients. This motivates checking whole-serving experience; it does not predict deterioration in healthy Czech adults drinking clear RTDs. [ME03, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodqual.2010.04.009).

**All sample counts, volumes, times, temperatures, analysis choices and gates below are proposed study settings, not findings or standard-mandated cut-offs.** Adapt them with the external sensory/formulation partner before recruitment.

## Stage 0: prepare and screen cheaply

1. Recruit 8–12 adult Czech residents for vocabulary/occasion interviews and questionnaire comprehension. Purposive mix of current energy/focus users, tea/water users, sweetener avoiders and evening non-alcoholic buyers. Ask about last actual occasions before showing flavours. Do not estimate population prevalence from this sample.
2. Ask an outsourced beverage technologist to produce finished, food-safe bottled bench samples for **F1 citrus focus** and **F2 peach/tea focus** first. Request the same declared target serving, ingredient identity/dose, sweetness architecture and processing record for each comparison. Begin with lawful simple bases; integrate actual proposed actives before selecting a winner. Base-only liking cannot validate a final active formula.
3. Freeze a product manifest: exact SKU/market, can/bottle volume, lot, best-before, ingredients/allergens, caffeine and all actives per container, permitted use, serving instructions, storage, preparation/opening time, pH, titratable acidity and soluble solids where measured, carbonation where measured, and batch contact. Unknown active exposure or unsuitable legal/safety status excludes a product from ingestion until resolved. No unapproved CBD ingestion.
4. Two people independently check codes, sample identity and dose calculations. One unblinded coordinator keeps the key; participants and the questioner/analyst are blind to brand, claims, price and recipe identity until initial analysis is frozen. Staff must disclose required safety information during consent, using a common list across the flight when practicable; never hide allergens or risks to protect blinding. Record any compromised blinding.

## Stage 1: preference screen before diagnostics

**Suggested pilot:** 48 adults in the priority workday cohort; 4 products, all 48 taste all 4. This is directional convenience/quota research, not a representative Czech survey or a powered launch claim. A later social or hydration cohort must be recruited for its own recent-use behaviour. Do not split 48 across four unrelated occasions and imply reliable category estimates.

**First focus flight:** F1 prototype, F2 prototype, one exact local focus control and one exact still RTD tea control: Nupreme HiMatcha BIO 330 ml bottle is locally listed, but conflicting sugar declarations require a physical-label check before use. CANS+ Performance raspberry 330 ml can is the proposed first local focus control; Vilgain lemon/lime is the later sweetened alternative, limited to one pack/day. These choices are conditional on the Stage 0 gates. If imports are practical later, run a separate US comparison flight rather than adding a fifth/sixth sample. An unsweetened local control and a sweetened one can be compared in a second four-product session. No comparison can isolate carbonation, flavour, caffeine or sweetness when several change together; this first screen compares complete propositions. Follow with matched-base experiments to identify drivers.

Recruit 18+ adults with at least two recent relevant occasions/month, varying usual sweetness and carbonation preferences. Record residence, age band, recent beverage choices, usual caffeine intake, sweetener familiarity and actual use occasion. Aim for variation in genders and age bands and recruit beyond founder friends. Do not impose gender/flavour assumptions. Exclude people with temporary taste/smell impairment and screen allergies, pregnancy/breastfeeding, caffeine sensitivity, relevant medication/conditions and ingredient suitability via a competent study partner. These exclusions define this pilot's limits; do not describe the excluded populations as universally unable to consume the product.

### Controlled serving

- Quiet, odour-free room, neutral light, separated responses, no brand packs visible and no group discussion until forms are locked. Same neutral cup type, fill and headspace. Colour stays visible and is part of product experience; blinding hides identity, not all sensory differences. Record recognition after taste ratings.
- For the first cold comparison, use **8 ± 2°C measured at service**, **30 ml per coded cup**, no ice/garnish. Log each pour and start tasting within **2 minutes of opening/pouring**. Use a new sealed container if delay exceeds the window; do not pool leftovers. Apply the same window to still samples. Record actual departures. Temperature/CO2 can affect oral sensation, as shown in controlled carbonated solutions; the chosen 8°C setting is our practical proposal. [ME04, Green 1992](https://doi.org/10.1093/chemse/17.4.435).
- Do not shake carbonated drinks. Apply any required inversion only to appropriate still products, with the same documented method per SKU. Never degas commercial drinks to manufacture a “still version”; prepare matched formulations for that question.
- Serve monadically, one sample at a time. Use a fresh random three-digit code per product/session; avoid 111/123 or any name clue. Assign the four balanced sequences **A B D C; B C A D; C D B A; D A C B**, 12 people each. They balance position and immediate ordered pair carryover. Randomize participant allocation to sequences; codes are not sequence letters.
- Allow at least **90 seconds** between products with room-temperature still water; extend to 3–5 minutes if a participant reports persistent aftertaste. Neutral unsalted crackers only if needed, same procedure recorded for everyone. Do not use flavoured rinses. Ask for no strongly flavoured food, gum or smoking/vaping in the hour before the test; record departures instead of manufacturing perfect compliance.
- In **Pass A**, ask only overall liking (1–9), an open comment, and desire to drink a full serving (0–10) per sample. After all four, collect a preference choice **with tie/neither options** and a reason. Lock these primary responses before Pass B.
- After a break, **Pass B** uses fresh pours and independent counterbalanced order for diagnostics: 0–10 intensities, a limited descriptor list plus “other/none,” and JAR for sweetness, acidity and carbonation. Body JAR is optional if texture is a material variable. Ask aftertaste at 30 and 90 seconds after the final swallow, before rinse. Avoid forcing participants to rate a long vocabulary on every sample. Do not revise Pass A ratings.

Total planned exposure is **4 × 30 ml × 2 passes = 240 ml**, but all caffeine/actives are summed by sample, including repeats and any replacement pours. Formula: `exposure = labelled amount per container × consumed ml / container ml`. For illustration, a 120 mg/355 ml drink contributes 10.14 mg per 30 ml pour; this example is arithmetic, not a dose recommendation. Spitting is optional but does not justify ignoring potential ingestion. Screen total same-day background intake and container-specific restrictions; do not infer safety from small volumes alone.

EFSA's 2015 caffeine assessment gives no-concern levels for healthy adults of 200 mg per single exposure and 400 mg/day from all sources, and notes possible sleep effects at 100 mg near bedtime. These are **not study targets, recommendations or individual guarantees**. The organizer should set a lower exposure budget when appropriate and follow product restrictions and qualified advice. [ME05, EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/corporate_publications/files/efsaexplainscaffeine150527.pdf). Caffeine-free cohorts get a separate flight; no energy drinks in bedtime tests. Do not seek a perceptible “kick” during sensory testing.

## Stage 2: matched recipe diagnostics

Choose a promising direction, then vary one factor at a time or use a pre-specified small factorial design. A useful first experiment crosses two sweetness levels with still versus low carbonation, holding aroma, acids and active doses fixed. Another compares exact magnesium material/dose versus matched base, not “magnesium” as a generic ingredient. Match sweetness perceptually when comparing sweeteners; matching grams or Brix does not achieve this. Keep total product count at four per session and repeat the primary-preference-first procedure. Account for repeat exposure in the analysis; a returning participant is not a new independent person.

## Stage 3: full serving and second serving

Select two finalists plus the best local control only if there is learning value. A proposed **24–30-person crossover pilot**, ideally recruited independently of Stage 1, tests one actual intended can/bottle serving on each of three matched occasion days. Randomize the six possible product orders approximately equally. Keep time of day, meal relationship and setting as similar as practical; record deviations. At least 24 hours between product days is a practical schedule, **not a demonstrated pharmacological washout**; an ingredient-specific reviewer may require longer spacing or exclusion.

Use overwrapped packs with anonymous codes; mandatory safety/use information remains available. Different shapes or obvious taste may reveal identity; ask recognition and report the limitation. Record liking after initial sips, around half consumed, and at stopping/end, plus actual ml consumed, duration, reason for stopping, aftertaste at 90 seconds and perceived suitability. **Never require finishing.** Measure residual volume or weight with known density; do not assume every drink has density exactly 1 g/ml. Completion is an observation, not compliance pressure.

For each finalist and the local control, provide a second whole can/bottle on a **separate matched day** by default. Pre-assign and counterbalance the second-round product order independently of the first round (the same six sequences distributed approximately equally); keep occasion/time conditions matched. If resources allow repeating only the finalists, document that change before testing and do not claim their repeat trajectory is better than an unrepeated control. Ask how much was consumed and repeat the brief ratings; this is real second-serving experience rather than intention. A same-occasion second serving is optional only for formulas explicitly cleared for that total exposure, permitted by the label and appropriate to the occasion; log it separately. A one-per-day product cannot enter a two-can same-day condition. Declining a second serving because of caffeine, thirst, bedtime volume or cost is different from disliking flavour. Record the reason. No incentive should depend on finishing or consuming a second pack.

A separate optional choice measure offers another serving, water or no drink without disclosing brands; the organizer does not infer paid repeat from free acceptance. After blind data are locked, a truthful concept/price exercise may measure expectation and hypothetical choice with “current drink/no purchase.” A future paid repeat test requires a separate approved commercial setup; no purchase is performed in this research assignment.

## Occasion modules: fit, not clinical claims

| Module | Conditions and comparator | Neutral observation/questions | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workday focus/energy | Usual desk break, similar task and time; local focus/energy or qualifying RTD tea/coffee | Does the taste/portion suit this break? Distracting aroma, residue, carbonation, pace, temperature drift | No objective productivity or alertness effect inferred |
| Exercise hydration | Separate light/moderate usual activity context; still bottled water plus eligible electrolyte bottle | Thirst, salt/mineral impression, ease of drinking, stomach comfort, desired volume; activity/duration/weather logged | Water is a real alternative; no dehydration challenge or hydration/recovery efficacy claim |
| Food pairing | Taste alone first; then standardized neutral savoury snack/meal, same recipe/portion, allergen alternatives blocked separately | Does food make this drink more or less enjoyable? Does either flavour dominate? | Meal context can change rankings; do not merge with unpaired ratings |
| Social relaxation | Caffeine-free shortlist in small ordinary gatherings; independent individual forms before discussion; no alcohol co-consumption in test | Pace, adult-occasion fit, botanical enjoyment, willingness to finish/choose again | Social interaction changes experience; not evidence of pharmacological relaxation |
| Bedtime | Separate caffeine-free, legally cleared shortlist; intended small serving and habitual evening time | Aroma intensity, acidity, carbonation, volume comfort, desire to repeat tomorrow | Routine fit, not sleep onset/quality proof; exclude unreviewed sedatives/actives |

## Analysis and pre-specified progression rules

- Primary endpoint: within-person Pass A overall liking difference versus the selected local control. Report n, missingness, distribution, mean/median, paired mean difference and participant-bootstrap 95% interval. At n=48 these remain pilot uncertainty intervals, not national estimates. Do not count repeated sips as independent observations.
- Report preference counts including ties/neither, full-serving desire, and individual response patterns. Segment by pre-recorded current habit only when cell sizes are useful; mark tiny cells exploratory. Do not search retrospectively for a “winning” demographic.
- Diagnostics: intensity distributions; CATA term counts; JAR too-low/right/too-high percentages with denominators. A JAR “penalty” is descriptive association with liking, not a causal reformulation instruction. With small cells, report counts instead of unstable penalty estimates.
- Later serving results: individual change from first sips to end; amount left and reason; second-serving liking and consumed volume; report among everyone offered and among tasters separately. Do not drop nonfinishers to improve the average.
- **Provisional gate, to agree before data:** advance if mean blind liking is at least 6/9, no worse than control by more than 0.5 points on the estimated mean difference, and no major repeated objection. A wide interval crossing −0.5 means “uncertain/retest,” not proven equivalence. For finalists, flag a mean decline greater than 1 point from first sips to end, or over 20% stopping because of sensory dislike. These are founder decision tolerances, not validated industry thresholds.
- Require independent second-serving enjoyment and a credible switch reason, not just a small-sip winner. Where taste is similar, prefer fewer materials/process steps, robust shelf life and feasible outsourced batches. If no prototype survives, reformulate or stop; do not select the least disliked by default.

Store anonymized study IDs only in repository templates. Consent/contact/health screening records belong in a restricted study system, not public GitHub. Freeze exclusions and endpoints before data collection. These empty templates contain no respondents or results.
