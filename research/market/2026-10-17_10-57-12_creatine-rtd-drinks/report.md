# Creatine-led RTD drinks: real products, uncertain repeat economics, unresolved dose retention

**Created:** 2026-10-17T10:57:12+02:00 · **Europe/Prague (UTC+02:00)**
**Research access date and cut-off:** 17 October 2026
**Status:** public-source research; no customer, formulation or laboratory validation; local repository deliverable only.

## Answers to the research questions

**Creatine-led sodas exist, including in the EU, but the retrieved evidence does not establish a proven, broadly underserved EU category.** There is a plausible convenience proposition for adults who already use creatine. Its value depends on a retained, intelligible dose and a drink people will repeatedly buy at a premium over powder. Neither a new entrant's formulation nor its commercial economics is validated by the current evidence.

| Question | Finding | Confidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| What products exist? | Current offers include US Creia and KONSOOM, Canadian-origin Joyburst, French Humble+ and Finnish Virta. Additional energy/recovery, still and shot products are adjacent; Danish ION18 is currently sold out. | Product offers and label declarations, not proof of delivered dose or sales. Exact SKU records and exclusions: [products](data/products.csv). |
| Is there credible commercial success? | There is retail access, some continuing product history and selected company-reported sales/volume evidence. Public proof of scaled, profitable **creatine-soda-specific** revenue, repeat and velocity is weak. | Missing private data is unknown, not failure. Funding, forecasts and whole-company sales are kept separate in [commercial evidence](data/commercial-evidence.csv). |
| Are comparable products actually offered in EU markets? | Yes. France and Finland have direct sparkling products; Sweden/Finland have still and caffeinated comparators. Germany and Czechia also have prominent-creatine dairy RTDs. | Retailer/brand offers, with stock and shipping limitations. UK/Switzerland are separate. [EU availability](data/eu-availability.csv). |
| Is there an underserved EU opportunity? | **Not established.** A narrower local convenience/taste opportunity remains testable. Sparse Czech soda findings are compatible with several explanations, including technical and economic constraints. | No representative format-demand survey, willingness-to-pay distribution or paid-repeat cohort was located. |
| Is a viable product feasible? | Technically plausible, legally conditional and economically unproven. Monohydrate has a usable ingredient-history and exercise-claim route; prolonged liquid storage is the critical engineering problem. | No validated recipe, overage, process, expiry or supplier quote is supplied here. [Technical review](supporting/formulation-and-stability.md), [legal review](supporting/eu-regulation-and-claims.md). |

**Recommendation — analyst judgment:** proceed only with a tightly scoped feasibility investigation if the founder remains interested. Do not treat Creia as an established success template or commission a full commercial run on this evidence. Require exact-form legality, finished-package retained-dose evidence, credible quotes and a measurable customer switch reason before inventory commitment. The most plausible initial hypothesis is an enjoyable, caffeine-free convenience option for existing adult creatine users, with occasional use potentially complementing powder. A general mental-energy soda has weaker scientific and EU-claims support.

## Scope, evidence and repository context

Primary products are finished non-alcoholic carbonated drinks in cans/bottles that prominently market creatine. Still drinks, energy/recovery products, protein/dairy drinks and ready-to-drink shots are adjacent. Dry-cap bottles that require activation/mixing, powders, sachets, gummies and concentrates are excluded from qualifying finished liquids. Historical and discontinued products are not counted as current launches.

The prior repository reports supply useful discipline on commercial attribution, EU competition, customer hypotheses, claims, package/process fit and quote-based economics. They did not contain a verified creatine-soda evidence base. Their recommendations, model scores and proposed taste tests are not actual demand or technical validation. The [methods and evidence map](supporting/methods-and-evidence-audit.md) identifies relevant reports, source-chain corrections, concurrent `/ideas` work and remaining gaps. Repeated announcements and repeated references across agents/reports count once.

Facts below distinguish **company claims**, **retailer observations**, **independent research/legal records**, **analytical inferences** and **commercial hypotheses**. Prices are snapshots in original currencies; no FX conversion, national market average or implied affordability equivalence is used.

## Creia: promising proposition, unproven commercial and technical outcomes

The starting lead is **Creia Drinks at creiadrinks.com**, not the separate UK Creia sachet business. Its current positioning combines sparkling refreshment, creatine, electrolytes, no caffeine and a daily ritual. The range names Peach Ginger, Yuzu and Watermelon Lime; the product page says Watermelon Lime returns in November. The page's broad mental-clarity/everyday-strength language is company marketing, not demonstrated finished-drink efficacy or EU-ready copy. [Creia offer](https://creiadrinks.com/products/variety-pack).

Three distinctions materially change the assessment:

1. **Dose basis:** the current headline is **5 g CreaBev**, a preparation containing monohydrate and carriers. That is not automatically 5 g creatine. The supplier's older specification distinguishes 68% and 74% monohydrate preparations. Actual material version, dose basis and deliberate overage require confirmation; do not calculate a reliable price per active gram from the headline alone. [Supplier page](https://creatine.com/science/).
2. **Shelf-life evidence:** Creia says its plotted CreaBev curve is estimated from supplier work, with independent testing of its own cans underway. It describes pH management, shorter production runs and filling above the declaration, but does not disclose a complete finished-can assay series, numeric pH, processing history, overage or validated retention to expiry. Acknowledging those controls is useful; it is not proof they achieve the declared outcome. [Creia technical explanation](https://creiadrinks.com/products/variety-pack).
3. **Commercial proof:** a store locator, testimonials and a purchasable page establish presence and promotion. This review found no verified product revenue, units/store/week, retailer reorder series, subscription retention or profitability for Creia. That leaves success undetermined; it does not establish failure. [Global evidence review](supporting/product-discovery.md).

The inspected official artwork declares a **355 ml can, 40 kcal and 10 g sugar, including 9 g added sugar**, plus sodium 75 mg, potassium 50 mg and magnesium 25 mg. The public catalogue lists **US$39.99 per 12-pack**, or approximately **US$3.33 per can**. These are declarations and offer prices, not independent compositional tests. [Official label](https://creiadrinks.com/cdn/shop/t/10/assets/creia-nutrition-all.png?v=116422104381408979331785627586), [offer and catalogue evidence](supporting/product-discovery.md).

Its offer also requires careful reading. The page has contradictory subscription state/price text, including a statement that subscriptions are not yet available. Shipping is described as free within the contiguous US. The product register preserves observed pack/pricing details rather than interpreting every rendered control as an active subscription. EU delivery was not established. Current availability is flavour-specific, and no purchase was completed. The brand's **62 listed stockists** and Airgoods wholesale offer are commercial leads; neither establishes live shelf inventory or retailer reorders.

Creia's habit proposition is coherent with repeated intake, but its exact accumulation percentages and broad workday effects are not outcomes measured for this drink. The CreaBev human pharmacokinetic trial used freshly mixed powders and did **not** find significant superiority of either preparation over ordinary monohydrate in its detailed between-group results. It did not test stored Creia cans or chronic training benefits. [Antonio et al., 2022](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/83527/20220523-15441-1ndk31g.pdf).

The resulting assessment is **real product and clear convenience hypothesis; commercial scale unknown; retained-dose evidence incomplete**. Resolving those uncertainties could improve or worsen the assessment. Attractive positioning alone cannot settle them.

## Commercial evidence: existence is ahead of demonstrated success

The [dated commercial register](data/commercial-evidence.csv) separates product-specific figures from company totals and labels attribution. It includes positive and negative evidence; the brand-by-brand memo explains what cannot be determined.

The strongest cautionary disclosure is ION18's **17 July 2026** seller listing: an initial **4,000-can batch**, about **800 cans sold**, revenue **below DKK10,000**, three reseller agreements and about **DKK17 production cost per can** after unused syrup minimums. The context is a Danish early launch with extensive sampling; exact accounting period, sales reconciliation and independent verification are unavailable. These seller claims are evidence of some paid buying and real operational friction, not a measured scale-up model. Current sold-out pages cannot transform them into proof of booming demand. [Seller disclosure](https://www.saxis.dk/saelges/virksomhed/webshop-til-salg/39967).

FITAID provides a longer product-history comparison and some company-reported portfolio mix, but percentages without an absolute denominator and defined period do not establish creatine-line revenue or profit. Joyburst retailer access and preorder claims are useful commercial signals, yet preorders, delivered wholesale units, retail sell-through and repeat purchases are different events. Large parent-brand figures cannot fill that gap. Clean's retailer rollout is evidence of channel adoption, not measured sales velocity. [Brand-by-brand traction](supporting/product-discovery.md), [EU channel evidence](supporting/eu-market-and-consumer.md).

For Creia, KONSOOM, Humble+, Virta, VitClear and the shot comparators, the accessible evidence remains predominantly offers, reviews and brand statements. No audited, comparable creatine-soda sales/retention dataset was obtained. A review can reveal taste or convenience language but cannot establish cohort retention; retailer stock can reflect either replenishment or unsold inventory. Absence of financial disclosure is not evidence that a privately held brand is unsuccessful.

Historical negatives have different meanings. Sub Zero is a reported discontinued commercial attempt; old creatine-serum tests demonstrate a measured content problem in a **different, excluded liquid format**; Bang is a misleading-claims case; ION18 is a small business seeking a buyer. They should not be collapsed into a universal failure rate, nor omitted from a positive launch narrative.

A wider multilingual screen informs the map; it does not justify claiming absence across all 27 member states. The key direct comparison is **the price of convenience**, not grams alone. Humble+'s €3 French can declares 3 g creatine equivalent in its detailed panel: €1 per declared gram, excluding delivery. The **French** 300 g monohydrate powder listing at €5.99 costs about **€0.0681 per 3 g creatine-equivalent portion**, using 3.41 g compound per portion: `5.99/300×3.41`. The can is about **44.1 times** that powder-dose price. Humble+'s panel rounds compound mass to 3.4 g; using that rounded amount gives 44.2 times, without changing the conclusion. The can also includes a flavoured drink and convenience, while powder requires a separate beverage/preparation. The same calculation at the observed Czech CZK149.90/300 g anchor is **CZK1.70** per equivalent portion. These are snapshot arithmetic, not average market prices or guaranteed potency. [EU price sources and calculations](supporting/eu-market-and-consumer.md), [compound equivalence](supporting/formulation-and-stability.md).

Daily use at €3/can would be €90 for 30 days before shipping, versus approximately €2.04 for that powder intake at the French €5.99 anchor. This makes full replacement of powder a demanding hypothesis. An occasional gym/travel convenience purchase may be more plausible, but would produce a different consumption frequency and subscription business. Standard sports/energy drinks, water, capsules and powder plus a preferred soda are real alternatives. No assumption is made that creatine-powder category growth transfers to the soda format.

## Consumer proposition: hypotheses to test, not invented interviews

| Potential reason to buy | Supporting evidence | Counterevidence or unresolved issue |
|---|---|---|
| Avoid preparation and powder texture | Brand propositions explicitly address opening a can instead of mixing; some individual reviews echo this | Powder can be added to an existing routine; capsules avoid mixing; no representative preference or time-saving valuation |
| Better taste and enjoyable ritual | Real flavoured offers and some favourable public reviews | Reviews also show taste disagreement; no blinded comparative sensory study of these sodas was found |
| Better daily adherence | Repeated-use science makes regularity relevant; brands emphasise habit | No controlled soda-versus-powder adherence or paid-repeat study; cost and stock interruptions can reduce adherence |
| Portability/on-the-go access | A sealed single portion can be used at a gym, desk or in transit | Liquid is heavier/bulkier to carry and ship; consumers may use powder at home and skip expensive out-of-home doses |
| Training support | Stronger monohydrate exercise rationale and a specific EU claim route | Occasional use is not an acute boost; active amount through expiry and cumulative supplementation matter |
| Recovery, cognition or immediate energy | Appears in broader brand positioning and public interest | Exact claims exceed what a normal stored soda has demonstrated; caffeine can confound perceived energy; cognition claim refused in EU |

Likely occasions are hypotheses: after a workout or at the gym fridge, a planned daily home/desk drink, commuting/travel, or replacing a habitual soft drink. Consumers may use a post-workout occasion as a reminder; that does not prove that immediate post-workout timing is essential. The desired recurring purchase reason is a consistently enjoyable and trusted convenience, not a guaranteed felt effect after each can.

Research should distinguish existing powder users, people who repeatedly forget doses, supplement-curious adults and ordinary soft-drink buyers. Those groups need not have the same willingness to pay, dose knowledge or desired frequency. Concerns to examine include sugar/calories, sweeteners, caffeine, fullness/carbonation, bloating or water-weight expectations, dose stacking and whether the labelled creatine is still present. Public discussions and self-selected reviews supply language, not prevalence estimates. No interviews were invented or conducted.

## Formulation and shelf life: the principal unresolved gate

The [technical review](supporting/formulation-and-stability.md) and [study extraction](data/studies.csv) distinguish original liquid experiments, finished-product analyses, supplier studies, patents and unverified marketing.

Dissolved creatine can convert to creatinine; pH, temperature, time and matrix matter. Solubility, dispersion and chemical retention are separate. A warm solution may dissolve more compound while accelerating reaction; cooling can produce precipitation. Carbonation has not been isolated as an independent stabilising or destabilising factor in the reviewed evidence. A sealed opaque can protects against some external factors but does not stop an intrinsic aqueous reaction.

Original experiments show why transfer must be cautious. Ganguly's 2003 effervescent citrate-derived liquid showed substantial reported measured-creatine loss over 45 days and refrigerated crystals; accessible methods do not resolve the partition between conversion and precipitation, and it was not a modern sealed soda. Uzzan's 2009 pH4 model reported sharply different fitted half-lives at 4, 23 and 35°C, but it used a defined glycerol/buffer system; a half-life is not a commercial expiry. Older finished-liquid serum analysis found a severe label-content shortfall, yet that excluded drop product cannot establish the performance of current sodas. [Primary studies and conditions](supporting/formulation-and-stability.md).

The CreaBev supplier evidence is constructive but limited: the older public page reports substantially different retention in neutral and acidic systems, with incomplete protocols. It cannot substantiate a universal 12-month shelf life. Patents show proposed technical approaches; short processing holds do not validate months in distribution. Human absorption of freshly mixed powder does not verify aged product content or superior long-term efficacy.

For a new product, require a stability-indicating assay separating creatine and creatinine, with validated extraction/recovery from the actual matrix and whole container. Check pre/post-process loss, dissolved and total fractions, sediment, fill uniformity and multiple production lots. Real-time packaged studies must cover intended storage, justified excursions, each flavour and package, along with microbiological/process safety, sensory acceptance and package compatibility. Define truthful release and expiry criteria with analytical uncertainty and claim conditions included. The report specifies **no validated formula, processing method, overage or shelf life**.

## Science, safety and EU claims

Repeated creatine use can support short, high-intensity exercise performance; foundational human work measured muscle accumulation over days/weeks. An occasional soda is not equivalent to consistent supplementation, and creatine is not an acute stimulant. Emerging cognition results differ by population, dose and stress conditions; high-dose sleep-deprivation experiments do not justify a normal-dose everyday mental-clarity drink. [Human science review](supporting/science-and-safety.md).

Healthy-adult monohydrate safety evidence is reassuring within studied conditions but does not establish unrestricted multi-can consumption, every alternative form or every population. Account for other supplements, caffeine, initial overage and degradants. Research doses are not formulation recommendations. Serum creatinine can complicate renal-marker interpretation; creatinine in a beverage is neither proof of toxicity nor a reason to ignore chemical loss. A qualified product-specific exposure and impurity assessment remains necessary.

The EU's monohydrate non-novel finding and two exercise-related authorised claim routes provide a potential basis for development. Exact identity, food category and destination rules still matter. The high-intensity exercise claim requires a daily intake of **3 g creatine** and appropriate adult targeting/information; the over-55 resistance-training claim has additional training conditions. The EU formally **refused the cognitive-function claim in May 2026**. Czech supplement notification is not approval; a full-size soda is not made a supplement merely by choosing that label. [EU/Czech review](supporting/eu-regulation-and-claims.md), [2026 refusal](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32026R1118).

In particular, Virta's 3 g and Clean's 2.5 g **monohydrate** declarations are not 3 g of creatine equivalent. Their presence in shops does not demonstrate that one container meets an authorised claim's daily-dose conditions. The exact label, recommended daily portion and retained active amount require separate assessment.

## Is there an EU opportunity? Competing explanations

| Explanation for a sparse local soda market | Evidence supporting it | What would discriminate |
|---|---|---|
| Overlooked convenience need | Independent brands choose similar propositions; some buyers mention less preparation | Repeated paid choice versus powder/capsules and ordinary drinks at the actual price |
| Weak format demand | No representative format-specific demand evidence; inexpensive substitutes are easy to obtain | Actual refusal reasons, conversion and mature repeat cohorts, including nonbuyers |
| Poor economics | High retail/powder premium; ION18's small-run cost experience; liquid shipping and expiry exposure | Written landed-cost/channel terms and observed contribution after waste/refunds/acquisition |
| Technical instability | Original aqueous studies, historical failure reports and current brands' retention caveats | Independent batch-identified retained-dose results through a commercially useful shelf life |
| Regulatory constraints | Exact-form questions and narrow authorised claims; cognition refusal | A documented lawful product/claim route that still appeals to the target buyer |
| Earlier failed attempts | Sub Zero history and other distinct technical/marketing failures | Actual reasons, period and formula matched to the proposed architecture; avoid universalising |
| Incomplete research coverage | DTC/local brands surfaced only through native-language searches; no proprietary census | Repeated local retail audits and additional verified competitors; never equate no result with no supply |

**Strongest evidence for entry:** the category is real; EU consumers can already purchase relevant products; the principal sports ingredient has substantial human evidence and a narrow legal claim route; convenience/taste may add value independently of a new physiological discovery. These support investigation, not a commercial conclusion.

**Strongest evidence against committing now:** no convincing public exact-format repeat/velocity benchmark; a very cheap and familiar substitute; unresolved active-equivalent declarations and end-of-life retention; broad cognition marketing does not transfer to EU law; distribution time, minimum runs and testing can conflict with short chemical life. Existing EU products also weaken any claim to an empty regional category.

Manufacturing is possible in principle, but generic co-packer capabilities are not creatine acceptance. Actual ingredient, development, filling, analytical validation, stability, packaging, freight, trade terms and expiry costs are **quote required**. The [economics memo](supporting/economics-and-manufacturing.md) exposes illustrative formulas: under deliberately assumed channel margins and 20% VAT, a €3 shelf price yields €1.30 brand receipt and only €0.70 allowable COGS after €0.20 other variable cost and €0.40 desired contribution. None of those commercial terms is a quote or a forecast.

## Evidence that would change the decision

1. **Technical:** complete current specifications and independent final-package assays showing the claimed creatine-equivalent dose remains available through an economically usable expiry across representative lots and distribution conditions. Transparent negative results would be equally decision-relevant.
2. **Legal/safety:** documented exact-material/category permission and approved Czech/destination wording, with a credible repeated-consumption exposure assessment. A lawful claim must still fit what customers actually value.
3. **Demand:** prospective comparison with powder plus a preferred drink, capsules, ordinary soda and no purchase; then actual paid repeat once compliant stock exists. Do not set success from free tasting or claimed intention alone.
4. **Commercial:** product-specific dated revenue/units, store-week velocity, stock-adjusted reorders and mature retention, with discounts and returns visible. Company totals and founder projections are insufficient.
5. **Economics:** quotes for the actual recipe/process/pack and realistic run size, plus a positive contribution after shipping, channel deductions, expiry and acquisition. A price customers like can still fail this test.

These are proposed next evidence requirements, **not activities performed or authorised in this assignment**. No company was contacted, product purchased, participant recruited or laboratory commissioned. Private sales and formulation data remain inaccessible; current stock pages and source extraction have stated limitations. The evidence supports a conditional research option, not a predetermined launch recommendation.
