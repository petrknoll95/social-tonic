# Claims and messaging for an EU functional RTD drink

**As of 17 October 2026 · EU legal framework, Czech launch implications and US commercial references.** Completed desk research and provisional decisions; no formula, supplier, final pack or advertisement has been cleared. All consumer formats are finished ready-to-drink liquids in cans or bottles. The primary concept remains CBD-free. No launch country, category, budget or recipe is selected by this report.

The practical starting point is **taste and a recognisable occasion, supported where useful by a narrowly worded, qualifying nutrient claim**. A focus-oriented drink can potentially use pantothenic acid's authorised normal-mental-performance claim. That does not make “boost productivity”, “mental clarity in 20 minutes” or “no crash” permissible. An evening drink can communicate a social or serving occasion without promising that its ingredients change mood, relieve stress or induce sleep. These are provisional directions for low-cost validation, not findings that customers will buy either concept.

The main deliverable is the [44-row claims matrix](claims-matrix.md), also provided as [CSV](data/claims-matrix.csv). Use it with the [provisional message hierarchy](supporting/provisional-message-hierarchy.md), [final legal-review checklist](supporting/final-legal-review-checklist.md) and [sources](sources.csv).

## Findings that change development decisions

1. **Caffeine science is not caffeine claim authorisation.** The live EU register contained no authorised caffeine alertness/concentration claim in the retrieved dataset. It lists the 40–75 mg alertness application as non-authorised. Separate caffeine IDs remain on the Commission's linked pending list. The 2016 proposed 75 mg conditions were vetoed, and a positive EFSA opinion does not enact them. Any transitional use needs its own legal dossier; exclude caffeine effect wording from the initial message hierarchy. [Current register entry](https://ec.europa.eu/food/food-feed-portal/screen/health-claims/eu-register/details/POL-HC-8450), [detailed status and evidence](supporting/caffeine-and-nutrients.md).
2. **Normal function is a narrower promise than an improvement.** B5 normal mental performance, zinc/iron/iodine normal cognitive function, selected nutrients' energy-yielding metabolism and fatigue/psychological-function claims are distinct authorised relationships. They are not synonyms for extra productivity, acute calm, all-day energy or sleep. The drink must qualify for the named nutrient and preserve its meaning and attribution. [Regulation 432/2012, current consolidation surfaced 20 August 2025](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/432/2025-08-20).
3. **On-hold is not a general botanical permission.** The 30 April 2025 CJEU judgment C-386/23 requires the correct transitional analysis for psychological/behavioural and general wellbeing claims. Historical conditions matter. Older Czech guidance must be read subject to the judgment. No transition dossier was established here for a proposed botanical can. [Full judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A62023CJ0386), [worked botanical records](supporting/botanicals-and-czech-enforcement.md).
4. **Czech melatonin classification is a concrete obstacle.** The EU sleep-onset claim needs 1 mg per quantified portion and the close-to-bedtime instruction. SÚKL's published decision practice, dated 14 March 2023 and rechecked, classifies oral products at **≥0.5 mg/day** as medicines by function. An ordinary Czech beverage route cannot be inferred from the EU claim. Below that dose does not supply an alternative sleep claim or automatically clear a food. [SÚKL notice](https://sukl.gov.cz/dulezite-informace/zmena-rozhodovaci-praxe-v-pripade-vyrobku-obsahujici-latku-melatonin/).
5. **CBD does not enlarge the claims available to the core.** No applicable CBD beverage-use authorisation or CBD focus/calm/sleep claim was established. EFSA's September 2026 positive opinion concerns a specified MCT-oil supplement, not an aqueous RTD or efficacy. Keep any CBD extension behind separate demand, ingredient, safety and claims gates. [EFSA opinion, 18 September 2026, amended 21 September](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10154), [separate CBD assessment](supporting/cbd-sleep-and-secondary.md).

## What the different types of wording require

| Type | Example in a proposed can/bottle context | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Authorised specific health claim | B5 contributes to normal mental performance | Use the authorised meaning, qualifying quantity/form, conditions and required supporting information. Permission is conditional on this finished drink. |
| Nutrition claim | Low sugars; source of magnesium; high fibre | Must fit an Annex claim and its conditions. A nutritional property does not authorise any associated health promise. |
| General health-related wellbeing | Feel good for mind and body | Article 10(3) requires a related specific authorised health claim and adequate linkage; relevant transitional exceptions need separate analysis. “Wellbeing” is not a free category outside claims law. |
| Sensory or occasion information | Citrus and ginger; serve chilled; for evenings with friends | Usually lower risk if accurate, but context can imply an effect. Evaluate the name, active-ingredient story, pictures and testimonials together. |
| Name, image or adopted testimonial | FOCUS with a brain graphic; customer says it relieved anxiety | Can communicate a claim without a complete explicit sentence. A trade mark is not an exemption; selected commercial testimonials are not a workaround. |
| Medicinal/disease presentation | Treats insomnia, anxiety or ADHD | Ordinary food information cannot claim prevention, treatment or cure of disease. Authorised disease-risk-factor claims are a different legal category, not a route established for these proposed promises. Product classification also matters. |

The framework applies to commercial labels, presentations and advertising, including e-shops, social media and retailer materials. Regulation 1924/2006 Articles 1–6, 8–10 and 12 govern claim scope, substantiation, permitted nutrition claims, names and accompanying information; Regulation 1169/2011 Article 7 covers misleading information and disease-treatment presentation. Neutral facts can become claims when the surrounding message suggests a beneficial health relationship. [Claims regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02006R1924-20141213), [food-information regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2011/1169/oj/eng).

A general claim and its supporting specific claim need a substantive connection and a perceptible visual relationship. C-524/18 does not provide a routine licence to place a large front-of-can promise and unrelated fine print on the back. An asterisk is an exceptional layout solution under the judgment's conditions, not a cure for an unsupported promise. C-544/10 also shows why a claimed absence of adverse effects may still be a health claim; its alcohol-specific outcome is not being applied to these nonalcoholic drinks. [C-524/18, 30 January 2020](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=celex%3A62018CJ0524), [C-544/10, 6 September 2012](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/SUM/?uri=CELEX%3A62010CJ0544).

## Keep the desired benefits separate

| Desired endpoint | Available legal/evidence distinction | Unresolved for the founder |
|---|---|---|
| Productivity | Real-world useful output. No applicable productivity claim identified; not equivalent to normal mental performance. | Whether an occasion-led RTD has paid repeat and any meaningful work-output effect. |
| Alertness | Wakefulness; caffeine has a stronger acute ingredient rationale than many blends, but the claim authorisation/transition question is separate. | Exact lawful wording, final dose, tolerability, total exposure and time of use. |
| Concentration/attention | Attention tasks differ from broad work performance; caffeine-plus-theanine findings cannot automatically be attributed to theanine alone. | Exact blend, dose, endpoint and finished-drink equivalence. |
| Cognitive function / mental performance | Narrow authorised normal-function relationships exist for specified nutrients. | Noticeable additional benefit in adequately nourished adults is not established by legal eligibility. |
| Energy-yielding metabolism | A normal biochemical role, not the calorific value or a feeling of stimulation. | Consumer interpretation of “energy” on the actual creative. |
| Perceived energy / no crash | Subjective intensity and time course; no-crash also implies avoiding an adverse experience. | No exact-product evidence or applicable claim route established. Sugar-free does not answer either question. |
| Relaxation / stress / mood / sociability | Different psychological outcomes; normal psychological function is not reduction of stress, improvement of mood or inhibition. | Botanical transition, safety and acute drink effects; willingness to pay for occasion versus sensation. |
| Sleep | Onset, quality, duration and next-day function differ. Melatonin's authorised onset wording does not cover all of them. | National classification, lawful formulation and actual benefit; Czech ordinary-food barrier above. |

Scientific efficacy, legal eligibility, safety and demand are **four separate assessments**. The earlier [scientific evidence memo](../../market/01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md) remains useful context, including its small caffeine/theanine studies, chronic botanical trials and mixed lion's-mane findings. This topic is a targeted claims investigation, not a new systematic review. Capsules or laboratory drinks can contribute ingredient evidence, but transfer needs matching identity, dose, route, population, duration and endpoint, plus stability/bioavailability in the processed and stored can. A US brand's clinical language does not supply those links. For CBD specifically, the 2024 insomnia pilot used 150 mg sublingually for two weeks and does not establish a reliable effect from a low-dose aqueous social drink. [Primary CBD trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11063694/).

## Ingredient quantity, portion and processing can change eligibility

The legal floor is not a manufacturing target. Count the claimed nutrient in the **finished, ready-to-consume drink**, in its relevant available form, after processing and through shelf life. Elemental magnesium is not the mass of a magnesium salt. A supplier's raw-premix certificate does not establish the final can's content. Added nutrient forms need ordinary-food permission under Regulation 1925/2006 and any novel-food restrictions; supplement permission is not automatically soft-drink permission. [Detailed source/threshold rules](supporting/caffeine-and-nutrients.md).

For vitamins/minerals, the significant-amount rule is generally **7.5% NRV per 100 ml for beverages, or 15% NRV per portion if the package contains only a single portion**. A multi-serving bottle cannot be relabelled by arithmetic alone as a one-portion package. B5's 6 mg NRV gives 0.45 mg/100 ml; that is 1.125 mg in 250 ml or 1.485 mg in 330 ml. The genuine single-portion alternative is 0.9 mg per package. Magnesium's corresponding values are 28.125 mg/100 ml and 56.25 mg per single-portion package. “High” doubles the source threshold. These are eligibility calculations, not recommended intake or safety-cleared doses. [Threshold dataset and method](data/README.md).

Caffeine labelling uses concentration, not a reassuringly small per-can number. A hypothetical 60 mg/250 ml is **240 mg/L** and 24 mg/100 ml, above the Annex III threshold of more than 150 mg/L. The coffee/tea exception requires the specified base and coffee/tea in the name; tea-derived caffeine added to a citrus soda is not automatically exempt. EFSA's healthy-adult safety reference levels of 200 mg/single dose and 400 mg/day from all sources are not claim permissions, formulation targets or individual guarantees; 100 mg near bedtime can affect sleep in some adults. [EFSA caffeine topic](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine), [caffeine calculations and conditions](supporting/caffeine-and-nutrients.md).

Nutrition wording also needs its own calculation. Low sugars means no more than **2.5 g/100 ml** for liquids; sugars-free no more than **0.5 g/100 ml**. No added sugars depends on ingredients and whether foods are used for sweetening, not merely a low measured total. Reduced sugars generally needs at least **30% reduction**, no higher energy and the prescribed representative same-category comparison. Fibre uses **g/100 g or g/100 kcal**, not an invented blanket per-can minimum. A hypothetical 3 g fibre/30 kcal can gives 10 g/100 kcal, numerically meeting the high-fibre energy route, but that is not a gut-health claim or proof of a noticeable effect. [Commission nutrition conditions](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/nutrition-claims_en).

## Czech nuances and unresolved transitions

Article 28(5) concerns Article 13(1)(a) body-function claims. Article 28(6) addresses other qualifying historical claims, including psychological/behavioural claims, with lawful pre-19 January 2007 use and the applicable national evaluation/notification or timely application history. Under 28(6)(b), the application deadline is **before 19 January 2008**. A founder need not personally have traded then, but must establish the claim's eligibility and the right to rely on it. An EFSA receipt date is not necessarily the original filing date. No new 2026 filing can simply recreate a missing historical condition. [C-386/23 and Article 28 analysis](supporting/botanicals-and-czech-enforcement.md).

The review matched pending botanical records for lemon balm, ashwagandha, valerian and ginseng against official files. They are research leads, not usable copy. The current-accessed SZPI main guidance was last updated **3 January 2024**, before the judgment. No newer Czech official post-judgment clarification was found in this targeted search. Treat that as an unresolved legal-review item, not enforcement permission. [SZPI guidance](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/clanek/voditka-k-problematice-zdravotnich-a-vyzivovych-tvrzeni).

Two additional distinctions matter. Czech 2017 nonbinding recommendations describe a limited “contains probiotics/prebiotics” nutrition-claim approach, but this is not EU-wide authorisation or a current safe harbour; the Commission's implied-health-claim approach and the 2024 Ombudsman decision must be considered. Czech “natural/přírodní” restrictions also have exceptions for demonstrably justified, non-misleading descriptive information and legally defined naming: neither blanket prohibition nor unrestricted permission is correct. Prefer specific supported ingredient/process information. “Natural lemon flavouring” is a different regulated description: Article 16 of Regulation 1334/2008 requires the flavouring component to come exclusively or at least 95% by weight from the named source, with other natural-flavouring descriptions having different conditions. This does not make the entire drink “all natural”. [Flavouring regulation, Article 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32008R1334). [National recommendations](https://www.mze.gov.cz/public/web/file/546391/Narodni_doporuceni_pristupu_k_nekterym_oznacenim_potravin_zejmena_ve_vztahu_k_narizeni__ES__c._19242006.pdf), [Ombudsman, 20 December 2024](https://www.ombudsman.europa.eu/cs/decision/cs/197581), [Decree 417/2016 §2](https://mze.gov.cz/public/portal/mze/legislativa/vap87546-131).

“Social tonic” must be evaluated in context. The specific tonic-descriptor derogation covers designated words for nonalcoholic carbonated drinks containing specified quinine flavourings. It derogates from Article 1(3), not from the entire claims regime, and cannot be borrowed for an arbitrary botanical drink or promises of social confidence. [Regulation 2019/343](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32019R0343).

## Secondary opportunities with distinct conditions

Hydration, recovery and gut propositions are viable research categories, but each adds constraints. The water normal-cognition/physical-function claim is restricted to qualifying water; a mostly-water soda does not automatically qualify. Carbohydrate-electrolyte absorption and endurance claims require defined carbohydrate energy, sodium and osmolality and the exercise context. Protein maintenance is not instant recovery. Carbohydrate recovery after glycogen-depleting exercise requires **4 g/kg total carbohydrate from all sources** within the specified recovery period; it is not a one-can or post-workday claim. Native chicory inulin bowel-function wording needs **12 g/day** of the specified material, not any fibre blend. Live-culture lactose-digestion wording applies to the specified yoghurt/fermented-milk cultures and target group, not arbitrary kombucha or probiotics. Exact conditions are in rows M29–M38 and the [secondary evidence memo](supporting/cbd-sleep-and-secondary.md).

These conditions may make an ordinary taste-led drink with a measurable nutritional property cheaper to validate than a highly differentiated physiological promise. That is a development-cost hypothesis; no production quotation, stability test or consumer test was obtained here.

## What the US benchmarks do and do not establish

The [nine exact-format examples](supporting/us-rtd-messaging-benchmarks.md) span focus tea, productivity positioning, caffeine-free stimulation, social participation, magnesium mood, everyday calm, bedtime, fibre soda and hydration. They show how names, occasions, imagery and testimonials combine into a benefit proposition. New launches and announced shelf access support commercial interest in these categories. They do not establish that these propositions are effective, that shoppers repeat, or that the wording transfers to Europe.

Hiyo is one reference among them. VYBES' CBD-to-magnesium pivot shows the brand retained similar mood/relaxation positioning while changing ingredient strategy. Retained customer demand and paid repeat remain unverified; the pivot does not prove consumers rejected CBD. Neutonic's 2026 line-level rollout is kept separate from the exact older Orange Sunrise page and its different formula. No PepsiCo revenue, separate THC-business sales, excluded-format brand revenue or unsourced SKU sales are used as proof of liquid-line success. For commercial evidence strength, cross-check [topic 02](../../market/02-commercial-traction/report.md), [EU/Czech competition](../../market/04-eu-czech-competition/report.md) and [recent signals](../../market/26-trends-and-industry-intelligence/report.md).

## Separate CBD extension decision

**Demand:** Czech CBD-only RTD incremental demand and willingness to pay remain unknown. General relaxation interest, THC beverage growth and CBD supplement sales cannot fill that gap. **Effects:** the evidence reviewed does not establish low-dose-can focus, calm or sleep promises. **Sourcing:** no qualified supplier, batch certificate or quotation exists in this work. An isolate description or “THC-free” label is not permission or a contaminant/stability specification.

**Safety:** EFSA's February 2026 provisional 0.0275 mg/kg/day figure equals 1.925 mg for 70 kg and applies to qualifying ≥98% CBD supplements under specified restrictions. It is not a beverage safe dose. The September opinion is specific to synthetic CBD in MCT-oil supplements, with a 2 mg/day maximum for adults over 25 excluding pregnancy/lactation and unresolved medicine-user safety. **Legality:** targeted checks of current authorisation resources did not establish a route for the intended CBD drink; Czech enforcement records document action against unauthorised CBD foods. An opinion or application is not Commission market authorisation. [EFSA February update](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/news/provisional-safe-level-cannabidiol-novel-food), [Union-list resource](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/authorisations/union-list-novel-foods_en), [SZPI, 29 May 2025](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/soubor/27-2025-redigovano.aspx).

**A separate court-law point:** C-663/18 (19 November 2020, commonly called Kanavape) concerned CBD used in electronic cigarettes and free movement. The Court did not regard the CBD at issue as a narcotic, while allowing proportionate public-health justification for restrictions. That is not a novel-food authorisation or a food-health-claim approval; its older safety discussion must not replace the 2026 EFSA assessments. This is a legal-case reference, not an excluded-format product benchmark. [Court press summary](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2020-11/cp200141en.pdf).

**Messaging:** a future lawfully marketable CBD variant could in principle carry the same independently qualifying magnesium/B5 claim, attributed to that nutrient. It could not imply CBD causes it, improves it or supplies stronger calm. Factual CBD identity/amount would still need whole-presentation review. The core should stand on taste, occasion and any eligible nutrient relationship; the optional variant is an additional research question, not the brand's foundation. A weaker lawful differentiation may undermine the commercial reason to carry the extra complexity. This is an analyst inference, not a measured consumer result.

## Next actions and limits

1. Select **two alternative concept cards**, not a launch formula: daytime work break versus evening social/serving occasion. Use only qualifying can/bottle formats and the provisional hierarchy; retain a caffeine-free option without assuming it delivers focus or calm.
2. Have a Czech food-law reviewer classify the exact names, layout and copy and document any claimed transition. Check ingredient identity/category before any consumable pilot. Resolve caffeine claims, botanicals, melatonin, natural terminology and probiotic wording only where actually proposed.
3. Conduct low-cost private adult interviews and priced concept choices using [topic 05's protocols](../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/interview-guide.md). Ask what benefit, speed and strength participants infer. Do not treat private research as an automatic exemption from commercial claims rules or imply an unavailable CBD product is for sale.
4. Once the concept and legal route survive, brief outsourced formulators/packers for a simple existing beverage platform. Request evidence of permitted forms, batch and shelf-life specifications, analytical controls and complete costs. These are future requests; no outreach or purchase was made.
5. Verify sensory acceptance and paid repeat separately from physiology. An authorised nutrient statement does not require inventing a finished-product clinical result; a new stronger claim cannot be made merely because a founder-funded test is positive. Any new claim requires its own applicable legal route.
