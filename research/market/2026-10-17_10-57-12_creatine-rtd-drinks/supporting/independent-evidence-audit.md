# Independent challenge of the integrated findings

**Audit date:** 17 October 2026, Europe/Prague (UTC+02:00). This is a focused second-pass evidence challenge of [the integrated report](../report.md), [the EU/Czech review](eu-regulation-and-claims.md) and [the global product memo](product-discovery.md). It is not an external legal opinion, systematic review or laboratory audit. The reviewing agent authored the technical/science workstream, so the independent component is chiefly the challenge of the other workstreams and their integration; technical conclusions were checked against original documents rather than treated as independent replication.

## Decisive source checks

**ION18, P017 — original seller listing independently reopened.** The Danish [Saxis listing](https://www.saxis.dk/saelges/virksomhed/webshop-til-salg/39967) gives the 17 July 2026 creation/update date, first 4,000-can run, approximately 800 cans sold, revenue just below DKK10,000, three reseller agreements and approximately DKK17 unit cost after a syrup-minimum problem. The report correctly treats these as unaudited seller statements, with no reconciled period or transferable cost quote. Positive feedback, solved stability and regional whitespace statements on the same page lack corresponding independent evidence. A sale listing establishes an offered business, not a completed transaction or proof that the concept failed.

**FITAID, P034 — original release independently reopened.** The [30 April 2024 LIFEAID release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fitaid-breaks-records-with-innovative-reformulation-of-fitaid-rx-drink-302132191.html) does contain the reported 23% portfolio-sales and nearly 30% Recovery-volume statements. The report correctly identifies company attribution and missing denominator, time window and geography. These percentages do not supply absolute creatine-line revenue, margin or velocity. Its technical stability language is promotional, with no packaged retention series attached. The 2024 event is a reformulation of an older line, not the original 2017 launch.

**Sub Zero / Joyburst, P036 — source-chain challenge.** The [22 June 2026 trade article](https://www.beveragedaily.com/Article/2026/06/22/creatine-drinks-gain-ground-as-stability-hurdles-fall/) supports a reported historical Sub Zero withdrawal and separately attributes the prelaunch Joyburst volume assertion to the brand/founder. It does not supply invoices, consumer sell-through, an original Sub Zero assay or a named Bio-Synergy source. Its confident suggestion that formulation rather than lack of demand explains sparse supply exceeds the evidence retrieved in this project. The integrated report appropriately tests weak demand, economics, technical issues, regulation and incomplete coverage as alternatives rather than adopting that explanation.

**Current EU cognitive-claim refusal, L03/T027 — official text independently retrieved.** [Regulation 2026/1118](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32026R1118) confirms the refusal following EFSA's 2024 opinion; the legal record gives entry into force on 16 June 2026. The report correctly distinguishes this from a merely pending claim or a general creatine-ingredient ban. The older-adult training conditions were also checked against official Regulation 2017/672 text. Re-access to the specific monohydrate non-novel PDF failed in this audit interface; the lead's original verification remains the basis for that passage, and this audit does not claim an additional successful check.

## Technical conclusions challenged against originals

The human CreaBev comparison (T010) used equal nominal monohydrate amounts in freshly mixed powders. Its full-text pairwise results do not show superiority over ordinary monohydrate and do not test a reduced active dose. Consequently neither that paper nor the brand's interpretation proves lower-dose equivalence or aged-can efficacy. This was checked in the technical workstream; the lead also independently read the original PDF.

The historical Creatine Serum assay (T001) is relevant negative evidence but concerns excluded liquid drops. Its three analytical replicates must not be presented as three independently purchased batches or a modern soda test. Uzzan's high-water-activity half-lives (T003) concern a defined buffer/glycerol model; their temperature dependence supports development risk, not a universal expiration date. Ganguly's different system and cold precipitation are not contradictory proof that refrigeration always worsens chemistry. Different matrix and measurement questions must stay distinct.

A 3 g monohydrate declaration does not equal 3 g creatine equivalent, and the authorised daily claim condition is not itself a safety maximum. Conversely, absence of retrieved proof for a particular alternative form is not proof of illegality. The legal memo retains that distinction. No reviewed claim makes a competitor's US availability or online listing sufficient for EU/Czech compliance.

## Quantitative and classification checks

The initial powder arithmetic was correct but used a German/Dutch powder price against a French can. A second reviewer identified the geography mismatch. The final report uses **France for both offers**: `5.99 / 300 × 3.41 = €0.06809` per 3 g creatine-equivalent portion; `3 / 0.06809 = 44.1` times the price; 30 powder portions cost about €2.04. The Czech arithmetic `149.90 / 300 × 3.41 = CZK1.7039` is also correct. These remain price snapshots and compound-equivalence calculations, not assays, representative market prices or equivalent beverage experiences.

Dry-cap systems require activation/mixing and are reasonably excluded under the specified finished-liquid scope. Dairy shakes, still waters, energy/recovery drinks and liquid shots remain adjacent, not evidence of identical soda demand. Bang's historic marketed ingredient controversy is not a verified creatine-delivery success story. No population-wide or country-wide absence claim follows from a search failing to locate a core soda.

## Residual limits and overall challenge result

### Second reviewer: chemistry and science challenge

The EU researcher independently checked original material outside that researcher's own workstream: Antonio's full pharmacokinetic Table 3, Dash's author-hosted original analytical paper, Uzzan's half-life arithmetic and the 2026 Wingate abstract. These checks supported the bounded numerical conclusions. The reviewer identified two precision corrections, now incorporated: the 2025 lean-mass study was described as single blind but had no placebo or participant blinding; the 2023 cognition crossover had 123 analysed participants from 148 randomised. Neither supports an immediate normal-dose soda benefit.

Exact-SKU follow-up also corrected Ehrmann's current discounted offer, resolved Pistacia-specific ingredients/nutrition and preserved source conflicts in Sav:D's calories, sugar, dose and serving directions. The lead incorporated Sub Zero's attribution correction, Ganguly's sampling uncertainty and the distinction between a monohydrate dose and an authorised daily creatine-equivalent claim quantity. These are corrections to desk research, not independent finished-product testing.

The cautious central conclusion survives the checks: products and EU offers exist, but publicly verified repeat/velocity and retained-dose evidence are insufficient to establish a broad underserved category or a ready-to-copy commercial formula. This conclusion is compatible with private brands succeeding; unavailable financial information is not negative sales evidence.

The audit did not independently revisit every retailer, image, launch date, legal provision or source row. It did not test products, contact companies, purchase data or validate a recipe. Before committing, the lead should still check final CSV joins, local links, source IDs, units, reviewed-version changes and the two corrections above. No new numeric market-size estimate, efficacy score or shelf-life promise is justified by this audit.
