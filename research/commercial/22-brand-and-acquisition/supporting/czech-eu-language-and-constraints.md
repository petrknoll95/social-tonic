## What Czech audiences understand: evidence versus hypotheses

**No representative 2025–2026 Czech or cross-EU study establishing comprehension of the eight requested terms was found.** A 2020 Czech questionnaire study of 1,049 adults reports awareness and use of dietary supplements and functional foods, but these are broad combined categories; the accessible abstract does not establish current comprehension of functional RTD labels, unaided definitions, sampling representativeness or English fluency. It is historical context, not a percentage to use in a launch forecast. [CE06: primary research abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/abs/pii/S0007070X20000506).

There is observable Czech exposure to this vocabulary in qualifying liquids. CANS+ Performance and Balance are 330 ml cans; their Czech pages combine an English variant name with functional-water language, ingredient detail and daily occasions. Magnesia Plus uses English Focus alongside a Czech concentration explanation and named nutrients; the existing exact Czech retail SKU is a 700 ml bottle. Vilgain Energy & Focus lemon/lime is a 330 ml can. Rossmann's exact Brite Lemon Lime 330 ml offer uses nootropic and mental-performance language; the can format is corroborated in the existing competition map. All are **marketing observations**, not findings that the public understands, believes or buys the promise. These pages also do not establish lawful copy or effects. [CE01](https://cz.cans.com/products/cans-performance), [CE02](https://cz.cans.com/products/cans-balance), [CE03](https://www.magnesia.cz/magnesia-plus), [CE04](https://aktin.cz/vilgain-energy-focus-drink-2/12x44884), [CE05](https://www.rossmann.cz/prirodni-energy-napoj) (undated, accessed 2026-10-17, Czech offers).

| Term | Provisional interpretation to test, not a population finding | Best first research wording / diagnostic |

| Energy / energie | May mean calories, caffeine, feeling awake or exercise fuel. These are distinct. | Test factual “s kofeinem” plus mg/container against energy terminology; ask whether a sugar-free drink can still be understood as “energy”. |

Do not generalize a Czech result to the EU. Repeat the short protocol with native wording in each candidate country; translation equivalence is a hypothesis. ## Claims boundaries that change the brand brief

The EU food-claims regime applies to labels, presentation and commercial advertising, including names or graphics that imply a benefit. General health language needs an appropriate specific claim; trade names are not a loophole. Ingredient education alongside a sales offer can still constitute commercial claims. Keep the evidence library separate in purpose and clearly describe population, dose and delivery form; separation into a web tab does not exempt a claim from law. [CE07: Regulation 1924/2006](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32006R1924), [CE08: implementation guidance](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2013/63/oj/eng/pdf).

The 2025 CJEU judgment concerned botanical food-supplement advertising and held that the psychological/behavioural claims at issue, and general botanical health references without the required specific claim, cannot be used while evaluation remains suspended unless Article 28(6) permits them. Its relevance to proposed beverage copy must be assessed under the same food-claims framework; do not describe every botanical claim as automatically banned, or every on-hold claim as permitted. Require the actual ingredient, wording and documented transitional basis. [CE10](https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/redirect/juris/documents.jsf?critereEcli=ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2025%3A304) (EU, 2025-04-30).

Authorized nutrient claims offer narrower communication routes: pantothenic acid and normal mental performance, zinc and normal cognitive function, and magnesium/B vitamins with specified normal functions or fatigue claims, subject to exact source conditions. These do not mean an acute productivity gain, instant calm or an enhanced normal brain. Melatonin has a specific time-to-fall-asleep claim with 1 mg per quantified portion and near-bedtime information; the claim does **not** settle whether a melatonin beverage is permitted/classified appropriately in Czechia or another destination. No sleep formula is recommended here. [CE11: Regulation 432/2012, consolidated 2025-08-20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?qid=1787879176706&uri=CELEX%3A02012R0432-20250820).

Caffeine has a credible ingredient rationale for alertness, and can disturb sleep; EFSA's scientific/safety assessment is not itself advertising authorization or a formulation target. No public ad here should launch with a caffeine-concentration claim merely because a competitor prints one. Check the current claim status and any applicable transition. [CE13: EFSA caffeine](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine). High-caffeine beverages above 150 mg/L generally trigger the Annex III warning and mg/100 ml disclosure; the regulation has a defined coffee/tea-name exception. Accurate per-can caffeine information can aid choice but cannot replace mandatory wording. [CE12: Regulation 1169/2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN-DE-FR/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32011R1169).

For visual hierarchy, prioritize drink identity, flavour and occasion, then confirmed caffeine status and amount, then conditional ingredient detail. Keep relevant authorized claim/conditions visibly connected where required. A brain icon, laboratory costume or transformation video can imply effects even without the word “focus”. Avoid suggesting endorsement by an individual health professional; the claims regulation restricts those health-claim recommendations. These are creative-risk assessments, not approval of every phrase in the table. [CE07–CE09].

| Question | Evidence and decision consequence |
|---|---|

No credible Czech CBD-only RTD demand estimate was established. Privately compare identical concepts with/without factual CBD wording, after collecting unaided expectations; separate curiosity and intoxication confusion from purchase intent. The workstream could not fully re-access PubMed; the independent reviewer subsequently retrieved the indexed primary PMC article and confirmed the design/results. Safety was not established for under-25s, pregnant/lactating people or medicine users. This is not a drink dose or efficacy threshold. Obtain the exact lawful ingredient/use authorization before formulation investment or supply. | Sourcing | No supplier quote or audit was obtained. Require identity matching permitted use; traceability; independent cannabinoid/THC assays and detection limits; contaminants/residual solvents; emulsion/dose uniformity; packaging interaction and shelf-life stability in the finished drink. “Nano” or “water soluble” marketing is not an authorization or proof of the same exposure as an oil study. |
| Claims and acquisition | No applicable authorized CBD focus/calm/sleep claim was established; ingredient permission and claims are separate. Google/TikTok beverage restrictions and Meta's documented certification limits make standard paid acquisition unavailable as a base-case assumption. Decision: keep core brand, customer research and CBD-free concept testing independent. CBD remains a separately gated research extension. Do not buy CBD inventory or design a CBD campaign until ingredient/use legality, formulation safety, usable claims, platform eligibility and incremental customer preference are documented. No THC-led or intoxicating proposition is recommended.

## What to learn before finished inventory exists

**Integration note:** the standalone CZK7,200/24-person and 330 ml examples below are workstream alternatives, not additional approved tests or part of the consolidated budget. The lead selected the [integrated T01–T03 protocol](acquisition-test-protocol.md), with distinct panels, CZK200 interview incentives and a neutral 250 ml concept assumption. Its decision rules and budget supersede the alternatives below; neither pack nor incentive is a market finding.

Run **private, randomized term/creative interviews** before a public landing-page test. Proposed small research sample: 24 Czech adults recruited by recent behaviour, with contrast users who avoid caffeine or are satisfied with their current option. This is an exploratory sample, not population estimation. Proposed incentive assumption: 24 × CZK300 = CZK7,200, excluding recruitment and founder time; not a market quote.

Show each respondent a neutral can/bottle mock-up for five seconds, then remove it. Ask unaided: what is it; when would you choose it; taste; expected caffeine/alcohol/CBD; expected benefit, onset and certainty; who is it unsuitable for? Randomize term order and keep formulation/price constant when testing wording. Reveal factual ingredients only after the first answer. Code understanding separately from trust, appeal, stated willingness to pay and belief in an effect. Test alternative packaging hierarchies with flavour/type first versus functional term first. Research-only effect concepts must be described as propositions under evaluation, not promises.

**Provisional decision rules**, chosen for learning and not industry benchmarks: retain a territory for public lawful-copy testing only if at least 18/24 identify the intended drink/occasion correctly, at most 2/24 infer intoxication/medicine/guaranteed cognitive improvement, and at least 12/24 can name a recent occasion it could replace. Review qualitative contradictions even if counts pass. A high desire for the benefit with poor understanding triggers wording revision, not a demand conclusion. These thresholds do not establish efficacy, legal compliance or profitable demand.

Without inventory one can compare category comprehension, visual recognition, occasion fit, qualified opt-in and price-dependent stated intent. One cannot establish taste acceptance, functional effects, sell-through, paid repeat, CAC, LTV or retail velocity. Public mock-up pages must say the product is in development; avoid taking money or presenting a nonexistent product as available. In the next stage, lawful finished sample inventory and actual paid reorders must test whether the promise survives taste, price and routine use.
