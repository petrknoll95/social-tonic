# Independent review of the tasting protocol

**Reviewer:** US benchmark workstream, independently reviewing the lead-authored protocol and scorecard. **Date:** 17 October 2026. **Status:** suitable as a proposed pilot, with the pre-execution refinements below; no tasting or empirical validation performed.

Reviewed [tasting protocol](tasting-protocol.md) and [bilingual scorecard](tasting-scorecard.md). This review concerns sensory design, exposure accounting and interpretation, not clinical approval of any formulation. It also crosschecks the [US benchmark dataset](../data/us-sensory-benchmarks.csv).

## Substantive findings

| Finding | Why it matters | Recommended resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Control selection is still conditional | Stage 1 names a local focus RTD and a still tea, but a generic category is not an executable control. Halfday is an eligible contextual still tea; its 45 mg caffeine is retailer-declared and current fiber descriptions disagree. | Before recruitment, freeze exact local SKU/market/lot and Halfday Peach Tea 12 oz can after physical label verification. If the version cannot be reconciled, replace the sample or hold ingestion. Do not call Pure Leaf Mental Focus still; it is sparkling. |
| Second-serving sequence should be specified | Stage 3 counterbalances the first three product days. Without comparable scheduling for the two additional finalist days, a recurring weekday, first/last exposure or food context could be confounded with product. | Randomize or counterbalance the two finalist second-serving days, retain comparable timing/context, and record order and deviations. Analyze serving-1-to-serving-2 changes within participant, keeping period/order visible. |
| Repeating finalists only limits the control comparison | The protocol provides a second serving for finalists; it does not explicitly repeat the local control. That is acceptable for absolute acceptability, but cannot establish that a finalist retains enjoyment better than the control. | Either repeat the control on a matched day or explicitly restrict the second-serving conclusion to the finalists' observed trajectories. Do not infer paid repeat from receiving a free second can. |

These are refinements for execution and interpretation. They do not require delaying publication of a clearly labelled research plan. All exact doses, label restrictions, batch identity, participant suitability and legal use remain mandatory before any proposed ingestion.

## Checks that passed

- **Liking before diagnostics:** Pass A collects overall liking before intensity/JAR/CATA measures; responses lock before the diagnostic pass. The scorecard does not lead participants with benefit promises. A separate labelled exercise prevents claims/price from contaminating blind liking.
- **Balanced first-pass design:** the four sequences `A B D C`, `B C A D`, `C D B A`, `D A C B` place each product in each position once and contain all 12 possible directed adjacent product pairs once. Assigning 12 participants to each sequence yields 48 total participants. This balances immediate pair carryover; it does not eliminate persistent sensory or pharmacological effects.
- **Exposure arithmetic:** `4 × 30 ml × 2 = 240 ml` total planned liquid across both passes is correct. `120 mg × 30/355 = 10.1408 mg` per pour is correctly rounded to 10.14 mg. Two such pours contribute 20.2817 mg, before other samples/background intake. Replacement pours must be included as the protocol already states. Ingredient-specific limits cannot be replaced by a caffeine calculation.
- **Serving conditions:** controlled temperature, opening/pour window, neutral cups and water, independent responses, delayed recognition questions, recorded deviations and fresh samples are coherent. Not all taste identities can be hidden; this is acknowledged.
- **Causal boundaries:** the plan expressly avoids treating multi-attribute commercial-product comparisons as causal tests of carbonation or sweetener identity. Halfday versus Pure Leaf differs in tea type, fiber, sugar, sweeteners and caffeine as well as carbonation. Matched-base experiments are the correct next stage for component questions.
- **Whole-serving and repeat experience:** participants may stop; residual volume and reason are recorded; density assumptions are avoided; second serving is normally on another day; label limits override desire for a two-can test. Unknown actives and unsuitable products are held. This avoids turning preference testing into escalating exposure.
- **Occasion separation:** workday, exercise, food pairing, social and bedtime modules have different questions and fit measures. No perceptible “kick,” relaxation effect or sleep improvement is used as the sensory endpoint. Caffeinated products do not enter bedtime tests.
- **Sample size and inference:** 48 and 24–30 are explicitly proposed directional pilots, not power calculations or representative Czech estimates. Participant-level paired analysis, uncertainty intervals, missingness and all offered/nonfinishing participants are retained. Proposed advancement tolerances are labelled business rules rather than validated universal thresholds; crossing the −0.5 interval boundary is uncertain, not equivalence.

## Optional editorial clarification

The protocol's phrase “forced choice with tie/neither options” should simply read “choice with tie/neither options.” The instrument itself correctly includes tie, none and uncertain. This wording does not change the statistical design.

The controls' availability, every active dose and participant eligibility require a fresh check at fieldwork; an October 2026 desk review is not a later batch release. No consumer preference, winning flavour or efficacy result can be concluded from this review.
