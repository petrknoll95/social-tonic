# Six flavour directions to test

**Planning hypotheses, not recipes or tasting results · 17 October 2026.** No winning flavour is predicted. Each direction is a finished RTD liquid in a can or bottle; an outsourced formulator must select lawful exact materials, doses, process, pack compatibility and shelf life. All six core briefs are CBD-free. Caffeine is an independent concept decision, not a property implied by a fruit name. Test one principal occasion first; do not promise that one recipe serves work, exercise and bedtime equally well.

“Low,” “medium” and “high” below describe intended relative sensory intensity within a pilot, not measured finished products. pH is not a proxy for perceived sourness, and Brix does not measure equivalent sweetness across sugar, sweeteners and dissolved actives. Numeric formulation specifications follow bench work, not assumed consumer preferences.

## F1 · Lemon, mandarin and a light citrus-peel finish

- **Primary job:** workday focus/alertness. First-priority hypothesis: recognizable fruit refreshment with a short finish can work at a desk without an aperitif identity.
- **Taste brief:** low-to-medium sweetness; medium crisp acidity; low bitterness; fresh peel aroma without perfumed cleaner notes; light body; short, clean finish. First contrast: still versus low carbonation in an otherwise matched base. A 250–330 ml can/bottle is a portion hypothesis to compare, not a launch commitment.
- **Functional routes:** caffeine-only reference or a legally reviewed caffeine/theanine version; a separate caffeine-free base tests taste but does not prove a non-stimulant cognitive effect. Natural caffeine sources still contribute caffeine and may add other tastes. Do not disguise extra extract load as a “flavour tweak.”
- **Bench anchors:** US Neutonic Orange Sunrise's declared orange profile, with its exact-label conflict retained; Czech Vilgain lemon/lime as a sweetened focus control and CANS+ Performance as an unsweetened contrast. Cross-fruit comparisons cannot isolate the effect of sweetness.
- **Risks to test:** caffeine bitterness, peel-like versus pith-like finish, lingering sweetener, carbonation bite becoming harsh with acidity. Test at service temperature and after 15–20 minutes on a desk.
- **Reject/rework if:** high initial liking falls through the container, “energy candy” or cleaner associations dominate, or the meaningful advantage disappears against the local control. Do not add stronger herbs to make it appear more adult.

## F2 · Peach and gentle tea

- **Primary job:** workday concentration/break, including people who prefer tea cues. First-priority contrast to F1; **still** is the starting hypothesis, with low-carbonation version only as a matched test.
- **Taste brief:** soft stone-fruit aroma, low-to-medium sweetness and acidity, light-to-medium body; low tea bitterness and drying; restrained floral note; a finish that clears between sips. Proposed 250–330 ml bottle/can.
- **Functional routes:** quantified tea-derived caffeine, with/without a separately reviewed theanine addition. Exact tea cultivar/extract, catechin specification, caffeine and processing matter; “tea” cannot guarantee low bitterness or calm. A caffeine-free botanical imitation is a different formula and must be assessed separately.
- **Bench anchors:** US Pure Leaf Mental Focus Peach is **sparkling**, so it provides a tea/function contrast but cannot validate still preference. Use the Czech Nupreme HiMatcha BIO 330 ml bottle as a still-tea architecture control after physical-label reconciliation: retailer sugar declarations conflict, and 67 mg caffeine per bottle is declared. It is matcha rather than peach tea, so this is not a controlled flavour or carbonation comparison. Halfday Peach Tea is an optional US still control after its own label check. Any unknown exposure means no ingestion until checked.
- **Risks to test:** catechin drying that builds over a full serving; cooked peach after heat processing; haze/sediment; “weak tea” versus “pleasantly light”; bitterness increasing as it warms. Evaluate base and actual active formula, not just scented water.
- **Reject/rework if:** consumers want a conventional tea they can already buy more cheaply, or full-serving drying overwhelms the fruit. Test fit with an ordinary lunch independently of desk liking.

## F3 · Lemon and a restrained blackcurrant note

- **Primary job:** hydration after light/moderate activity or on hot days; conditional secondary route. **Still** starting point, with low carbonation as a separate option if relevant users want it. Proposed 400–500 ml bottle to test drinking pace and volume, not an established optimum.
- **Taste brief:** medium refreshing acidity, low-to-medium sweetness, low bitterness, clean fruit aroma, light body and deliberately measured salt/mineral impression. Avoid making “low sweetness” a fixed constraint if it causes rejection.
- **Functional routes:** defined sodium/potassium electrolyte system only after specifying activity need and dose; magnesium is optional, not mandatory. Specify salt form, elemental load, water mineral background and total solids.
- **Bench anchors:** exact US Gatorade Lower Sugar Lemonade bottle; local bottled sports drink and still water. Blackcurrant offers a proposed fruit contrast; availability or cultural association is not evidence of preference.
- **Risks to test:** potassium bitterness/metallic note, saltiness, acid/mineral interaction, pigment stability, thirst or digestive discomfort during the full bottle. Test plain water as an honest alternative; do not use an energy-drink flight to choose a hydration formula.
- **Reject/rework if:** water wins on the relevant job and the formulation has no credible incremental reason to buy, or mineral load limits drinking pace. No recovery/hydration effect inferred from refreshment ratings.

## F4 · Elderflower, lemon balm aroma and lemon

- **Primary job:** caffeine-free after-work or social refreshment; conditional second category. Light carbonation starting hypothesis with a still variant available. Proposed 250–330 ml can/bottle.
- **Taste brief:** low-to-medium sweetness/acidity, low bitterness, gentle floral/citrus aroma, light body, short herbal finish. Test two floral levels because “delicate” and “soapy/perfumey” may be close for some people.
- **Functional routes:** start with lawful flavouring levels and sensory ritual; a magnesium/theanine route requires its own material/dose/legal review. A lemon balm aroma does not establish a botanical extract dose or stress effect.
- **Bench anchors:** verified Czech BOHEMSCA linden/basil 200 ml bottled tonic as a botanical repertoire contrast (not an elderflower match); VYBES Strawberry Lavender demonstrates a US still floral architecture; Hiyo provides one fruit-forward sparkling social comparison. These contrasts do not imply identical flavours or validated relaxation.
- **Risks to test:** floral fatigue, persistent sweetness, functional-mineral notes, headspace aroma under carbonation, mismatch with savoury food. Evaluate alone and with a standardized savoury snack on separate passes.
- **Reject/rework if:** interest depends on a promised “buzz” or unproven stress claim, or aftertaste/food pairing reduces whole-can enjoyment. Do not infer national fondness for elderflower.

## F5 · Sour cherry, orange peel and a restrained bitter finish

- **Primary job:** adult social/food-pairing occasion. An aperitif-inspired **option**, not a master brand profile or productivity template. Proposed 200–250 ml bottle/can; light carbonation versus still to be tested.
- **Taste brief:** medium acidity and fruit body; low-to-medium sweetness; low-to-medium bitterness as a variable, not a requirement; cherry/citrus aroma; controlled drying and a finish that invites another sip.
- **Functional routes:** CBD-free and caffeine-free; begin with flavour rather than an expensive active stack. Botanicals with regulatory, allergen or interaction implications require exact-species/material review. Do not assume quinine, gentian or grapefruit extract are needed to make it adult.
- **Bench anchors:** qualifying Crodino Original RTD bottle as an EU bitter aperitif reference; BOHEMSCA linden/basil tonic in its verified 200 ml glass bottle as local repertoire, exact label required. Hiyo is a fruit-led US contrast, not an aperitif reference by default.
- **Risks to test:** cherry/cough-syrup association, long pith-like bitterness, aronia-like drying if a polyphenol-rich material is used, sweetness needed to balance acid. Two bitterness levels on the same base separate “adult” expectation from actual enjoyment.
- **Reject/rework if:** only dedicated bitter-drink enthusiasts like it and that group cannot sustain the intended channel, or a second serving feels tiring. A niche can be valid, but label it as a segment hypothesis.

## F6 · Pear, vanilla aroma and gentle chamomile

- **Primary job:** bedtime wind-down ritual; later exploratory route, **still**, caffeine-free, proposed small 150–200 ml bottle/can. This small portion must be tested; it is not a sleep recommendation.
- **Taste brief:** low acidity, low-to-medium perceived sweetness, rounded but non-syrupy body, quiet pear/vanilla aroma, low herbal bitterness and short finish. No carbonation by default, yet stillness preference must be measured.
- **Functional routes:** start with legally cleared food flavours and ritual positioning. Any magnesium, chamomile extract, theanine, glycine or sleep-active addition needs exact-material assessment; no melatonin or sedative combination is assumed. If a gentle-acid taste brief leads to a higher-pH formula, its process and shelf stability need separate validation; perceived sourness and pH are not interchangeable.
- **Bench anchors:** US BRĒZ Dream Cherry Chamomile establishes a current hemp-free sleep-positioned can with **different** flavour/carbonation architecture, not evidence that pear or stillness will win. Test against a qualifying caffeine-free packaged beverage, not home-brewed tea as a product benchmark.
- **Risks to test:** medicine association, cloying vanilla, residual mineral flavour, unpleasant room-temperature finish, unwanted evening liquid volume. Bedtime suitability and actual sleep are separate outcomes.
- **Reject/rework if:** users prefer no drink, serving size is unwelcome or the proposition needs unsubstantiated sleep claims to attract interest. Efficacy testing would require a separate design.

## Choose a small range using evidence

Start with **F1 and F2 as competing workday directions**, not two guaranteed launch SKUs. Keep sweetness/carbonation variables explicit. Reserve F4 or F5 for a separate caffeine-free evening hypothesis only if occasion interviews justify it. F3 and F6 remain conditional until the workday study clarifies the founder's best learning path. A one-SKU launch is acceptable; two is a ceiling for the first production-learning proposal, not a business constraint imposed on the founder.

Progression requires: acceptable blind whole-serving liking against a relevant local control; second-serving enjoyment on a permitted schedule; a useful segment/occasion distinction; no recurring taste defect; a viable legally reviewed formula; stability in the intended can/bottle; and an outsourceable batch without needless unique materials. Require evidence of incremental reach before a second flavour: count people who choose it and reject the first, not just two similarly high average scores. Hypothetical purchase and free-sample repeat do not equal paid repeat.
