# EU social-tonic and functional supplement-drink opportunities

**Research date: 17 October 2026.** Run created **2026-10-17 10:45:35 Europe/Prague (CEST, UTC+02:00)**. This is a research and validation recommendation, not a launch decision or a cleared formulation.

**Decision:** pursue evidence for a narrowly defined customer switch before commissioning development. No opportunity yet has sufficient evidence of incremental paid demand, repeat buying and contribution to justify production. Two tracks merit inexpensive further investigation: **S1, a less-sweet nonbeer social drink for specific venues**, and **F1, a transparent-caffeine work-break drink for a demonstrated dissatisfied segment**. The first has simpler claims requirements; the second has a more frequent plausible occasion and more established general ingredient science, but much more direct competition and an unresolved effect at the eventual dose. Neither is a proven underserved market. S2, a portioned meal-pairing drink, is a reserve research hypothesis, not a third investment recommendation.

## Executive findings

1. **Fresh checks narrow the apparent supply gaps.** Czechia already has functional focus/calm cans and mineral waters, inexpensive 0.0-style cocktail cans, and imported bottled aperitifs. France has multiple ready-to-drink alcohol-free aperitifs in grocery. Supply is not limited to boutique “social tonic” brands. The new product checks below add Mattoni mocktail cans, Crodino and several French aperitifs to the previous competitive frame.
2. **Moderation and work-break occasions are documented; dissatisfaction with existing drinks is much less secure.** Czech category data, Dutch commissioned consumer research and French IFOP research support alcohol-free social occasions. Coffee and energy purchasing establish the workday substitute habit. They do not establish that consumers want another premium can, a physiological relaxation effect, or a subscription.
3. **Public commercial evidence mostly measures availability or adjacent categories.** Exact local SKU velocity and verified repeat cohorts remain unknown. Rohlík's displayed “regular buying” percentages are useful leads with undisclosed denominators and time windows, not comparable retention rates. A retailer listing, a funding round, a company's total revenue and an announced store rollout answer different questions.
4. **Sensory social value can survive without a health claim.** A credible food-pairing, sweetness or serving-format improvement could be valuable without sedation. Caffeine has evidence for alertness; normal-function nutrient claims are narrower than “focus”, “stress relief” or “no crash”. Botanical capsule trials and EU supplement permissions cannot simply be transferred into unrestricted social cans.
5. **Outsourcing is possible, but minimum runs are a material risk.** Public supplier examples range from approximately 3,000 standard-recipe cans to 6,000 custom cans, with much higher industrial minimums. They are advertised terms, not quotations or acceptance of this project's formula. Small shots do not necessarily mean small capital commitments: one supplier advertises a 20,000-shot minimum.
6. **Czechia is a practical learning candidate, not the market winner.** Germany offers a larger but competitive test setting; France and the Netherlands provide a sharper test of differentiated adult taste; Austria provides a nearby operational comparison; Poland tests local price and channel feasibility. Spain has strong substitute-market evidence and deserves follow-up if a local route becomes accessible. The six-country investigation cannot establish EU-wide absence or demand.

Supporting outputs: [evidence map](evidence-map.md), [structured opportunity matrix](opportunity-matrix.csv), [external source register](sources.csv). Existing research is linked rather than rewritten. Facts, interpretations and proposed products are separated throughout.

## Scope and method

**Included:** finished non-alcoholic liquids in cans or bottles, including genuinely ready-to-drink bottled shots. Two commercial concepts only: social tonic for socialising/unwinding or replacing an alcoholic occasion; functional supplement drink with a specific nutritional or functional proposition. “Supplement drink” does not predetermine legal classification. Proposed primary products are CBD-free and non-intoxicating.

**Excluded as proposed products:** powders, pouches, tablets, gummies, dilution concentrates, THC-led/intoxicating products and unrelated soda innovation. These can appear as realistic competing habits, not qualifying product benchmarks. CBD is assessed only as a separate gated extension. No company was contacted, participant recruited, product/report purchased, advertisement launched, test commissioned or consumption experiment conducted. No tasting or interview findings are claimed.

**Evidence language:** **Finding** means a source supports the stated observation, with its attribution and limitations. **Inference** means our interpretation of those findings. **Hypothesis** means an unvalidated product/customer proposition. “Unknown” is not zero. An unlocated competitor, closure or sales figure is not evidence of an empty market, failure or lack of demand.

The lead read the root README and research index and checked for applicable AGENTS.md instructions; none were found in the repository or its ancestor directories. Complementary agents audited repository evidence, investigated social demand and functional demand, and subsequently checked regulatory/scientific feasibility and challenged the emerging recommendations. The lead investigated broader country context and commercial production, integrated the outputs, checked contradictions and owns publication. The evidence map states precise file review depth; neither every historical external link nor every page of every old source was reverified.

The repository was changing concurrently. The initial audit snapshot began at 10:46:06 CEST. Before publication, this branch was reconciled with main commit `3cc3b0e83c5c6f0dbc757fb2f28c407618994b7b`, including the completed claims/acquisition reports and an earlier creative-ideas run. The evidence map separates the original snapshot from this later targeted review. Publication is isolated on a new branch; existing conclusions and unrelated edits are preserved.

Searches used Czech, German, Polish, Dutch, French and Spanish terms alongside English, prioritising official legal texts, food authorities, labels, local retailers, primary survey releases and company disclosures. Price/stock checks are snapshots accessed on the research date, not launch dates. Undated product pages do not establish launch timing. The external register contains 106 distinct URLs, including 96 directly cited in this report; these are not 106 independent studies. Sources record publication dates separately from the period/event measured, and mark unknown dates. Syndicated versions of the same announcement are not independent corroboration.

**Coverage limits:** no representative original survey, transaction panel, venue census, distributor terms, manufacturer quote or launch-ready ingredient dossier was obtained. Search and retailer availability are location-dependent. Hospitality is materially undercovered relative to e-commerce. Many sources concern adjacent categories, not the exact proposed beverages. The research generates falsifiable opportunities, not a market-size forecast.

## Broad EU screen and selection of six deeper markets

This is a staged evidence-and-operating-context screen, not a numerical attractiveness ranking. Common EU sources give broad context; exact category/customer evidence varies considerably. We considered existing coverage, documented incumbent occasions, product variety, plausible access from the founder's base, and the value of testing different price/retail settings. Founder access itself remains unverified.

Two useful boundaries from current common sources:

- Eurostat's **31 August 2026** release on **2025 production** records 2.1 billion litres in the combined low-alcohol/ non-alcoholic beer group. This is production, including export, and combines alcohol definitions; it is neither social-tonic demand nor consumer spend. Some country production measures differ because of confidentiality. [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20260831-1).
- Eurostat's **18 June 2026** release on **2025 prices** shows substantial differences in household prices across Member States. The indices are not income-adjusted and are not willingness to pay for functional drinks. High-price Denmark/Ireland/Luxembourg should not automatically outrank Poland or Czechia. [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20260618-2).

## Social-tonic findings: real supply and the unresolved switch

A social product can compete on taste, participation and the serving ritual. It need not cause physiological relaxation. That lowers dependence on difficult health claims, but also means an existing aperitif, alcohol-free beer or well-chosen soft drink may already solve the job. Clinical evidence is not required for a flavour preference; customer evidence is still required for a profitable new product.

### Need, payment and repeat


France provides the strongest fresh stated receptivity-to-variety signal, while the Netherlands supplies recent commissioned, weighted survey data on occasions and a reason not to assume dissatisfaction. Czech support for a less-sweet evening drink remains qualitative. In a French public discussion the original poster dislikes sweetness and preparation for a Friday aperitif; another says a large bottle is too much as the only abstainer. Other commenters prefer ordinary AF beer, water, ginger beer or home preparations. These comments motivate segmentation questions only; they neither estimate prevalence nor prove a paid requirement. [Exploratory discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskFrance/comments/1r1q35e/a_part_des_sodas_et_jus_de_fruits_sucr%C3%A9s_vous/).

Rohlík's checked Czech pages display “regular buying” indicators of **38% for Crodino** and **8% for Mattoni G&T**. Their denominator, purchase threshold and time window are undisclosed. They cannot be compared as retention rates, treated as national proportions, or inserted into a startup forecast. Campari's 2025 annual report describes Crodino growth across European seeding markets, supporting continuing brand activity; its combined “Crodino & Other Aperitifs” financial category includes other products and is not eligible Crodino-only revenue. [Crodino retailer](https://www.rohlik.cz/1462212-crodino-biondo-nealkoholicky-aperitiv-3-0-175-l-0-0-obj), [Mattoni retailer](https://www.rohlik.cz/1480085-mattoni-nealko-drink-g-t-plech), [Campari annual report](https://www.camparigroup.com/sites/default/files/downloads/01.1%20Campari%20Group_Annual%20Report%20for%20the%20year%20ended%2031%20December%202025_0.pdf).

Existing VYBES CBD exit is US regulatory/retailer evidence only. Crodino’s long history and qualitative brand growth support category durability, without validating a new startup’s economics.


The prior dataset also includes cheap alcohol-free beer, premium sodas, kombucha and mineral water across the six countries. These remain essential controls even though this report does not propose an unrelated soda. Actual beer growth is stronger paid-category evidence than a tonic listing, but it also signals improving competition. Brewing statistics can include draught and residual-alcohol products; they are substitute-category context, not qualifying packaged social-tonic unit totals.

## Functional supplement-drink findings

This space has a different proof burden: a customer buying an effect must receive a proposition credible at the actual ingredient identity, dose, matrix and usage pattern, and any commercial claim must have a valid legal route. Energy, normal nutritional function, subjective relaxation and sleep onset are different endpoints. The strongest ingredient mechanism can still be a weak startup opportunity if coffee, tablets or grocery bottles already solve the need.

## Regulatory and scientific feasibility

Research checked 17 October 2026. Scope: CZ, DE, AT, PL, NL and FR; ordinary finished beverages and genuinely ready-to-drink bottled daily doses. This is an evidence-based route screen, not formula approval. Publication dates, legal event dates and unknown dates are separated in sources.csv. ### Findings that change opportunity selection

**Positive routes exist without exotic actives.** Conventional food ingredients offer a plausible ordinary-beverage route for S1–S3. A measured-caffeine beverage and optionally a permitted nutrient offer F1 a feasible technical route; nutrient claims cannot substitute for evidence that the target customer benefits or will pay. F2 has an authorised normal-nutrition route, but no established immediate-calming effect. F3 has materially divergent medicine boundaries and should stay on hold. F4 has a plausible ordinary-protein-food route, with no basis here for disease management or a universal older-adult need.

Supplement daily-portion warnings and national procedures apply when that category actually fits. FSMP is specifically for dietary management of patients under medical supervision and has separate rules, including a prohibition on nutrition/health claims. ### Exact forms, realistic servings and honest claims

**B5 and magnesium offer a positive conventional-food route.** The current 26-November-2025 consolidated Regulation 1925/2006 lists calcium D-pantothenate and magnesium salts of citric acid among permitted forms for addition to ordinary foods. This is stronger than assuming permission from supplement listings. Selected supplier grade, identity, purity, manufacturing process, contaminants and national amount restrictions still require review. This does not approve an arbitrary “magnesium complex” or magnesium L-threonate in beverages. [R02](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02006R1925-20251126).

The live Commission claims dataset was retrieved on 17 October 2026: 2,337 published records. It confirms authorised entries for B5/normal mental performance (POL-HC-6433), magnesium/normal psychological function (POL-HC-6407), protein/muscle maintenance (POL-HC-6447) and melatonin/sleep-onset time (POL-HC-6419). B5/Mg claims require at least a source of the relevant nutrient. This is nutritional-function evidence, not proof of an immediate focus/calm sensation in already sufficient users. Protein's source criterion is at least 12% of energy from protein; that percentage alone does not define a meaningful intake for the target person. [Current register and data (R03)](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en); [Regulation 1924/2006 annex (R05)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02006R1924-20141213).

For beverages, significant-amount calculations ordinarily use 7.5% NRV per 100 ml; a genuine single-portion package can use 15% per portion. B5 NRV is 6 mg and Mg NRV 375 mg. Thus 250 ml under the per-100-ml route corresponds to 1.125 mg B5 or 70.3125 mg elemental Mg; 330 ml corresponds to 1.485 mg or 92.8125 mg. The single-portion alternative corresponds to 0.9 mg B5 or 56.25 mg Mg. These are arithmetic illustrations of claim eligibility, **not a selected recipe, adequate effective dose for a symptom, or safety approval**. The complete declaration/claim and retained content at shelf-life end need assessment. [1169/2011 Annex XIII (R04)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?qid=1771601123549&uri=CELEX%3A32011R1169).

**Magnesium science does not validate an acute calm drink.** A 2018 randomised study of 264 stressed adults with low magnesemia compared 300 mg/day magnesium with versus without B6 over eight weeks. Both improved from baseline; there was no placebo group and no significant overall between-arm difference. Sanofi funded the study and participated in its conduct/analysis. It cannot establish an immediate effect from a smaller citrate beverage in magnesium-replete social drinkers. Later analyses of the same participants are not independent replication. [Pouteau et al. (R12)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0208454).

The SCF upper level is 250 mg/day for readily dissociable added/supplemental magnesium, excluding magnesium naturally present in ordinary foods/beverages; its key adverse endpoint was diarrhoea. It is a scientific exposure benchmark, not an EU-wide statutory beverage maximum. Three hypothetical 100 mg cans give 300 mg before other supplements; this is a poor basis for unrestricted session drinking. Actual individual medical suitability is not established here. [SCF 2001 opinion (R13)](https://food.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2020-12/sci-com_scf_out105_en.pdf).

**Caffeine: efficacy, safety and claims are different questions.** EFSA's positive alertness/concentration assessment at at least 75 mg does not create authorisation. Regulation 2016/1411 refused the proposed 40–75 mg alertness claim following inadequate substantiation below 75 mg. The live published register contains no authorised caffeine alertness/concentration claim. Some pending claims can engage transition rules; their validity depends on exact claim/history and national enforcement. Do not convert that nuance into either an authorised claim or a universal assertion that all caffeine wording is banned. A refused claim and a withdrawn/vetoed draft are not usable authorisations. [R06](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R1411); [R03](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en); [pending-claims list (R07)](https://ec.europa.eu/food/food-feed-portal/backend/claims/files/claims_pending.pdf).

A later double-blind crossover trial reported improved sustained attention/alertness after 60 mg in 82 healthy low/non-caffeine consumers aged 40–60. Published online September 2016 and in a 2017 issue, it is relevant counterevidence to a simplistic claim that lower doses never work. The abstract does not verify a final beverage matrix; company-affiliated authors and the narrow sample limit transfer. It neither changes claim authorisation nor proves work output, repeat demand or efficacy of the proposed stored drink. [Wilhelmus et al. primary trial (R08)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27649778/).

EFSA's healthy-adult references are 200 mg for a single exposure and 400 mg/day from all sources; 100 mg near bedtime can affect some adults' sleep. Pregnancy has a lower 200 mg/day reference. These are not an invitation to dose to the limit, and do not cover every medical condition or medication combination. Lower caffeine is not a sleep-safety guarantee. [EFSA (R09)](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine).

For covered beverages, above 150 mg/L triggers the EU high-caffeine warning, with the coffee/tea-name exception narrowly defined. Examples: 40 mg/250 ml = 160 mg/L; 40 mg/330 ml = 121.2 mg/L; 50 mg/330 ml = 151.5 mg/L. Germany caps caffeine at 320 mg/L in the covered water-based caffeinated soft-drink category, including caffeine from other ingredients. Poland restricts sales of covered drinks with added caffeine above 150 mg/L or taurine to under-18s, educational premises and vending machines; naturally occurring substances are excluded from that definition. Category/ingredient decisions therefore affect channel options. [R04](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?qid=1771601123549&uri=CELEX%3A32011R1169); [current German §§4–5/Annex 8 (R10)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/frsaftv_2004/BJNR101600004.html); [Polish authority (R11)](https://www.gov.pl/web/psse-wroclaw/napoje-energetyzujace).

### Ingredients that need a different burden of proof

**L-theanine:** the Finnish food authority explicitly distinguishes green-tea-derived isolated L-theanine's supplement-use history from other foods, which require novel-food authorisation. Slovenia's 2023 enforcement against Prime Energy identified L-theanine as impermissible in the beverage. This is a serious warning against an ordinary-beverage theanine recipe, not an exhaustive ruling for every preparation. The 2026 consultation on ≥98% L-theanine produced using non-GM E. coli B found that specific preparation novel. Current applications include L-theanine; an application is not permission. Ordinary tea with naturally occurring theanine is a different ingredient history. Require the exact extraction/fermentation process, status evidence and intended category; do not design first and use a supplement label as a workaround. [Finnish authority (R14)](https://www.ruokavirasto.fi/en/foodstuffs/food-sector/food-ingredients-and-contents/novel-foods/establishing-novel-food-status-of-a-food/novel-food-catalogue-of-european-commission/); [Slovenian enforcement (R15)](https://www.gov.si/novice/2023-11-13-ugotovitve-nadzora-inspekcije-za-varno-hrano-veterinarstvo-in-varstvo-rastlin-nad-pijaco-prime/); [2026 consultation (R16)](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/1e468cb7-af61-4aa2-8d4f-976f9783dbae_en?filename=novel-food_consult-status_l-teanina.pdf); [applications (R17)](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/authorisations/summary-applications-and-notifications_en).

**Botanicals:** conventional culinary infusion is not equivalent to a concentrated, standardised root/leaf extract. Food history, species/part, solvent, extraction ratio, marker compounds, contaminants, daily exposure and country lists must be examined for the chosen preparation. For ashwagandha, BfR reports liver/endocrine and other concerns and could not derive a safe intake guidance value from available data. That makes a repeat-social-can active especially difficult to justify; it does not mean every botanical infusion is unsafe. Capsule studies at a particular extract/dose/duration cannot validate a beverage with a differently processed mixture or repeated servings alongside alcohol. [BfR September 2024 (R18)](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/349/ashwagandha-food-supplements-with-potential-health-risks.pdf).

**Botanical claims update:** CJEU C-386/23 on 30 April 2025 rejects treating an on-hold claim as automatically usable; specific applicable transitional protection remains a separate legal question. On 17 September 2026, C-657/24 Kyberg Pharma concerned whether certain mandatory accompanying information already on labelling must be repeated in separate advertising. It does **not** reverse the earlier botanical-claim ruling or authorise “calm,” “sleep” or “focus.” [2025 judgment (R19)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A62023CJ0386); [2026 official summary (R20)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A62024CJ0657_RES).

### CBD separate variant: not presently a beverage development rationale

### Economics boundary

No reliable manufacturing margin, CAC, LTV, distributor margin, retailer fee, freight charge or market size is estimated here. Prior [range economics](../../research/product/12-range-and-format/data/README.md) and [demand sensitivity](../../research/market/06-demand-and-country-selection/data/README.md) are reusable calculation structures. Their assumptions must not be treated as quotations or combined with the different topic 22 assumptions.

Use this ledger before a production commitment:

> **Contribution per delivered order = cash sales excluding VAT and refundable deposits − product/pack/filling and QA allocation − inbound transport − excise/beverage levies and nonrefundable packaging fees − warehousing/pick-pack − outbound delivery subsidy − payment fees − discounts/returns/expiry losses.** Acquisition and fixed launch costs must then be covered by observed contribution over real reorder periods, not an assumed lifetime.

For wholesale, start from the actual net brand invoice and avoid subtracting retailer margin a second time. Deposits are not revenue or profit. A consumer-facing price generally differs from the manufacturer's receipt; VAT treatment in the benchmark tables is stated only where the page makes it clear. A price displayed without captured VAT/deposit/shipping detail is marked unknown, never quietly normalised. The next commercially decisive evidence is a complete written quote and actual sales/reorders at the intended channel price, not a speculative gross-margin target.

**Pre-development dependencies:** final product/category/country brief; one target serving and consumption pattern; ingredient dossier; lawful full presentation; manufacturer process and pack acceptance; ownership/transfer of formula; MOQ per flavour; payment milestones; stability/release plan; minimum remaining shelf life; all delivered costs; and named route capacity. This report recommends obtaining those later if the customer gate passes; it does not commission them.


## Seven opportunity hypotheses

The following are **creative commercial hypotheses**, not findings that an unmet market exists. Each includes the evidence for and against it. Three sit in social tonics and four in functional drinks; the weak candidates remain visible to explain why they do not earn development spend. Format sizes and ingredients are design directions only. No validated recipe, selected price or formula is presented. Proposed social cores should target a verified 0.0% specification if that is essential to the customer; nationally permitted non-alcoholic terminology and analytical tolerance require confirmation.


### S1 — A dry, caffeine-free nonbeer drink for a whole social evening

1. **Proposition:** An adult, less-sweet drink that remains pleasant over a long dinner, games night or pub visit when choosing no alcohol.
2. **Concept:** Social tonic; sensory/participation benefit, no physiological relaxation promise.
3. **Customer/occasion/geography:** Czech adults who dislike beer and sweet mocktails; initially Prague/Brno independent hospitality plus take-home. German urban secondary comparison, not assumed common segment. Recurring evening gatherings; precise frequency unknown.
4. **Substitutes/unresolved need:** AF beer, Mattoni RTD, Crodino, tonic, zero cola, kombucha and water. Hypothesised unresolved combination is low perceived sweetness, no caffeine, nonbeer flavour and affordable multiple serves—not basic availability.
5. **Need evidence:** Topic 05 Czech self-reports and French exploratory discussion; category social participation evidence. No representative Czech measurement of this particular segment.
6. **Paid evidence:** Existing CZ retailer offers and opaque repeat badges, especially Crodino, support some paid use; no willingness to pay for extra dryness or startup premium.
7. **Closest competition/difference:** Mattoni G&T has 4 g sugar/100 ml and CZK 19.90 makes price-led mocktail imitation weak; Crodino has higher labelled sugar (15 g/100 ml), but perceived sweetness was not tested and depends on acidity, bitterness and serving conditions. Must win perceived dryness/length of drinking, not claim everyone dislikes sweetness.
8. **Format/experience:** Conceptual 330 ml can or 330–500 ml bottle, controlled carbonation, citrus/herb/bitter profile using ordinary food preparations. Format a test variable; no recipe validated.
9. **Functional promise/claims:** Enjoyment/occasion only. Ordinary beverage classification most plausible; less-sweet is sensory descriptor, “low sugar” only if legal nutrient threshold met and lab confirmed. No “reduces stress”, “social buzz” or driving-safety efficacy claim.
10. **Repeat reason/pattern:** Preferred flavour/participation during the next gathering, potentially multiple servings. Need proof of second-serving liking and total-evening spend; no recommended dose.
11. **Price/channel benchmark:** CZK 19.90/330 ml Mattoni promo, 24.90 reference; CZK 59.97/175 ml Crodino; CZK 19.90/500 ml Birell inherited. Independent venues/retailer mixed baskets reduce single-order liquid delivery burden. No target price decided.
12. **Dependencies:** Multiple-serving ingredient review, exact ABV verification, bitterness/astringency/sweetener aftertaste, can lining/acid compatibility, pasteurization and ambient shelf life. Conventional formulation likely simpler than sedative extracts; quotation/MOQ unknown here.
13. **Counterevidence/gap reason:** Cheap domestic incumbents plus improving AF beer; satisfied cola/water drinkers. The niche may be small, and sweetener-free dryness may taste thin or harsh.
14. **Invalidators:** Preference disappears at economically viable price; only novelty trials; customers choose cheaper beer/Mattoni; venues lack repeat orders.
15. **Cheapest next validation:** Before any development spend, a structured comparison of existing real alternatives with target-segment occasion/receipt diaries and a paper choice exercise including total-evening cost. No tests conducted. Subsequent controlled sensory/preorder work requires a separately authorised protocol; do not sell an unvalidated formula.

**Judgment:** Best social question to investigate in CZ only if a precisely identifiable dissatisfied subgroup emerges. Current evidence does not justify production. France could be stronger on need but would require a different route-to-market test.

### S2 — A small dinner-pairing bottle for the only non-drinker at the table

1. **Proposition:** A ready-to-pour adult drink matched to food, with enough complexity for dinner and a portion that does not require opening a 750 ml bottle for one person.
2. **Concept:** Social tonic; gastronomic/social, no functional efficacy core.
3. **Customer/occasion/geography:** French urban diners/hosts choosing AF while companions drink wine; NL specialty food/hospitality secondary test. Restaurant dinner or weekend hosting, frequency unmeasured.
4. **Substitutes/gap:** Water, soda, AF wine/beer, sparkling tea, made-to-order mocktails. Hypothesis is small-format food pairing and low wastage/commitment, not absence of sophisticated alcohol-free options.
5. **Need evidence:** IFOP variety demand, exploratory small-bottle comment, existing premium gastronomic positioning; specific portion dissatisfaction remains weak evidence.
6. **Paid evidence:** French domestic Copenhagen 750 ml at EUR 25.50 listings show a price on offer; no verified selling volume/repeat or small-bottle premium acceptance.
7. **Competition/difference:** Copenhagen Sparkling Tea, Symples 330 ml, Yoleau 250 ml, AF wine and kombucha. Portion alone is copyable; need a proven food/sensory fit plus venue access.
8. **Format/experience:** 250–330 ml glass single dinner serving or 375 ml for two; tea/verjus/culinary botanical direction, still or light sparkle; no validated recipe, caffeine must be measured if tea used.
9. **Promise/claims:** Food pairing and sensory description; ordinary beverage plausible, not a supplement. Exact botanical preparations, residual alcohol and labelling still require review.
10. **Repeat:** Reorder with familiar meal or venue; small size reduces leftover commitment. Whether frequency covers packaging costs unknown.
11. **Benchmarks/channel:** Copenhagen 750 ml at €25.50 retail; Symples 330 ml at €2.75; Yoleau 3×250 ml at €6.49; use food shops, restaurants and grocery mixed baskets. No assumed restaurant margin or customer price ceiling.
12. **Dependencies:** Tea haze/astringency, oxygen/light, micro control and glass freight; portion packaging economics; restaurant refrigeration/menu turnover and distributor terms; no guaranteed startup MOQ.
13. **Counterevidence/gap reason:** Existing single-serve mocktails/herbal drinks already solve portion; restaurant pour-by-glass can solve 750 ml waste. Wine imitation may disappoint; lower opening volume raises unit packaging costs.
14. **Invalidators:** Diners prefer water or existing brands; no repeat venue demand; consumers treat it as rare celebration; flavour preference not stable across dishes.
15. **Cheapest validation:** Desk-audit menus/retailer small formats and public reviews for genuine portion complaints, then authorised structured customer/venue research comparing full bottle, by-glass and single portion; do not assume a bottle problem from one comment.

**Judgment:** Distinct, informative second social hypothesis; could outrank S1 if paid dining/portion data appear, but presently weaker evidence of the specific gap. No launch recommendation.

### S3 — A chilled weekday “day is done” ritual without an efficacy promise

1. **Proposition:** A ready-to-open evening drink that marks stopping work and replaces the habit of an alcoholic first drink.
2. **Concept:** Social tonic (unwind occasion), not a sleep supplement.
3. **Customer/occasion/geography:** German/French adults reducing weekday drinking at home; optional Czech pilot only if demand shown. Potentially several weekdays, not measured.
4. **Substitutes/gap:** Tea, water, AF beer, Symples Relax, Neuroblends RELAX, kombucha and ordinary soft drinks. Hypothesised unmet convenience/ritual, not established lack of supply.
5. **Need evidence:** Repository public evening voices; established calm-positioned products. No representative frequency/purchase study of this exact problem.
6. **Paid evidence:** Neuroblends 24×330 ml at €64.90 and Symples 330 ml at €2.75 offers; subscription button not proof retention. US experience cannot validate EU purchase.
7. **Competitors/difference:** Symples already close; Neuroblends adds actives. Differentiate only through demonstrated preferred experience/ritual. A claim-free formula may be safer but commercial differentiation weaker.
8. **Format:** 250–330 ml chilled can/bottle; aromatic food-use infusion, low sweetness, no caffeine; conceptual.
9. **Promise:** Sensory pause, not anxiolysis/sedation/sleep improvement; ordinary beverage plausible. No clinical effect assumed; strong therapeutic framing would undermine category choice.
10. **Repeat:** Pleasant stopping-work cue; purchasing must survive absence of a noticeable psychoactive effect. Ritual can also be served by cheap tea.
11. **Benchmarks/channel:** EUR 2.70/can Neuroblends 24-pack; EUR 2.75 Symples retail; specialty grocery or mixed case, not assumed profitable single-can DTC.
12. **Dependencies:** Food-use botanical preparation; taste/stability; honest wording; shipping pack weight; no multi-can sedating actives; exact label review.
13. **Counterevidence:** Existing supply already fits; premium drink might be rejected without promised feeling, as exploratory US Hiyo voices show. Need may be fulfilled free by changing routine.
14. **Invalidators:** No paid repeat without efficacy narrative; consumers prefer warm tea or reducing liquid at night; unit economics require unjustified premium.
15. **Cheapest validation:** Future diary/receipt study with current beverages and explicit no-efficacy positioning before formula development. No recruitment or study was performed.

**Judgment:** Hold/deprioritize. Do not manufacture a positive recommendation merely to fill a social-tonic quota; S3 may collapse into S1 if actual occasion/segment research finds no distinct buying behaviour.


### F1 — A clearly portioned early-afternoon drink where coffee and full-strength energy are poor fits

1. **Proposition:** a pleasant chilled drink with an explicitly modest, predictable caffeine portion for an early-afternoon work break.
2. **Concept:** functional supplement drink commercially; likely ordinary beverage if simple beverage ingredients/presentation, not automatically a supplement.
3. **Target/occasion/geography:** adults already buying drinks for the lunch-to-work transition; Czech urban workplaces/coworking first research cell, Polish equivalent as a comparison, contingent on founder access. Occurrence could be multiple weekdays, but actual segment frequency is unknown.
4. **Substitutes/unresolved need:** free coffee, smaller coffee, tea, cola, CANS+, Vilgain/Olimp, water and a walk. Possible gap is convenient cold availability plus preferred flavour/portion, not absent caffeine supply. It remains unproven that alternatives are inadequate.
5. **Need evidence:** repository Czech/English exploratory accounts distinguish caffeine avoidance, overstimulation and the break itself. EFSA states some adults' sleep is affected by 100 mg near bedtime; this supports careful timing and dose transparency, not a promise that a lower amount is sleep-safe. [Customer evidence](../../research/customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/work-focus-evidence.md); [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine).
6. **Paid demand:** exact local competing offers establish a functioning supply and plausible price reference; neither our target's willingness to pay nor repeat exists. Neutonic is an external UK/global signal, not local validation.
7. **Differentiation/competitors:** CANS+ at 42.24 mg is exceptionally close; a new formula alone has no defensible difference. A specific workplace route, sensory preference and transparent whole-can dose might differentiate if users/payors confirm it. No exclusive venue or route is secured.
8. **Format/experience:** one 250–330 ml can or bottle, tea/fruit flavour and controlled sweetness; no validated recipe. No caffeine dose is selected. A 40–60 mg preference concept would need separate evaluation and cannot inherit the evidence supporting alertness at at least 75 mg.
9. **Functional promise/limits:** modest caffeine delivery, with no promise of more completed work or “no crash.” Ingredient caffeine evidence is much stronger than evidence for a proprietary blend. A 21-person randomized caffeine-matched beverage study found no significant between-condition advantage on tested outcomes; it does not prove all blends ineffective. EFSA specifically found the proposed alertness relationship unsubstantiated at 40–less than 75 mg in its 2014 assessment. This is a material limitation for transferring that assessment to a modest-dose concept, not proof that every lower dose is ineffective. A later randomised study reported attention/alertness improvements at 60 mg in 82 healthy adults aged 40–60 with low/nonhabitual caffeine intake; the narrow population, tested delivery and company-affiliated authors limit transfer to this unformulated drink. It does not change claim authorisation or establish work productivity. [Wilhelmus et al., published online September 2016; 2017 issue](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27649778/). Evidence at at least 75 mg still does not create an authorised claim. [EFSA 2014 opinion](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/3574). Claims need current jurisdictional assessment. [Pereira et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39257647/).
10. **Repeat:** taste/convenience at a recurring work break, one portion replacing an existing purchase, not encouraged stacking. Frequency and number of paid weeks are unknown.
11. **Benchmarks/channels:** 39.50–45 CZK/330 ml Czech retail; PLN 4.99 Polish focus can; offices, coworking cafés, small convenience outlets before freight-heavy single-can DTC.
12. **Dependencies:** country/label review, caffeine and total intake instructions, stable flavour and dose, process/pack compatibility, local wholesale contribution and secured trial outlet. Published co-packer minima from topic 12 are not offers for this recipe.
13. **Counterevidence/gap explanation:** CANS+ already fits the thesis, free coffee is strong, dissatisfaction may concern overwork rather than drinks, and lower caffeine may feel insufficient. The apparent access gap may vanish in a local outlet audit.
14. **Invalidating assumptions:** few genuinely dissatisfied frequent buyers; only “stronger focus” copy wins; acceptable price falls below landed contribution; identical existing product is readily available; employer is the payor and refuses.
15. **Cheapest next validation:** first a local shelf/menu/office-offer audit against CANS+ and ordinary tea/coffee, followed (only in future separately authorised fieldwork) by recent-purchase interviews with explicit no-purchase and existing-brand options. Kill before formulation if no repeated unsatisfactory paid occasion is identifiable.

**Judgment:** worth investigating conditionally; strongest functional research route because conventional caffeine drinks provide a familiar mechanism and manufacturing comparison; efficacy at the eventual selected dose is unresolved. Need/paid-repeat evidence remains weak; ranks below any social option with stronger local occasion evidence and route access.

### F2 — A caffeine-free nutrition drink for the afternoon pause, for existing magnesium-supplement users

1. **Proposition:** make a chosen magnesium/nutrient routine convenient in a pleasant drink during an afternoon pause; do not promise immediate calm.
2. **Concept/classification:** functional supplement drink. A 200–250 ml ordinary beverage with permitted nutrient forms is one possible route; a 25–60 ml explicit daily-dose shot is more plausibly a food supplement and needs separate national assessment.
3. **Target/occasion/geography:** adults already using magnesium supplements who dislike tablets/preparation, Czechia or Germany as separate initial cells; daily routine frequency is hypothetical until observed.
4. **Substitutes/unresolved need:** tablets, powders, mineral water, CANS+ Balance, Magnesia Antistress, Biolectra liquid, herbal tea. Need would be convenience, taste and replacement of an existing routine; dissatisfaction with tablets in this exact customer group is not yet documented.
5. **Need evidence:** topic 05 finds caffeine-free pause interest and ritual needs; this is adjacent rather than direct evidence for nutrient-routine replacement. Existing liquid-dose products show feasible format supply, not unmet need. [Biolectra](https://www.biolectra-magnesium.de/produkte/biolectra-magnesium-300-mg-liquid/).
6. **Paid demand:** exact price/stock offers exist, but no finished-drink repeat, convenience premium or switch data. Public testimonials cannot fill this.
7. **Competition/difference:** CANS+ Balance is already a 330 ml magnesium/theanine/chamomile drink (1049 CZK/24 in same-day audit); Magnesia Antistress is lower-cost 700 ml; German Biolectra directly replaces pills. A lower-dose familiar-flavour drink may simplify use but has no established moat.
8. **Format:** one still or lightly sparkling portion, possibly a small bottle if pill-replacement proves the real job. Avoid choosing shot and full refreshment simultaneously.
9. **Promise/limits:** permitted magnesium claims concern normal psychological/nervous-system function and fatigue under conditions; they do not establish acute stress relief in magnesium-replete users. No clinically validated final product. Any botanical/theanine addition adds exact-preparation/legal questions rather than automatic differentiation.
10. **Repeat:** an already chosen nutrient routine paired with taste, one portion/day if appropriate; repeat must not rely on feeling sedation or using several social servings.
11. **Price/channel:** CANS+ ~43.71 CZK/can DTC versus inexpensive mineral water and tablets; pharmacy/drugstore/specialist route may fit a supplement, café/office route a beverage. Price for a new offer unknown.
12. **Dependencies:** exact magnesium form, dose, stability, bitterness, bowel tolerance and total intake; no use of Biolectra's 300 mg as a formulation target. Claims and serving instructions must fit classification. Social multi-can consumption is poorly matched to concentrated daily doses.
13. **Counterevidence:** current alternatives may fully satisfy users; transport of water adds cost to an inexpensive nutrient; the popular “anti-stress” narrative may create an expectation the compliant product cannot meet.
14. **Invalidators:** customers want a fast felt effect; no reason to abandon pills/water; acceptable premium insufficient; desired doses create sensory/tolerance problems.
15. **Next step:** audit existing magnesium alternatives and conduct future task-based interviews with current supplement users, explicitly comparing the nutrition promise with neutral flavour/refreshment. No formula work until the non-sedating promise survives.

**Judgment:** reserve hypothesis, currently weaker than F1. Do not count a general stress prevalence figure as beverage demand.

### F3 — A small bedtime bottle for adults who already choose a sleep supplement

1. **Proposition:** a low-volume ready-to-drink bedtime dose for an existing supplement user who dislikes preparation or tablets.
2. **Concept/classification:** functional supplement drink commercially; food supplement is more plausible than a casual social beverage, with national medicine-borderline assessment required.
3. **Target/occasion/geography:** occasional sleep-onset-support users, research first in France/Germany as separate regulatory and customer markets; not people seeking an insomnia treatment, not a social round, and not a recommendation for shift workers.
4. **Substitutes/gap:** tablets, gummies, sprays, tea, routines and clinical care; alleged convenience gap unverified. Large bedtime drink volume itself may be undesirable.
5. **Need evidence:** French 2024 survey reports 33.1% insomnia complaints and 21.5% short sleepers; outcomes/geography are specific. The highest burden among financially strained adults argues against assuming premium affordability. [SPF](https://inpes.santepubliquefrance.fr/sommeil/donnees).
6. **Paid evidence:** no credible local sleep-RTD repeat found; US mixed-format Dream Water results cannot supply it. Public demand for existing cheap dose forms is a substitute signal only.
7. **Competition/difference:** a mini bottle could remove preparation, but supplements already do. Historical Austrian Snoooze proves the format was tried, not why it disappeared or whether a current opportunity exists.
8. **Format:** 30–100 ml finished bottle, one explicit dose; no formula, dose or safe chronic pattern selected.
9. **Promise/limits:** any melatonin route must fit an authorised claim's exact conditions plus national classification/safety restrictions; an EU claim does not authorise every product category/country. Botanical-only “sleep” cannot borrow clinical efficacy from different extracts. Never imply insomnia treatment.
10. **Repeat:** occasional or course-based use according to a justified label, not automatic nightly subscription; lower repeat frequency weakens liquid economics.
11. **Benchmarks/channel:** German dm lists melatonin tablets/gummies around cents per dose and many alternatives; no current comparable EU sleep-RTD price verified. Pharmacy/drugstore more credible than bar or office routes.
12. **Dependencies:** melatonin national legal status, label warnings, interactions, stability/homogeneity, adult-only audience, no multi-serving use, no driving/social-relaxation conflation. Detailed official regulatory workstream controls this route.
13. **Counterevidence:** heavy need does not imply product fit; cheap pills/sprays solve convenience; ritual may require warm tea; sparse RTD supply can reflect poor demand and regulation. Snoooze absence is not evidence of failure.
14. **Invalidators:** no preparation/tablet dissatisfaction; purchasers mainly seek a medical claim; target country's route is unsuitable; only persistent/chronic use makes economics work.
15. **Next step:** country-specific classification/claim desk check first, then future interviews of existing adult supplement users about actual format dissatisfaction. No manufacturing unless both gates pass.

**Judgment:** hold; evidence supports a health burden, not a credible startup drink opportunity today.

1. **Proposition:** a modestly sized bottle delivering meaningful protein for people who demonstrably struggle to fit it into food, with everyday rather than bodybuilding presentation.
2. **Concept/classification:** functional supplement drink commercially; ordinarily a conventional protein beverage. Any product for dietary management of disease/malnutrition may enter foods-for-special-medical-purposes requirements; do not use this as a shortcut into treatment.
3. **Target/occasion/geography:** independently living older adults with an identified habitual intake problem; NL/FR research cells because evidence and relevant grocery alternatives exist. Daily snack/breakfast adjacency is a hypothesis.
4. **Substitutes/gap:** milk, yogurt, quark, eggs/food, cheap protein RTDs, powders, medical nutrition when appropriate. Claimed gaps in taste, portion or identity have not been established for a defined segment.
5. **Need evidence:** INRAE describes age-related anabolic resistance and research on nutrition composition, but not blanket deficiency or need for a drink. Dutch 2010–12 consumption survey found protein sufficient for most sampled community-dwelling older adults; frail people were underrepresented. Dutch 2021 advice found no convincing/probable general benefit above its recommended intake in healthy older adults. [INRAE 2025](https://www.inrae.fr/actualites/jusquou-peut-vegetaliser-lalimentation-seniors-assurant-leurs-besoins-nutritionnels); [RIVM older-adult survey](https://www.rivm.nl/voedselconsumptiepeiling/overzicht-voedselconsumptiepeilingen/vcp-ouderen-70-jaar-en-ouder-2010-2012); [Dutch protein reference-values advice](https://www.gezondheidsraad.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2021/03/02/voedingsnormen-voor-eiwitten/Advies-Voedingsnormen-voor-eiwitten-referentiewaarden-voor-de-inname-van-eiwitten.pdf).

7. **Competition/difference:** AH vanilla 250 ml/~20.75 g protein/€1.49; Danone 25 g drinkable dairy range. Everyday branding alone is easy to imitate. A genuine sensory/intake problem must be shown against these.
8. **Format:** 200–250 ml bottle, still, easy to open; no validated protein source, recipe or clinical effect. Ambient shelf stability is a desired dependency, not established capability.
9. **Promise/limits:** protein maintenance-of-muscle claim is scientifically more grounded than most exotic cognition blends, subject to legal conditions. It does not establish recovery, frailty prevention, sarcopenia treatment or benefit in already adequate diets.
10. **Repeat:** replace a demonstrably missed protein-rich food occasion; avoid displacing sufficient meals. Actual routine and sustained acceptance unknown.
11. **Benchmarks/channel:** €1.49 Dutch grocery bottle is a difficult reference; independent grocers/active-age groups or pharmacy distribution would require access and clear positioning, none secured.
12. **Dependencies:** protein quality, allergens, viscosity, sedimentation, aseptic/UHT or refrigeration, shelf life, filling MOQ, clinical-category boundary and target-population safety. More manufacturing complexity than a simple acidic drink.
13. **Counterevidence:** healthy older adults are not universally protein-deficient; cheap familiar products already work; marketing “for seniors” may deter rather than help; clinical needs bring a different business and evidence burden.
14. **Invalidators:** no meaningful shortfall; only a medical audience has need; existing bottle is acceptable; sensory or process requirements make price uncompetitive.
15. **Next step:** secondary-data segmentation plus a future comparison of existing bottles/foods among people with observed intake difficulty. No prototype before proving a product-specific deficit in alternatives.

**Judgment:** reject the generic demographic thesis; retain only a tightly evidenced subgroup investigation if new customer evidence appears. It should not displace stronger opportunities solely because protein claims are easier.

### Why the two research priorities rank above the alternatives

**S1 earns the first social investigation because it can stand on an adult drinking experience without an unapproved effect claim.** Local moderation context, exploratory sensory complaints and actual competing retail offers make the question testable. Its advantage is cheap falsifiability and a comparatively conventional product route, not a demonstrated market gap. Strong counterevidence is Mattoni's inexpensive mocktail supply and improving beer substitutes. The unknown is whether a sufficiently frequent, dissatisfied, nonbeer-preferring subgroup will pay enough for a second and subsequent serving. **Reverse the recommendation** if existing products satisfy those consumers, the preferred sensory profile fails blind comparison, or viable pricing loses them. **Before development spend:** document specific recent unsatisfactory occasions, audit their actual venues/retail choices, establish a lawful taste-led proposition and show that plausible channel receipts could cover the relevant outsourced run.

**F1 earns the first functional investigation because the occasion is familiar and potentially frequent, and a conventional formulation can avoid a speculative nootropic stack.** That does not validate the eventual serving: the historical 40–less than 75 mg assessment was unfavourable, while a later 60 mg trial supplies narrower counterevidence. Neither selects a dose or validates this finished drink. CANS+, Vilgain, Olimp and free coffee make the default answer “already served” until a concrete switch reason is found. Unknowns are the segment's desired dose/experience, who pays, real cold availability and repeated paid preference. **Reverse the recommendation** if only an unapproved focus/no-crash promise attracts buyers, CANS+ or ordinary tea already solves the complaint, or the requested effect requires a dose the customer rejects. **Before development spend:** separate caffeine avoiders from dose-conscious caffeine users, verify the exact competitive offer in reachable outlets, test the proposition with no efficacy exaggeration, and build the delivered-cost model around the country/channel actually accessible.

These are **two learning priorities, zero production recommendations**. We do not add S2 merely to fill a top-three list. S2 could outrank either if credible paid dining/portion evidence appears, or France-based channel access proves materially stronger than the assumed Czech access. Conversely, both priorities can rationally end in no launch.

## Counterevidence and rejected directions

- **“EU Hiyo with a buzz” or a social-confidence/anti-anxiety promise:** no demonstrated EU finished-drink efficacy, claim route or safe repeated social-consumption pattern. US supplement studies and commercial “float” language do not transfer. A claim-independent sensory drink remains possible, but it is a different proposition.
- **“First low-caffeine focus drink” / “first office functional drink”:** contradicted by current CANS+ supply and the existing CANS office offer. An underserved *specific* outlet remains a hypothesis, not a channel innovation.
- **Generic premium alcohol-free mocktail:** Czech low-price Mattoni and multi-brand French retail directly challenge both scarcity and price. Different flavour is not yet defensible differentiation.
- **Caffeine-free acute cognitive enhancement through a novel stack:** ordinary-beverage ingredient permissions, dose transfer and claims remain unresolved; no ingredient-presence shortcut establishes efficacy. It cannot inherit F1's caffeine rationale.
- **Daily melatonin social drink or multi-can magnesium relaxation ritual:** poor fit between dose-controlled exposure and casual repeated consumption. A conditional supplement route is not an ordinary social beverage clearance.
- **Generic protein for everyone over a certain age:** demographic growth and nutrient science do not establish a deficit, dissatisfaction or willingness to switch from inexpensive food/bottles. Clinical malnutrition is a distinct regulated proposition, not a convenient route to a stronger marketing claim.
- **Generic gut-health soda or hydration at a desk:** excluded from the shortlist unless a precise functional need, relevant exposure and credible benefit-versus-water/food are demonstrated. We did not broaden the brief to an unrelated soda portfolio. Sports hydration for prolonged exercise remains an established adjacent functional field, but this review found no sufficiently specific startup gap to add an eighth weak hypothesis.
- **CBD launch core:** no qualifying beverage authorisation was established; keep it a separate legal/commercial research hold. THC/intoxicating extensions are outside scope.

Failure evidence is handled conservatively. A missing page, out-of-stock Yoleau/TRIP variant or historical Snoooze listing does not prove closure or cause. The prior [commercial report](../../research/market/02-commercial-traction/report.md) and [trend signal register](../../research/market/26-trends-and-industry-intelligence/data/signals.csv) include genuine negative/pivot reports, including VYBES' US CBD exit and prospective Unwell wind-down; their category, geography and event status are retained. Neither proves that all non-CBD social drinks fail. No verified EU social-tonic failure rate was available, and no absence of failure data is treated as reassuring.

## Unresolved questions and cheapest informative validation

These are **proposed future steps**, not activities conducted or commissioned by this assignment. Current boundaries were respected.

1. **Finish the precise desk check before spending on development.** For S1, map actual nonbeer/no-caffeine alternatives and prices in the intended venues and local delivery areas; for F1, map cold drinks, free coffee/tea, CANS+ and who pays. For S2, check existing small tea/wine/aperitif bottles and by-the-glass options. Record absence only at the observed outlet/time, not nationally. Recheck final claims, exact preparations, category and local requirements with the chosen country brief.
2. **If later authorised, investigate recent behaviour rather than abstract enthusiasm.** Use the repository's [interview guide](../../research/customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/interview-guide.md) and [diary protocol](../../research/customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/diary-study.md), separating social sessions, dinner, home ritual and work breaks. Capture last actual purchase, price, dissatisfaction, frequency, substitutes and no-purchase occasions. Separate users from payors; public forums do not establish segment size.
3. **Require a switch reason that survives realistic alternatives and price.** Compare the proposed experience with current products, an ordinary low-cost substitute and “buy nothing”. For S1 consider total-evening spend and second-serving preference; for F1 compare chosen dose and flavour separately from functional language. A stated preference or signup is still not paid demand. No unvalidated formula should be sold or used in an unsupervised consumption experiment.
4. **Only after that customer gate, seek concrete technical and commercial terms.** One country, one formula, one serving, one flavour initially. Obtain an exact ingredient/category/claim assessment and comparable development/filling/packaging/QA/freight quotes. A supplier accepting a process is not legal clearance; a legal claim is not proof that users notice a benefit.
5. **A later compliant paid pilot must establish repeat and contribution.** Track unique buyers exposed to an in-stock priced offer, first purchases, nonbuyers, refunds, time to second purchase, mature cohort repeat, units per buying occasion, net receipts and delivered contribution. Venue reorders must reflect depletion rather than unsold stock transfers. Compare with the MOQ/shelf-life capacity required by the actual quote. Define any success threshold from those costs and reachable channel capacity, not a borrowed “30% retention” rule.
6. **Select the launch country only after comparable evidence.** Czechia has a possible execution advantage, France a stronger stated variety signal, Germany a substantial but contested substitute market, and the Netherlands a well-served moderation context. A customer/channel result can outweigh all of those priors. Austria and Poland remain useful operating/price comparisons. Do not extrapolate one city's test to the EU.

The unmeasured variables that most affect the decision are: prevalence of the specific unresolved need; repeat frequency at the real price; feasible claims without destroying the proposition; exact ingredient preparation/category status; shelf-stable sensory quality; smallest compatible run; delivered contribution; channel access; and the founder's actual capacity to sell through stock. No formula, positioning, budget, price or launch country is selected by this report.
