# 20 distinctive names that sound like beverage brands

**Created 2026-10-17T13:24:08+02:00 · Europe/Prague**

**Rindigo, Rattlefruit and Puckerbird are my strongest creative directions.** Each has something concrete to remember: a colourful citrus word, a noisy fruit identity, or a tart-tasting character. Zindle and Fizoro offer shorter alternatives with less dependence on a particular fruit profile.

The brief is a finished, nonalcoholic drink, primarily CBD-free, with English-based or invented naming. This round puts taste, sound and character first. None of these names repeats an earlier candidate. All pronunciation and consumer-association judgements are proposals, not test results. Existing uses are recorded in [screening.md](screening.md); “invented” describes the construction proposed here, not a claim that the spelling has never existed.

## What the successful references contribute

These references come from the project's [beverage naming report](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/report.md) and its [commercial evidence register](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/data/commercial-evidence.csv). The report distinguishes established brands from brands with emerging traction; it does not show that a naming style causes sales.

| Reference | Useful naming mechanism | How it shapes this round |
|---|---|---|
| **Pepsi, Kofola, Birell** — established in the research | A distinctive sound can become the identity of a drink without explaining the whole formula. | Compact constructions with a clear spoken rhythm: Zindle, Fizoro, Riffra, Nimzo and Ruzzel. No cola suffixes or close respellings. |
| **OLIPOP, poppi** — established in the research | Playfulness and a sensory association can make a functional proposition feel like a pleasurable drink. | Flavour-led words such as Rindigo and Peeltrick. No “pop” suffix, doubled-p imitation or near-rhyme. |
| **Red Bull, Monster** — established commercial context | A concrete creature or character gives people an image to recognise and remember. | Puckerbird, Rindhound and Thirst Moth use their own imagery around taste and thirst. No stimulant promise or imitation of energy-drink aggression. |
| **Liquid Death** — established in the research | A name with attitude can support a consistent creative voice across packaging and communication. | Rattlefruit, Fruit Static and Sourweather each offer a coherent visual and verbal world. No death vocabulary, skulls or copied confrontational voice. |
| **innocent** — established in the research | A name can establish a tone of voice before a buyer reads the description. | Odd Orchard and Tart Habit have a point of view that can carry into ordinary product copy. |
| **Recess, hiyo** — emerging traction in the research | The identity can belong to a social ritual, rather than claiming a medical outcome. | The social candidates can be shared and ordered as drinks. None promises sociability, a buzz or an immediate mood change. |

The customer and sensory research still matters: the product must win on taste and the actual occasion. **The fruit, tart and bitter names below are deliberate positioning choices.** They are appropriate only if the eventual drink delivers that profile. They should not be used to make an unselected recipe sound established. A plain local beverage descriptor remains necessary.

## The twenty names

The “on the can” examples are hypothetical naming demonstrations, not selected recipes or approved labels.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rindigo** | rin-DIG-oh | **Rind + indigo.** A colourful, citrus-led construction with enough rhythm to work as a name on its own. ## The shortlist I would put on a can first

**Rindigo — strongest compact brand word.** Its proposed root is useful without a long explanation, and it can sit comfortably next to a recognisable flavour. Keep the identity adult; do not build it around the existing children's-book use.

**Rattlefruit — strongest complete beverage personality.** “A little rattle. A lot of fruit.” is an example of the voice, conditional on a fruit-forward product. Loose fruit illustrations and rhythmic type would follow naturally from the name. The drink needs a taste that lives up to it.

**Puckerbird — strongest visual hook.** A sharp bird profile and a tart flavour give the name and product the same job. The appeal is taste and character, not borrowed energy-brand symbolism.

**Zindle — strongest less-specific alternative.** It can accommodate a tea or fruit drink and puts less pressure on the formula. Its weakness is that the brand must build meaning through the actual product.

**Fizoro — strongest direct sparkling cue.** Easy to demonstrate in a simple pack stack, but would make a later still drink harder to explain.

These are creative judgements, not measured rankings. Rindigo and Rattlefruit favour fruit-forward refreshment; Puckerbird favours tartness. If the final direction is a delicate, still work-break tea, test Zindle, Riffra and Nimzo instead of forcing an unsuitable identity onto it.

## Product grounding and practical test

The [customer research](../../research/customers/05-segments-and-occasions/report.md) separates work breaks, everyday refreshment and social participation. The [sensory research](../../research/product/13-flavour-and-sensory/report.md) proposes citrus and peach/tea tests without selecting a final recipe. The [ingredient research](../2026-10-17_10-56-22_claim-led-ingredient-research/report.md) gives sensory-led social soda a credible development route while leaving strong stimulant-free effect promises unresolved. That is why this round has a stronger taste identity and makes no focus, calm or buzz promise.

Start with the relevant product concept, then compare its names using the same pack layout, flavour, serving size and price. Ask people to order it aloud, spell it from hearing, and identify the expected taste, alcohol and caffeine content. Test the name before explaining its construction. Use the existing [naming evaluation protocol](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/supporting/evaluation-and-testing.md). No customer testing or full trademark clearance has been performed here.
