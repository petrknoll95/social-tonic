# CBD, sleep and secondary functional claims

**Research date: 17 October 2026. Geography: EU claims framework; Czech launch classification and enforcement singled out. Status: research, not a cleared formula or final label.** Consumer products remain finished liquids in cans or bottles, including a genuinely ready-to-drink bottled shot where appropriately classified. Ingredient studies and regulatory supplement evaluations are evidence, not product-format benchmarks.

This workstream reread the repository [regulatory update](../../../market/04-eu-czech-competition/supporting/regulatory-and-evidence-update.md) and [benefit/regulatory gates](../../../market/06-demand-and-country-selection/supporting/benefits-benchmarks-and-regulatory-gates.md). It rechecked their decisive official sources and adds a material Czech melatonin constraint plus exact secondary-claim conditions. Primary source metadata is in [the source ledger](cbd-sleep-and-secondary-sources.csv). The Commission's register is an information tool; the underlying acts control. The current consolidation surfaced by EUR-Lex was **20 August 2025**; specific annex entries were checked in indexed official text because direct access intermittently returned a browser-verification page. No unsupported claim of a complete machine-readable register search is made. [CS01][CS01] [CS02][CS02]

## CBD extension: six separate questions

| Question | Evidence and assessment | Decision consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand** | Earlier repository studies found CBD canned/bottled offers but no reliable Czech CBD-only RTD penetration, repeat or incremental willingness-to-pay. This pass does not create a new demand estimate. US THC sales, CBD oils and general desire to relax are different markets. | **Unknown**, not zero. A concept-only adult interview can compare ingredient awareness and incremental preference without implying a product is offered. |
| **Effect** | A 2024 Australian randomized insomnia pilot analyzed 30 completers receiving 150 mg CBD nightly by the sublingual route for two weeks. Most sleep outcomes did not differ from placebo; some secondary wellbeing/objective sleep-efficiency signals occurred. [CS03][CS03] | Neither reliable calm/sleep effects from a 10–25 mg mixed aqueous drink nor productivity benefits are established by this study. Dose, route, population, duration and matrix differ. This is a targeted transferability check, not a systematic evidence review. |
| **Safety** | EFSA's 9 February 2026 provisional intake level is 0.0275 mg/kg/day: **1.925 mg/day for 70 kg**. It applies only to qualifying ≥98% CBD **supplements**, without nanoparticles, subject to production/genotoxicity conditions. Under-25s, pregnancy/lactation and medicine users fall outside the safety conclusion. [CS04][CS04] | Not an RTD safe dose, formulation recommendation or market permission. A beverage's aqueous/nano emulsion cannot be assumed equivalent. |
| **Specific new safety opinion** | EFSA's 18 September 2026 opinion, amended 21 September, positively assessed a specified synthetic ≥98% CBD material, exclusively in MCT-oil supplements, maximum 2 mg/day, for adults over 25 excluding pregnancy/lactation. Safety for people taking medication could not be established. [CS05][CS05] | Corrects any outdated blanket assertion that EFSA has never found CBD safe under specified conditions. It establishes neither beverage safety nor benefit efficacy nor Commission authorization. |
| **EU/national legality** | The current Commission Union-list/update page and targeted authorization searches did **not establish an authorization for the intended CBD beverage**. Article 4 status consultations classify CBD isolate in hemp oil and a CBD extract as novel. Czech SZPI's May 2025 disclosure documents sanctions, withdrawals, recalls and prohibitions for CBD-food novel-food violations since 2021. [CS06][CS06] [CS07][CS07] [CS08][CS08] | Keep CBD outside the main launch. Require a documented route matching exact ingredient, process, food category, use level and country. Do not treat UK proceedings, an EFSA opinion, a pending application, low THC or a retailer listing as permission. This is an absence-of-established-route conclusion, not a claim that all hemp-derived foods are prohibited. |
| **Sourcing** | No supplier has been qualified or quoted. A regional application lead in prior research is not approved beverage supply. | Before procurement, document exact identity/authorization; production traceability; independent CBD/THC/other-cannabinoid assays and limits of quantification; contaminants/residual solvents; emulsion composition/particle characteristics; uniformity; actual-container shelf-life stability. These are proposed diligence requirements, not tests already completed. |

**Claims:** no applicable authorized CBD claim for calm, relaxation, stress, mood, focus, productivity or sleep was established. The novel-food gate and claims gate are independent. A claim authorized for magnesium, for example, cannot be attributed to CBD or to a special CBD–magnesium synergy. If a future CBD drink becomes lawfully marketable and independently satisfies a nutrient claim's conditions, that nutrient claim would in principle remain available for the nutrient; all presentation and ingredient rules would still apply. It cannot rescue an unauthorized ingredient. [CS01][CS01] [CS02][CS02] [CS12][CS12]

## Secondary claims matrix

All rows also require the general rules on truthful presentation, substantiation, quantities and health-claim accompanying information. “Conditional” means an available legal route exists, not that any proposed drink already satisfies it. Scientific efficacy and commercial attractiveness are separate from eligibility.

| Proposed wording / full context | Classification and legal basis | Exact material conditions | Risk, uncertainty and justified alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **“Sleep” on a melatonin can; “Melatonin contributes to the reduction of time taken to fall asleep”** | The latter is an authorized specific health claim under 432/2012. “Sleep” is broader and implies a benefit in this context. [CS02][CS02] | Food must contain **1 mg melatonin per quantified portion**; tell consumers the beneficial effect is obtained by consuming 1 mg close to bedtime. Ingredient/category permission remains separate. | **High for an ordinary Czech drink:** current SÚKL decision practice treats orally administered products with **≥0.5 mg/day** as medicines by function. The 1 mg claim threshold therefore does not supply an ordinary Czech-food route. Do not extrapolate to treating insomnia, deeper sleep, sleep quality or waking refreshed. [CS09][CS09] |
| **“Sleep” on a <0.5 mg melatonin drink or botanical evening drink** | A benefit-bearing name can be an implied health claim; the 1 mg melatonin claim condition is unmet. Botanical transitional rules need a separate exact-claim review. [CS02][CS02] [CS12][CS12] | SÚKL's <0.5 mg statement only addresses medicine classification by this functional threshold; it does not authorize every food, label or sleep claim. Commission clarification confirms a different melatonin condition requires claim authorization. [CS09][CS09] [CS10][CS10] | Use a provisional occasion such as **“A sparkling drink for your evening ritual”** plus accurate flavour/serving information. Sleep imagery, pill-like directions or testimonials can reverse the lower-risk assessment. A ready-to-drink shot does not escape medicine classification. |

[CS11][CS11] | Mostly-water composition does not automatically qualify a flavoured functional beverage. It is not evidence of special focus performance. Live count must survive to end of shelf life; species/strain and matrix need verification. |
| **“CBD calm”; “CBD focus”; “sleep with CBD”; “CBD for stress relief”** | Specific or implied health claims; no applicable CBD authorization established; illness references can become medicinal. [CS01][CS01] [CS12][CS12] | Ingredient permission, finished-food safety and claims route each required; none is supplied by CBD presence or a nutrient footnote. | **Do not launch these promises.** For internal concept research, describe an optional CBD formulation as research-only with no availability claim. |
| **“With CBD” / “CBD-free” / “THC-free” as the difference between core and extension** | Composition/absence wording needs full-context classification; promotional “contains CBD” can itself imply beneficial nutrition/physiology and is not an automatic exemption. ## Practical distinction between the core and a possible extension

For a **CBD-free evening core**, the defensible initial hierarchy is flavour and beverage identity → adult occasion → verified composition → only an independently eligible, correctly attributed nutrient claim. An illustrative test line is “Sparkling citrus drink. For evenings with friends.” It remains subject to the whole visual and advertising context; adding “calm”, a promise to reduce inhibition or a testimonial about sleep changes the assessment.

A **CBD extension remains a gated research option**, not a launch recommendation. In a future lawful route, the factual variant distinction could be ingredient identity and verified amount, accompanied by the exact applicable restrictions. It cannot be presented as the core product plus a more powerful calming/sleep effect without an independent lawful claim basis. A shared magnesium claim must say what magnesium does and must not imply CBD causes it. Commercial differentiation may therefore be weaker than US CBD positioning suggests. This is an analyst inference from the separate authorization/claims gates, not a consumer-research result.

## Final review questions for this workstream

1. Is the finished can/bottle an ordinary beverage, food supplement or medicinal product in each intended country? Has Czech melatonin practice been applied to the **daily dose**, including multiple servings?
2. Has every ingredient's exact form/process and beverage-use permission been documented before a physical pilot? For CBD, is there an actual applicable authorization rather than an application or safety opinion?
3. Do the claimed substance, measured shelf-life quantity, serving instructions and target population match the specific legal row? Check electrolyte osmolality, source/high nutrition thresholds and fibre identity separately.
4. Does the full pack/ad/retailer page turn an occasion phrase into a physiological promise? Does an asterisk, nutrient claim or testimonial misleadingly attribute a benefit to CBD or the whole blend?
5. Has the efficacy argument been limited to the studied ingredient/dose/route/population/endpoints? Has demand remained unknown where only a listing or anecdote exists?
