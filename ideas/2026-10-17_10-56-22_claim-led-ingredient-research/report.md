# Claim-led ingredient research for an EU ready-to-drink soda

**Created:** 2026-10-17T10:56:22+02:00 · **Europe/Prague (UTC+02:00)**

**Evidence access/cut-off:** 17 October 2026

**Scope:** ordinary non-alcoholic canned/bottled drinks; Czech startup using outsourced development/manufacture. CBD-free core; CBD assessed separately. No formula, serving, claim, supplier or launch has been approved.

## Findings that should drive development

**This review did not establish an ingredient that can honestly be presented as a dependable, noticeable, sustained, caffeine-free and stimulant-free replacement for caffeine in a casual soda for healthy, rested adults.** That is a conclusion about the evidence and product-transfer requirements reviewed, not proof that such a product can never exist.

- **Paraxanthine is a stimulant substitution**, with preliminary, heavily sponsored human evidence and an unresolved EU ordinary-beverage authorisation gate. Some favourable headlines conceal null overall analyses. It cannot presently anchor an EU “caffeine-free energy without the drawbacks” launch. [EN04](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8622427/); [EN05](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8708375/); [EN06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11089923/); [EU10](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/330ca889-27cc-4db5-835b-a0670180b85a_en?filename=novel-food_sum_ongoing-app_2022-90305.pdf)
- **Theobromine has unusually relevant negative evidence:** direct beverage comparisons did not reproduce caffeine's mood/vigilance benefits. A long residence time does not establish useful lasting energy. Taurine likewise lacks a coherent independent alertness effect despite its energy-drink association. [EN02](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23764688/); [EN03](https://palmerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/Baggott-et-al-Psychopharm-2013.pdf); [EN12](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0091305712001980); [EN13](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0195666313000044); [EN14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4226910/)
- **Theanine is a calm/attention research candidate, not an energy ingredient by default.** Selected attention signals deserve consideration; the current synthesis did not find a fatigue benefit. Positive caffeine-combination results cannot automatically be attributed to theanine. Several stress trials have null placebo-controlled subjective outcomes. [CA01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18006208/); [CA02](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17891480/); [CA07](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8475422/); [CA08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11263523/); [CA24](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-026-03727-9)
- **Tyrosine, creatine, rhodiola and ginseng address narrower questions.** Protection during sleep loss or demanding tasks, selected fatigue outcomes and chronic supplementation are different from a felt boost after one normal drink. Creatine's cognitive-function claim was formally refused in the EU in May 2026. [EN07](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7794222/); [EN08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3863934/); [EN09](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10647179/); [EN10](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54249-9); [EN11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13119191/); [EN15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19016404/); [EN16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11081987/); [EN17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15982990/); [EN18](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1028415X.2001.11747370); [EN19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16401645/); [EU12](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ%3AL_202601118)
- **A credible social drink can deliver taste, ritual and inclusion without pharmacological disinhibition.** No reviewed candidate establishes alcohol-equivalent social effects. There is direct interpersonal evidence for repeated tryptophan, but it is population-specific and contradictory; it does not support an acute social tonic. [CA21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5462438/); [CA22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1488902/); [CA23](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4571634/)
- **The most tractable functional claim routes are nutritional, with narrower meaning.** Qualifying magnesium/B-vitamin products may communicate specific normal-function benefits; that does not show that a replete person will feel calmer or more energetic. Sensory appeal must carry the immediate experience unless a different effect is demonstrated and lawfully communicable. [EU07](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/432/2025-08-20/eng); [LE01](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6298677/); [LE02](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00213-010-1870-3.pdf)

**Analytical recommendation:** prioritise a sensory-led social drink or a clearly described nutritional work-break drink. Retain preparation-specific lemon-balm/theanine research as conditional work, without promising a felt effect. Keep a transparent caffeine-containing comparator if actual acute alertness is essential. Do not force a caffeine-free “boost” concept to survive an unfavourable evidence review.

## Method and the four layers

Six complementary sub-agents covered energy/alertness, calm/social evidence, consumer benchmarks, EU/Czech legality/safety, formulation and independent review; concurrency limits required two waves. The lead owned shared files, integration and publication. This is a targeted, current desk review, **not a preregistered systematic review or regulatory clearance**. Searches combined exact compounds/preparations with human randomisation, fatigue/attention/stress/social outcomes, negative findings, food stability and official authorisation terms. Full primary texts were preferred; abstracts, supplier material, inaccessible details and a clearly marked 2026 preprint retain their limitations in the ledger.

| Layer | What counts | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketed perception** | A named brand's wording, label, targeted survey or accurately bounded review observation | Physiological efficacy, representative awareness, legality or purchase causation |
| **Subjectively experienced effect** | Controlled energy/calm/fatigue ratings; separately labelled uncontrolled self-reports | Objective performance or causality when uncontrolled; repeatability from one anecdote |
| **Demonstrated effect** | A credible between-group human result for the specified preparation, population and outcome | Consumer noticeability, a different preparation/route, all-day function or legal claim permission |
| **Permissible communication** | Applicable law/authorisation and conditions for the exact ingredient, category and finished product | Efficacy beyond the authorised wording or a guarantee that consumers feel it |

Subjective outcomes can be commercially important. They require sound controls just as objective tests do. Cortisol, EEG, blood flow and neurotransmitter mechanisms are not substitutes for feeling calmer, staying attentive or enjoying conversation. Statistical significance is not a minimum noticeable difference. A treatment improving from its own baseline while placebo does not reach significance is not itself a significant treatment difference.

Study exposures are recorded to assess transfer, **not prescribed as a drink formula**. No experiments, supplier contacts, purchases or recruitment occurred. There are no measured prototype tastes, prices, safe serving counts or approved labels.

## Phase 1: define the promised experience

The [full claim-to-experience map](supporting/claim-experience-map.md) specifies consumer interpretation, measurable outcomes, single-drink expectations, timing questions, repeat-use conflict and legal risk for every priority promise and modifier. The essential distinctions are:

| Desired promise | What must actually improve | Main trap |
|---|---|---|
| Caffeine-free energy boost | Felt energy/alertness soon after a genuinely caffeine-free serving; objective vigilance if claimed | Calling another stimulant “stimulant-free”; substituting metabolism language for sensation |
| Natural sustained energy | Provenance plus a meaningful benefit across a defined period | Inferring duration from half-life or weeks of supplementation; assuming plant-derived is safer |
| Alertness without overstimulation | Wakefulness/vigilance **and** acceptable anxiety, jitters, cardiovascular and sleep outcomes | Small trial with no significant harm is not evidence of zero harm |
| Clear-headed focus | Accurate attention/task performance, separately from wakefulness and motivation | Faster responses alone may trade off with accuracy or leave real productivity unchanged |
| Alcohol-free socialisation | Taste, participation, ordering ease and satisfying ritual without alcohol | Treating inclusion as pharmacological disinhibition |
| Relaxed confidence/social ease | Less self-consciousness and better interaction in the actual social context | Extrapolating from sleep, clinical anxiety or laboratory arithmetic stress |
| Natural calm | Reduced tension with a defensible source/process description | A botanical story is not controlled evidence or a safe-intake assessment |
| Unwinding without sedation | Relaxation with preserved wakefulness and psychomotor function | Equating sleepiness with desirable calm, or claiming driving compatibility |

“Lasting”, “no crash”, “without jitters”, “non-drowsy” and “natural” each add a separate substantiation question. Onset and duration remain **research questions** unless the studies establish them; none is invented as a product specification. Repeated consumption is part of the intended occasion, so a plausible one-serving effect is insufficient when several cans create an exposure or impairment problem.

The broad screen spans methylxanthines/related stimulants, amino acids, cellular-energy approaches, standardised botanicals, fungi, nutrients, fuel/hydration and sensory botanicals. Sixteen routes were selected for depth because they combine relevant evidence, benchmark usage, plausible liquid delivery or a decisive reason to reject a tempting proposition. ### Identity, origin and evidence transfer

A molecule occurring in nature, its industrial manufacturing method and its consumer-naturalness story are different facts. Pure caffeine from tea and synthetic caffeine have the same chemical identity at matched purity; “natural” does not establish a smoother effect. Verified high-purity L-theanine materials may be pharmacologically more comparable than two botanical extracts, yet their **legal process history** can differ. Fermented theanine, tea extraction and enzymatic synthesis must therefore be recorded separately. [EU08](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/1e468cb7-af61-4aa2-8d4f-976f9783dbae_en?filename=novel-food_consult-status_l-teanina.pdf); [EU09](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a498e343-3403-427d-a95d-6a8fa4226c3b_en?filename=novel-food_sum_ongoing-app_2024-15277.pdf); [CA06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6836118/)

For plants/fungi, specify species, part, solvent, extraction ratio, carrier and marker assay. G115 is not generic “ginseng”; SHR-5 is not every 3% rosavins product; saffron stigma is not petals or isolated safranal; fruiting body is not mycelium. Equal marker percentages or a “10:1” description do not establish equal composition, bioavailability or clinical effect. Trademarked study material can be transferred to a generic alternative only with a defensible comparability dossier, not by matching the ingredient name. [EN22](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-l-rhizoma-et-radix-revision-1_en.pdf); [EN28](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/final-assessment-report-panax-ginseng-camey-radix-revision-1_en.pdf); [CA13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28735826/); [CA14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7882499/); [CA15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10346743/); [CA19](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10675414/); [LE30](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1405796/full)

## Phase 4: what could replace caffeine?

### The reference is a measured effect, not the word “energy”

Caffeine can improve subjective arousal and vigilance; benefits are not restricted to withdrawal reversal, because a controlled study in light/nondependent users also found them. It does not improve every cognitive task: memory performance worsened in that study. Habitual use, abstinence, sleep debt, baseline anxiety and task difficulty matter. A well-designed comparison must record them. [EN01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16541243/)

EFSA describes effects beginning roughly 15–30 minutes after consumption and lasting for hours, with substantial metabolic variation. That is a reference range, not a guaranteed onset or a promise that a whole workday improves. Caffeine can disturb sleep and increase anxiety; tolerance and withdrawal can complicate repeated-use experience. EFSA's healthy-adult assessment of single intakes up to 200 mg and habitual intake up to 400 mg/day is **not a product dose recommendation**, a guarantee for every person, or a framework to copy to paraxanthine. Even 100 mg close to bedtime can affect sleep in some adults. Pregnancy and other vulnerable groups require different assessment. [EN20](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine); [EU22](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine)

Scientific support also differs from claim permission. A favourable caffeine efficacy opinion is not an authorised EU health claim. This review did not establish an applicable authorised alertness claim for the proposed drink; exact wording and any transitional basis need separate legal review. [EU32](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20160701IPR34496/parliament-vetoes-energy-drink-alertness-claims); [EU33](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/1411/oj)

This is evidence about that low-caffeine formulation, not a universal disproof of every caffeine-free route. [RV01](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7049526/)

### Direct answers to the central questions

1. **Genuinely caffeine-free, noticeable energy?** Context-dependent improvements exist—for example selected task protection during sleep loss, or fatigue relief when a relevant deficiency is corrected. The reviewed evidence does not establish a reliable, noticeable general-population boost from a feasible EU soda. Paraxanthine may be chemically caffeine-free yet remains another stimulant, with preliminary efficacy and an unresolved food route.
2. **What is actually lasting?** A significant contrast at one late test is not continuous benefit. Paraxanthine's six-hour observation window, creatine's overnight task protection, and a four-week botanical regimen describe different things. None establishes a smooth, all-day, no-crash soda experience. A claim must define an interval and demonstrate meaningful benefit across it, including the period after it ends.
3. **Which are stimulant replacements?** Paraxanthine; potentially theacrine/methylliberine proposals, whose isolated evidence and EU food route are weaker here. Tea, mate and guarana generally supply caffeine and do not solve the caffeine-free brief. A blend containing caffeine cannot prove its other ingredients replace it. [LE21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41950524/); [LE22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26610558/); [LE23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35103121/)
4. **Which deliver a different job?** Nutrients support normal function; carbohydrate provides fuel; fluid addresses hydration; tyrosine/creatine may protect specific performance under challenge; some botanical trials concern repeated stress/fatigue management. These can be worthwhile jobs without being caffeine equivalents.
5. **Where is “natural energy” chiefly a story?** When the argument is plant origin, ATP/mitochondrial mechanism, normal metabolism, branded blend marketing or an uncontrolled review without the relevant acute outcome. Natural sourcing alone predicts neither efficacy nor safety. “Synthetic” likewise does not imply ineffective or unsafe.
6. **What would support comparability?** A preregistered, adequately powered, blinded finished-drink study against both a sensory-matched placebo and a defined caffeine comparator, in the intended consumers, with prespecified meaningful effect margins. It should measure energy/fatigue and attention separately, adverse arousal and subsequent sleep, and repeated use. An equivalence/non-inferiority claim requires its own design and confidence-interval criterion; a nonsignificant difference is insufficient. Ingredient legality, safe exposure and claim permission must already be addressed.

## Phase 5: social ease, calm and non-sedating unwinding

The most defensible immediate social benefit is a satisfying drink and participation in the occasion. Experimental ritual research found enjoyment/valuation effects in foods and lemonade; it did not establish social confidence or pharmacological disinhibition. A label-expectancy experiment changed reported intoxication/confidence around an actual alcohol-and-energy drink, which cannot be transferred as proof that a sober tonic reproduces alcohol. These findings justify testing presentation and ritual honestly. [CP18](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-af41-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b); [CP19](https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1016/j.jcps.2017.03.003)

Direct alcohol-group research measured observable conversation and facial behaviour in 720 participants. Its alcohol effects are a reference for what “social bonding” evidence could look like, not a result transferable to another ingredient. Direct non-alcohol ingredient evidence is unusually limited: repeated tryptophan reduced self-reported quarrelsomeness in a selected high-quarrelsomeness sample, whereas another selected sample showed greater quarrelsomeness and lower agreeableness at home. Neither supports first-drink social confidence. [CA21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5462438/); [CA22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1488902/); [CA23](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4571634/)

### Theanine deserves a precise, mixed conclusion

Theanine alone, caffeine alone and their combination have been separated in factorial trials. Some combination outcomes are positive; that does not identify theanine as the cause of every improvement. One trial found isolated theanine increased headache and worsened serial subtraction; another did not find the expected alertness/jitter benefit. A 2026 synthesis reports a positive choice-reaction-time effect (SMD 0.51, 95% CI 0.25–0.77), a smaller bias-sensitive acute-stress signal, and **no fatigue improvement**. Its publisher abstract was reviewed; the full subscription methods were not inspected. [CA01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18006208/); [CA02](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17891480/); [CA24](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-026-03727-9)

The stress story also needs its nulls. In the 2021 AlphaWave trial, EEG-related results did not establish a between-group subjective stress/anxiety improvement. In a 28-day 2024 trial, perceived stress and cognition did not outperform placebo, and some DASS outcomes favoured placebo. The 2019 nightly-use study does not establish a whole-group placebo-controlled cognitive advantage. A positive beverage study used other ingredients and selected stress responders, preventing attribution to theanine alone. [CA05](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4728665/); [CA06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6836118/); [CA07](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8475422/); [CA08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11263523/)

**Analytical judgment:** a legally qualified theanine material could be a research candidate for a gentle attention/calm proposition. A noticeable caffeine-free experience, predictable onset, several hours of benefit, non-drowsiness and no-jitters are not established finished-drink promises. “Calm focus” still needs substantiation and legal review; it is not a free workaround for the word “energy.”

## EU/Czech feasibility: ingredient, claim and label are separate gates

The [regulatory workstream](supporting/workstreams/regulatory-findings.md) records official sources and a 16-route status table. Nothing in this report clears a finished SKU. The Novel Food catalogue is nonbinding, an application is not authorisation, and supplement permission is not ordinary-beverage permission. Exact food history, process and category are decisive. [EU01](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food_en); [EU02](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/consultation-process-novel-food-status_en); [EU03](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/authorisations/union-list-novel-foods_en); [EU04](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/novel-food-status-catalogue_en)

Important current findings:

- The March 2026 Italian decision concerns a particular high-purity, non-GM *E. coli* fermentation-derived theanine and finds it novel. It does not classify all theanine. The reviewed Taiyo application is supplement-only. [EU08](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/1e468cb7-af61-4aa2-8d4f-976f9783dbae_en?filename=novel-food_consult-status_l-teanina.pdf); [EU09](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a498e343-3403-427d-a95d-6a8fa4226c3b_en?filename=novel-food_sum_ongoing-app_2024-15277.pdf)
- The September 2026 Czech mushroom decision supports specified aqueous fruiting-body processes and comparable composition, including food use for lion's mane, reishi and shiitake. It does not cover every hydroethanolic extract, enriched fraction or mycelium; chaga has a different supplement-only conclusion. [EU18](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/05e13eb0-7bc0-4c94-b2ac-180ad3f43a05_en?filename=novel-food_consult-status_2006-8614745.pdf)
- Defined creatine monohydrate has non-novel status evidence, while its improved-cognitive-function claim was refused in Regulation 2026/1118. Ingredient eligibility and claim refusal can coexist. [EU02](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/consultation-process-novel-food-status_en); [EU12](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ%3AL_202601118)
- Theobromine's flavouring entry includes a 100 mg/kg condition for nonalcoholic beverages. That is not general functional enrichment clearance, a study-equivalent dose or a safe maximum for this drink. [EU11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2018/1482/oj)
- CJEU C-386/23 makes botanical psychological-claim transitions an exact legal question. “On hold” is not blanket permission; nor does the judgment make every botanical phrase universally prohibited. The applicable Article 28 route and historical conditions need evidence. A startup cannot replace this analysis with a supplier's phrase list. [EU15](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A62023CJ0386); [EU16](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2025-04/cp250057en.pdf); [EU17](https://ec.europa.eu/food/food-feed-portal/backend/claims/files/claims_pending.pdf)
- Specified citicoline and magnesium L-threonate permissions do not create general soda routes. Current CBD opinions address tightly defined supplements, not an ordinary CBD social soda. [EU29](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2024/2694/oj); [EU41](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2014/423/oj); [EU42](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/news/provisional-safe-level-cannabidiol-novel-food); [EU43](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10154)

### Nutritional claims are useful but narrower

Eligible magnesium products can use specified normal-function and fatigue claims; pantothenic acid has a normal-mental-performance claim. Other B vitamins have their own precise claims; they are not interchangeable. These are normal nutrition claims, not guarantees of immediate perceived effects. Typically, the source condition for vitamins/minerals in beverages is a significant amount based on 7.5% NRV per 100 mL, or the applicable 15% per genuine single-portion package route. Full conditions, retained content, intended consumption and label context must be checked. These thresholds are eligibility arithmetic, not optimum doses. [EU07](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/432/2025-08-20/eng); [EU19](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/clanek/obohacene-potraviny.aspx); [EU31](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2011/1169/2025-04-01/eng)

Safety does not follow automatically from claim eligibility. The original EFSA B6 opinion sets an adult upper level of **12 mg/day from all sources**. The SCF magnesium upper level is **250 mg/day for the relevant readily dissociable salts/MgO in supplements, water, or added to foods/beverages**, excluding naturally occurring food magnesium; it is not a per-can recommendation or an absolute toxicity boundary. Multi-SKU intake, supplements and background consumption matter. A study using higher exposure is not a legal or safety instruction. [EU20](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2023.8006); [EU21](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/efsa_rep/blobserver_assets/ndatolerableuil.pdf)

“Natural”, “lasting”, “non-drowsy”, “without jitters” and “no crash” need their own substantiation and applicable wording review. Czech naturalness rules and EU non-misleading-information requirements mean neither natural flavour nor plant origin automatically supports calling the entire functional formulation natural. [EU06](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2006/1924/2014-12-13); [EU34](https://mze.gov.cz/public/portal/mze/legislativa/vap87546-131)

## Beverage feasibility: liquid delivery is only the first test

The [formulation review](supporting/workstreams/formulation-findings.md) distinguishes primary experiments (**P**), supplier assertions (**S**) and analytical test questions (**I**). Every candidate's actual taste, shelf life, carbonation/pack compatibility and cost-in-use remain untested; public defensible prices were not established, so **quote required** applies throughout.

The clearest constraints are creatine degradation/crystallisation in aqueous systems, gram-load tyrosine dissolution, and purified theobromine's limited plain-water solubility. Creatine citrate is not a demonstrated fix; the chemistry caveat below approximately pH 2.5 does not create a usable soda process. A dose swallowed in orange juice may have been suspended rather than dissolved. [FM04](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4923/13/8/1118); [FM06](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/tyrosine); [FM07](https://doi.org/10.1021/je9501853); [FM08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2750587/); [FM09](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19635041/); [FM10](https://www.fda.gov/media/143525/download?attachment=)

There are positive but bounded transfer leads. A defined lemon-balm extract has been administered in a drink; GABA has primary fruit-juice heat/storage research; theanine and rhodiola suppliers provide beverage-oriented claims; magnesium salts have commercial specifications. None proves the intended can's sensory acceptance, end-of-life composition or benefit. Ginseng processing can change the marker profile rather than simply reduce a single “active” total. B-vitamin retention is form- and matrix-specific, including interactions involving B12 and ascorbic acid. [FM02](https://www.taiyointernational.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/ST_Data-Beverage_2015.pdf); [FM03](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FDA-2014-S-0023-0044/attachment_1.pdf); [FM13](https://www.plthealth.com/resources/plt-introduces-water-soluble-rhodiola-rosea-ingredient-into-the-north-american-market); [FM14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30263712/); [FM16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4245564/); [FM20](https://doi.org/10.5891/jafps.34.145); [FM23](https://www.jungbunzlauer.com/ingredients/magnesium-lactate/); [FM24](https://www.jungbunzlauer.com/ingredients/trimagnesium-citrate/); [FM25](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.53.688); [FM26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8650259/)

The development order should be: exact material and legal basis → exposure/safety rationale → sensory and process screening → composition retention and package testing → justified human experience study. The co-packer/process authority must set a safe manufacturing process; a botanical's preferred temperature or pH cannot dictate microbiological safety. No ingredient blend earns a synergy claim from the sum of its component studies.

## Phase 6: six qualified product directions

These are **analytical judgments and research directions**, not approved recipes or launch recommendations. None requires accepting an unsupported caffeine substitute.

### 1. Sensory-led social soda — strongest immediate development fit

**Desired experience:** a satisfying adult drink for meals, meetings and social rounds, with an intentional pour and enjoyable flavour complexity. **Routes:** conventional food/flavour preparations such as culinary citrus, herbs and appropriately assessed bitter notes; no active is required to make the first prototype meaningful. Sensory ingredients carry taste and recognition; ritual/occasion can support participation. Controlled ingredient-based disinhibition is not established.

**Claim territory:** factual alcohol/caffeine composition if analytically and legally substantiated, flavour and occasion descriptions; avoid confidence, anxiety-treatment or alcohol-equivalence language. **Dependencies:** full-serving acceptance, actual alcohol/caffeine specifications, conventional-material permission, acidity/carbonation and pack/process validation. **Truth now versus testing:** the experience can be described as a design intention; enjoyment, inclusion and repeat preference require consumer testing. **Strongest failure risk:** it tastes good but does not justify its price or displace the drink people already choose. [CP18](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-af41-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b); [CP19](https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1016/j.jcps.2017.03.003); [CA21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5462438/)

### 2. Caffeine-free pause drink — conditional active research

**Desired experience:** a pleasant pause while remaining mentally available. **Routes:** a lawful exact theanine material and/or a matched lemon-balm preparation, each investigated separately before any blend inference. Herb aroma may support sensory identity; selected human calm/attention findings supply a research hypothesis, not a promise. Consumer recognition remains unmeasured.

**Claim territory:** flavour, ritual and verified caffeine absence; psychological benefit wording remains subject to exact legal permission. **Dependencies:** theanine source/category gate, extract equivalence, sedation/interaction assessment, several-can exposure and retained composition. **Truth now versus testing:** some ingredient studies exist, with meaningful nulls; noticeable calm, preserved performance and duration need a sensory-matched finished-product trial. **Strongest failure risk:** any effect is too small or inconsistent to notice, while the permitted wording cannot support the proposed premium. [CA01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18006208/); [CA07](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8475422/); [CA08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11263523/); [CA11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4245564/); [CA12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15272110/); [EU08](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/1e468cb7-af61-4aa2-8d4f-976f9783dbae_en?filename=novel-food_consult-status_l-teanina.pdf); [EU09](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a498e343-3403-427d-a95d-6a8fa4226c3b_en?filename=novel-food_sum_ongoing-app_2024-15277.pdf)

### 3. Nutritional work-break soda — narrower, more feasible function

**Desired experience:** refreshment with clearly explained normal-function nutrition. **Routes:** permitted magnesium salt and/or individually selected B vitamins, with B5 a possible mental-performance claim route. Taste carries the immediate experience; nutrient recognition is a hypothesis to test. Demonstrated nutritional roles do not establish acute enhancement in replete people.

**Claim territory:** exact authorised nutrient wording and conditions; distinguish normal mental performance, energy metabolism and reduced tiredness. **Dependencies:** elemental/vitamer specifications, end-of-life assays, pleasant mineral/premix profile and aggregate intake across cans/SKUs. **Truth now versus testing:** authorised roles can be communicated by a qualifying finished product; an immediate felt boost cannot presently be promised. **Strongest failure risk:** compliant language still leads consumers to expect stimulation the drink does not deliver. [EU07](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/432/2025-08-20/eng); [EU19](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/clanek/obohacene-potraviny.aspx); [EU20](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2023.8006); [EU21](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/efsa_rep/blobserver_assets/ndatolerableuil.pdf); [LE01](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6298677/); [LE02](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00213-010-1870-3.pdf)

### 4. Occasion-specific fuel/fluid drink — a different energy job

**Desired experience:** provide carbohydrate and fluid during an occasion that actually needs them, rather than claim universal desk-work stimulation. **Routes:** conventional carbohydrates and, only where justified by the occasion and conditions, electrolytes. Sweetness/body are sensory contributions; calories/fluid are real delivery functions. Baseline hydration/fuelling determines relevance.

**Claim territory:** factual nutrition and narrowly applicable authorised exercise/carbohydrate-electrolyte claims after exact assessment; no generic lasting-alertness language. **Dependencies:** identify a real underfuelled/sweaty use case, assess sugars, dental exposure, osmotic/sensory load and compare with ordinary food/water. **Truth now versus testing:** composition can be verified; cognitive enhancement, no crash or better-than-water benefit requires its own evidence. **Strongest failure risk:** sugar/calorie trade-offs conflict with the customer's reason for choosing the soda. [LE03](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4265209/); [LE04](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190027/); [EU30](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2015/7/oj/eng/pdf)

### 5. Transparent caffeine-containing alertness drink — fallback outside the caffeine-free promise

**Desired experience:** actual acute alertness where consumers knowingly choose caffeine. **Routes:** a specified caffeine source; any theanine addition must justify itself against caffeine alone. Source origin can support traceability but does not establish gentler pharmacology. Caffeine supplies the strongest relevant ingredient evidence; flavour and brand supply enjoyment.

**Claim territory:** accurate composition, required warnings and only legally justified benefit communication. Scientific efficacy does not bypass the unresolved exact alertness-claim route. **Dependencies:** amount selection through a full safety/occasion assessment, sleep timing, cumulative intake, taste and validated content. **Truth now versus testing:** caffeine is a credible ingredient reference; no-jitters/no-crash or superior blended focus remains unproven. **Strongest failure risk:** it misses the caffeine-avoiding audience and enters a crowded category without meaningful differentiation. [EN01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16541243/); [EN20](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine); [CA02](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17891480/); [EU22](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine); [EU32](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20160701IPR34496/parliament-vetoes-energy-drink-alertness-claims); [EU33](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/1411/oj)

### 6. Repeated-use mood ritual with specified saffron — research reserve

**Desired experience:** a daily wellbeing ritual over weeks, explicitly distinct from first-can sociability. **Route:** a precisely matched, legally qualified stigma extract; colour/aroma may provide sensory identity, while selected repeated-use studies provide a limited function hypothesis. Do not substitute isolated safranal or a different saffron fraction.

**Claim territory:** currently no cleared mood/stress promise for this product; the botanical transition and any authorisation route require specialist review. **Dependencies:** exact extract legality, adulteration control, safety under repeated casual access, marker retention, cost and adherence. **Truth now versus testing:** selected ingredient trials exist alongside primary nulls; durable meaningful benefit and superiority to a pleasant ritual alone require replication. **Strongest failure risk:** weeks-long uncertain benefit and restricted communication make a casual soda the wrong delivery/business model. [CA13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28735826/); [CA14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7882499/); [CA15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10346743/); [EU15](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A62023CJ0386); [FM17](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2621.1993.tb06116.x); [FM18](https://www.spkx.net.cn/EN/10.7506/spkx1002-6630-201205016); [FM19](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/5f85d0c81b6f5da2719b6b03/629685544b566312c0b6a95d_313_Brochure%20Affron%20USA_Print%20version.pdf)

Ashwagandha, paraxanthine, GABA, creatine cognition and CBD are deliberately not forced into a launch direction. Their barriers differ; the rejection/defer register explains each rather than treating “unresolved” as “illegal.”

## What to resolve next, before claiming an effect

1. Choose the consumer job and target population, then define a meaningful outcome and time window. Separately test whether people understand nutritional wording as an immediate boost; misleading takeaways matter even when individual words are authorised.
2. Select an exact material dossier, ordinary-food basis and provisional label territory. Resolve national botanical/medicinal boundaries and cumulative exposure before a manufacturer is asked to copy a study dose.
3. Commission bench and shelf-life work only after those gates; compare the active drink with an equally appealing base. Record taste, aroma, colour, haze/sediment, full-serving acceptance, process retention and actual package performance.
4. If an experience claim remains commercially necessary, plan—not run here—a preregistered finished-drink study with appropriate comparator, blinding assessment, prespecified meaningful difference, subjective and objective endpoints, adverse effects and later sleep. Repeated-use claims require repeated-use evidence, not extrapolation from one serving.
5. For social concepts, measure participation, enjoyment and interaction directly. Do not call a cortisol change social confidence. For stimulant comparisons, quantify benefit and harms over the same interval; for combinations, use component controls if attributing synergy.
