# 20 brand ideas for the next social drink

**Created:** 2026-10-17T12:55:03+02:00 · Europe/Prague

**Based on:** [Beverage naming research, 17 October 2026](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/report.md) and its [naming brief](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/supporting/naming-brief.md).

**Status:** creative exploration, with a limited public-web collision screen. No consumer testing or trademark clearance.

## Where I would start

**Spoluno, Vlnora and Přisedni** are my first three to explore. They offer different answers to the brief: an expandable social identity, a sensory identity, and a direct Czech invitation. **Malý rozruch** is the bolder challenger; **Dílna doušků** is the taste-and-craft challenger. These are editorial choices, not measured winners.

The 20 concepts below are independently developed proposals for this product. “Original” describes the creative work, not a guarantee that nobody has used a word or similar mark. Early names with conspicuous collisions were removed; the [screening notes](screening.md) retain those decisions and the shortlisted domain checks.

The working product is a Czech-founded, CBD-free, nonalcoholic drink in a can or bottle. Formula, audience and launch occasion remain open. To make the ideas concrete, most examples use an **adult sparkling soft drink** as a provisional design canvas. Carbonation, flavours and copy are concepts to develop, not approved product specifications. Each idea states the occasion it would suit; an evening social drink and a functional daytime drink should still be evaluated separately.

The report's central principle shapes this work: **let the brand provide identity and let a plain descriptor explain the drink.** None of these names depends on a claim of calm, focus, intoxication, sleep or an ingredient that has not been selected. Czech meaning is prioritised, with several coined and English-language challengers to test for expansion. Pronunciations, associations and likely misunderstandings below are desk hypotheses.

## The 20 concepts

### 01. Mezidoba

**Idea:** Make the in-between part of the day worth choosing. The name is built around Czech *mezi* and *doba*—between and time/period. Its intended meaning is an interval with its own value, rather than a drink that changes your mental state.

- **Brand world:** editorial, spacious and observant; two broad colour fields with a narrow, contrasting space between them. A good fit for café fridges and an afternoon break, with an alternative evening interpretation to test separately.
- **Working line:** „Mezi plány je místo.“ — There is room between plans.
- **Pack example:** **MEZIDOBA / grapefruit / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *ME-zi-do-ba*. No accents in the name; English speakers may move the stress. Test „Dám si Mezidobu“ and whether listeners write *mezidobí* instead.
- **Tradeoff:** thoughtful and relevant, but potentially passive or temporary. The name alone does not identify a drink. It can accommodate new flavours and ingredients; a strongly athletic performance range would be a less natural fit.

### 02. Půlkrok

**Idea:** The half-step that takes you somewhere unplanned: a small stop, a different route, a conversation before heading home. The literal Czech construction means “half-step.”

- **Brand world:** playful precision; offset circles, interrupted paths and a wordmark that takes one visual step sideways. This is a compact local identity for spontaneous occasions.
- **Working line:** „Malá odbočka. Dobrý doušek.“ — A small detour. A good sip.
- **Pack example:** **PŮLKROK / citron / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *PŮL-krok*. Digital spelling to investigate: *pulkrok*. The final consonant cluster and long vowel need audio testing; „Dám si Půlkrok“ is the proposed order.
- **Tradeoff:** may suggest a half-measure, hesitation or an incomplete product. Broad enough for changing recipes, but the meaning will need rebuilding outside Czech-speaking markets.

### 03. Druhá scéna

**Idea:** Work, errands and obligations are one scene; the café, exhibition or dinner afterwards is another. The name literally means “second scene,” with a cultural rather than clinical frame.

- **Brand world:** theatre programmes, cinema titles and changing illustrated backdrops. Flavours become different settings, while their literal names remain easy to find.
- **Working line:** „Dnešek má pokračování.“ — Today has another scene.
- **Pack example:** **DRUHÁ SCÉNA / višeň / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *DRU-há SCÉ-na*. Test „Jednu Druhou scénu“ against the shorter spontaneous order „Scénu“. The accent-free form is *druhascena*.
- **Tradeoff:** people may expect a venue or cultural project, and the longer name needs space on a small can. Strong for after-work social life; less convincing as a morning functional proposition.

### 04. Přisedni

**Idea:** Put the welcome directly on the can. The Czech invitation “come sit with us” makes social participation the organising idea, without saying the drink makes someone more sociable.

- **Brand world:** chairs drawn in different styles, an open place at a table, handwritten invitations and warm but uncluttered packaging. The brand behaves like a considerate host.
- **Working line:** „Tady je místo.“ — There is a place here.
- **Pack example:** **PŘISEDNI / grep a rozmarýn / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-first; *PŘI-sed-ni*. The letter **ř** is a material export challenge. Test „Dám si Přisedni“ without teaching people how to treat the imperative as a brand.
- **Tradeoff:** the clearest local social concept, but not the easiest international name. **prisedni.cz has a registration record** in the limited check; a workable digital identity remains unresolved. A solitary performance-oriented range would need a stronger reason to belong here.

### 05. Místo navíc

**Idea:** A drink for the extra chair, the friend who turns up late and the host who always finds room. The literal meaning is “an extra place/space.”

- **Brand world:** expandable tables, generous margins and one unexpected extra element in every layout. Multipacks could carry a different invitation on each side.
- **Working line:** „Ještě někdo?“ — Anyone else?
- **Pack example:** **MÍSTO NAVÍC / hruška / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *MÍS-to NA-víc*. Try „Máte Místo navíc?“ in a café: the double meaning is both the creative asset and the ordering risk. Digital form: *mistonavic*.
- **Tradeoff:** may sound like a booking service or social initiative. It supports hosting and sharing better than an individual supplement-like proposition; new flavours need little change to the story.

### 06. Spoluno

**Idea:** A coined social identity built from Czech *spolu* (“together”) and an invented ending. It suggests company without requiring every product to be tied to a particular hour or ingredient. The ending has no claimed historical or foreign-language origin.

- **Brand world:** overlapping forms that make a third colour; friendly typography with enough restraint for adult hospitality. Brand recognition comes from the overlap motif rather than a literal drawing of people.
- **Working line:** „Otevři. Přidej se.“ — Open. Join in.
- **Pack example:** **SPOLUNO / grapefruit / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** intended Czech *SPO-lu-no*; English *spo-LOO-no* is a plausible alternative to test. Listen for *Spoluna*, *Spolu* and dropped syllables. Proposed order: „Jedno Spoluno“.
- **Tradeoff:** the social root may disappear abroad, and the product category is opaque without the descriptor. My strongest flexible social proposal; social and daytime variants are plausible design hypotheses, not established consumer fit.

### 07. Vlnora

**Idea:** A coinage around Czech *vlna* (“wave”), with an invented ending. The intended association is ripple, movement and the sensation of a drink, not a wave of euphoria.

- **Brand world:** one bold wave crossing the can, with each flavour given its own waveform and colour. Energetic visual movement can coexist with an adult, minimal label.
- **Working line:** „Chuť v pohybu.“ — Flavour in motion.
- **Pack example:** **VLNORA / citron a zázvor / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** intended Czech *VL-no-ra* with syllabic **l**; overseas readers may insert a vowel and say *vil-NOR-a*. That is a substantive test requirement, not an assumed easy international pronunciation.
- **Tradeoff:** a strong visual platform with an unusually demanding opening cluster. **vlnora.com has a registration record**. Flavour and ingredient extensions are conceptually broad, but do not add neurological or “frequency” explanations to the wave imagery.

### 08. Jiskrol

**Idea:** An invented form inspired by Czech *jiskra* (“spark”). It turns a small flash into a sensory identity for citrus, acidity and carbonation, conditional on the eventual drink delivering that character.

- **Brand world:** tiny flashes, sharply cut colour blocks and a restrained metallic accent. More vivid and angular than Vlnora's flowing system.
- **Working line:** „Malý záblesk chuti.“ — A little flash of flavour.
- **Pack example:** **JISKROL / limetka / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** intended Czech *JIS-krol*, with **j** as the English **y** in “yes”; English readers may start with a different consonant. Test final **l** recovery and resemblance to chemical or medicinal names.
- **Tradeoff:** could be read as an energy drink, medicine or alcohol-related name. Keep as a sensory challenger and drop it if those expectations dominate. A soft, still drink would have weaker sensory fit.

### 09. Copper Picnic

**Idea:** Combine the warm colour of copper with the easy social ritual of a picnic. The contrast is a small luxury in an informal setting, not a statement that copper is an ingredient.

- **Brand world:** copper-coloured lines, off-white backgrounds and a geometric picnic-check system. Premium without borrowing a vineyard or distillery history.
- **Working line:** “Bring a little occasion.”
- **Pack example:** **COPPER PICNIC / pomeranč / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** English-led; *KOP-er PIK-nik*. A likely Czech rendering is *kopr piknik*, which may bring an unintended dill association. Test spelling after hearing, particularly **c/k** and the doubled **p**.
- **Tradeoff:** can imply metallic flavour, cookware or a mineral ingredient. The full name is longer; it is strongest for hosting and outdoor meals, with less immediate relevance to a daytime nutrient drink.

### 10. Oblá vlna

**Idea:** “A rounded wave” makes the physical shape of flavour the brand metaphor. It is the gentle, food-friendly option: rounded rather than aggressively sharp, if the recipe can deliver that experience.

- **Brand world:** sculptural curves, embossed-looking contour drawings and warm, muted colour. It should feel like a well-made object on a dinner table.
- **Working line:** „Chuť má svůj tvar.“ — Flavour has a shape of its own.
- **Pack example:** **OBLÁ VLNA / meruňka / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *OB-lá VL-na*. Both the cluster and accent-free *oblavlna* need testing. Ask whether „Oblou vlnu, prosím“ feels natural or awkward.
- **Tradeoff:** the phrase already occurs as a description of corrugated material; see screening notes. It may suggest a still, soft or creamy drink rather than fizz. Keep only if sensory tests support the promised texture; formula changes must preserve that fit.

### 11. Klevuna

**Idea:** An invented name with a conversational rhythm. The possible Czech echo of *klevety* (gossip) is an intentional association to test, not its literal definition. Build a bright, neighbourly character around it.

- **Brand world:** speech-shaped cutouts, asymmetric lettering and short observations from everyday life. A lively café personality rather than a clinical functional-drink identity.
- **Working line:** „Něco k povídání.“ — Something to talk over.
- **Pack example:** **KLEVUNA / černý rybíz / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** intended Czech *KLE-vu-na*; *kleh-VOO-na* is a possible English reading. Test spelling and whether people hear gossip, a personal name or nothing in particular.
- **Tradeoff:** gossip can imply unkindness, and an invented name may resemble a medicine in some markets. Broad recipe flexibility; its character should remain social even if a later variant has a defined nutritional composition.

### 12. Tavunelo

**Idea:** A deliberately longer coinage with an open, rolling rhythm. Its meaning would be built around generous meals and flavour discovery; it is not presented as an Italian word, a place or a family name.

- **Brand world:** elongated letterforms, menus and bands of colour that wrap around the pack like a table setting. More expansive and culinary than the other coined options.
- **Working line:** „Ke stolu. Ke slovu.“ — To the table. Into the conversation.
- **Pack example:** **TAVUNELO / krvavý pomeranč / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** intended Czech *TA-vu-ne-lo*; *ta-voo-NEL-oh* is a possible English reading. Test whether listeners shorten it, lose a syllable or write a doubled **l**.
- **Tradeoff:** four syllables impose more spoken work, and the sound may imply a Mediterranean origin. Do not add invented heritage. Offers room for flavours and food-pairing ranges; less suited to a terse technical performance identity.

### 13. Doveli

**Idea:** A compact invented identity for small, well-considered pleasures. It is deliberately quiet in meaning so the drink and design can do more of the storytelling. No universal meaning or empty linguistic background is claimed.

- **Brand world:** small colour panels, precise typography and a discreet recurring dot. Designed for everyday use in a café, at lunch or at a desk, without promising productivity.
- **Working line:** „Obyčejný den. Vlastní chuť.“ — An ordinary day. A flavour of its own.
- **Pack example:** **DOVELI / mandarinka / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** intended Czech *DO-ve-li*; possible English *doh-VEL-ee*. *Dovely* and *Dovelli* are spelling foils. A Croatian inflected-word result surfaced in screening; local meaning and associations need review.
- **Tradeoff:** could resemble a deli, cosmetics brand or surname. It has broad conceptual range flexibility, but initially communicates almost nothing about beverages or a social occasion.

### 14. Velvet Tram

**Idea:** An unexpected pairing of soft texture and urban movement. It offers a contemporary city identity with room for Czech character, without claiming a particular place of manufacture or a historical connection.

- **Brand world:** abstract route lines, destination-style typography and deep plum with a bright ticket-colour accent. The mood is an evening out, not a transport souvenir.
- **Working line:** “Take the scenic sip.”
- **Pack example:** **VELVET TRAM / višeň / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** English-led; *VEL-vit TRAM*, with a likely Czech *VEL-vet tram* variant. Test whether people retain both words and whether “velvet” creates a creamy texture expectation.
- **Tradeoff:** transport or tourism may dominate the beverage idea. Stronger for cultural venues than technical nutrition. It can span recipes, provided neither the copy nor imagery implies a false local heritage.

### 15. Malý rozruch

**Idea:** “A little commotion” gives a modest everyday drink a mischievous personality. The occasion supplies the excitement; the liquid is not promised to cause a behavioural change.

- **Brand world:** oversized punctuation, off-centre layouts and small surprising details. Bold enough for an event fridge without copying death, intoxication or rebellion clichés.
- **Working line:** „Obyčejný večer? Uvidíme.“ — An ordinary evening? We will see.
- **Pack example:** **MALÝ ROZRUCH / grapefruit / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *MA-lý ROZ-ruch*, with Czech **ch**. Test the whole phrase and the likely nickname *Rozruch* separately. Digital form: *malyrozruch*.
- **Tradeoff:** may suggest stimulation or a loud personality. It is a poor starting point for sleep or soothing positioning, but a strong contrast candidate for a lively social drink. Flavour launches can vary visually while retaining the same attitude.

### 16. Dobrý zmatek

**Idea:** “Good chaos/confusion” celebrates the enjoyable untidiness of a gathering: overlapping stories, improvised food and people arriving at different times.

- **Brand world:** collaged shapes and deliberately mismatched illustrations, anchored by very clear flavour and category information. Disorder is in the artwork, not in product navigation.
- **Working line:** „Plány nejsou podmínkou.“ — Plans are optional.
- **Pack example:** **DOBRÝ ZMATEK / malina a citron / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *DOB-rý ZMA-tek*. Test “good chaos” as an interpretation rather than assuming the positive adjective cancels the negative noun. Digital form: *dobryzmatek*.
- **Tradeoff:** confusion is especially problematic beside a cognitive or functional claim. Retain as an expressive social challenger; do not stretch it into a focus product. Changing recipes is easy, but the tone is intentionally polarising.

### 17. Tekutá tečka

**Idea:** “A liquid full stop” turns the drink into punctuation: the finish to a meal, a small conclusion or the boundary between two activities. The liquid reference helps establish beverage relevance.

- **Brand world:** a large dot that becomes a fruit silhouette, with precise editorial typography. Each flavour can have a different punctuation illustration while the masterbrand stays fixed.
- **Working line:** „Za tím si dej tečku.“ — Put a full stop after that.
- **Pack example:** **TEKUTÁ TEČKA / višeň / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *TE-ku-tá TEČ-ka*. Test „Jednu Tekutou tečku“ and whether buyers shorten the name to *Tečka*. Digital form: *tekutatecka*.
- **Tradeoff:** an existing editorial use associates the phrase with sweet after-dinner drinks; it may feel like a digestif or dessert. More useful for a defined ritual than a broad daily functional range.

### 18. Dílna doušků

**Idea:** “A workshop of sips” makes the process of composing flavour the story. Credibility must come from actual recipe development and named contributors, including external formulators where relevant.

- **Brand world:** recipe annotations, flavour sketches and a contemporary maker's stamp. Use documented development details rather than invented batch histories or claims of hand production.
- **Working line:** „Recept začíná douškem.“ — A recipe begins with a sip.
- **Pack example:** **DÍLNA DOUŠKŮ / hruška a zázvor / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *DÍL-na DOUŠ-ků*. Test the alliteration and the long digital form *dilnadousku*. „Nápoj od Dílny doušků“ may be easier than inflecting the name in an order.
- **Tradeoff:** may sound like a tasting workshop, or imply small-scale in-house manufacture. A truthful “developed with…, manufactured by…” story can support outsourced production; the name does not by itself substantiate craft quality.

### 19. Várkot

**Idea:** A coined maker's signature inspired by *várka* (“batch”), with a clipped ending. It gives the craft territory a shorter, more graphic alternative to a descriptive workshop name.

- **Brand world:** a simple stamp, confident block lettering and real recipe identifiers only where useful. Build around repeatable taste and iterative development, not a fictional old brewery.
- **Working line:** „Recept s charakterem.“ — A recipe with character.
- **Pack example:** **VÁRKOT / jablko a zázvor / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** intended Czech *VÁR-kot*; accent-free *Varkot*. Test resemblance to *varkoč*, surnames, brewing terms and other existing words or marks in neighbouring languages.
- **Tradeoff:** could suggest beer, boiling or a fermented process that the formula does not use. Suits a taste-led portfolio more naturally than a clinical nutrition identity. Recipe changes fit the story, but claims about the process must remain factual.

### 20. Rukopis chuti

**Idea:** “The handwriting/signature of flavour” positions the product as an authored recipe. Its distinctive asset is a recognisable taste perspective, supported by real development work rather than a fabricated founder legend.

- **Brand world:** one continuous line interpreted differently on each flavour, restrained type and signed development notes only when genuinely attributable to their author.
- **Working line:** „Každý doušek má svůj rukopis.“ — Every sip has its own signature.
- **Pack example:** **RUKOPIS CHUTI / pomeranč a tymián / perlivý nealkoholický nápoj**.
- **Language:** Czech-led; *RU-ko-pis CHU-ti*. Test whether the full phrase can be recalled and ordered; *rukopischuti* is a long digital spelling. It may be heard as a slogan rather than a brand.
- **Tradeoff:** the craft metaphor is relatively descriptive and food-adjacent naming already uses *rukopis*. Strongest for hospitality and pairing; new recipes should retain a recognisable house style rather than relying on ingredient novelty alone.

## Which differences are worth testing first?

| Candidate | Why it earns a first test | The question that could eliminate it |
|---|---|---|
| **Spoluno** | Social meaning in Czech with room to build a broader identity | Can people hear, spell and recall the whole name without reducing it to *spolu*? |
| **Vlnora** | A sensory metaphor with a coherent packaging system | Does the initial consonant cluster make spoken discovery impractical in the intended markets? |
| **Přisedni** | The invitation is the idea; little explanatory brand story is needed locally | Does it work as an orderable brand, and is there a usable digital identity despite the registered .cz? |
| **Malý rozruch** | A distinctive local attitude that contrasts with wellness conventions | Do people infer a stimulant or behavioural effect that the product cannot deliver? |
| **Dílna doušků** | A credible route if the recipe and development story become the advantage | Does it suggest a workshop service or a manufacturing method we do not actually use? |

For an expansion-led brief, also test **Doveli** as a relatively simple spelling challenger and **Tavunelo** as a longer-name challenger. Their international usability is unresolved; being invented does not make them language-neutral.

## Architecture stress test

Start with **brand + flavour + truthful local beverage descriptor**. The examples above intentionally omit a product-family layer. “Perlivý nealkoholický nápoj” is a working category description to reconcile with the eventual recipe and label, not a complete approved label.

1. **Two flavours:** compare **Spoluno / grapefruit** with **Spoluno / višeň**, holding brand size and descriptor constant. Repeat with each shortlisted name. Test correct selection without relying on colour alone.
2. **Social versus functional:** show the same brand on a social sparkling drink and on a separately described nutrient-containing drink, only after the actual formulation is defined. Ask what changes and what stays the same. Do not insert “Calm” or “Focus” merely to make the architecture look complete.
3. **Ingredient change:** replace or remove a botanical while keeping the brand. The abstract/social names generally permit this conceptually; sensory names still need the promised flavour character, and craft names still need a truthful process story. This is a desk assessment, not tested acceptance.
4. **Localisation and claim removal:** retain the masterbrand, translate flavour and category, and remove any unsupported functional statement. Check whether buyers still recognise and understand the drink. Czech literal meanings may need new communication abroad; do not silently rename the masterbrand by translation.

Use the source report's [neutral testing protocol](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/supporting/evaluation-and-testing.md): name alone, audio-to-spelling, delayed recall, then matched packaging and a realistic order. Select the intended buyer and occasion before testing. Native-language review and qualified trademark assessment remain separate unresolved steps for every candidate.
