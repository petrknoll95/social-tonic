# Formulation feasibility workstream

Accessed 17 October 2026, Europe/Prague (+02:00). Desk research only; no supplier contact, quotation, sensory assessment, ingredient purchase or dosing experiment. No recipe, process or safe serving has been approved. Study quantities below describe transfer challenges, not recommended additions.

## Evidence map and interpretation

Reviewed existing topic 13 ingredient sensory memo/risk register, topic 12 safety/regulatory gates and topic 09 claims index. They already correctly distinguish manufacturer claims from measured finished-drink results, require elemental-magnesium accounting and isolate CBD. Their strongest reusable findings are theanine model-solution sensory data, salt-specific sensory uncertainty, and the difference between a freshly prepared mushroom research drink and a processed RTD. This work adds primary chemical/processing evidence for creatine, tyrosine, theobromine, taurine, GABA, ginseng and saffron. No ancestor or repository AGENTS.md was found in applicable paths.

The crucial inconsistency to avoid is treating “water-soluble,” “heat-stable” or an ingredient's three-year **dry-powder** life as a demonstrated shelf life for an acidified canned drink. Similarly, a beverage-optimised extract is not automatically equivalent to the capsule extract with the best clinical result. Co-extractives, carriers, extraction solvents, marker ratios and manufacturing route can all differ.

Evidence labels: **P** = published primary experiment (scope stated); **S** = supplier specification/claim, not independently validated; **I** = analytical inference or proposed development question. All materials require actual food-grade lot qualification and destination-specific permission. “Commercial grade exists” means a marketed supply route was found, not that the supplier or material is cleared. Public defensible EU commercial cost-in-use was not established for any candidate: **quote required for all**, including MOQ, testing, wastage and processing costs.

## Sixteen deep candidates

### E01 — L-theanine, pure L enantiomer

**Evidence:** P: theanine in model solutions had concentration-dependent mixed umami/sweet/bitter taste, and IMP enhanced umami; this is not proof of taste neutrality at an RTD serving [FM01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18997398/). S: Taiyo advertises ≥98% enzymatically produced L-theanine for beverages; Blue California's submitted experiments report good retention under specified pH/heat conditions [FM02](https://www.taiyointernational.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/ST_Data-Beverage_2015.pdf), [FM03](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FDA-2014-S-0023-0044/attachment_1.pdf).

**Transfer judgment:** Hundreds-of-milligrams research exposures are not intrinsically a mass obstacle in a can. Exact purity, enantiomer ratio, residual caffeine for tea-derived material, carrier and production route matter. Do not transfer a source's clinical or legal status to all “natural theanine.” Quantitative cold solubility, aroma, precipitation, carbonation, can-lining interactions and end-of-life retention in the intended formula remain unknown. Test pure ingredient versus base at matched pH as well as original pH; altered sourness is not necessarily perceptual masking. White powder/low-colour claims are supplier information. Obtain formulation-specific acid/heat/light data; no proof here of shelf-stable physiological effect.

### E02 — Theobromine, purified 3,7-dimethylxanthine

P: a primary solubility paper reports the literature water baseline at approximately 330 mg/L at 25 °C and experimentally demonstrates strong solvent dependence [FM04](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4923/13/8/1118). Arithmetic: that baseline corresponds to approximately 109 mg dissolved in 330 mL **plain water**, not a validated soda limit. The 400 mg exposure in a human beverage study in the energy workstream would be about 1.21 g/L in a 330 mL package; simply copying that mass into a clear soda is unsupported.

I: expect a substantial solubility/settling-development question at several hundred milligrams; cold precipitation and uniformity require measurement. Cocoa brings particles, colour, other methylxanthines and flavour, and cannot be treated as pure theobromine. Bitter intensity is a sensory question for the exact preparation, not a measured defect in our drink. The research's organic-solvent or eutectic systems are not proposed food ingredients. Exact beverage-grade supply, acidic heat/light stability, carbonation/pack compatibility and cost remain unverified.

### E03 — Paraxanthine, purified 1,7-dimethylxanthine

S: one research-reagent supplier gives aqueous solubility as 1 mg/mL [FM05](https://www.scbt.com/de/p/paraxanthine-611-59-6). This is explicitly research-use material, without a validated beverage protocol; it is not evidence of a lawful food grade. At 200 mg/330 mL, arithmetic concentration is 0.61 mg/mL, but being below that vendor number does not establish cold stability or commercial feasibility.

I: require the exact commercial synthetic preparation's solvent/residual specifications, caffeine assay, taste/bitterness, dissolution, sedimentation, heat/acid/light stability and packaging data. All remain unknown here. US food/supplement marketing or powder certification cannot supply missing EU permission. A novel-food application is not authorization. Do not solve formulation before the legal gate. It is a stimulant candidate, not evidence of a stimulant-free experience.

### E04 — L-tyrosine

P/compiled chemistry: approximately 0.45–0.48 g/L in water at 25 °C; primary experiments show pH and salts change solubility, with NaCl reducing it under the tested conditions [FM06](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/tyrosine), [FM07](https://doi.org/10.1021/je9501853). The 2 g/400 mL orange-juice trial in the energy extraction demonstrates oral liquid delivery, **not complete dissolution or shelf stability**. Its 5 g/L load far exceeds the neutral-water benchmark.

I: gram exposure in a clear soda is a major physical-fit concern; suspension, grit and sediment are hypotheses requiring measurement. Acidification changes solubility but cannot be assumed to solve it at palatable soda pH; heat dissolution can be followed by cooling precipitation. N-acetyl-L-tyrosine or another derivative is a different material, not an interchangeable evidence shortcut. Exact food grade, heat/light degradation, taste, carbonation and pack compatibility remain unknown. A 150 mg/kg stress-study dose also creates a large gram load, unsuitable to import into casual-can design without independent safety and technical review.

### E05 — Creatine monohydrate

P: a di-creatine-citrate effervescent study found dissociation and eventual creatine-monohydrate crystallisation; refrigerated samples formed crystals by day seven. Substantial creatine loss occurred during 45-day storage in that particular system [FM08](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2750587/). Separately, pH-4 buffer/glycerol experiments established temperature/water-activity dependence [FM09](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19635041/).

**Critical caveat:** degradation does not accelerate monotonically to arbitrarily low pH. The FDA-hosted applicant dossier describes a slowdown below approximately pH 2.5; that chemistry exception is **not** a palatability, dental-safety, microbiological or shelf-life solution [FM10](https://www.fda.gov/media/143525/download?attachment=). Changing to citrate does not by itself solve stability.

I: 5 g/day long-term cognition studies and 0.2–0.35 g/kg sleep-loss experiments pose materially different loading problems; 14–24.5 g for a 70 kg adult is arithmetic, not a can recommendation. Assay creatine and creatinine separately after process and through storage, assess crystal formation under cold cycling and quantify soluble versus total content. Conventional shelf-stable acidic soda is a poor initial fit. Dry ingredient stability is irrelevant to this judgment. No tested RTD taste, colour, light or packaging profile exists here; marketing an encapsulated/new form also requires evidence matching and regulatory review.

### E06 — Taurine, 2-aminoethanesulfonic acid

Liquid human-study delivery and established RTD benchmarks in the energy workstream make gram-scale formulation more plausible than tyrosine or theobromine; they do not establish standalone energy benefit. P: taurine loss under heating was measured in milk and lactose buffer at pH 6.7, with browning implicated [FM11](https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.S0022-0302(90)78846-7)78846-7). Do not extrapolate that loss rate to an acidic soda or claim universal heat stability.

I: specify synthetic/other route, purity and food grade, then test actual sugar/amino-acid system, temperature history and shelf life. Taste, colour, solubility margin at cold temperature, carbonation and packaging in our drink are unknown. Commercial prevalence establishes a practical sourcing lead only. At gram loads assess total dissolved solids and full-serving flavour; “sulfur-containing” does not establish a sulfurous smell.

### E07 — Rhodiola rosea root/rhizome extract

P: a 2025 root-processing experiment found preparation-dependent marker content and recovery under different pH/temperature conditions; short infusions are not long-term RTD stability tests [FM12](https://academic.oup.com/ijfst/article/60/1/vvaf049/8119135). S: PLT/Nektium report a water-soluble Rhodiolife grade tested in an acidic orange drink, with retained rosavins and unchanged reported sensory attributes after pasteurisation/storage; underlying complete data were not inspected [FM13](https://www.plthealth.com/resources/plt-introduces-water-soluble-rhodiola-rosea-ingredient-into-the-north-american-market).

I: potentially feasible **if exact grade is matched and qualified**. Soluble Rhodiolife is not automatically clinical SHR-5. Require species/part authentication, rosavin/rosarin/rosin/salidroside fingerprint, DER, solvent, carrier, residual solvents and the actual concentration/process/storage dossier. Colour/aroma/astringency, haze, foaming under carbonation, cold settling and can compatibility remain formula-specific unknowns; these are test questions, not observed problems. Subgram study masses alone do not decide feasibility.

### E08 — Panax ginseng root extract

P: red-ginseng aqueous extract studies show Rg1/Rb1/Rh1 falling under some heat/acid/storage conditions while Rg3 can increase; “total ginsenosides retained” can conceal a changed profile [FM14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30263712/). The study matrix was a 25.5 °Brix red-ginseng concentrate, not G115 in soda. Its preferred pH/process conditions cannot be copied as a microbiologically validated beverage process.

I: require white/red/fermented status, extraction process, complete ginsenoside profile and post-process equivalence to the clinical material. Hundreds-of-milligrams loads may physically fit but rooty/bitter notes, brown colour, haze and foam are screening questions for the exact grade. Water solubility, light retention, low-pH shelf life and package compatibility remain unverified. A material optimised for clarity may no longer match G115 evidence. Quote and beverage-grade qualification required.

### C01 — Withania somnifera root extract

S: KSM-66's own page asserts neutral taste, solubility, heat tolerance and three-year life [FM15](https://ksm66ashwagandhaa.com/functional-food-and-beverages.php). Those statements cannot be generalized to leaf-containing/hydroalcoholic extracts or taken as proof of a three-year acidic RTD. Published finished-can processing/light/storage data were not established here.

I: root/leaf, withanolide assay method and profile, withaferin A, carrier and process must be specified. Several-hundred-milligram capsule studies do not determine liquid tolerability or dispersion. Earthy/bitter notes, colour, haze and sediment are material-specific test descriptors, not established product properties. Carbonation/pack compatibility unknown. Safety/national-law concerns make sensory optimization a later gate; do not choose the material just because a vendor calls it beverage-ready.

### C02 — Melissa officinalis leaf extract

P: a standardized aqueous leaf preparation (>6% rosmarinic acid, Bluenesse) was tested in 480 mL drinks at 0.3 or 0.6 g; sweetener differences complicate physiological interpretation, but this is direct evidence that a defined extract was delivered in a palatable liquid [FM16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4245564/). It does not validate industrial processing/storage.

I: a smaller can retaining the same mass increases concentration and may change sensory burden. A lemon-balm infusion, essential oil, or differently standardized extract cannot borrow this result. Citrus/herbal aroma, bitterness/astringency, colour and haze must be measured rather than assumed pleasant. Require leaf/solvent/carrier/rosmarinic-acid specification plus marker and volatile retention during heat/acid/light storage. Exact beverage-grade current availability and carbonation/pack effects remain unqualified. A low-flavour extract may lack the pleasant volatile cues associated with the herb.

### C03 — Crocus sativus stigma extracts

P: saffron aqueous pigments and isolated crocin-1 show condition-dependent degradation with temperature, pH and light; pigments are analytical markers, not the whole clinical preparation [FM17](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2621.1993.tb06116.x), [FM18](https://www.spkx.net.cn/EN/10.7506/spkx1002-6630-201205016). S: affron is advertised as water soluble [FM19](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/5f85d0c81b6f5da2719b6b03/629685544b566312c0b6a95d_313_Brochure%20Affron%20USA_Print%20version.pdf).

I: clinical tens-of-milligrams loads pose less bulk than gram-scale amino acids but do not settle cost or potency retention. Exact stigma extract, markers/carrier and adulteration controls matter; affron, affron eye and generic saffron are not interchangeable. Yellow colour, bitterness and characteristic aroma require actual batch/finished-drink measurement. Screen oxygen and light exposure and compare opaque can to bottle under the actual distribution environment. No quantitative shelf life for the proposed drink, carbonation compatibility or ingredient price established.

### C04 — Oral GABA, exact fermentation or synthetic grade

P: Shimizu/Sawai examined fruit-juice RTDs and reported retention of GABA in representative grapefruit juice after specified heating (80–115 °C), temperature storage and fluorescent-light challenges [FM20](https://doi.org/10.5891/jafps.34.145). This is stronger beverage-process evidence than a capsule-only literature, but exact durations were not extracted from full Japanese text. P: separate severe heating of glucose/GABA at pH 6 showed browning; this does not negate the grapefruit results [FM29](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308814608005037).

S: PharmaGABA advertises ready water solubility and broad heat/pH stability [FM21](https://pharmagaba.com/). I: plausible physical route at subgram masses, with actual taste/colour, assay, impurity, storage, carbonation and pack testing still necessary. Fermentation-derived and synthetic GABA may share chemical identity but source impurities/legal status need separate review. Reject the supplier's blanket implication that synthetic production is inherently unsafe. Technical stability does not demonstrate brain uptake or felt calm.

### C05 — Hericium erinaceus fruiting-body preparations

P: a 2025 trial used 3 g of 10:1 extract, 95% extract/5% maltodextrin, water:ethanol extraction 8:2, mixed into 250 mL lemon-squash/water. Drinks were prepared shortly before ingestion [FM22](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1405796/full). This is **12 g/L fresh preparation**, not an industrial shelf-stability or sensory-preference trial.

I: aqueous extracts, hydroethanolic preparations, whole fruiting-body powder and erinacine-rich mycelia have different composition and legality. Require beta-glucan versus starch/carrier assays and relevant validated marker fingerprint; a “10:1” ratio alone is inadequate. Mushroom/earthy aroma, umami, colour, haze and sediment are test hypotheses. Actual solubility, heat/acid/light retention, carbonation foam and can compatibility remain unknown. Study delivery shows feasibility of swallowing a liquid dose, not a commercially pleasant one-can cognitive effect.

### N01 — Magnesium salts (elemental magnesium basis)

S: Jungbunzlauer specifies lactate dihydrate at about 10% Mg and 84 g/L water solubility; trimagnesium citrate is about 16% Mg anhydrous or 12% nonahydrate, with hydration-dependent solubility [FM23](https://www.jungbunzlauer.com/ingredients/magnesium-lactate/), [FM24](https://www.jungbunzlauer.com/ingredients/trimagnesium-citrate/). Its neutral-taste statements are supplier claims. These are actual food/beverage supply leads, still requiring lot qualification.

I: every 1 mg elemental Mg entails about 10 mg lactate-dihydrate material, 6.25 mg anhydrous citrate or 8.33 mg nonahydrate citrate at those assays; arithmetic only. Compare salts at equal elemental exposure. Magnesium atoms do not thermally degrade, but precipitation, complexation, acidity shifts and sensory changes can alter performance. Determine dissolved/total magnesium, pH, titratable acidity, grit and bitter/salty/mineral taste after cold/heat cycling. Other salts cannot borrow these specifications. Light effects on the combined matrix, carbonation and corrosion/lining suitability require testing; do not infer acute relaxation from soluble mineral delivery.

### N02 — B vitamins, named chemical forms

P: one fortified nutrition drink retained B2/B6/B12 while folate declined markedly over long storage; its nutrient-rich matrix is not a soda [FM26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8650259/). P: cyanocobalamin stability can worsen in the presence of ascorbic acid in aqueous buffer [FM25](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.53.688). S: beverage-oriented riboflavin products exist and can contribute yellow colour [FM27](https://www.dsm-firmenich.com/en/businesses/taste-texture-health/markets-products/beverage/functional-drinks/vibelly-riboflavin.html).

I: “B complex” is insufficient identity. Specify thiamine salt, riboflavin form, nicotinamide versus nicotinic acid, pantothenate, B6 vitamer, folate form and B12 form/carriers as relevant. Milligram/microgram nutrient amounts are not generally bulk-limited, but premix mass and homogeneity matter. Taste/aroma/colour, solubility, light/heat/oxygen stability and interactions must be assessed nutrient by nutrient. Assay the legally relevant retained content at end of shelf life. Unreviewed overages can multiply repeated-can intake. Opaque cans address light exposure only, not every degradation pathway.

### N03 — Carbohydrate/glucose provision

The energy workstream's 25 g glucose study entails 100 kcal from carbohydrate and, if delivered in 330 mL, approximately 7.6 g sugars/100 mL. This is arithmetic, not a proposed recipe; it would conflict with an EU low-sugars proposition under the reviewed threshold. Sugars are a readily available food ingredient route with established liquid delivery, but no supplier or price is qualified here.

I: glucose, sucrose and slowly digested carbohydrates are not physiologically or sensorially interchangeable. Define actual carbohydrate and any carriers, sweetness, body, osmotic load and nutritional trade-offs. Reducing sugars can interact with amino compounds; the GABA and taurine model studies illustrate why combined-matrix retention needs verification [FM29](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308814608005037), [FM11](https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.S0022-0302(90)78846-7)78846-7). No model reaction rate is transferred to soda. Acidity, thermal exposure, colour/aroma drift, dissolved solids and microbial/process requirements need a normal co-packer program. A sugar can does not automatically provide lasting alertness or “no crash.”

## CBD optional variant only

P: Mullen et al. tested one nano-emulsified CBD preparation in acidic foods/beverages and observed THC formation in some stored beverages [FM28](https://doi.org/10.1089/can.2024.0064). This is a **specific-material/matrix** result, not proof that every CBD emulsion behaves alike. A day-zero “THC-free” certificate cannot establish a finished product's whole shelf-life profile. CBD remains outside the core proposition.

I: isolate chemical purity differs from aqueous dispersion. An emulsion adds oil, carrier/emulsifier, particle-size and dose-uniformity variables plus potential taste, foam and package interactions. Require lawful exact-material beverage status, cannabinoid impurity/degradation testing and retained exposure through shelf life, along with emulsion stability under acid/heat/light/carbonation. No universal emulsion recipe, safe dose, shelf life or price is supplied. Greater water dispersibility does not establish equivalent pharmacokinetics or safety.

## Common co-packer validation gates (proposed work, not completed tests)

1. Match the actual proposed material to legal status and the human study: species/part, chemical form, manufacturing route, extract ratio, assay method/markers, carriers, impurities, microbiology and lot identity. The supplier must substantiate both claimed food grade and destination/category permission; supplier statements alone do not close either gate.
2. Define intended container and full-container concentration. Convert study mass to ingredient mass and concentration; separately calculate active versus salt/carrier mass and cumulative multi-can/background intake. Lowering the mass just to make a clear drink may eliminate the already limited evidence connection.
3. Have the co-packer/process authority select a safe process for the entire formula. An active's preferred pH/temperature does not set microbiological requirements. Compare pre/post-process marker profile, not just one total “active” number.
4. Measure pH, titratable acidity, dissolved solids, colour, turbidity, sediment, dissolved/total actives, carbonation, foam, aroma and full-serving sensory acceptance using the actual pack. Finished-product bitterness, aroma and taste observations are currently **unknown for every candidate**.
5. Validate intended shelf life under realistic distribution, with cold/heat cycling, oxygen/light exposure as applicable and package/lining/closure compatibility. Assess adsorption/migration/corrosion or flavour loss where the actual pack/material warrants it; none was established in this desk research. Accelerated work screens risk but does not alone prove a market shelf life.
6. Only then assess whether a sensory-matched finished-drink human study is justified. Stable delivery cannot convert weak physiology into strong efficacy. A pleasant, recognisable social drink may remain the better product without pharmacological promise.

**Analytical priorities:** the physically simplest classes appear to be properly specified nutrient fortification and soluble low-mass ingredients, conditional on efficacy and permission. Creatine, gram-dose tyrosine and several-hundred-milligram purified theobromine carry especially clear aqueous RTD difficulties. Botanical “beverage grade” is a starting specification, not proof that the studied preparation, storage profile or benefit transfers.
