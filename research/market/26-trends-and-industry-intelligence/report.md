# Functional beverage trends and industry intelligence

**Research date: 17 October 2026 · Decision audience: Czech founder considering an EU launch**

## Briefing: what should change our decisions

**Test a concrete drinking occasion before committing to a category or ingredient stack.** The best early comparison is a modest-caffeine work-break drink, a caffeine-free evening social drink, and a familiar refreshment proposition with a simple nutritional benefit. A brewed tea or small coffee deserves equal consideration with a sparkling “productivity” drink. Hiyo is useful for social ritual, but is not a template for the entire business.

- **In our judgment, the most consequential recent signal is retail organization around occasions.** Walmart's Mood & Mind assortment launched on 3 October in about 1,000 US stores, according to reporting that quotes its beverage VP. It puts focus, energy and unwinding into one discovery destination. This is credible channel commitment; October sales velocity and repeat purchase are still unknown. Several participating brands do not equal several independent retailer adoption events. [Progressive Grocer][L39]
- **Focus is becoming a proposition within familiar drinks.** Pure Leaf's sparkling black tea, Chameleon's functional coffee and Neutonic's productivity cans are independent examples. Their activity supports a recurring positioning pattern, not proof that a nootropic mixture improves real-world work or that focus-specific drinks command a durable premium. [Pure Leaf][FI03], [Chameleon][FI12], [Neutonic][FI01]
- **Energy has much stronger commercial evidence than specialist cognitive enhancement.** Comparable US tracked RTD retail figures show Alani Nu growing while CELSIUS and Rockstar decline. Taste, branding and execution can win share; the category does not lift every entrant. [Celsius results][FI17]
- **Calm is becoming less dependent on cannabinoids.** New non-cannabinoid propositions from Unwind and established calm brands sit alongside CBD-brand extensions. That creates a plausible route for the primary CBD-free product. It does not establish a reliable immediate “buzz” or sleep effect. [Unwind launch][L40], [Recess retail announcement][L41]
- **Europe is already participating.** Czech Magnesia Plus and UGO offer focus/stress-related positioning; Germany has a new Dr. Oetker fibre drink; Nichols acquired Irish-founded VITHIT. The useful gap to investigate is a specific combination of taste, occasion, price and channel, not “functional drinks have not reached Europe.” [Magnesia][L09], [UGO][L11], [Dr. Oetker][L08], [VITHIT acquisition][F012]
- **A modest formula and production process can be a competitive choice.** Protein/fibre/energy combinations are multiplying, but their liquid-line economics are not yet established. OWYN's disclosed velocity and execution problems show why new doors and a fashionable benefit do not remove formulation and inventory risk. [OWYN Q2][L18], [Q3][L19]
- **Keep CBD as a gated extension.** UK listings and US cans do not establish an EU route. Current official guidance, EU authorization records and Czech enforcement evidence do not support treating a CBD beverage as cleared for launch. A CBD-free business should work independently. [FSA guidance][L42], [Commission][L27], [SZPI][L29]

These are analyst recommendations about the next experiments. No formula, supplier, budget or market entry has been approved or tested.

## Scope, dates and evidence standard

The priority window is **19 July–17 October 2026**. The rolling momentum window is **17 October 2025–17 October 2026**; developments from 2025 and earlier 2026 provide comparison. Older products and studies appear only as dated context. Publication, commercial event and original product introduction are separate fields in the [signal tracker](data/signals.csv). A fresh interview about a 2020 product is not a 2026 launch; a 2027 plan is not current availability.

Product benchmarks are finished liquids sold in **cans or bottles**, including coffee, tea and bottled shots. Consumer powders, pouches, tablets, gummies, drops and concentrates requiring preparation are excluded. Mixed-format company financials are used only with their broader scope stated. THC-led products are not launch blueprints; cannabinoid-free extensions from those companies are assessed separately. “Non-alcoholic” marketing is not an independent assay of residual alcohol, especially for fermented drinks.

| Evidence level | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated launch/announcement | An offered or planned product, formula or channel move | Repeat, incremental efficacy, durable demand |
| Recurring pattern | Similar choices across independent companies | Category market share or commercially validated growth |
| Commercially validated trend | Measured sales or sustained business results with a defined period and scope | Every entrant's success, exact formula benefit, Czech product-market fit |

Confidence concerns the documented fact, not the claimed functional effect. **Verified documentary fact**, **company claim**, **reported measured result**, **estimate** and **analyst hypothesis** remain distinct. Syndications count once. Retail listings show an offer; a press release can establish an announcement; neither establishes completed consumer purchases. Promotional prices, discounts and pack arithmetic are snapshots, not market averages.

The [methods and review record](supporting/methods-and-review.md) records delegation, deduplication, contradictions and checks.

## Strongest recurring trends, with counterevidence

### 1. Workday focus is moving into tea, coffee and smaller everyday rituals

Pure Leaf Mental Focus offers 69 mg caffeine in a 12 oz sparkling tea can; Neutonic centres the work/productivity occasion; Chameleon adds theanine and zinc to functional 8 oz coffee cans. Mateína, Saint James and ULTIMATE show a parallel route through familiar brewed tea. These examples compete with ordinary coffee and energy for the same break, even if retailers classify them differently. [Pure Leaf][FI03], [Neutonic][FI01], [Chameleon][FI12], [Mateína][F017], [Saint James][FI06], [ULTIMATE][FI08]

**Bottled shots are a distinct format to learn from.** Magic Mind’s current Original is a 2 fl oz bottle with 55 mg caffeine; its range also advertises 0 mg and 165 mg variants. A March 2026 interview discusses an existing brand founded in 2020, not a new launch. It illustrates caffeine segmentation and a compact ritual; its growth and absorption claims are not independently validated here. [Current product][L46], [Interview][L45]

**Counterevidence:** no reviewed disclosure isolates incremental sales caused by cognitive ingredients or demonstrates better real-world productivity than caffeine alone. Pure Leaf's theanine dose was not disclosed in its announcement. Neutonic's new PR and older exact US SKU page describe different adjunct stacks. Tea-based energy is already familiar in Europe; its existence alone is not an adoption gap. [Product version][FI02], [ULTIMATE][FI08]

**Assessment:** customer relevance is high for an identifiable work-break problem. Evidence is moderate for recurring positioning, limited for focus-specific commercial success. Momentum is increasing through retail entry; competition from coffee, tea and established energy is intense. The occasion is likely durable; expensive nootropic differentiation is less certain. EU transfer and affordable development look more plausible for a conventional beverage base with restrained claims than for copied US stacks. This is a hypothesis for local testing, not a cleared formula.

### 2. Calm is gaining shelf space while separating from cannabinoids

Recess, TRIP and Unwind use calm/wind-down language within the new Walmart assortment. Goodrays has non-CBD extensions, while VYBES' earlier CBD withdrawal provides a separate strategic precedent. These are independent brand choices, though the Walmart stories are one coordinated channel event. “Relaxed social participation” and “recover from a busy day” may be stronger customer jobs than explaining an adaptogen category. [Retailer interview][L39], [Unwind][L40], [VYBES withdrawal][C23], [Goodrays launch][C17]

**Counterevidence:** a retailer test does not prove sell-through; brand forecasts and cannabinoid-parent revenue do not validate the new cans. Stress, daytime calm, evening ritual and sleep onset are different outcomes. A magnesium-containing drink may support permitted nutrient wording under appropriate conditions without causing an immediately felt tranquilizing effect.

**Assessment:** customer relevance is credible; evidence is moderate for a recurring CBD-free pattern and channel commitment, weaker for repeat or perceptible acute effects. Competition is rising quickly. A pleasant adult evening ritual could endure; an expectation of an alcohol-like sensation is riskier. EU transferability is conditional on the exact ingredients and claims. A sensory-first caffeine-free drink using an existing filling process has reasonable development potential; an elaborate psychotropic-style promise does not.

**Dedicated sleep remains a less validated subcategory.** Sly announced wider Sprouts distribution in October for an existing noncarbonated DREAM can, while BRĒZ’s cannabinoid-free Dream was already introduced in 2025. Sly includes melatonin; BRĒZ says no added melatonin. Unwind explicitly describes itself as something other than a sleep drink. These differences matter for expectation, timing and legal classification. We found retailer access and recurring innovation, but no comparable line-level sales, retention or finished-drink sleep outcomes. A bedtime need can be durable while a specific bottled solution remains unproven. [Sly announcement][C14], [Sly formula][C15], [BRĒZ][C28], [Dream][C29], [Unwind FAQ][C05]

### 3. Fibre is entering familiar soda, tea and dairy formats; it is no longer a quiet niche

Pepsi's nationwide Prebiotic Cola, Coca-Cola's Simply Pop, Halfday tea and Dr. Oetker's German milk-based fibre bottles illustrate a recurring cross-company pattern. Poppi's acquisition and OLIPOP's reported historical scale add commercial weight to US better-for-you soda. Yet these examples differ in fibre type, dose, sweetness, processing and storage; “gut health” is not one interchangeable formulation. [Pepsi][L03], [Simply Pop][L04], [Halfday][F009], [Dr. Oetker][L08], [Poppi][L01], [OLIPOP][L05]

**Counterevidence:** incumbent entry makes distribution and pricing harder for a startup. A few grams of fibre do not establish every claimed digestive benefit or tolerability at several cans daily. Some products require refrigeration. The UK Poppi launch uses 330 ml cans and high-fibre/low-sugar wording; UK activity is not EU authorization or Czech demand. [UK announcement][L06]

**Assessment:** customer relevance is strong when the product replaces an enjoyable daily soda/tea. Commercial momentum is more substantiated for the broad US soda segment than for each new fibre SKU. Competition is high and durability depends heavily on taste and price. EU transfer is possible in principle but ingredient and claim specific; the German launch proves participation, not unrestricted legal equivalence. Development feasibility is moderate for a simple beverage with a validated fibre system, lower for live-culture or dairy complexity.

### 4. Caffeine propositions are diversifying, not converging on “less” or “none”

| Proposition | Examples | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| Lower/moderate caffeine within a familiar ritual | Pure Leaf 69 mg; ULTIMATE approximately 100 mg; Neutonic 120 mg | Test predictable dose and taste; “lower” here is relative to sampled high-dose cans, not a market-wide time trend. |
| High-dose lifestyle or performance energy | P90X 200 mg; C4 All Hopped Up 200 mg; Alani standard can 200 mg | Established need and distribution; saturated competition and timing constraints. |
| Caffeine-free but stimulant-led | UPDATE; seller discloses300 mg paraxanthine | “No caffeine” must not be confused with non-stimulant or sleep compatible. EU ingredient route remains a separate gate. |
| Genuinely non-caffeine calm / recovery positioning | Unwind; C4 Sparkling Protein | Different customer jobs; neither demonstrates caffeine-free productivity. |

Sources: [Pure Leaf][FI03], [ULTIMATE][FI08], [Neutonic][FI01], [P90X][F006], [C4 flavour][FI22], [Alani][FI18], [UPDATE][FI23], [seller dose][FI25], [Unwind][L40], [C4 protein][FI26].

**Assessment:** the recurring pattern is segmentation by occasion and consumer sensitivity. Energy's commercial validation is strong, but evidence for added cognitive benefit and genuinely non-stimulant focus is weak. Competition is intense; the underlying need is durable. Conventional tea/coffee and transparent caffeine are more straightforward development candidates than novel stimulants. Avoid a universal “no crash” promise and test the time of consumption, serving size and repeat-use expectations.

### 5. Protein and multiple benefits are spreading, but complexity is ahead of sales proof

Starbucks Coffee & Protein adds 22 g protein and 5 g fibre to a 12 oz bottle; C4 introduces caffeine-free Sparkling Protein; Clean Simple Eats reports Costco expansion for its protein soda; Beyond's new energy can combines protein, fibre and caffeine. These are distinct companies and genuine liquid formats. They establish recurring innovation, not yet a commercially validated protein-soda category. [Starbucks][FI10], [C4][FI26], [Clean Simple Eats][F021], [Beyond exact preorder][L24]

**Counterevidence:** Starbucks café drinks do not establish bottled-line performance; Clean Simple Eats' Walmart powder distribution is excluded; Beyond's product reviews may be shared across variants and its energy page remained preorder. OWYN's issuer disclosures show how product quality and weak velocity can undermine an otherwise attractive nutritional proposition. [OWYN][L18]

**Assessment:** nutrition convenience is relevant, and innovation momentum is strong. Line-level evidence is limited and competition includes large firms with specialist production. Protein's basic nutritional role is durable; stacking every benefit may not be. EU transfer requires ingredient/claim checks, but practical feasibility is the greater early burden: taste over shelf life, precipitation, heat stability, allergens and suitable filling. Keep this behind simpler first-launch candidates unless a production partner already has a validated platform.

### 6. Local competition and shelf economics matter more than copying a US aesthetic

Magnesia Plus Focus/Antistress dates back to a 2023 range, and Rohlik currently offers UGO Focus with matcha, niacin and magnesium. Magnesia already lists 2.4 g sugar/100 ml; UGO is a chilled fruit smoothie with 13.8 g/100 ml, not a low-sugar tea. VITHIT has a long-established European business; Spain’s Komvida SuperCalm has an Alcampo listing for a 250 ml refrigerated bottle. These facts contradict a broad “EU has not adopted function” thesis. Czech trade reporting also describes strongly promotional soft-drink shopping; US DTC pricing is not a Czech mass-market business case. [Magnesia][L09], [catalogue][L10], [UGO][L11], [VITHIT][F012], [Komvida announcement][F029], [Spanish retail listing][F030], [Czech retail][L14]

**Counterevidence:** local availability does not prove satisfaction or remove all gaps. UGO is a chilled fruit smoothie, Magnesia a larger mineral-water bottle: an ambient adult can could still win a different occasion. No representative Czech study of willingness to pay for this proposed can was found. Offices and coworking spaces are sensible test locations, but this review found no audited office-channel success to justify treating them as a validated entry route.

**Assessment:** local relevance is high, supported by actual offers and trade data. Momentum and competition already exist; category-wide novelty is weak. The durable opportunity would be a useful drinking habit with repeatable economics. EU transferability improves when development starts from local claims and production constraints. Feasibility depends on keeping assortment small and measuring paid repeat before expanding doors.

## EU/Czech transfer: distinguish the need, the ingredient, the evidence and the claim

| Gate | Current evidence and decision |
|---|---|
| Biological rationale | Caffeine has a more established acute alertness rationale than a proprietary blend. A small caffeine/theanine task study does not establish all-day productivity or superiority of a particular commercial drink. Ingredient identity, dose, delivery, population and duration must match the intended claim. [EFSA][L32], [Owen et al.][L33] |
| Permission to use an ingredient | Check exact material, extraction process and intended beverage category in current authorizations. A US product, GRAS statement, supplier application or EU supplement permission does not clear an ordinary soft drink. Citicoline's beverage-extension application illustrates the distinction. [Union list][L27], [application][L38] |
| Permission to market the benefit | Use the EU register and relevant conditions, then destination-country review. Nutrient claims about normal functions are different from improved work output, anxiety relief or induced sleep. Botanical transitional regimes require specific assessment; the April 2025 CJEU ruling is material. [Register][L31], [court summary][L30] |
| Caffeine and serving design | Specify caffeine per container and concentration; assess required warnings, intended age group and timing. A small can with moderate total caffeine can still exceed the labeling concentration threshold. This report approves no label wording. See the existing [EU evidence memo](../01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md). |
| Sensory and manufacturing | Establish taste, microbial stability, active/nutrient retention and packaging compatibility through the intended shelf life. Protein, suspended botanicals, live cultures and emulsified cannabinoids impose different process needs. Supplier capability is not a product approval. [Refresco][L34], [Hardtank][FI30] |

**CBD extension:** the August 2026 FSA guidance explicitly distinguishes a validated application from authorization and reports no EU CBD novel-food authorizations. Its England/Wales listing system is not an EU route. EFSA's February 2026 provisional level applies only to narrowly specified high-purity supplement formulations, not canned beverages; do not turn its approximately 2 mg/day adult illustration into a drink dose. The Commission's 16 October termination of one CBD-isolate application is an individual outcome, not evidence that every application has failed. Czech enforcement confirms practical consequences for unauthorized foods. [FSA][L42], [EFSA][L26], [Commission decisions][L28], [SZPI][L29]

CBD might add value if a distinct adult segment demonstrably prefers it at a viable price and an exact lawful ingredient/use route becomes available. Goodrays also shows why old CBD benchmarks need rechecking: the FSA’s April 2026 record changed Raspberry & Guava from 30 mg/250 ml to 10 mg/330 ml, corroborated by the current brand page. The commercial change date and sales effect remain unknown. [Register history][C35], [Current SKU][C36]

The cheapest current step is a documentary authorization screen and a **concept-only** comparison of CBD-free calm versus CBD naming, not a consumable pilot. There is no basis here to budget CBD as a near-term fallback for a weak core proposition.

**Evidence limitation:** this is a targeted commercial/innovation review, not a systematic clinical review or final legal assessment. The prior [science and EU memo](../01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md) discusses dose and study mismatches for mushrooms, ashwagandha and CBD; this report does not silently upgrade those ingredient findings into finished-drink evidence.

## Opportunities to validate before choosing the product

These are qualitative priorities, not numerical market forecasts. Compare three customer jobs first: work-break refreshment, evening social participation, and enjoyable everyday nutrition. Coffee/tea is a format variant within the first job. Keep formula and launch country open until customer and production evidence narrows them.

| Opportunity / customer problem | Examples and why a gap may exist | What could invalidate it | Cheapest useful next validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Work-break tea or fruit drink with transparent, modest caffeine.** Adults want a pleasant afternoon reset without another heavy coffee or performance-energy identity. | Pure Leaf, ULTIMATE, Neutonic. The opportunity is a locally preferred taste/serving/occasion, not proven cognitive superiority. | Ordinary coffee/tea/energy wins at equal price; customers only respond to unsupported claims; local claims or costs undermine the concept. | Test identical mock-ups under work-break versus general-refreshment positioning with Czech target buyers; compare existing liquids blind and realistic local prices. Then a small paid repeat-choice test after formula clearance. |
| **Caffeine-free adult evening/social refreshment.** People want to participate and mark the end of work without alcohol or late caffeine. | Recess, Hiyo, Unwind, Goodrays' non-CBD extensions. Ritual, adult flavour and convenience can carry value independently of an immediate active effect. | Buyers require an intoxicating-like sensation; ordinary tonic/non-alcoholic beer wins; low repeat or price resistance. | Occasion interviews and blinded citrus/bitter/herbal samples alongside current substitutes; compare social versus wind-down framing. Keep sleep claims out of the experiment unless independently cleared. |
| **Small familiar coffee or tea with simple nutritional positioning.** A portable daily routine feels more trustworthy than a long ingredient list. | Chameleon; conventional bottled coffee; VITHIT as a simple-function business model. A small standard can and existing recipe could limit initial development. | Crowded local cold-coffee offers already solve the job; taste after storage disappoints; chilled logistics or filling costs remove margin. | Review existing local products and supplier published platforms; bench compare standard coffee/tea bases at two sweetness levels. [Current range][L46] | Demand collapses without a promised acute effect; effective/legal dose cannot fit taste and cost; existing herbal tea solves it. | Concept test explicit caffeine-free refreshment against focus wording under legal review; ask what result and onset the buyer expects. Stop if the proposition depends on unsupported performance promises. |

CBD is a separate conditional variant, not a seventh core launch direction. Dedicated sleep products remain a watchlist: bedtime ritual is clear, but meaningful clinical outcomes, dose, claims and country-specific classification require more work than a general evening drink.

**Suggested spending sequence:** first compare customer jobs and existing substitutes; second screen the most promising concept's ingredients/claims and production platform; third commission limited formulation and shelf-life work; fourth run a small paid-use pilot with a stated repeat interval and channel contribution margin. Define stop criteria before each stage. A small market pilot would not validate physiological efficacy. No customer testing or supplier outreach has been performed in this research.

For production, Hardtank in Poland publicly states roughly 3,000 cans/SKU for standard recipes and 6,000 for custom recipes, in 200/250 ml cans; its FAQ excludes dairy and protein drinks. A listed €3,000 recipe-development fee has reimbursement conditions. These supplier statements make a simple brewed pilot worth costing, but are not quotations or guaranteed suitability. Refresco confirms broader can/bottle capability without establishing a startup-sized minimum for this project. [Hardtank FAQ][FI30], [facility][FI29], [Refresco][L34]

## Overhyped, premature or easy to misread

| Narrative | Evidence-led correction |
|---|---|
| “Every new nootropic can is a productivity breakthrough.” | Ingredient presence, sponsored studies and product launches do not establish incremental finished-drink performance. Demand and biological effect are separate questions. |
| “Caffeine-free means non-stimulating.” | UPDATE's paraxanthine is explicitly a stimulant-led alternative. [UPDATE][FI23] |
| “A CBD brand's growth validates its new CBD-free line.” | New extensions need their own sales. Likewise, THC business results cannot validate CBD-only or non-cannabinoid drinks. |
| “Walmart's three announcements prove three channel trends.” | Recess, Neutonic and Unwind participate in one set launch. That is important, but should be counted once as retailer adoption. [Progressive Grocer][L39] |
| “Celebrity distribution guarantees repeat.” | Unwell's reported wind-down and divergent energy-brand performance contradict that shortcut. Causes should not be invented. [BevNET][L15], [Celsius Holdings investor relations][FI17] |
| “Protein+fibre+energy is automatically an affordable first launch.” | It is a recurring innovation pattern with additional technical burden and insufficient new-line economics. Beyond's energy variant was preorder. [Beyond Plant Protein][L24] |
| “Europe is a blank canvas.” | Existing Czech products, Spanish calm kombucha and Irish functional-drink businesses contradict it. Availability does not prove a saturated market either. [Magnesia][L09], [Rohlik][L11], [Komvida][F029], [Nichols][F012] |
| “A billion-dollar valuation/target is sales.” | Hiyo funding, OLIPOP's ambition and acquisition values answer different questions. Keep each metric's definition and period. |
| “Shots are booming, so every shot ingredient is attractive.” | Fitt's September shot story is a discovery lead. Its broad market forecast and capital events do not isolate focus-shot repeat, and some adjacent formats are excluded. [Fitt shots][L43] |

### What setbacks teach

**Unwell:** reported planned closure despite a creator audience and major partner; no established causal claim about formulation or consumer rejection. **Red Bull/Kroger:** distribution can be withdrawn even for a strong brand; the original report did not establish a definitive cause. **OWYN:** the issuer identifies weak velocities, including new distribution, and integration/product-quality problems; quarterly shipments can improve while takeaway declines. **Kin:** historic revenue growth coexisted with larger losses. **VYBES/Goodrays:** active and pack changes mean a brand name is not a stable formula. These are reasons to control SKU count, verify taste through shelf life and measure reorder economics—not proof the whole functional category is failing. [Unwell][L15], [Red Bull][FI19], [OWYN][L18], [Kin filing][C31], [VYBES][C23], [Goodrays register][C35], [dated tracker](data/signals.csv)

No verified failed-launch rate or comprehensive delisting database was obtained. A stockout, empty collection page or unavailable checkout is not classified as closure without additional evidence.

## What to monitor next

Use the [watchlist](supporting/watchlist.md): weekly launches and retail/negative signals, monthly exact-SKU/price and Czech competitor checks, quarterly earnings and evidence review, and immediate rechecks at a formula decision. Promotion from “launch” to “commercially validated” should require defined sales/velocity and sustained follow-up, not more articles. **This is a monitoring proposal only; no scheduled tasks were created.**

The largest unresolved questions are Czech paid repeat at a viable price, exact shelf-stable process economics, functional effects beyond a familiar caffeine/nutrient rationale, and legally usable positioning for a selected formula. Those are the next validation targets; more generic market-size forecasts will not settle them.
