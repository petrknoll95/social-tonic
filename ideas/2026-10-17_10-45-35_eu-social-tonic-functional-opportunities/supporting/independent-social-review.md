## Conclusion

The report's current decision to fund **learning only** is defensible. The evidence does not justify a claim that a new social tonic serves a demonstrated underserved market, much less a production order. S1 is a relatively simple proposition to falsify; its choice over S2 reflects tractability and potential local learning access more than stronger proved customer demand. Neither “less sugar” nor “smaller dinner bottle” is validated as a paid unmet requirement. Keep S1 conditional, S2 reserve and no country winner.

## S1 challenge and what would change its rank

S1 combines nonbeer, no caffeine, less perceived sweetness and a long social evening. Each property is plausible, but the conjunction is not quantified. Czech public comments establish that some dissatisfaction exists, not how many recurring paying adults share it. Mattoni, unsweetened/non-sweet water alternatives, caffeine-free colas, tea and AF beer substantially narrow the switch story. A new product may be merely another flavoured drink at a higher price.

There is no contradiction in recommending investigation despite this weakness, provided the report says **why S1 is a cheap question rather than a proven opportunity**: ordinary food ingredients and sensory value can avoid a drug-like efficacy dependency; a local shelf/menu comparison can reject it before formula spend. It should rise only if repeated real occasions reveal an unsatisfied subgroup and existing alternatives lose at viable whole-evening prices. It should fall if customers accept Mattoni or a nonbeer zero-sugar substitute, dislike dryness, buy only once out of novelty, or venues will not reorder.

Avoid making all S1 differences mandatory before customers confirm them. “Dry,” bitter, no sweeteners, low sugar, zero sugar and no caffeine are not interchangeable preferences. A sensory concept can be less sweet while containing some sugar, and a sugar-free beverage can be perceived sweet. Maintaining this distinction prevents a formula-first brief disguised as need research.

## S2 challenge and what would change its rank

The single-abstainer750ml waste complaint is one exploratory comment. Existing250–330ml Yoleau/Symples/SirJames products already solve opening-volume inconvenience. Restaurants may pour a larger bottle across several customers; a small bottle can raise packaging cost without adding sufficient value. Retail price€25.50 for Copenhagen750ml proves only an offer exists, not how often people pay it or what they would pay for330ml.

S2 could outrank S1 if actual diners repeatedly choose a smaller food-pairing drink and venues document avoided waste plus repeat orders. It should be dropped if the problem disappears with by-the-glass service/current small formats, is limited to rare celebrations, or consumers prefer water with food. Food-pairing preference needs direct evidence against existing products; it cannot be inferred from a premium bottle's marketing language.

## Country-selection challenge

The six-country sample mostly follows prior repository coverage and operating proximity. Spain has a credible social/food occasion and AF-beer activity; Italy is the original aperitivo reference. Neither is shown less commercially attractive than Austria or Czechia. The initial report explicitly acknowledges this and avoids a score ranking, which is good. Preserve that qualification in the final recommendation: Czechia is a conditional learning location if founder/channel access is real, not an evidence-established market winner. A Spanish/French/Italian local partner or verified underserved cohort could reverse it.

## Commercial challenge

The public lowMOQ is still thousands of cans. If using the lead's6000cans/12venues/26weeks arithmetic, describe it as an assumed sell-through requirement, not predicted demand; product sampling, waste, transport time, retained stock and retailer minimum remaining shelf life all increase pressure. Wholesale shipments are not consumer repeat. DTC cases lower shipping cost per can but increase first-order commitment; affordability must include the entire order and delivery charge. Multiple flavours multiply inventory before they prove incremental demand. Public supplier ingredients lists do not establish regulatory acceptance of exact botanicals or claims.

The final gates should therefore be: documented repeated paid occasion; explicit failure of current alternatives at a viable price; credible country/category/claims route; then documented landed costs and process requirements; only later controlled preference and paid repeat. No fixed numerical repeat hurdle should masquerade as a validated market benchmark. No experiments were conducted in this review.
