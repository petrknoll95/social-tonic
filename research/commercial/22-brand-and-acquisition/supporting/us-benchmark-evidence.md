# US brand and acquisition benchmark evidence

**Research/access date: 17 October 2026, Europe/Prague.** Completed delegated desk-research workstream. US scope unless stated otherwise. Source IDs resolve to the [dated source register](../data/us-benchmark-sources.csv). This is evidence for brand and acquisition experiments, not a formula recommendation or approval of claims. No contacts, purchases or customer tests were made.

Reviewed the root README, research index, [commercial traction](../../../market/02-commercial-traction/README.md), its focus and calm workstreams, and [trends](../../../market/26-trends-and-industry-intelligence/report.md). The distinction between independently measured retail sales, company claims, channel activity and unknown repeat remains essential.

## What the benchmarks establish

The sample supports testing **one clear drinking occasion, a recognizable beverage category, appealing taste and convenient trial**. It does not establish that a complex nootropic formula is necessary, that consumers will repeatedly pay a Czech premium, or that any observed creator campaign caused sales.

- **Stronger commercial evidence:** Bloom's qualifying energy line recorded $82.459m US retail dollars in Nielsen xAOC plus convenience for the 13 weeks ending 15 November 2025, as reproduced in Monster's SEC-filed presentation. This is line-level retail value, not founder revenue or Crisp Apple flavor sales. Circana ranked Bloom Sparkling Energy second among food/beverage products in its 2025 Pacesetter class, released 21 May 2026. Neither source supplies acquisition attribution or cohort retention. [US03], [US04]
- **Narrower company-reported panel evidence:** Recess reports first place by velocity in Sparkling Flavored Water Singles, US MULO, 24 weeks ending 22 March 2026. VYBES reports first place by dollar sales in the SPINS Mood Support segment, US Natural Expanded, 12 weeks ending 9 August 2026. These are different comparison sets; neither discloses numerical velocity, absolute sales or repeat. [US13], [US18]
- **Founder-reported social scale:** a May 2025 Hiyo interview describes over 500,000 cases annually and 212% year-on-year retail growth attributed to SPINS. Annual dates, case definition, country allocation and channel scope are insufficiently specified; do not annualize, convert to cans or infer CAC. [US16]
- **Recent innovation rather than proven success:** Pure Leaf Mental Focus, Chameleon Flash Brew, Magic Mind MAXX, Sly DREAM Orange Cream and the new Neutonic Walmart assortment provide useful launch or merchandising lessons, but inspected sources do not establish SKU-level sales, retention or profitable acquisition. [US05], [US06], [US07], [US08], [US09], [US10], [US20], [US21]

**Provisional package hierarchy to test, not a selected design:** (1) recognizable drink type and flavor; (2) one use occasion; (3) clear caffeine presence/absence and amount if applicable; (4) one accurately substantiated compositional feature; (5) ingredients, nutrient amounts, evidence explanation and required warnings elsewhere. This is an analyst proposal. Do not make “focus” the main benefit merely because a US can does so; even names and implied benefits require review.

## Acquisition mechanisms and what is actually known

| Mechanism observed | Audience / purchase moment | Verified observation versus performance | Low-cost transfer to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder/creator media | People already following productivity or wellness creators; discovery before a work session or workout | Neutonic's founders and Pure Leaf's ambassador are observable. Reach and credibility are not incremental paid purchasers. [US05], [US07] | One small relevant adult creator with identifiable offer and tracked first order; use a holdout or matched posting periods. Distinguish creator fee, production and samples from media cost. |
| Partnership that demonstrates occasion | Desk work/distraction; non-alcoholic choice at a concert | Pure Leaf/Brick and Live Nation/Hiyo explicitly connect setting and product. The inspected announcements do not report partner-attributed CAC, conversion or repeat. [US07], [US15] | Compare a work-session partner with an evening venue only after the core occasion is selected. Each should offer the drink where the need occurs and allow a paid follow-up. |
| Sampling and flavor discovery | Curious shopper or festivalgoer | Hiyo describes event sampling; Magic Mind's historical email bundled MAXX bottles; VYBES announced Instagram giveaways. No common sampling conversion benchmark emerges. [US09], [US16], [US19] | After lawful inventory exists, record sample recipients, paid buyers, net contribution and paid repeat separately. Samples redeemed and attendance are not buyers. |
| Retail discovery | Grocery trip and immediate cold-single purchase | Neutonic's single/four-pack launch and Bloom's Target-first seasonal SKU show different trial/replenishment pack roles. Distribution announcements do not prove active stocked doors or velocity. [US01], [US05] | A few relevant outlets, small starting stock, real checkout price, weekly SKU units and out-of-stock tracking. Do not infer demand from wholesale sell-in. |
| Paid advertising / geo-targeting | Known audience near stocked retail; replenishment after trial | Hiyo's founder interview describes DTC-to-retail geo-campaigns. No inspectable spend/holdout/incremental sales data. [US16] | Geographic ads only around verified stock, using tracked redemption plus outlet comparison. Before inventory, clicks/qualified signup measure comprehension and interest only. |
| Subscription, guarantee, rewards | Known customer replenishing a chosen flavor | Product pages disclose commercial mechanics. Hiyo has reward points; Magic Mind and VYBES promote guarantees. No verified incremental retention or guarantee-adjusted economics. [US08], [US17], [US18] | Establish voluntary second purchases before pushing subscriptions. Track cancellations, refunds, usage frequency and margin after discounts. A reward program is not evidence of an effective referral program. |
| Referral | Satisfied customer sharing an occasion with a peer | Public incremental referral conversion, fraud and retained-cohort results are **unknown** in this sample. | Test only after positive taste/repeat evidence, with reward earned after a real paid order. Avoid rewarding testimonials about effects. |

## Conflicts, exclusions and limits

1. **Formula/version discipline:** Neutonic's October 2026 release lists alpha-GPC and Panax ginseng, whereas the older Tropical Ice marketplace text lists rhodiola. They establish canned formats and separate communications; they do not describe one proven interchangeable formula. The existing US Orange Sunrise link also redirected to a regional homepage in the live browser, so its regional hero art was not used as evidence of the new US pack. [US05], [US06]
2. **Sly conflict:** exact DREAM Orange Cream consumer page lists magnesium citrate and botanicals; a brand wholesale RV page instead discusses magnesium glycinate, theanine and specific quantities. Product version, dates and consistency are unresolved. Exclude that wholesale page's efficacy, absorption and dose narrative from any formula brief. [US20], [US24]
3. **VYBES claim inconsistency:** the launch email equates a large prevalence estimate with deficiency; current site has different prevalence phrasing and implies predictable effects over weeks. These are brand claims, not independent prevalence or finished-drink results. The subscription-versus-daily-use mismatch is a planning lesson, not evidence of customer complaints. [US18], [US19]
4. **Mixed-format contamination:** Magic Mind's gummies, Neutonic's pouches/powders/capsules, Recess powders and Bloom supplement lines are excluded from product benchmarking and revenue attribution. Current site banners for future products, reviews spanning variants, and giveaway counts do not establish drink sales. THC beverages are excluded. No US CBD sales are inferred from a CBD-free line.
5. **No causal channel ranking:** reported growth coexists with distribution, new products, funding, brand equity, promotions and existing audiences. Public source material cannot isolate which element generated demand or retained customers. Repeat-purchase rate, CAC, payback, incremental creator sales and sample-to-paid conversion are unknown across this comparison unless explicitly described otherwise.

## What can be learned before finished inventory

These are proposed tests, not research results: compare unbranded occasion descriptions; measure unaided interpretation of Czech category words; conduct five-second mock-can comprehension; compare a taste-led and a function-led landing page with equal price; ask which current canned/bottled drink would be displaced; test partner-audience fit with concept material; and measure a qualified waitlist against a clearly disclosed concept. A preference, click or signup cannot validate taste, physiological efficacy, actual paid conversion or retention. Ingredient education should remain separate from product promises; scientifically plausible language is not automatically legally usable.

The most useful US lesson is **designing an observable route from a specific moment to a first paid can and then a voluntary repeat**. The least defensible transfer is copying “no crash,” “stress relief,” “float,” “mental performance,” “clinically backed,” deficiency, sleep-onset or absorption promises simply because a US competitor publishes them.
