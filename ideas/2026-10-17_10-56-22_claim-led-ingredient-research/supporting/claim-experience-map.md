# Claim-to-experience map

Research definitions, not proposed pack copy or dosing instructions. A health-related product name, image or testimonial can imply the same claim as an explicit sentence. Outcomes below are proposed ways to test interpretation; an experiment does not itself authorise a food claim.

| Promise | Reasonable consumer interpretation | Outcomes that would address it | Must one drink work? | Onset/duration research question | Repeated-consumption conflict | Communication risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Caffeine-free energy boost | I will feel more awake/energetic soon, without caffeine | Felt energy/vigour and fatigue scales plus sustained attention/psychomotor vigilance; caffeine assay; stimulant comprehension | Yes for “boost”; otherwise say daily nutrition explicitly | At which prespecified post-drink intervals is a meaningful difference detectable, and when does it disappear? | Other stimulants can accumulate; avoiding caffeine may reflect anxiety or sleep sensitivity | Caffeine-free composition is not evidence of an energy benefit or absence of stimulation |
| Natural, sustained energy | Plant/recognisable origin plus several useful hours of felt energy | Provenance/process audit; time course of subjective energy and attention against control; rebound/sleep follow-up | Usually yes in a soda occasion | What interval is sustained, under which baseline fatigue and caffeine use? | Long exposure may conflict with sleep and repeat rounds | Two claims: “natural” and sustained functional benefit; neither follows from ingredient origin |
| Alertness without overstimulation | More awake but no anxiety, palpitations, agitation or excessive arousal | Alertness/sleepiness, vigilance lapses, anxiety/jitters, heart rate/BP, adverse-event frequency | Yes | Does alertness persist without a clinically or personally unwelcome arousal cost? | A second can changes exposure; sensitivity varies | Absence of significant adverse events in small trials does not establish “without” |
| Clear-headed focus | Better selective/sustained attention with fewer mistakes, not merely wakefulness | Task accuracy and speed jointly; distractibility; switching; perceived clarity; ecological task outcomes | Yes for immediate work cue; daily cognitive support is different | Is any effect sustained over actual work and across tasks? | More arousal may impair performance; late use may reduce next-day function via sleep | “Normal mental performance” nutrient wording does not mean enhanced productivity |
| Alcohol-free socialisation | An enjoyable, accepted drink that lets me participate in the occasion without alcohol | Alcohol content through shelf life; liking, inclusion, embarrassment, ordering ease, occasion satisfaction | Experience during occasion; pharmacology unnecessary | Does enjoyment and inclusion survive a full serving and later rounds? | Sugar/acidity/active load multiply even without ethanol | Defensible composition/occasion territory; “alcohol-free”/“0.0” exact Czech conditions differ |
| Relaxed confidence / social ease | Less self-conscious, more conversational, socially comfortable | Controlled actual interaction: self-consciousness, approach/avoidance, conversation participation, observer ratings; anxiety separately | Usually yes | Is there a detectable effect during real interaction, with unblinding/expectation controlled? | Disinhibition and impairment are not desirable hidden endpoints; sedatives conflict with casual repeat use | Strong implied psychological effect; general stress or sleep data do not substantiate it |
| Natural calm | Noticeable reduction of tension with credible natural provenance | State tension/calm/stress ratings under ordinary and experimental stress; process verification | Often yes; chronic stress must be separately described | Does any relaxation arise after one serving or only after weeks? | Sedation, drug interactions and accumulated botanicals | Natural ≠ safe; botanical claims need exact authorised/transitional basis |
| Unwinding without unwanted sedation | Easier transition out of work while remaining mentally and physically capable | Calm and enjoyment plus vigilance, psychomotor speed, sleepiness and adverse events | Yes in evening occasion | When does calm appear; are impairment and next-day effects excluded within meaningful margins? | Repeated cans, alcohol or sedatives can change response | “Non-drowsy” is not established by failing to measure impairment; never infer driving compatibility |
| Reduced fatigue | Less exhaustion, potentially without stimulation | Validated fatigue instruments; baseline cause/status; task endurance | Depends: acute fatigue vs weeks-long symptoms | What population and time scale improve? | A temporary effect can mask a need for rest; nutrient or botanical intake accumulates | Deficiency correction, clinical fatigue and rested-person enhancement are distinct |
| Supports energy metabolism | Nutrients help normal cellular processes | Qualifying nutrient content/retention; applicable authorised claim; nutritional-status context | No immediate sensation implied by science | No acute onset is specified by a normal-function claim | Fortification across products can raise intake beyond appropriate levels | Lawful wording must not be surrounded by an unsupported “instant boost” message |
| Hydration-related clarity | Less foggy/tired when underhydrated | Hydration state, thirst, fatigue, vigilance; same-volume water control | Possibly if initially underhydrated | When does fluid replacement correct impairment; do electrolytes add anything? | Mineral excess and high sugar may be unnecessary for sedentary use | Restoring impairment is not enhancement above hydrated baseline; sports claims have specific conditions |
| Motivation / readiness to act | More willingness to start/continue work | Effort-based choice, task initiation/persistence, subjective motivation separately from speed | Usually yes | Does willingness improve beyond novelty and expectation, and for how long? | Stimulant reinforcement and sleep trade-offs | Reaction-time improvement is not motivation or real productivity |
| Better mood / emotional wellbeing | Feeling happier or less distressed | Validated positive/negative affect scales; baseline symptoms; daily function | Acute and repeated versions must be separated | Transient enjoyment or sustained mood change? | Repeated psychotropic use needs stronger safety evidence | Clinical depression/anxiety treatment language crosses food-claims boundaries |
| “Lasting” | Benefit persists for a meaningful portion of my occasion | Prespecified time-by-treatment contrasts, meaningful-difference threshold, all relevant time points | Generally yes | Continuous benefit, isolated late test difference, drug half-life, or chronic adaptation? | A long half-life may extend adverse effects | These four meanings are not interchangeable; a six-hour test schedule proves none alone |
| “No crash” | No later fatigue/mood dip, withdrawal or impaired sleep | Repeated measurements through same day, next-day sleep/fatigue, habitual-use and withdrawal controls | One serving and repeated-use scenarios | Which rebound interval and comparator define a crash? | Frequent use can create tolerance/withdrawal even when acute ratings look smooth | Undefined absolute comparative promise; lack of evidence of crash ≠ evidence of absence |
| “Without jitters” | I will not feel shaky/anxious even if sensitive | Validated symptoms, adverse-event incidence and severity, sensitive consumers, prespecified noninferiority margin | Yes, including foreseeable repeat use | Across what exposure and follow-up? | Small trials often exclude the people most likely to react | Do not convert lower mean anxiety or nonsignificance into a zero-risk guarantee |
| “Non-drowsy” | Calm with preserved wakefulness and ability to function | Sleepiness, objective vigilance/psychomotor measures, meaningful exclusion margins | Yes | At peak exposure, with repeated servings, and after the occasion? | Calm ingredients may have additive effects with medicines/alcohol | No impairment data = unknown, not safe for driving |
| “Natural” | Source and processing match what the buyer considers natural | Supplier traceability, production description, residuals/purity, consumer comprehension | Composition claim, not time-dependent efficacy | Not an onset question | Natural origin does not lower toxicological risk | No universal shortcut from plant origin to whole-drink natural claim; natural flavouring has its own rules |
