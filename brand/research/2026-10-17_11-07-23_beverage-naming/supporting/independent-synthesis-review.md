# Independent synthesis and protocol review

**Reviewed 17 October 2026, Europe/Prague (UTC+02:00).** Reviewer: complementary empirical workstream agent, reviewing the lead synthesis and language/legal workstream. This is an independent challenge of the synthesis and an outside review of the language/legal reasoning, **not an independent replication of this reviewer's own empirical/consumer coding**, a legal opinion, or a new consumer study.

Reviewed [report](../report.md), [naming brief](naming-brief.md), [evaluation and testing](evaluation-and-testing.md), [language/legal memo](workstreams/language-legal.md) and its source metadata. Sample counts, beverage-source integration and link resolution were still being finalised by the lead; this review does not certify those later merges. No shared report, brief or protocol files were edited by this reviewer.

## Overall assessment

The synthesis answers the central research question without claiming that a naming style causes commercial success. It appropriately separates original intent, analyst associations, market evidence and controlled studies. It keeps the founder's positioning open, preserves the CBD-free core, and recommends territories rather than final names. No unsupported claim that a sampled name is legally available was found. The substantive changes below improve the eventual test design and make the architecture brief more directly responsive to the user's request.

## Review findings and corrections

| Priority | Finding | Why it matters | Proposed resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | In the protocol, “counterbalanced first-exposure arms” should explicitly mean that only each participant's first route contributes to an **unprimed** pronunciation or audio-spelling result. | Counterbalancing order balances some order effects; it cannot make a second exposure unfamiliar again. Seeing the spelling first contaminates later audio transcription, and hearing the intended reading first contaminates spontaneous visual pronunciation. | Use independent audio-first and visual-first groups, or analyse only the first-route task as unprimed. Label later crossover observations as learned/familiarised usability. Do not pool them with first exposure. |
| Medium | The delayed-memory task follows pronunciation/transcription, associations, category expectations and benefits questions. This is an **elaborated-exposure** memory task rather than a clean one-exposure memory comparison. | Open questions can lead people to repeat and rehearse some names more than others. Longer responses can also change the interval before recall. This is not fatal, but the test label and exposure controls must match the inference. | Either create a separate memory arm with equal timed exposure, fixed filler and recall before the explanatory tasks, or explicitly call the result delayed recall after a standardised elaboration task, control timing/repetitions, and refrain from claiming hear-once recall. Keep audio and visual memory results separate. |
| Medium | The architecture stress tests cover flavours, social/functional families and reformulation, but not explicitly country-level claim restrictions. | The user specifically asks whether the architecture can survive differences in permitted claims. Merely moving a benefit into a family name does not remove the claim. | Add a fourth prototype: keep the parent constant, translate the factual category and flavour, and remove or replace the functional family/claim for a market where its legal route is unavailable. Test whether buyers still recognise the source and understand the product without an implied promise left in the parent. Treat this as a planned scenario, not a claim that a particular country prohibits a word. |
| Low | Rhythm, alliteration and syllable structure are mentioned, but the evidence boundary could be stated more explicitly. | Readers may otherwise substitute familiar naming folklore for an unaddressed mechanism. | Add a short statement that this review found no basis for an independent universal “best rhythm”, alliteration rule or syllable ceiling. Record speech rhythm and hesitation during testing; do not treat a memorable jingle-like sound as proof of better spelling, recall or sales. |

**Resolved during review:** the initial report listed the six territories but delegated all their risks and validation questions to the supporting brief. A later read showed the lead had added the complete six-territory comparison to report section 10. That resolves the principal self-contained-report concern. The supporting brief can remain the operational handoff rather than carrying essential conclusions unavailable in the report.

The low-priority rhythm note is a coverage clarification for the lead to consider, not a substantive blocker. ## Checks that passed

- **Survivorship and denominators:** the report explicitly says the purposive sample is not representative and cannot establish whether a pattern is more frequent among winners than entrants. Its small adverse-outcome group is not presented as a matched control. Final counts still require recomputation after the beverage merge.
- **Commercial evidence:** manufacturer revenue, scanned retail sales, segment results, distribution, longevity, losses and ownership changes are not collapsed into one success metric. Funding and valuation do not substitute for sales or repeat purchase. The report correctly avoids assigning all Monster segment sales to one consumer name and avoids using Oatly group revenue as a canned-SKU result.
- **Historical versus current status:** Casper's historical loss/liquidity evidence is not used to declare it nonexistent; ZICO's phase-out and buyback can coexist with a current offer. A brand's current offer is not proof of current commercial scale.
- **Origins and interpretation:** the text warns that real-word associations are not founder intent. In particular it does not invent a Casper ghost story or treat Warby Parker as founders' surnames. The final merged dataset must preserve origin-confidence fields rather than turn an analyst interpretation into a documented origin.
- **Empirical boundaries:** the table distinguishes observational copytesting, fictional-name experiments, personal-name fluency evidence, abstracts and author summaries. It does not treat 3,528 repeated observations in the length study as 3,528 people. It distinguishes recall, recognition and spoken retrieval, and does not treat cross-language bilingual evidence as Czech or EU-wide validation.
- **Flexibility:** a broad name is not automatically preferred to a focused ingredient platform. Expansion depends on learned associations and product fit, not only word breadth. Masterbrand, family, flavour, descriptor and company are usefully separated.
- **Packaging and channels:** proposed shelf, listing, creator and bar tasks are labelled operational tests. There is no inferred conversion lift from an attractive can or engagement. Current online ordering does not establish DTC origin.
- **Neutrality:** no final candidate is generated. The protocol uses matched design, unprompted associations, delayed recall before recognition, no-answer options, language-specific reporting and hard holds separate from preference. The sample-size illustration is explicitly not a power calculation or representativeness claim.

## Outside review of language/legal source logic

The workstream appropriately uses official or credible linguistic references for linguistic facts, then labels the naming implications as desk hypotheses. Czech inflection rules do not become a claim that every Czech consumer uses one form. Canadian French guidance is explicitly limited to the general phonetic observation; it is not passed off as French-market testing. The chosen language panel is justified as diagnostic rather than an assertion about all Europeans.

The trademark/claims framework keeps distinctiveness, similar-mark risk, company registration, domain availability and food presentation separate. It does not confuse a Nice class with clearance or claim that an invented spelling solves phonetic conflict. It labels excerpt-only EUIPO/EUR-Lex access and avoids predicting registration outcomes from those excerpts. Specialist review of actual goods, territories and final presentation remains necessary.

Selected primary-source spot checks performed independently on 17 October 2026:

1. [ÚJČ, inflection of institutional/company names](https://prirucka.ujc.cas.cz/?id=398) supports the memo's focus on established grammatical gender, spoken endings, variation and indeclinable forms. It supports the proposed conversation tests, not a commercial acceptance rate.
2. [Regulation 1924/2006 consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2006/1924/2014-12-13), Article 1(3), supports the treatment of potentially claim-bearing brand/fancy names and the need for a related compliant accompanying claim. The source page identifies this consolidated version as current. The report does not turn this into permission for an arbitrary stronger promise.
3. [SZPI, alcohol wording guidance, 12 March 2025](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/clanek/pouzivani-informaci-o-potravine-zero-alkohol-bez-alkoholu-no-alkohol-nealkoholicky-0-alkoholu-0-0-alkoholu-na-nealkoholickych-napojich.aspx), questions 1–5, supports the distinction between the statutory nonalcoholic category and voluntary zero/absence language. The report correctly treats 0.05% as an analytical-method discussion rather than a new general legal allowance. The original guidance excludes dealcoholised grape-wine products; the report's warning against generalising across categories should remain.
4. [CJEU C-721/24, Vista-Life, paragraphs 41–53](https://juris.curia.europa.eu/juris/document/document_print.jsf?cid=8493225&docid=315283&doclang=EN&pageIndex=0&text=) supports context-sensitive treatment of consumption-method/frequency statements. Pure practical instructions and an explicit or implied health connection are distinguished. The memo uses this as a contextual framework, not as a beverage-name clearance result.

No material legal-source contradiction was identified in these spot checks. They are not a complete audit of all current law, every cited judgment or an assessment of any future candidate. Some source access remains partial, as disclosed in the register. The final review record should preserve these limits and record which proposed corrections the lead applied.
