# Germany, Austria and Poland: competition evidence

As of **17 October 2026**. Completed desk-research workstream; local retail audits, buyer interviews and sell-through data remain outstanding. Read with the [integrated report](../report.md), [21-row country dataset](../data/central-eu-competitors.csv) and [38-source register](central-eu-sources.csv). All source access dates are 17 October 2026; undated product pages are recorded as publication date unknown. This workstream extends the [US landscape](../../01-us-product-landscape/README.md) without treating US formulations, claims or traction as evidence of EU legality or sales.

Germany, Austria and Poland are practical comparison markets because each borders Czechia. Germany adds a large nearby German-language comparison, Austria allows examination of another German-language retail system, and Poland tests whether the same propositions appear at different local price points. This is a purposive sample, not a market-size ranking or an exhaustive census.

## Reading the evidence

Every product row is a finished liquid in a can or bottle. A bottle or can is the comparison serving, even where a manufacturer describes the product as a food supplement; that classification is not an endorsement of its legal status. Supplement routines and freshly prepared coffee can be interview comparators, but powders, capsules, tea bags, concentrates and café-prepared drinks are not product rows. UK-origin brands listed here count only where Austria/Poland distribution evidence is separately identified.

Prices are observed asking prices, not weighted market averages. They retain local currency, distinguish packs from singles and remove refundable deposits only when the source explicitly includes them. Delivery fees are excluded. The Crodino restaurant price is a hospitality occasion benchmark and must not be ranked directly against grocery prices. A promotion is not a stable willingness-to-pay estimate.

Evidence quality is claim-specific. Manufacturer pages are useful for declared packaging/formula; retailer pages for the specific channel and asking price; announcements for intended distribution. None establishes clinical effects or exact-SKU sell-through. Dataset **B** means a direct listing with material availability/scale limitations; **C** means an announcement, cross-border listing with delivery uncertainty, or indexed menu. No national brand-awareness survey was found for these exact comparisons. “Established,” “emerging” and “unknown” are analyst business-stage classifications, not sales rankings, profitability assessments or measured consumer recognition. An established parent may have an emerging drink line.

Germany has direct focus and calm propositions in addition to conventional stimulants and social substitutes. This sample therefore contradicts any claim that “focus in a can” or “an evening functional drink” is absent. It does **not** establish saturation: no exact-SKU sales, velocity, repeat purchase or national awareness data was verified for the functional challengers.

The mature alternative category is measurable, but its geography must be respected. Destatis reported **579 million litres of alcohol-free beer produced for sale in Germany in 2024**, up 96.1% from 2014, with €606 million production value. This is German production, not German consumption or BRLO sales; coverage includes larger manufacturing establishments and alcohol-free/mixed beer up to 0.5% ABV, with no packaged-only split used here. Austria combines purpose-built DTC challengers with established retail substitutes. Its 2025 private-label kombucha launch is evidence of buyer participation in fermented drinks, not evidence of unmet demand for another kombucha. An ambient functional drink could avoid the cold-chain requirements of the specific raw kombucha listed above; whether that advantage matters enough to buyers is a hypothesis to test, not a demonstrated gap.

The Austrian brewing association reports **298,000 hl of domestic alcohol-free beer sales in 2025**, or **3.8%** of the total domestic beer/AF-beer series, versus revised 281,000 hl in 2024. The table explicitly excludes exports. This is category evidence, not Gösser sales and not restricted to cans/bottles. [2026 statistics, page 10](https://bierland-oesterreich.at/news-medien/statistikbrosch%C3%BCren/send/8-statistik/447-statistik-2026.html).

## CBD: a separate and weakly evidenced route

SoStoned's exact listing explicitly declares CBD, making it more than generic hemp-flavour branding. However, the dose is undisclosed, THC-free is an unverified seller statement, delivery is not confirmed, and the operator is in Bulgaria. It is evidence of a German-targeted online offer, **not domestic German shelf distribution, demonstrated German demand or legal compliance**. The sample did not establish a comparably verifiable current CBD RTD listing in Austria or Poland. This is a research limitation, not proof of absence or zero demand.

National source context supports independent legal checks:

- **Germany:** the BfR FAQ dated **15 November 2022** records the historical BVL position on CBD foods and stresses that crop THC thresholds do not establish finished-food safety. It is not a current authorization register and must not be used to override later EFSA or EU decisions. [BfR](https://www.bfr.bund.de/fragen-und-antworten/thema/fragen-und-antworten-zu-den-gesundheitlichen-risiken-von-hanfhaltigen-lebensmitteln-und-futtermitteln/).
- **Austria:** the Burgenland administrative court's **9 April 2025** decision discusses the novel-food authorization requirement for CBD extracts. The case-specific reasoning does not establish clearance of any beverage in this matrix or resolve later authorizations. [RIS decision](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Lvwg/LVWGT_BU_20250409_E_156_02_2020_001_063_00/LVWGT_BU_20250409_E_156_02_2020_001_063_00.html).
- **Poland:** regional sanitary-inspector guidance places cannabinoid extracts within the novel-food authorization framework. The undated page also contains an obsolete crop THC threshold, which is deliberately **not** used here as current law. [Podlaski inspector](https://www.gov.pl/web/wsse-bialystok/zastosowanie-konopi-wloknistych-w-produkcji-zywnosci).

The integrated regulatory memo provides the current EU-level synthesis, including the September 2026 synthetic-CBD supplement opinion. A positive opinion for a specified ingredient, use and dose cannot by itself clear an arbitrary CBD beverage. No compliant CBD launch route is established by this workstream.

## Implications and questions for validation

These are hypotheses for low-cost research, not chosen markets, formulas or budgets:

1. **Workday focus with clear caffeine disclosure and a specific occasion.** Existing focus products show that the proposition is legible; no verified retail velocity tells us which consumers still struggle to find a satisfactory option. Test the particular afternoon/work task, preferred portion, caffeine tolerance, taste and acceptable price against an exact locally stocked RTD tea/energy drink, not an abstract energy category.
2. **Caffeine-free adult wind-down ritual.** RELAX and the social substitutes show existing competition. Investigate whether buyers want a predictable evening ritual, appealing adult flavour or a promised effect, and whether those reasons support repeat purchase without non-compliant claims. Demand for CBD should be measured separately from demand for calm.
3. **Distribution access as a possible advantage.** DTC focus/calm products can require multipacks and shipping. A single-can trial in an office, specialist retailer or hospitality venue may reduce trial friction. This is an identifiable channel difference in the sampled offers, not proof of a nationwide distribution gap; store audits and buyer interviews are required.
4. **Ambient adult refreshment versus chilled fermentation.** A shelf-stable drink could be easier to handle than the specific chilled raw kombucha sampled in Austria. Validate buyer constraints and customer taste preferences before treating operational convenience as consumer demand.

Before relying on an opportunity, obtain store-level presence and facings, on-shelf price and promotion frequency, units/store/week, repeat purchase, delisting reasons and competitor share by occasion. Buyer interviews should establish margins, minimum shelf life, deposit handling, case sizes and replenishment expectations. Consumer interviews should separate felt need, willingness to pay, claimed benefit belief and observed consumption. This research conducted no interviews, purchases, supplier outreach or tests.

Excluded/limited leads: powder and concentrate products; generic hemp branding without declared CBD; older Tranquini listings without sufficiently current Austrian availability; ambiguous NOCCO regional caffeine formulations; LevlUp powder FAQs; a 330ml Kombuchery clearance listing that did not describe the current 400ml DTC SKU. These exclusions prevent false category breadth and formulation mixing.
