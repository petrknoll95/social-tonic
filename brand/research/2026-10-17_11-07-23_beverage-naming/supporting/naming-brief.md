# Brief for the subsequent naming team

**Prepared 17 October 2026 · Europe/Prague, UTC+02:00.** Research brief only. No candidate name, positioning, formula or launch is selected. Read the [research report](../report.md), [sample](../data/brand-name-analysis.csv) and [testing protocol](evaluation-and-testing.md) together.

## Decision to make before generating a shortlist

Define the first buyer, drinking occasion, expected sensory experience, credible product promise, indicative price/channel and launch country. Compare a social/unwinding proposition with a defined functional proposition; do not quietly merge them. Existing repository recommendations about work breaks, 250 ml cans or one launch flavour remain hypotheses. The fixed brief is a finished nonalcoholic liquid in cans or bottles, a Czech founder and potential EU reach; the core must work without CBD.

Write one sentence each for: “chosen instead of…”, “at the moment when…”, “tastes/feels like…”, and “we can substantiate…”. Identify what the drink explicitly promises and what customers might infer. If the team cannot agree on these, generate several labelled territory explorations but postpone a final naming decision.

## What the name should do

Provide a recognisable verbal identity that people can say, recall and find. Give a useful cue to the intended world without requiring a story to justify every syllable. Work beside a plain local-language drink descriptor. Remain credible if flavour, permitted ingredients or a supported benefit changes. Do not demand that one word explain company, beverage, function, flavour, lifestyle and mechanism at once.

The evidence supports testing these jobs separately; it does not establish a universal word count, a winning phoneme or a sales-predictive naming formula. An evocative name with a confusing pronunciation can be repaired only if the real channel makes that repair practical. A clear descriptive name may be strategically right for a narrow product, despite weaker exclusivity and flexibility.

## Six territories to explore, without name candidates

| Territory | What it could communicate and accommodate | Research basis / precedent | Risks | Required language and legal validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A shared moment or transition** | An invitation to pause, gather or mark the end of one activity; potentially social and work-break families. | Recess and Daytrip show occasion/movement language; context-dependent semantic retrieval findings support relevant cues, not these specific names. | Crowded pause/reset language; a school break may feel childish; a transition may imply an immediate mood effect. | Unaided Czech interpretations; whether the intended moment survives German/Polish/French; similar names; health implications in full pack context. |
| **Connection and welcoming identity** | Participation, conviviality and a shared table; social drink first, potentially broader refreshments. | Hiyo/Kin/TÖST illustrate belonging and ritual cues; these are observed positioning choices, not proven sales mechanisms. | Generic “together” language; forced sociability; implication of confidence, disinhibition or intoxication. | Natural bar-order usage; inclusion without moralising about alcohol; slang; phonetic/semantic mark conflicts; implied-effect review. |
| **A sensory or natural-world metaphor** | Texture, brightness, atmosphere, landscape or movement; adaptable to flavours and different moments. | Sierra Mist, Red Bull, Pentire and Patagonia illustrate varied metaphor/place mechanisms and very different outcomes. | Generic botanical sameness, imagined provenance, organic/natural inference, geographically narrow credibility. | Literal translations, protected geographical references where relevant, truthful origin/ingredient expectations, bilingual associations. |
| **An approachable coined word** | A distinctive identity whose meaning can be built through experience; broad social and functional product families. | Pepsi, Hiyo, ZICO and OLIPOP demonstrate different coinage routes. Short invented names also occur in failed/relaunched businesses. | Spelling from audio; sound-alikes; hidden claim roots; absence of initial category meaning; crowded trademark space. | Pronunciation and audio transcription in each market, search confusion, variant spellings, full legal similarity search. No automatic two-syllable preference. |
| **An unexpected everyday word or juxtaposition** | A memorable point of view and recognisable voice; suitable where brand entertainment is part of the proposition. | Apple/Away and Liquid Death show ordinary vocabulary used outside literal category expectations. | Ordinary-word search competition; humour fails or overwhelms appetite; inappropriate death/drug/sedation cues; similarity to category rebels. | Spontaneous associations before product reveal; equivalent packaging; creator and café speech tests; confusing existing marks. Explore the mechanism, not imitation of the precedents. |
| **Credible craft, place or personal signature** | Care, recipe authorship or local origin; taste-led premium positioning with multiple flavours. | Founder/personal and place precedents across the sample; exact origins documented only where sources support them. | Founder dependence, invented heritage, possessive punctuation, origin expectations, difficult Czech or foreign inflection. | Verify factual story and origin; identify who owns relevant personal rights; local pronunciation and naming conventions; geographic and trademark review. |

These are six alternative starting territories. They are not a ranking. A narrowly ingredient-led or direct-benefit name remains an eligible challenger once the durable ingredient/claim route is chosen; it is deliberately not the default while the product is unsettled.

## Architecture to prototype

Use placeholders only: **[masterbrand] / [optional family] / [flavour] / [plain category descriptor]**. Add a functional statement only when the exact formula and country permit it. Family is optional for one formula; do not manufacture complexity to fill a hierarchy.

Prepare three architecture stress tests: two flavours of one formula; social and functional families under the same parent; ingredient reformulation without changing the masterbrand. Add a fourth stress test: remove or localise a functional statement for another country and verify that the parent, family distinction and lawful drink description remain understandable. A possible later CBD family must be explicitly separated and earn its own legal and commercial case. Do not embed CBD or a physiological outcome in the parent solely to preserve an unapproved option.

A company name may differ from the consumer brand. A trademark registration, company registration, domain and social handle answer different questions. Do not describe a candidate as “available” from a domain check.

## Output expected from the naming team

For each candidate in a later assignment, deliver: territory; intended language and pronunciation variants; literal versus intended meaning; likely category/benefit inferences; descriptor and flavour stack; origin story with evidence if claimed; obvious confusion checks with dated search scope; architecture stress tests; unanswered language/claims questions. Keep rejected candidates and reasons so selection is reviewable.

Use the [evaluation form](evaluation-and-testing.md) to distinguish hard gates, measured usability and preference. No trademark filing, domain registration, recruitment or claim approval is authorised by this document. The next naming exercise should return multiple viable options and evidence, not disguise personal taste as a winner.
