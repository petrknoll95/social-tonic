# Benefit evidence, US benchmarks and regulatory gates

**Research date: 17 October 2026.** This supporting workstream covers the proposed Czech/EU market comparison; it does not approve a formula or label. All consumer benchmarks below are finished liquids in cans or bottles. Raw manufacturing inputs and scientific study preparations are discussed only as ingredients/evidence, not consumer product benchmarks. No outreach, purchase, supplier quote or consumer test was performed.

## Implication for category selection

**The best first hypothesis is a CBD-free, modest-caffeine workday drink, compared directly with a caffeine-free adult evening drink.** This is an analyst prioritization for validation, not a finding that either new proposition has product-market fit. Acute alertness has a stronger ingredient rationale than a generic cognition stack. The largest commercial evidence belongs to established energy, while focus-specific sales and paid repeat remain unknown. A work-break tea/fruit can can be tested on taste, portion, caffeine amount and price without relying on a claim of superior cognition. Final focus/productivity wording—including a product name that implies a health benefit—requires its own claims review.

The evening challenger should test adult taste and social ritual separately from claims of a felt calming effect. Dedicated sleep, elaborate non-stimulant cognition, CBD, and protein-heavy formulas require more evidence or development. Gut/fibre and activity-specific hydration remain meaningful secondary alternatives. These judgments should inform, rather than replace, the country scorecard and reachable-customer experiments.

Nutrient-claim conditions and national classification must be checked against the final formula; a legally usable claim is not a forecast of consumer-perceived benefit.

|---|---|---|
| **Alertness/energy** | EFSA describes caffeine increasing alertness/reducing sleepiness; 200 mg single-dose and 400 mg/day healthy-adult safety references cover all sources. Even 100 mg near bedtime may impair sleep. [BR18][BR18] | Quantified caffeine in a can has a plausible acute rationale. These are not dose targets, personal safety guarantees, a claim authorization, or evidence of better work output. Coffee/energy habits show existing occasions, not switching to this new brand. |
| **Focus/concentration beyond caffeine** | Owen et al. ## Shared EU gates before any physical pilot

1. **Ingredient identity and food category:** obtain a full specification, processing history and food-use basis for each substance. The current Union list controls novel-food identity, authorized use, limits and restrictions. An application, US GRAS assertion or supplement authorization is not ordinary beverage permission. [BR29][BR29]
2. **Claims independently:** check the register and actual legal act, including conditions and warnings. The CJEU's 30 April 2025 Novel Nutriology ruling confirms the general prohibition on unapproved botanical health claims, with specific transitional exceptions; it is not permission for every pending claim. A “focus,” “stress” or “sleep” product name can itself create an implied-claim issue. [BR30][BR30], [BR31][BR31]
3. **Caffeine concentration and total exposure:** covered drinks above 150 mg/L require the Annex III warning and caffeine amount per 100 ml; the coffee/tea-name exception is specific. For illustration only, 60 mg in 250 ml = 240 mg/L and still exceeds the warning threshold. [BR32][BR32]
4. **Do not copy fashionable forms:** the citicoline extension document is an application for additional food/beverage uses, not evidence that these were authorized. Magnesium L-threonate's Regulation 2024/2694 authorizes a specified adult-supplement use, not a standard calm soda. The reviewed sources establish no general soft-drink clearance for these US stacks. [BR33][BR33], [BR34][BR34]
5. **Physical-product validation:** agree microbial controls, sensory shelf life, nutrient/active retention, caffeine batch variation, packaging compatibility and any residual alcohol. A contract packer's ingredient capability is not a food-use approval. A sensory pilot cannot establish clinical efficacy.

## CBD extension: separate demand, effect, supply, safety and legality

**Demand:** no reliable CBD-only RTD penetration, premium willingness-to-pay or repeat estimate for CZ/AT/DE/PL/NL was established. THC growth, oils/supplements and generic alcohol reduction cannot fill that gap. Daytrip establishes a commercial reference with unresolved current stock, not an EU addressable market. VYBES establishes a CBD exit and a CBD-free replacement, not proof that nobody wants CBD.

**Safety:** EFSA's 9 February 2026 provisional level is 0.0275 mg/kg/day (0.0275 × 70 = 1.925 mg/day), only for qualifying ≥98% CBD **supplements**, without nanoparticles, with production safety and genotoxicity conditions. Under-25s, pregnancy/lactation and medicine users were excluded from the safety conclusion. This is neither a beverage-safe dose nor a target dose. [BR27][BR27]

The 18 September 2026 opinion (amended 21 September) did conclude that a specific synthetic ≥98% CBD ingredient dissolved exclusively in MCT oil was safe under proposed **supplement** conditions, maximum 2 mg/day for adults over 25 excluding pregnancy/lactation; medicine-user safety was not established. It did not assess efficacy or authorize a beverage. It cannot be generalized to aqueous nano-emulsions or botanical extracts. [BR28][BR28]

**Legal route and claims:** the current authorization resources reviewed did not establish an applicable EU permission for the proposed CBD canned/bottled drink. A positive EFSA opinion is one step and is not the Commission authorization. Require an actual authorization matching the ingredient, food category, dose, process and any data-protection conditions—or a documented non-novel status determination for the exact material—before consumable development. No applicable authorized CBD focus/calm/sleep claim was established. National enforcement/classification still matters. [BR29][BR29], [BR30][BR30], [BR35][BR35], [BR39][BR39], [BR40][BR40]

**Sourcing:** no supplier is qualified or quoted. Prior research identified a regional application lead, not cleared beverage supply. Documentary diligence must cover source/production traceability; exact authorized identity and intended use; independent CBD/THC/other-cannabinoid assay with detection limits; residual solvents, pesticides, metals and microbiology; emulsion ingredients and particle characteristics; heat/light/oxygen/container stability; dose uniformity and end-of-shelf-life potency. THC-free marketing and hemp-crop thresholds cannot substitute for measured finished-drink compliance. This research does not recommend a CBD dose or an intoxicating variant.

**Decision:** keep CBD outside the base commercial scenarios. A concept-only comparison may test incremental adult preference for CBD naming; it must be explicit that no product is currently offered. Advance only when separate demand and a documented lawful beverage route justify the extra cost. A strong CBD-free proposition must stand on its own.

## Lowest-cost development and validation implications

Start with interviews about an observed occasion and priced mock-up choice against actual local canned/bottled alternatives; then use a compliant existing beverage base for blinded sensory preference. Measure paid trial separately from repeat at an explicit interval. Free sampling, a survey preference, an email signup and a first purchase belong to different funnel stages. The same adult who drinks coffee, energy and an evening tonic counts once in the reach model; category experiments are alternatives until a measured overlap matrix exists.

An outsourced standard tea/coffee base deserves costing before a proprietary stack. Hardtank in Poland publicly states about **3,000 cans/SKU** for standard recipes, **6,000** for custom, in **200/250 ml** cans; its custom formulation fee is €3,000 with conditional reimbursement. It excludes animal dairy and protein beverages from that platform. These are public supplier statements, not quotes, guaranteed capacity, formula approval or landed cost. [BR41][BR41] Do not order a minimum run simply to validate concept interest.

The cheapest sequence is: (1) document ingredient/claim feasibility for two simple concepts; (2) compare work-break versus evening need, taste and realistic local price; (3) obtain cost/terms only after the concept survives; (4) run a small paid-use repeat test with predeclared stop rules. Interviews and a commercial pilot cannot validate physiological efficacy. No such tests have been performed here.
