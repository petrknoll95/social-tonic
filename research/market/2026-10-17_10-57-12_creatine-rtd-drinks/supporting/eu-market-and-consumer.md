# EU availability, competition and consumer proposition

Research observed **17 October 2026**, Europe/Prague (UTC+02:00). This is the EU workstream input to the integrated report. Its first-pass evidence files are [availability observations](../data/eu-workstream.csv), [SKU details](../data/eu-products-workstream.csv) and [sources](eu-sources.csv). E-series IDs resolve in that source register. No purchases, company contact, consumer research or laboratory tests were conducted.

## What is established

The EU is **not an empty market** for creatine-led finished liquids. Two direct sparkling-water comparators were verified: French **Humble+ Créatine Water** and Finnish **Virta Creatine**. Swedish **Clean MONO** is still water; **Clean Sav:D +Creatine** is a caffeinated carbonated energy drink. German **Ehrmann High Protein Creatine Drink** is a still dairy drink, with actual Czech and Polish retail listings as well as German sales channels. These adjacent formats are relevant competition but must not be counted as creatine sodas. Availability demonstrates a purchasable proposition, not sufficient demand, retention or profitability. [E001, E004, E008–E019, E022–E029]

No qualifying locally stocked creatine soda was verified in Czechia, Poland or the Netherlands in this workstream. That is a bounded search result, **not a claim of market absence**. Humble+ explicitly offers delivery to several other EU countries; these are cross-border routes, not evidence of domestic retail distribution. No completed checkout verified any route. The searches found no persuasive representative EU survey, price experiment or paid repeat-purchase cohort for the creatine-soda format. Powder familiarity and protein/energy-drink distribution cannot fill that gap.

## Stock, shipping and launch caveats

Exact Ehrmann SKU audit superseded the collection’s€2.69 price: both Chocolate and Pistacia are offered at€2.19/single or€15.92/eight bottles, with€0.25 deposit per bottle extra; the promotion end is unknown. Pistacia is explicitly present in the Polish Intermarché index atPLN8.99, not inferred from Chocolate. Its separately verified label is48kcal and5.3g sugar/100ml, versus Chocolate49kcal and5.2g/100ml. Full Pistacia ingredients and variant-specific nutrition are now in the SKU table. Czech supplier-stock and Polish local-store observations retain their indexed limitations. [E027–E028, E043–E044]

An audit found an unresolved **Sav:D nutrition conflict** in original sources: the official page explicitly declares9.9kcal and3.3g sugars **per can**; retailer panels declare9.9kcal and3.5g sugars per330ml. This is not a per100ml transcription error; those energy/sugar figures are internally inconsistent. They are recorded as declarations, not accepted as valid analysis. MEDS also says3g creatine while official copy says2.5g; Golden Athlete specifies maximum2cans/day while Gymgrossisten says3. Exact Lemorange ingredients were subsequently verified at Golden Athlete rather than copied from Strawberry. [E013, E015, E041–E042]

The Gymgrossisten live re-open returned404 during audit although its search-index record still says in stock. Its availability row is downgraded to indexed evidence. New live MEDS (SEK17 promotion until18October2026) and Golden Athlete (SEK25 single Lemorange) offers independently support Swedish retail availability. MEDS displays best-before19June2027, but no manufacturing date or assay; that does not prove creatine retention across a specified shelf life. [E015, E041–E042]

Public merchant variant data was used to resolve ambiguous rendered stock text. Clean pages contain “slutsåld” text associated with unavailable variants, yet current merchant data identifies available variants. Virta’s public product data separates one-time price from an introductory subscription discount; using the headline introductory price as the permanent price would understate cost. This data verifies the seller’s public offer only. [E008, E014, E019]

Humble+ shipping FAQ names France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Spain, Italy, Portugal and Germany. The register records the country-specific fees; none establishes local retailer warehousing. The presence of a country selector alone was not accepted as shipping evidence. Virta’s generic policy does not establish non-Finnish destinations; dispatch from Finland and domestic Posti delivery are not pan-EU availability. [E001, E020]

Earliest retrieved evidence places Clean MONO and Sav:D +Creatine in January2026 secondary launch coverage; Finland’s retailer announcement is specifically23February2026. ## Commercial evidence: established versus unknown

- **Humble+:** DTC and an independent retailer’s orderable listing establish sale offers. A SIAL2026 product listing establishes trade-show presence. The founder’s approximately1,000 outlets concern **collagen water**, with creatine expansion described as future. A one-review1/5 product-page rating includes a taste criticism; it is not a representative outcome. Creatine-specific revenue, units, repeat, reorders and profitable distribution are unknown. [E001, E003–E004, E039]
- **Clean:** official variant availability plus actual Swedish and Finnish retailer listings are stronger evidence of distribution than a launch article alone. The Finnish R-kioski rollout is retailer-reported, with no observed store-by-store stock or sell-through. No product-specific revenue, repeat, sales velocity or retailer reorder data was obtained. Raspberry MONO being out of stock DTC cannot identify demand versus a supply decision. [E008–E017]
- **Virta:** an orderable DTC product and20 brand-level Trustpilot reviews exist. The4.4 brand score combines multiple drink lines. At least one June comment explicitly discusses creatine and avoiding shaker washing. The brand republishes these same comments, so those are not independent confirmation. No cohort, net revenue or customer acquisition evidence was found. [E018–E021]
- **Ehrmann:** multi-country retailer presence supports actual distribution of an adjacent product. The parent dairy company’s scale cannot be transferred to this SKU. Czech supplier stock and one dated review do not establish shelf velocity or habitual consumption. Exact line sales, repeat and margin remain unknown. [E022–E029]

The lack of public financials does not establish commercial failure. Conversely, present listings, funding, awards, follower counts or shelf announcements cannot establish profitable success. This workstream found no independently documented historical EU soda failure; it preserves unavailable/unverified products as unresolved history rather than inventing a failure narrative.

## Consumer proposition: evidence and hypotheses

| Possible reason to buy | Evidence supporting the mechanism | Contradiction or missing evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Convenience and portability | Finished cans/bottles remove measuring/mixing; Virta’s dated creatine-specific comment mentions avoiding shaker washing. | Mechanically plausible and anecdotal, not a representative reason distribution or proof of willingness to pay. Powder can be prepared inexpensively in an existing drink. [E018, E021, E030–E034] |
| Taste / beverage enjoyment | Several Virta reviewers like citrus and lack of artificial sweetness; brands sell familiar fruit flavours. | Humble+ taste complaint and Virta negative taste feedback show formulation preference can break the proposition. No blind sensory comparison against ordinary soda. [E001, E021] |
| Daily adherence / ritual | One can/day directions; subscription intervals; a reviewer says remembering supplements is easier in drink form. | Subscription availability is not retention. Need paid repeat and actual daily-use data; no current cohort or randomized adherence evidence for these drinks. [E018–E021] |
| Avoiding powder texture | Brand propositions explicitly remove shaker preparation. | Dissolved form, taste and dose at shelf-life end were not independently verified. A convenience benefit does not establish technical stability. |
| Fitness / strength | A known ingredient makes the proposition explainable; multiple brands prominently disclose doses. | Strength benefits require repeated intake and relevant exercise; a single drink is not an acute energy effect. The science/legal workstream must judge exact forms and claim conditions. |
| Recovery / healthy ageing / cognitive positioning | Broader brand language may attract customers outside heavy gym use. | Ingredient evidence, special-population evidence and specific drink evidence must remain distinct. Cognitive wording is not evidence of an authorised EU benefit; see legal review. |
| Immediate feeling / energy | Sav:D adds105mg caffeine, fitting an established energy-drink occasion. | An immediate caffeine sensation cannot validate a creatine effect. Caffeine can reduce fit for evening/daily use, and repeat beverages add caffeine as well as creatine. [E013–E015] |

Plausible occasions are a gym commute, lunch/workday soft-drink ritual and a daily home supplement routine. These are **commercial hypotheses**, not observed segment sizes. Daily consumption plausibly better matches repeated-use creatine than an occasional social-night soda; whether a buyer wants30 cans/month at the premium is unresolved. Customers may already take powder, creating uncertainty about total intake and the need to communicate dose clearly. Concerns about bloating, sugar, caffeine and authenticity should be tested, but this workstream did not obtain representative frequency estimates or establish that any brand causes those outcomes.

## Substitute economics: a stringent hurdle, not a market-size estimate

Action’s country listings provide a concrete low-price powder anchor:300g100% monohydrate at CZK149.90 in Czechia, €4.99 Germany/Netherlands, €5.99 France and PLN28.90 Poland. Availability at a specific store is unverified. At a **nominal3g monohydrate portion**, price/portion is pack price÷(300÷3): CZK1.499, €0.0499, €0.0499, €0.0599 and PLN0.289 respectively. This is compound mass, not3g free creatine. [E030–E034]

For a like-for-like disclosed Humble+ amount, use its label’s **3.4g monohydrate including3g creatine**. French powder cost is €5.99×3.4÷300=**€0.06789**; a €3 DTC can is approximately**44.2×** that retail powder portion. Thirty cans cost€90 before shipping versus approximately€2.04 powder for thirty3.4g portions. These calculations compare supplement delivery cost only; they do not value flavour, hydration, packaging or convenience and are **not ingredient purchasing quotes or willingness-to-pay measurements**. The integrated report uses the theoretical3.41g compound equivalent from the technical assessment instead of the rounded3.4g panel value:€5.99×3.41÷300=€0.06809, approximately44.1× and€2.04/month. This rounding difference does not change the comparison. [E001, E033]

Conventional energy alternatives also compete for the same cold-drink spend: Carrefour’s indexed Red Bull250ml offer was€1.39 and Albert Heijn’s Fuji apple sugar-free250ml offer€1.75, with local price/deposit caveats. Neither contains creatine. These are opportunity-cost anchors, not a claim that caffeine and creatine deliver the same benefit. [E035–E036]

## Implications for an EU opportunity

The positive case is narrower than “no competition”: real European producers and independent retail channels demonstrate that a finished-drink proposition can be offered, and there are plausible convenience and taste reasons to pay more than powder. A few dated comments support those mechanisms. Low direct-soda visibility in the selected Czech/Polish/Dutch searches could support a targeted test after technical and regulatory feasibility is resolved.

The opposing case is substantial: the format’s incremental demand and repeat premium remain unmeasured; the cheapest substitutes are orders of magnitude cheaper per disclosed ingredient dose; several visible launches are recent; multi-country adjacent products already compete; labels and promotional copy contain dose, sugar and availability ambiguities. None of these public listings supplies an independent end-of-shelf-life creatine assay. Sparse observed retail supply is compatible with overlooked need, weak demand, poor economics, technical constraints, regulatory limits, earlier unobserved failures **or incomplete coverage**. This workstream cannot distinguish those explanations decisively.

Evidence that would change the assessment: paid repeat at a sustainable delivered price; product-specific retailer reorders/velocity with dates and denominators; blind taste testing followed by home-use behaviour; verified shelf-life retention in the exact formula/package; and qualified legal confirmation of the chosen ingredient/form, category, dosage and claim. A small geographically defined pilot could test these hypotheses, but no customer validation has yet been performed.

## Explicit access and evidence limits

Some retailer observations were available only through search-index extraction after the live page timed out or rate-limited; these are labelled in the source and availability tables. No delivery address was entered, no transaction completed, and no warehouse/store visited. Stock flags and offers can change. There was no systematic marketplace-import census, paid market report access, customer recruitment or company outreach. Company claims and consumer comments are not independent product tests. Label text was transcribed where accessible, but this workstream did not obtain a complete image archive, lot-specific certificate or finished-product stability assay. UK/global products and formal science/regulatory determinations are handled by other workstreams.
