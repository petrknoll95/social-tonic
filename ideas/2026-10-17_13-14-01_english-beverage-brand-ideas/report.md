# 20 English-based and coined beverage brand ideas

**Created:** 2026-10-17T13:14:01+02:00 · Europe/Prague

**Brief:** a distinctive name for a Czech-founded, CBD-free, nonalcoholic drink in cans or bottles, with potential international reach. All names are English-based or independently constructed coinages; none derives from Czech words. No names from the previous set are retained.

**Foundation:** [Beverage naming research](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/report.md) and its [naming brief](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/supporting/naming-brief.md).

## My first three

**Frovie** is the strongest compact, invented-name direction: friendly, lively and capable of becoming a recognisable standalone word. **Pocket Sunday** has the clearest lifestyle story: a small piece of unhurried leisure. **Soft Racket** has the strongest attitude: a soft drink with something to say.

These are creative judgments, not consumer-test results. All three exact .com domains returned registration records, so digital identity needs further work. **Wild Courtesy** and **Pebbilo** are useful contrasting challengers. The [screening notes](screening.md) distinguish observed existing uses, limited searches and unresolved availability.

The names span six working directions: approachable coinage, social connection, occasion, sensory metaphor, playful contrast and premium everyday ritual. The mix deliberately includes short invented identities and more expressive two-word brands. Length alone is not treated as a quality score.

## The concepts

### 01. Frovie

**Pronunciation:** FROH-vee. **Type:** coined, loosely inspired by *frolic*; the spelling is constructed rather than a claimed dictionary definition.

A compact, lively identity for a drink people reach for because it belongs in an enjoyable moment. It feels capable of living on a can, a café menu or a tote without needing an elaborate backstory.

- **Working line:** “A little more play.”
- **Visual direction:** rounded custom lettering, one playful interruption in the wordmark, and confident fruit-colour blocks.
- **Pack example:** **FROVIE / Pink Grapefruit / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** broad social refreshment, with room for multiple flavours and later clearly described product families.
- **Tradeoff:** may suggest frozen food or a children's product. Test adult appeal and whether people write *Frovy* or *Frovvy*. The .com is registered.

### 02. Jovlet

**Pronunciation:** JOV-let, with the first vowel as in *job*. **Type:** coined from the intended association with *jovial* and the diminutive feel of *-let*.

A small celebration rather than a grand event. The brand could make a shared lunch, a Friday doorstep visit or an ordinary dinner feel worth marking. “Jovial” describes the brand's personality, not a promised physiological effect.

- **Working line:** “Make a small occasion.”
- **Visual direction:** compact lettering, small pennants and restrained confetti shapes; festive without birthday-party graphics.
- **Pack example:** **JOVLET / Blood Orange / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** accessible adult soda and social multipacks.
- **Tradeoff:** some readers may say JOHV-let or hear *jovial*. Personal-name uses surfaced in screening; this is not an unused word globally.

### 03. Pebbilo

**Pronunciation:** PEB-ih-loh. **Type:** coined from the sound and tactile association of *pebble*, with an invented ending.

A friendly brand built around pleasing small objects: something comfortable to hold, easy to bring and recognisable from a distance. The identity comes from shape and character rather than a health proposition.

- **Working line:** “Good things, close at hand.”
- **Visual direction:** irregular pebble-shaped colour fields, soft edges and a bold, simple wordmark.
- **Pack example:** **PEBBILO / Lemon & Ginger / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** design-conscious everyday refreshment across social and individual occasions.
- **Tradeoff:** the double **b** is inaudible, and the sound may skew towards toys. Do not imply mineral content or spring-water origin through the pebble imagery.

### 04. Fizzamble

**Pronunciation:** FIZ-am-buhl. **Type:** English blend of *fizz* and *amble*.

A sparkling drink for a long lunch, a slow walk to a friend's house or a conversation with no hard finish. It gives the category a useful clue while creating an unusual, ownable-in-spirit verbal shape; legal ownability is untested.

- **Working line:** “Take the scenic sip.”
- **Visual direction:** wandering paths and rising bubbles, with a wordmark that gently changes baseline.
- **Pack example:** **FIZZAMBLE / Mandarin / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** a deliberately sparkling, leisurely social proposition.
- **Tradeoff:** longer to say than it looks. The *fizz* root commits the brand to carbonation and creates a crowded similarity-search territory. A still-drink extension would be less natural.

### 05. Hulloft

**Pronunciation:** HUH-loft. **Type:** constructed blend of English *hullo* and *loft*.

The personality of an open house: someone has put a record on, there are extra glasses, and another guest is welcome. The word supplies an identity; the welcome is built through design and behaviour rather than being guaranteed by the syllables.

- **Working line:** “Come on up.”
- **Visual direction:** open windows, warm interior colours and a simple architectural wordmark.
- **Pack example:** **HULLOFT / Pear & Ginger / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** at-home hosting, neighbourhood cafés and shared multipacks.
- **Tradeoff:** may sound like furniture or property. The final **ft** cluster needs spoken testing. The exact string also surfaced in a Scandinavian technical document; linguistic review remains open.

### 06. Rillora

**Pronunciation:** rih-LOR-uh. **Type:** constructed around English *rill*, a small stream, with an invented ending. The same spelling also occurs as a surname; no family connection is claimed.

A fluid, composed identity for a drink with a more refined sensory profile. It is the least overtly playful coined candidate and could suit restaurant tables as well as retail shelves.

- **Working line:** “Find a little flow.”
- **Visual direction:** fine flowing lines, broad areas of colour and a clear flavour label.
- **Pack example:** **RILLORA / Grapefruit & Rosemary / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** premium, taste-led refreshment with a restrained personality.
- **Tradeoff:** could resemble skincare or a water brand. Avoid spring-source imagery or copy implying mental “flow”; test stress placement and single-versus-double **l** spelling.

### 07. Pocket Sunday

**Pronunciation:** POCK-it SUN-day. **Type:** English metaphor.

A small piece of the weekend that fits into an ordinary day. It is an occasion brand with a tangible object in the name, giving the can a role in the story without promising that its ingredients create relaxation.

- **Working line:** “A little day off.”
- **Visual direction:** a sun peeking out of a pocket-shaped panel; warm yellow with unexpected flavour colours.
- **Pack example:** **POCKET SUNDAY / Peach / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** daytime breaks, picnics and casual social occasions.
- **Tradeoff:** may imply a soothing effect when combined with functional messaging, or feel too weekend-specific. Existing golf-related search results add noise, and the exact .com is registered.

### 08. Soft Racket

**Pronunciation:** SOFT RAK-it. **Type:** English double meaning: *soft drink* plus the lively noise of a gathering.

A nonalcoholic drink with a confident voice. The contrast gives it a clear creative system: visually loud enough to get noticed, socially easy to bring along. It does not need to imitate energy-drink aggression.

- **Working line:** “Bring a little noise.”
- **Visual direction:** bold type, sound-wave marks and sharp colour pairings; flavour information stays orderly.
- **Pack example:** **SOFT RACKET / Tart Cherry / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** independent venues, music, events and a bolder adult-soda proposition.
- **Tradeoff:** sports equipment strongly dominates literal searches; *racket* can also mean a scam. The .com is registered. Keep only if testing shows the attitude is worth that discovery burden.

### 09. Small Parade

**Pronunciation:** SMALL puh-RAYD. **Type:** English contrast between modest scale and celebration.

A brand for making something of ordinary moments: a meal finished, friends arriving, a warm evening outside. Unlike a nightlife identity, its celebratory language can work at lunch as well as after work.

- **Working line:** “Worth a little celebration.”
- **Visual direction:** marching abstract shapes, small flags and a flavour-colour procession around the can.
- **Pack example:** **SMALL PARADE / Orange & Lime / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** inclusive hosting and accessible premium refreshment.
- **Tradeoff:** could feel like a children's party or an events company. Adult typography and taste cues would matter; test recall of both words rather than *Parade* alone.

### 10. Spare Spark

**Pronunciation:** SPAIR SPARK. **Type:** English alliteration and sensory metaphor.

The bright little extra you bring to a gathering. Its sound is crisp and compact, and its two words make a strong visual pair. The “spark” should refer to flavour or the occasion, not an unsubstantiated energy boost.

- **Working line:** “Bring something bright.”
- **Visual direction:** a small starburst within generous empty space; restrained metallic accents.
- **Pack example:** **SPARE SPARK / Lemon & Lime / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** lively citrus drinks and social convenience.
- **Tradeoff:** may sound like a spare electrical part or imply extra energy. The repeated consonants can blur when spoken quickly; a mild or still formula would fit less well.

### 11. Amber Aside

**Pronunciation:** AM-ber uh-SIDE. **Type:** English colour plus a conversational interruption.

A quiet, premium side conversation rather than the main event. It suggests warm light, amber glass and a small pleasure alongside dinner. It would work particularly well if the eventual drink has an amber or citrus-led appearance.

- **Working line:** “A moment on the side.”
- **Visual direction:** amber and cream, narrow editorial type and a small side-note motif.
- **Pack example:** **AMBER ASIDE / Orange & Thyme / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** restaurant tables, aperitif occasions and a taste-led adult drink.
- **Tradeoff:** can imply beer, spirits or a liquid colour the product may not have. It is less flexible than an abstract name across radically different recipes.

### 12. Second Hello

**Pronunciation:** SEK-und heh-LOH. **Type:** English social phrase.

The first hello is an introduction; the second is recognition. This brand is about becoming a familiar presence at a table or in a neighbourhood. That provides a social story without claiming confidence or disinhibition.

- **Working line:** “Good to see you again.”
- **Visual direction:** paired speech marks, recurring two-part illustrations and an inviting wordmark.
- **Pack example:** **SECOND HELLO / Grapefruit / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** cafés, recurring dinner occasions and approachable social refreshment.
- **Tradeoff:** might sound like a dating or messaging service. *Hello* already appears in beverage and hospitality names, so similar-mark review must go beyond the exact phrase.

### 13. Mirth Current

**Pronunciation:** MURTH KUR-ent. **Type:** English metaphor combining enjoyment and movement.

The shared rhythm of people enjoying themselves: the table's conversation, the music in the room, the small things happening between people. More grown-up and abstract than a direct party name.

- **Working line:** “Follow the conversation.”
- **Visual direction:** interweaving lines, warm electric colours and a compact two-line lockup.
- **Pack example:** **MIRTH CURRENT / Blackcurrant / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** adult social occasions and a culturally expressive brand.
- **Tradeoff:** *mirth* is less familiar English and its **th** is difficult in some markets. *Current/currant* ambiguity needs testing; the brand should not depend on blackcurrant being a permanent ingredient.

### 14. Open Mischief

**Pronunciation:** OH-pen MIS-chif. **Type:** English attitude and invitation.

An invitation to do something mildly unexpected: move dinner outside, make an odd playlist, bring an unfamiliar flavour. The brand is playful and candid, with enough tension to carry distinctive campaigns.

- **Working line:** “Good company. Unexpected plans.”
- **Visual direction:** slightly disrupted grids, hand-drawn surprises and a strong opening-tab motif.
- **Pack example:** **OPEN MISCHIEF / Cherry & Lime / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** a social drink with a cheeky, adult voice.
- **Tradeoff:** could imply bad behaviour or intoxication if the imagery pushes too far. *Mischief* is easy to misspell; this is an attitude commitment, not a neutral umbrella for every functional proposition.

### 15. Velvet Errand

**Pronunciation:** VEL-vit AIR-und. **Type:** unexpected English combination.

Make a routine outing feel like a small indulgence. The friction between a luxurious material and an ordinary task gives the brand a distinct editorial voice: observant, urban and slightly eccentric.

- **Working line:** “Make an occasion of ordinary.”
- **Visual direction:** plum, cream and one sharp accent; shopping-list typography beside sweeping texture-inspired shapes.
- **Pack example:** **VELVET ERRAND / Apricot / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** premium everyday refreshment and design-led retail.
- **Tradeoff:** the category is completely opaque without a descriptor. *Velvet* can imply creamy texture, while *errand* can feel like work; both associations need testing against the actual drink.

### 16. Casual Comet

**Pronunciation:** KAZH-oo-ul KOM-it. **Type:** English contrast and alliteration.

Something a little spectacular, arriving without ceremony. It offers a vivid identity for flavour launches and social occasions, with a character that can be playful without relying on cartoons.

- **Working line:** “Make an ordinary entrance.”
- **Visual direction:** a single sweeping comet trail wrapping the pack, punctuated by a large, readable wordmark.
- **Pack example:** **CASUAL COMET / Mandarin & Lime / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** contemporary soda, cultural events and a flexible flavour range.
- **Tradeoff:** cosmic imagery could suggest an energy drink or psychedelic effect. *Casual* is less straightforward to pronounce across languages than the written name suggests.

### 17. Sideways Picnic

**Pronunciation:** SIDE-wayz PIK-nik. **Type:** English occasion with an unexpected modifier.

A gathering that takes a different route: an indoor picnic, a late lunch, a dinner eaten from the good plates on the floor. A specific creative world rather than a generic “togetherness” message.

- **Working line:** “Take the gathering somewhere new.”
- **Visual direction:** diagonally shifted checks, unusual table settings and combinations of familiar objects.
- **Pack example:** **SIDEWAYS PICNIC / Pear & Lemon / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** social multipacks, outdoor occasions and playful hosting.
- **Tradeoff:** the name is long and may feel seasonal. *Sideways* can imply something going wrong; test the intended playful reading and whether the brand survives winter and restaurant settings.

### 18. Borrowed Noon

**Pronunciation:** BOR-ohd NOON. **Type:** English time metaphor.

A little daylight reclaimed from a busy day. This is the more poetic, occasion-led option: an invitation to sit somewhere pleasant and have a drink, without promising productivity or stress relief.

- **Working line:** “Keep a little of the day.”
- **Visual direction:** a moving patch of sunlight, long shadows and warm colour on a quiet background.
- **Pack example:** **BORROWED NOON / Peach & Lemon / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** daytime breaks, cafés and lunch occasions.
- **Tradeoff:** less natural for nightlife and evening use; *borrowed* can suggest something temporary or indebted. It is a focused positioning choice, not the broadest masterbrand.

### 19. Wild Courtesy

**Pronunciation:** WILD KUR-tuh-see. **Type:** English contradiction: adventurous taste, gracious behaviour.

A brand that can serve a surprising flavour in a beautifully considered way. It balances a bolder sensory personality with hospitality, giving it room to feel adult and expressive without being aggressive.

- **Working line:** “A little unexpected. Always welcome.”
- **Visual direction:** formal type interrupted by an unruly line or colour field; elegant structure with one deliberate surprise.
- **Pack example:** **WILD COURTESY / Grapefruit & Ginger / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** premium social drinks, restaurants and independent bars.
- **Tradeoff:** *courtesy* is not simple English for every buyer; *wild* should not turn into an implied foraging, naturalness or intoxication claim. The name is strongest when the taste is actually distinctive.

### 20. Sunny Tangent

**Pronunciation:** SUN-ee TAN-jent. **Type:** English conversational metaphor.

The enjoyable detour in a conversation: a story that starts somewhere ordinary and ends somewhere unexpected. It is bright, curious and open, with a story that fits a drink shared over a long conversation.

- **Working line:** “See where the conversation goes.”
- **Visual direction:** diverging lines, off-centre suns and clear blocks of flavour colour.
- **Pack example:** **SUNNY TANGENT / Orange & Ginger / Sparkling Soft Drink**.
- **Best fit:** daytime social refreshment and a curious, optimistic brand personality.
- **Tradeoff:** *tangent* has mathematical and off-topic associations; *sunny* may bias expectations towards summer or orange flavour. Test whether it feels welcoming or merely abstract.

## How these follow the report

The identity is separate from the product explanation: **brand / flavour / plain beverage descriptor**. “Sparkling Soft Drink” and all flavours here are illustrative, contingent on the recipe; they are not approved labels or a decision that the product must be carbonated. Localise descriptors and mandatory information while keeping the brand identity consistent.

The names emphasise occasion, sensory character and social personality rather than embedding CBD, a specific active ingredient, or a physiological promise. Claims can still arise from the complete presentation, including taglines: leisure, flow, spark and mood-related imagery require checking against what people infer. No name here establishes a product benefit.

For the first testing round, compare **Frovie**, **Pocket Sunday** and **Soft Racket** as genuinely different propositions. Add **Pebbilo** to test a more tactile invented identity, and **Wild Courtesy** to test premium hospitality. Use identical pack formats before exploring individual visual identities, so a preferred design does not masquerade as a preferred name.

Test unaided reading, hearing-to-spelling, delayed recall and an ordinary order: “Can I get a Frovie?” or “One Pocket Sunday, please.” Test Czech and the actual expansion-market languages separately; English-based does not mean universally understood. Then test two flavours, a credible future family, a recipe change and removal of functional copy. Fizzamble has the strongest carbonation constraint; Amber Aside, Borrowed Noon and Sideways Picnic have more specific sensory or occasion boundaries.

These are original creative proposals, not verified unused expressions. Public-web screening is limited, and some names have unrelated existing uses. Trademark rights, domains, handles and customer acceptance are separate questions. See the [screening record](screening.md) for what was actually checked.
