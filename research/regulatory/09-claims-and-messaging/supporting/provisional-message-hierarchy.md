# Provisional message hierarchy

**17 October 2026 · EU/Czech research drafts.** These are alternatives for legal and concept review, not final approved labels or a selected formula. Finished format: can or bottle. Main proposition: CBD-free. Replace factual descriptions with the actual recipe; do not use an ingredient, nutrition or absence statement until verified. No proposed benefit should be stronger in Czech than in the authorised source language.

## Shared hierarchy

1. **Beverage identity and taste:** what is in the container, flavour and carbonation/stillness. Use a neutral brand/name until the complete creative has been reviewed.
2. **Occasion:** when and with whom it fits. Describe an afternoon break or evening serving ritual without asserting that drinking causes a mental transformation.
3. **Verified composition:** net volume, nutrition, caffeine where present and relevant, and any eligible nutrition claim. Front-of-pack emphasis must not create unsupported superiority or safety implications.
4. **Optional specific health claim:** name the qualifying nutrient and retain the authorised meaning. Omit this layer if the formula does not qualify. Keep associated information and any general claim visually and substantively connected.
5. **Required information:** legal product name, ingredient/allergen and nutrition information, operator and other mandatory particulars, relevant warnings, health-claim consumption instructions and the varied/balanced-diet and healthy-lifestyle statement. This is a hierarchy, not an exhaustive legal label template.

Authority: Regulation 1924/2006 Articles 1, 5–10 and 12; Regulation 1169/2011; Czech SZPI guidance and C-524/18. See the [matrix shared gates](../claims-matrix.md) and [layout analysis](botanicals-and-czech-enforcement.md). Simply printing an asterisk does not make an excessive headline permissible.

## A. Daytime work-break concept — with or without caffeine

**Illustrative English front-panel draft, only if recipe and B5 level qualify:**

> Sparkling black tea · Peach flavour
>
> For your afternoon break
>
> Pantothenic acid contributes to normal mental performance

**Illustrative Czech counterpart:**

> Perlivý černý čaj s broskvovou příchutí
>
> Pro vaši odpolední pauzu
>
> Kyselina pantothenová přispívá k normální mentální činnosti

The last sentence is the official Czech B5 relationship, checked in the Czech legal text; the first two are provisional descriptive copy. A tea-free recipe must use its actual beverage identity. Do not replace the last line with “better focus”, “mental clarity”, “productivity” or “get more done”. B5 eligibility does not establish these additional outcomes. A caffeine-free version cannot be assumed to provide a stimulant-like experience.

For a hypothetical **250 ml can containing 60 mg caffeine**, any optional neutral per-can statement must sit within a compliant label. The caffeine calculation is 24 mg/100 ml = 240 mg/L. Covered beverages need the Annex III warning and mg/100 ml declaration, in the same field of vision as the name. Use the coffee/tea exception only if the actual product and name meet it; the sample tea name is not an instruction to engineer an exemption. The final label reviewer should use the official Czech warning text.

If relying on a one-can consumption pattern for B5, ensure the entire package genuinely is one portion and the shelf-life nutrient level supports the selected threshold route. The 250 ml per-100-ml route needs at least 1.125 mg B5; the single-portion alternative is 0.9 mg. These are calculation examples, not manufacturing targets. Pair any health claim with the appropriate consumption information and diet/lifestyle statement. **No exact production dose or recommended daily intake has been selected.**

Source trail: [B5 and official Czech wording](caffeine-and-nutrients.md); [live B5 entry](https://ec.europa.eu/food/food-feed-portal/screen/health-claims/eu-register/details/POL-HC-6433); [Regulation 1169/2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2011/1169/oj/eng).

## B. Caffeine-free evening/social concept

**Illustrative English draft, if flavour and caffeine absence are verified:**

> Sparkling citrus drink
>
> For evenings with friends
>
> Serve chilled

**Illustrative Czech counterpart:**

> Perlivý nápoj s citrusovou příchutí
>
> Pro večery s přáteli
>
> Podávejte vychlazené

Caffeine-free can be considered as a truthful compositional fact where useful; it is not a calm, sleep or no-next-day-effects promise. If the final formula lawfully contains enough magnesium, an optional English line is **“Magnesium contributes to normal psychological function”**. Use the official Czech equivalent in final legal review and keep the relationship attributed to magnesium. Do not headline the drink as “Calm” or “Mood” on the assumption that this line approves those meanings.

No magnesium is required to validate the occasion and taste hypothesis. If using it, review elemental amount, ordinary-food permitted form, shelf-life content, realistic intake and cumulative safety independently. A sleep-focused alternative would need its own classification and claim review; Czech melatonin practice makes the EU onset claim unsuitable as a presumed ordinary-food shortcut.

An evening colour palette or social table scene is not automatically unlawful. The combination of a sleeping-brain illustration, melatonin/botanical mechanism copy and customer sleep testimonials could imply a health benefit even if the headline says only “evening”. Review the whole creative.

## C. Optional CBD extension — research-only differentiation

Do not make CBD part of the masterbrand or a prerequisite for either core occasion. No applicable EU beverage route was established in this research. A private concept card may identify a **possible CBD-containing formulation under research**, clearly unavailable for sale, to test incremental preference. Do not advertise “CBD Calm”, “stronger unwind”, “CBD focus” or “sleep with CBD”. Concept research is not a blanket exemption from claims or novel-food rules.

If a future exact ingredient/beverage authorisation and safety route are established, distinguish the variant with neutral, verified ingredient identity/amount and the required restrictions. The shared eligible nutrient claim could remain attached to that nutrient; it cannot imply CBD contributes to the benefit or makes it stronger. “THC-free” would require a defined analytical specification and does not establish food legality or safety. Avoid an overall impression that the CBD-free core is physiologically inferior.

This may leave a weak commercial distinction. Test whether the ingredient label itself adds enough customer value to justify complexity, rather than inventing a benefit claim to create differentiation. Demand, safety, sourcing and legality remain separate gates. [CBD assessment](cbd-sleep-and-secondary.md).

## Low-cost comprehension protocol

Use the same can/bottle mock-up, price and factual formula across copy variants. Test the occasion-only version against one with a genuinely eligible normal-function claim. Ask adults, unaided: what does it do; how quickly; how certain is the effect; who is it for; what does the named nutrient contribute; and would the expectation differ after one can versus regular use? Record verbatim interpretations and distinguish preference from comprehension.

Predefine failure signals: inferred treatment of anxiety/insomnia, guaranteed productivity, instant calm, absence of a crash or an assumption that CBD is approved/available. Revise the whole name/image/copy combination when these appear. No such research has been conducted. Comprehension testing is not efficacy validation or legal authorisation.
