# Working sensory vocabulary: English and Czech

**Draft for piloting with Czech adults · 17 October 2026.** This is a proposed study lexicon, not a validated Czech translation or a claim about any product tasted. Ask people to describe drinks in their own words first; test comprehension with 8–12 interviews. Keep technical definitions with facilitators. Use a short relevant subset per flight, randomized in check-all-that-apply lists, plus “other,” “none” and “not sure.” Do not equate an intensity with liking.

| Dimension | Czech wording to pilot | Working meaning / separation |
|---|---|---|
| Sweetness | Sladkost | Sweet taste, including immediate and lingering sweetness; not sugar grams or caloric content |
| Sourness/acidity | Kyselost | Sour taste; distinguish from sharp CO2 prickling and bitter peel; pH alone cannot describe perceived intensity |
| Bitterness | Hořkost | Bitter taste, e.g. a peel/tea-like impression; may be liked or disliked; do not define as “bad” |
| Saltiness | Slanost | Salty taste; separate from generic mineral impression |
| Aroma strength | Intenzita vůně | Smell before drinking and aroma during consumption; record the timing |
| Fruit character | Ovocná vůně/chuť | Citrus, peach, pear, cherry, blackcurrant, tropical; perceived associations, not proof of ingredients |
| Floral character | Květinová vůně/chuť | Elderflower/chamomile/lavender-like associations; perfumey is an optional reported association |
| Herbal/botanical character | Bylinná vůně/chuť | Leafy, tea-like, balm-like, woody/spicy associations; “natural” is not a sensory intensity |
| Earthy character | Zemitá vůně/chuť | Soil/root/mushroom-like impression if reported; do not attribute automatically to all mushroom/herbal extracts |
| Mineral character | Minerální chuť | Broad consumer description to unpack: salty, chalky, bitter, metallic; not an assay of mineral content |
| Metallic impression | Kovový vjem / kovová chuť | Metal-like taste/aftertaste; may be cross-modal, not proof of contamination |
| Astringency/drying | Svíravost / vysušování úst | Tactile drying/puckering after swallowing; distinct from bitter taste and carbonation |
| Body/thickness | Plnost / hutnost | Perceived liquid weight/viscosity, from watery/light to thick; “watery” may be acceptable for hydration |
| Coating | Povlak v ústech | Residual coating/slippery feeling; distinguish from dryness and sweetness |
| Grittiness/chalkiness | Zrnitost / křídový pocit | Particulate/chalk-like mouthfeel; note sediment separately |
| Carbonation/prickling | Perlivost / štípání bublinek | Bubble/prickling intensity and persistence; not the same as acid sourness |
| Warming/cooling | Hřejivý / chladivý pocit | Trigeminal heat/cooling apart from measured drink temperature; note ginger/mint associations without assuming ingredients |
| Aftertaste strength | Intenzita doznívající chuti | Remaining sensations at 30 and 90 seconds; identify whether sweet, bitter, herbal, sour, metallic or other |
| Aftertaste liking | Příjemnost doznívající chuti | Hedonic response to finish, separate from strength and persistence |
| Refreshing | Osvěžující | Consumer judgement, not measured hydration benefit; ask why |
| Familiarity | Známá / povědomá chuť | Resemblance to a drink personally known; do not assume shared national familiarity |
| Occasion suitability | Vhodnost pro tuto příležitost | Fit to a named context; distinct from intrinsic liking and effect expectation |

Use 0–10 intensity scales anchored **0 = not perceived; 10 = extremely strong** for a selected attribute. A still drink legitimately scores 0 carbonation; distinguish this from a missing rating. Use 1–9 liking with fully labelled anchors in the [scorecard](supporting/tasting-scorecard.md). JAR (“too little / just right / too much”) expresses preference relative to the respondent's ideal, not an analytical intensity.

No prepared powders, syrups or mixed aperitifs are consumer product benchmarks here. If a qualified sensory team uses raw-material reference solutions for internal training, record them as laboratory references, never as competing consumer products or Czech preference evidence. Consumers need not undergo such training for hedonic tests.
