Research date: **17 October 2026**. Status: desk research completed; local interviews, physical shelf checks, repeat-purchase tests and buyer access remain unverified. Source IDs resolve in [the workstream source register](austria-germany-netherlands-sources.csv). The lead report owns country weights, macro data and reach calculations.

This workstream read the repository and research indexes, [Central European competitors](../../04-eu-czech-competition/supporting/central-eu-research.md), [Western European competitors](../../04-eu-czech-competition/supporting/western-europe-research.md), [US/evidence assessment](../../01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md) and [2025–2026 intelligence](../../26-trends-and-industry-intelligence/report.md). Prepared coffee is consumption-habit context, not a product benchmark. No contact, purchase or test occurred.

## Shortlist rationale and conclusion

Germany and Austria border Czechia and allow staged learning in one German-language region before wider expansion. The Netherlands tests a wealthier, geographically compact but more distant market with direct grocery evidence for premium functional cans. This is a practical shortlist, not a claim that these are the EU's three largest functional-drink opportunities.

**Conditional priority: test in Czechia first; make Austria and one German region the first foreign comparison cells.** The existing founder location creates an assumed cost advantage for observation and sampling; actual founder networks are unknown. Austria offers proximity and a smaller operational field, while Germany offers much greater eventual scale. The Netherlands is useful after a working proposition/channel emerges: its mainstream matcha-energy listing is encouraging but shipping, Dutch copy, national drink tax and incumbent competition increase pilot friction. A confirmed buyer or accessible audience could reverse Austria/Germany ordering. No national chain access is established anywhere.

The first benefit worth comparing across these countries is **workday alertness/focus**, expressed as an occasion and a transparent caffeine proposition rather than an unproven productivity promise. The evidence for a caffeine consumption routine is stronger than evidence for novel nootropics. **Caffeine-free evening/social refreshment** should be the challenger concept. Alcohol moderation validates an occasion; it does not validate a stress-relieving ingredient, an acute sensation or a premium price. Sleep-specific, CBD and secondary functional lines require distinct demand validation.

## Habits, spending and awareness

**Germany.** The German Coffee Association's 2 April 2026 report estimates **161 litres per capita in 2025**. Roasted-coffee volume fell **1.5%**, while nominal revenue rose **23.5%**. These are all-coffee figures, not packaged-RTD demand, and the revenue/volume divergence cannot establish premiumisation or real demand growth. Out-of-home coffee volume reached a reported record, confirming an established office/café/away-from-home routine, not an accessible channel for a startup. [WG01](https://www.kaffeeverband.de/media/presse_pdf/Deutscher_Kaffeemarkt_2025.pdf)

A 2024 study of **22,101 German adults**, representative on specified demographic variables, asked about caffeinated drinks used to improve cognition. The paper reports **62.4%** twelve-month prevalence, while the university release states **64.2%**. This discrepancy is unresolved; neither number enters the demand model. The defensible qualitative inference is that performance-directed caffeine consumption exists widely, across coffee, tea and soft drinks. It measures motive and reported use, not an RTD-only market or actual efficacy. [Paper WG24](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01639625.2024.2334274), [university WG25](https://www.uni-erfurt.de/en/university/consulting-service/press/press-releases/news-detail/germanys-largest-representative-study-on-brain-doping-published)

**Austria.** A Marketagent/Kanzi online panel (n=1,000; fieldwork 25 February–3 March 2026; ages **14–75**) reports **69.6% daily fresh-coffee use**. Among at-least-occasional coffee drinkers, taste **62.1%**, habit **40.8%** and energy/wakefulness **39.4%** were cited motives; multiple answers were possible. Value for money **87.1%** and quality **86%** were important. This is brand-partnered habit research including minors; do not multiply it by the adult population or transfer it to canned focus. It suggests that taste and value must accompany the functional story. [WG02](https://www.kanzikaffee.at/kaffeereport/)

**Netherlands.** The July 2026 Ruigrok study published 1 October uses a weighted screening base of **1,479 people aged 16+** and a main sample of **1,377 weekly coffee and/or tea drinkers**. **78%** of the screening base drank coffee weekly and **71%** tea; these overlap. Coffee drinkers averaged **3.5 cups/day**. The summary says taste is the leading coffee motive and younger generations more often mention alertness/energy. These are not adult-only prevalence, canned-coffee penetration or additive audience pools. [Report WG03](https://files.enflow.nl/51f20288-d7e0-4002-80ea-e1015aee96e5/7a6d8056-600e-4ff7-9f5b-3f346432563d/mediacenter/onderzoeken/nationaal-koffie-thee-onderzoek-2026.pdf), [summary WG04](https://koffiethee.nl/artikelen/nationaal-koffie-thee-onderzoek-2026)

**Purchasing power is capacity, not willingness.** Use the integrated report's harmonised Eurostat adult population/AIC table, rather than different national earnings series. AIC includes publicly provided individual services and cannot price a new drink. The repository's observed premium RTD prices establish available offers only; compare like pack sizes, promotions, deposits and channels before treating any price as a target. Comparable national functional-drink awareness, unaided challenger recognition, willingness-to-pay and repeat consumption are **unknown**. Higher income should receive a limited score weight, not inflate trial/conversion assumptions automatically.

## Alcohol reduction: stronger occasion evidence than calm-product evidence

- **Germany:** Destatis reported **579m litres** of alcohol-free beer produced for sale in 2024, **96.1%** above 2014. It measures German production (with statistical coverage limits), not consumption or a packaged-only subset. [29 July 2025, WG05](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/Zahl-der-Woche/2025/PD25_31_p002.html)
- **Austria:** the brewing association's 2026 publication gives **298,000 hl domestic AF beer sales in 2025**, **3.8%** of its domestic beer/AF series; exports excluded. It does not isolate cans/bottles or functional social tonics. [WG06](https://bierland-oesterreich.at/news-medien/statistikbrosch%C3%BCren/send/8-statistik/447-statistik-2026.html)
- **Netherlands:** the Trimbos May 2026 report uses a 2024 panel of **5,205 adults**. Among **1,185** monthly alcohol-free users who also drank alcohol in the previous year, reasons for choosing alcohol-free included driving **57%**, health **54%** and fitness the next day **49%**. These are subgroup motives, with multiple responses, not shares of all adults or demand for calming ingredients. [WG07](https://www.trimbos.nl/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/TRI-65-093-Infosheet-Alcoholvrije-dranken.pdf)

Date correction: a Trimbos factsheet placed online in October 2025 contains **2022** adult use data, including 16% monthly AF substitute use, overwhelmingly beer. It must not be presented as a 2025 penetration estimate. Its survey finds alcohol-free products often coexist with alcohol; do not assume all purchasers are abstainers. [WG08](https://www.trimbos.nl/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/AF2152-Factsheet-Alcoholvrije-dranken.pdf)

## CBD and evidence boundaries

CBD-only demand, willingness to pay and repeat in these three countries remain unquantified. German-targeted cross-border offers in topic 04 are not proof of domestic legality; the Dutch workstream did not confirm a reliably stocked exact CBD drink. CBD sourcing, effects, safety and authorization are separate from general calm demand. The [current EU regulatory memo](../../04-eu-czech-competition/supporting/regulatory-and-evidence-update.md) governs: no applicable ordinary-beverage route was established, and a supplement opinion cannot authorize a can. No CBD dose or scientific effect is selected here; the primary entry case is CBD-free, without THC-led/intoxicating products. Ingredient studies in capsules or other forms must be assessed for exact dose, preparation, population, duration and processing/stability transfer before a finished drink benefit is inferred.

## Minimum validation inputs for the lead model

1. Use a common screening question for **last four weeks' paid canned/bottled purchases by occasion**, recording category overlap and the primary occasion per purchase. Fresh coffee remains an interview substitute, never a product benchmark row.
2. Recruit adult buyers in an achievable local catchment. Treat unique people who can actually see/buy the product as reach; social ad impressions, population and coffee drinkers are not reachable customers.
3. Compare workday alertness, caffeine-free work break and evening/social concepts with exact local RTD alternatives. Record trial interest separately from a paid sample purchase and from paid repeat after 14/30 days.
4. Test price inclusive of VAT/deposit for customer acceptance, then convert separately to founder net receipts. Do not assume a supermarket shelf price is the manufacturer's revenue.
5. Audit a small same-channel set of shelves; record stock, pack size, promotion, deposit, temperature and placement. A website listing, brand funding or whole-brand sales cannot substitute for units/store/week.
6. Obtain actual co-packer, shipping, label/DRS and retailer terms only after a leading concept emerges. All such terms are unknown in this workstream; no outreach was performed.

Score implications: award **evidence for established caffeine/social occasions**, moderate scores for spending capacity, and low confidence for novel functional awareness and repeat. Give founder access/channel feasibility explicit weight. Do not rank broad energy growth above a directly measured small repeat cohort. A foreign market should move ahead only if paid repeat and landed contribution overcome its added operating cost.
