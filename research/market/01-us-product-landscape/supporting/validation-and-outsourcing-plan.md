# Low-cost validation and outsourced-production plan

Prepared 17 October 2026. **Proposed work only:** no respondents contacted, no supplier quotes requested, no purchases, prototypes, analytical tests or consumer tests performed. Counts and decision thresholds below are analyst proposals to agree before testing, not industry benchmarks or results. All consumer concepts and prototypes are finished liquids in cans or bottles.

## First decision: which recurring occasion deserves a drink?

Test three concepts with similar visual quality before selecting a brand or formula:

1. **Focused work:** a small canned tea or refreshing drink for a specific morning/early-afternoon work occasion; clearly disclosed caffeine, CBD-free. A moderate-caffeine version and a genuinely non-stimulant version should be presented separately. Ingredient legality and final dose are open.
2. **Evening transition:** a caffeine-free, CBD-free can or bottle with an adult flavor and an after-work ritual. Ask about switching away from alcohol, soda or nothing; do not promise anxiety treatment or sedation.
3. **Hydration or fiber alternative:** choose one based on interview evidence as a secondary comparison. Hydration must have a specific occasion; fiber must earn repeat consumption through taste/tolerance. Avoid an everything-in-one proposition.

Start in Czechia for interview access and learning convenience, not because demand has been established. Use Czech-language concept cards and local price tests. A launch-country decision remains open until demand, legal feasibility, channel access and production economics can be compared. US prices are anchors to investigate, not Czech recommended retail prices.

## Staged protocol

| Stage | Proposed inexpensive method | Evidence to record | Decision before more spending |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem interviews | 18–24 adults across work-focus, caffeine-sensitive/evening and exercise/hydration occasions; recruit beyond friends; use the last real occasion, not hypothetical enthusiasm | Frequency, current substitute, spend, caffeine avoidance, taste aversion, desired outcome, failure of current solution | Proceed only if a repeated occasion and a credible switch reason emerge; absence of pain is a valid rejection |
| 2. Randomized concept test | 60–100 target adults, equal presentation, random order; include no-purchase option and price choices; compare benefit-led vs ingredient-led language | Preference at price, comprehension, credibility, purchase occasion; distinguish CBD interest from relaxation interest | Predeclare a decision rule such as a material preference advantage over the control; small sample is directional, not population demand |
| 3. Regulatory/formulation screen | Desk review with a qualified EU/Czech specialist; one or two candidate formulas; obtain paid technical review only after concept signal | Exact ingredient identity/use permissions, allowed wording, caffeine classification, realistic dose and sensory/process risks | Reject ingredients or claims whose unresolved approval burden conflicts with an inexpensive launch |
| 4. Prototype sensory/repeat trial | Outsourced bench samples with lawful ingredients, finished sealed cans/bottles; 20–30 eligible adults; blind taste comparison before branded evaluation; repeat on several real occasions | Taste, finish, portion size, tolerability, perceived effect, repeat choice at price; record unwanted effects | Example preregistered gate: median taste at least 6/9 and a clear preference for the candidate over its control; thresholds are choices, not validated norms |
| 5. Small commercial pilot | One format, one or two flavors, one limited channel, contract packed only after legal and safety gates | Paid reorder, full-price repeat, returns/waste, channel economics, stock age and complaints | Expand only with repeat purchase and positive contribution after channel costs; free sampling and first orders are insufficient |

An exploratory consumer trial cannot substantiate a health claim. If an incremental cognitive effect becomes central, use an appropriate blinded, randomized study against a **caffeine-matched** control, with prespecified task outcomes and professional study design. A non-stimulant variant needs a separate comparison. Keep participants informed and follow applicable ethics/data rules; do not use an informal tasting as a clinical trial.

## Interview prompts

- “Describe the last time you wanted to concentrate but struggled. What did you do, what did it cost, and what happened?”
- “When did you last avoid a caffeinated drink? What did you choose instead?”
- “What do you actually drink when work ends or before bed? Which part of that routine would you change?”
- “What would make this can unnecessary, unbelievable or unpleasant?”
- “At this locally tested price, choose this drink, your usual alternative, or neither.”

Other habits may inform these interviews; they are not eligible product benchmarks. Present CBD only in a separate concept exercise with no consumption and no suggestion of authorized sale. Test the CBD-free proposition first so a CBD name effect does not confound the core concept.

## Contract-manufacturing brief to prepare after validation

Use existing 250–330 ml cans or an existing compatible bottle as prototype options; select the final size from sensory preference and available production capability. Bottled shots remain a legitimate alternative if convenience and price acceptance justify them. Do not buy a filling line or commit to custom packaging at the discovery stage.

Prepare the same brief for candidate Czech/EU beverage developers and contract packers: intended occasion; one clear benefit; ingredient exclusions; target sweetness/acidity/carbonation; desired shelf life; format; destination countries; forecast range explicitly labeled uncertain; and pilot quantities requested. Raw powdered ingredients are permitted inputs to the finished liquid.

Ask for documented capabilities and itemized, comparable quotations covering development, pilot minimum, full-run minimum, packaging minimum, ingredient sourcing, line changeover, filling/thermal process, freight, storage and lead time. No supplier availability, price, minimum order quantity or claimed certification has been verified in this report.

Technical questions: extract solubility and sensory masking; caffeine/nutrient assay; bitterness and sweetness drift; pH and microbiological validation; heat/light stability; can-liner or bottle compatibility; sediment and emulsion separation; carbonated protein foaming/precipitation; ingredient overage justification; transport testing; shelf-life and batch traceability; responsibility for release/recalls. Evaluate the specific finished formula and process, not just ingredient certificates.

## Unit economics without invented quotations

Build separate direct-sale and wholesale cases when real inputs exist:

- **Net receipts per unit:** actual invoiced revenue excluding applicable VAT/deposit, minus discounts, returns and channel fees.
- **Landed variable cost:** ingredients + package + filling + testing allocation + freight + storage/pick-pack + expected waste.
- **Contribution per unit:** net receipts minus landed variable cost and variable selling/fulfillment costs not already included.
- **Break-even units:** fixed launch costs divided by positive contribution per unit. If contribution is zero or negative, higher volume does not fix the base case.
- **Cash required:** deposits + initial inventory + technical/legal work + freight + operating buffer, with realistic payment terms.

Keep low/base/high volume and waste scenarios. Do not convert a US acquisition valuation, wholesale sales figure, subscription price or case shipment projection into a Czech gross-margin assumption.
