# The Socialite direction

17 October 2026 · Europe/Prague

## What changed

The founder's reference is **Socialite**: a person with charm, social confidence, good taste and somewhere to be. That is a sharper brief than the previous emphasis on quiet pauses and unhurried afternoons. The useful aspiration is **being good company and feeling part of the occasion**. A little glamour belongs here; exclusivity and social hierarchy need not.

The strongest words behind this direction are **charm, charisma, magnetism, flair, rapport, presence** and **company**. These are creative vocabulary, not screened brand candidates. “Charisma” best captures the personal quality; “rapport” captures what happens between people.

The [naming research](../../brand/research/2026-10-17_11-07-23_beverage-naming/report.md) supports exploring a recognisable personality alongside a clear drink descriptor. Innocent is a useful reference for a name carrying character; Pepsi and Kofola illustrate identities whose meaning is built through the product and brand. These are naming mechanisms to learn from, not evidence that a particular word will sell.

This is an exploration of the social-drink proposition. It does not settle the formula or replace the separate work-break product hypothesis. The name should describe the brand's personality and setting without promising that the liquid changes the drinker's confidence.

## Three candidates worth discussing

| Name | Intended reading | Connection to Socialite | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Reveli** | REH-veh-lee | Constructed from English *revel*: someone who enjoys the occasion. Expressive, celebratory and a little dressed up. My strongest creative fit for this direction. | Three syllables; stress and final vowel need testing. Existing Réveli jewellery brand. |
| **Convo** | KON-voh | An existing English shortening of conversation. Captures being good company, with a direct connection to a shared drink. The most grounded and immediately understandable option. | More casual than glamorous. Czech readers may initially read C as “ts”; do not assume a uniform reading. Common word with existing commercial uses. |
| **Jovell** | JOH-vell, with the English J sound | A proposed construction drawing on *jovial*, with the shape of a person's name. Warm, polished and sociable; the closest of these to a character who could host the evening. | The J will vary between English and Czech readings, and stress may vary. Its social meaning needs to be built through the brand. Existing surname and commercial uses. |

All three are shorter on the page than Socialite; easier pronunciation is a hypothesis, not an established finding. None is claimed to be globally original or legally available.

Example pack structures, purely to hear them as beverage brands:

- **REVELI / Citrus / Non-alcoholic drink** — expressive, bright, dressed for company.
- **CONVO / Citrus / Non-alcoholic drink** — welcoming, conversational, informal.
- **JOVELL / Citrus / Non-alcoholic drink** — warm, composed, more refined.

Citrus is an illustrative flavour direction from the existing work, not a selected recipe. The category descriptor would be localised. The same names can sit above a peach-and-tea flavour without changing their story; removing a future ingredient or functional statement would leave the identity intact.

## A useful reference to park

**Bonvi** (BON-vee) is a compelling creative direction: a proposed shortening inspired by *bon vivant*, the French expression also used in English for someone who enjoys good living. It gets close to Socialite's pleasure and sociability with a compact sound. However, the initial search found an exact BONVI hospitality trademark listing covering restaurants, bars and cafés in the EU. Keep it as a reference for the desired character, not a leading candidate pending investigation.

## Next decision

Compare **Reveli's celebration**, **Convo's conversation** and **Jovell's personality**. These are deliberately different interpretations of the founder's reference. My preference is Reveli for character and Convo for clarity. Jovell is the more refined challenger, with a larger pronunciation question.

Before selecting one, ask Czech and English speakers to read it unprompted, then hear it without seeing it and write it down. Test an ordinary order such as “I'll have a Reveli” and “Dám si Reveli.” Record the associations before showing a can. No participant testing has been conducted for this round.
