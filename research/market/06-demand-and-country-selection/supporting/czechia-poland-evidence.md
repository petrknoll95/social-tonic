# Czechia and Poland: demand and entry evidence

**As of 17 October 2026. Status:** completed desk-research workstream; demand validation and legal sign-off remain outstanding. No contacts, purchases or product tests. All access dates are 2026-10-17. [Source register](czechia-poland-sources.csv). The lead report owns comparative scores, population, spending-power data, reachable-customer scenarios and publishing.

Reviewed repository README and research index, [existing Czech competition](../../04-eu-czech-competition/supporting/czechia-research.md), [Polish competition](../../04-eu-czech-competition/supporting/central-eu-research.md) and [customer-context research](../../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/czech-context-evidence.md). Reused exact-SKU evidence, refreshed key offers, and found newer category data plus a material category-definition inconsistency. All product anchors below are finished canned/bottled liquids. Coffee habits and broad beer statistics are context only, never added to the qualifying RTD demand model.

## Decision implication

**Czechia is the stronger learning market for this Czech-based founder; Poland is a plausible later scale market, conditional on paid repeat and economics.** This is an operating hypothesis, not evidence of higher Czech underlying demand. Czech-language fieldwork and domestic replenishment should be easier to iterate; actual founder contacts, signed outlets, logistics rates and Polish-language capability are unknown. A small Prague/Brno test can examine afternoon focus with controlled caffeine against existing drinks. Polish conventional-energy purchasing is established, but existing low-priced focus competition, convenience distribution and national tax/deposit administration raise the hurdle for a premium imported challenger.

Neither market supports a claim that a new focus, calm or sleep drink already has demonstrated repeat demand. Test **afternoon alertness/focus as a specific occasion**, with a caffeine-free contrast concept; do not equate improved subjective alertness with measured productivity. Keep CBD outside the primary product and forecast.

## Czech energy-series contradiction and premium constraint

The 2026 NIQ trade article gives CZK7.127bn for the 52 weeks ending **30 November 2025**, value +21.2% and volume +24.4%, then explicitly describes a basket of 82% energy, 16% vitamin water and 2% sports/isotonic drinks. A subsequent sentence gives energy +15.6% value and +13% volume. The earlier report gave over CZK6bn and +10% for a differently described 52-week period ending 1 December 2024. **Scope/baseline remains unresolved; these are not a harmonized annual pure-energy series.** Do not derive a focus market from either total.

The newer article reports 70.2% of energy revenue sold in promotion. Vitamin waters have 69% promotional revenue, volume +74%, and 93.1% of volume in 501–1,000 ml packs. These facts constrain any premium 250–330 ml proposition: growth may favor cheap larger-bottle replenishment rather than a small expensive can. [CP02, Czech retail including petrol stations, 2 March 2026](https://www.zboziaprodej.cz/2026/03/02/zboziprodej-1-2-2026-energeticky-apetit-trva/).

## Coffee, moderation and awareness

Czech Ipsos research from **June 2021**, minimum n=994 adults in the Czech internet population, found 84% weekly coffee drinking; published 19 January 2022. It is older habit context and not a 2026 RTD audience. Coffee can provide taste, a break and a social ritual alongside stimulation. [CP10](https://www.ipsos.com/cs-cz/cesi-kava).

Polish BGR Quantic's April 2026 CAWI study, n=1,118, reports frequent home-coffee use. The publisher/secondary coverage variously calls the base consumers and coffee consumers. Therefore its approximately 72% daily figure is **not used as all-adult prevalence**. Relevance is the strength of an existing home routine and the need to test paid substitution, not a conversion forecast. [CP11, 17 June 2026](https://bgrev.pl/lifestyle/polacy-a-kawa-czego-nie-widac-w-codziennej-filizance/).

A Czech brewing-association page reports 85% occasional non-alcoholic-beer use in a 2025 adult survey; sample/method absent and its wording about the 60% regular base is inconsistent. Excluded from model denominators. [CP12](https://ceske-pivo.cz/nealkoholicke-pivo). Neither beer growth nor lower alcoholic-beer sales proves that drinkers seek stress relief, intoxication or a functional social tonic. National awareness of exact focus/calm brands and ingredient willingness-to-pay remains **unknown** in both countries.

### Poland beverage-tax sensitivity

Current official guidance gives a fixed PLN0.50/L for qualifying added sugars up to 5g/100ml or sweetener; PLN0.05/L per gram above 5g/100ml; and PLN0.10/L for added caffeine/taurine, subject to classification/exclusions and cap. [CP22–23, current tax guidance](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/pozostale/oplata-cukrowa/stawki-i-limity), [CP24, 29 September 2026 ministry comparison](https://www.gov.pl/web/finanse/rada-ministrow-przyjela-projekt-ustawy-o-tzw-oplacie-cukrowej).

The September 2026 **proposal**, intended for 1 January 2027, would raise those rates to 0.70, 0.10 and 1.00 respectively. Do not treat enactment as verified. An earlier November 2025 bill was vetoed on 18 December 2025, illustrating the risk of using proposed rates as law. [CP25, legislative history](https://www.sejm.gov.pl/Sejm10.nsf/PrzebiegProc.xsp?nr=1836).

**Illustrative arithmetic, not a tax ruling:** an ordinary sweetened drink with ≤5g/100ml sugar (or sweetener) and added caffeine, with no exemption, in a 330ml can: current levy (0.50+0.10)×0.330 = **PLN0.198/can**; proposed (0.70+1.00)×0.330 = **PLN0.561/can**, incremental **PLN0.363/can**. The separate PLN0.50 deposit is refundable and excluded from brand sales revenue. Net economics should stress-test this pending law before Poland expansion.

### CBD kept separate

No reliable Czech/Polish representative demand, repeat or beverage-specific willingness-to-pay estimate established. SZPI's 29 May 2025 disclosure confirms sanctions/measures including withdrawals for CBD foods under novel-food rules; the existence of a Czech seller is not evidence of permission. [CP26](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/soubor/27-2025-redigovano.aspx). Polish sanitary guidance also locates cannabinoid extracts in the novel-food framework, but carries an obsolete agricultural THC threshold that is not reused. [CP27](https://www.gov.pl/web/wsse-bialystok/zastosowanie-konopi-wloknistych-w-produkcji-zywnosci). The integrated EU/scientific review must settle any later authorization developments, ingredient sourcing, safety and claims; neither source alone establishes a current lawful beverage route. No THC/intoxicating launch concept is included.

## Cheapest discriminating tests

1. **Desk-to-receipt interviews:** 12–16 Czech adults recruited by recent afternoon drink behavior, including satisfied coffee users and caffeine avoiders. Record exact current spend, no-purchase occasions and last discontinued drink. Separately interview 6–8 Polish adults only if Poland remains a serious near-term candidate. Sample counts are proposed, not completed research.
2. **One controlled concept comparison:** randomized focus/alertness, caffeine-free unwind and hydration messages with identical package/price presentation. Count qualified adult reach, paid reservation or purchase choice, and no-choice; do not use likes or email sign-ups as repeat demand. Use claim-safe language after legal review.
3. **Small paid repeat cohort:** one qualifying canned/bottled outsourced pilot only after safety/compliance review; track full-price first purchase, second purchase within a prespecified window, units/order, reason for stopping and total acquisition/subsidy costs. Separate trial incentive from later full-price behavior.
4. **Channel reality check:** secure proposed terms from a few accessible venues before any national-retail scenario; until then outlets/reach are assumptions. Test case delivery cost and breakage alongside units per venue per week. No outreach was performed for this report.

The low-cost falsifier is simple: if a pleasant, clearly positioned drink cannot win repeat purchases against existing CZK39.50–45 cans and free/cheap habitual options, a larger national population or US launch story does not fix the proposition.
