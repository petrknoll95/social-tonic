# Botanicals, implied claims and Czech enforcement

Research date and access date: **17 October 2026**. Status: completed legal research workstream; provisional interpretation for final label review, not formula or advertising clearance. Scope: finished non-alcoholic ready-to-drink liquids in cans or bottles, including bottled shots where their food category permits; CBD-free core. The optional CBD assessment is separate. Sources are official EU/Czech materials; [source register](botanicals-and-czech-sources.csv).

Repository evidence reused: [topic 01](../../../market/01-us-product-landscape/supporting/eu-czech-and-evidence.md), [topic 04 update](../../../market/04-eu-czech-competition/supporting/regulatory-and-evidence-update.md), and [topic 06 benefit/claims gates](../../../market/06-demand-and-country-selection/supporting/benefits-benchmarks-and-regulatory-gates.md). They correctly separate ingredient evidence from claims permission, but this workstream supplies the missing transitional-law detail and Czech terminology exceptions. No consumer product outside the prescribed finished-liquid format is introduced as a benchmark. Court disputes involving supplements are legal authorities, not product benchmarks.

## Decision that matters for the founder

Build the first message around taste, a genuine drinking occasion and measured composition. A named nutrient's authorised normal-function claim may then be considered when the final drink qualifies. Do not base the proposition on a presumed botanical permission for focus, calm or sleep. This is an analyst recommendation for a lower-cost, more reviewable launch path, not a decision that botanicals are invariably illegal or ineffective.

**Legal eligibility and efficacy remain different questions.** A lawful normal-function statement does not prove a noticeable first-can effect, improved work output or changed mood in a healthy replete adult. An ingredient trial, authentic customer testimonial or positive supplier dossier does not by itself authorise a health claim. Relevant evidence must match identity, preparation, quantity, consumption pattern and the finished drink; a capsule study is neither automatically irrelevant nor automatically transferable.

## Governing framework and the 2025 botanical judgment

The [consolidated Regulation 1924/2006, Articles 1–6, 10 and 28](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02006R1924-20141213) applies to commercial communication, including implied pictorial messages. Trade/fancy names capable of conveying a nutrition or health claim require a related compliant claim under Article 1(3); registering the name is not a cure. The pre-2005 brand transition ended on 19 January 2022. General health-related wellbeing wording requires an accompanying specific claim under Article 10(3). [BC01]

The Court's **30 April 2025 judgment, C-386/23, Novel Nutriology**, confirms that delayed botanical assessment does not switch off Article 10. Its operative ruling addresses botanical psychological/behavioural claims and general wellbeing references, subject to Article 28(6). The case involved mood and stress claims for saffron/melon extracts; one application was late and the other absent. **It is neither a universal botanical ban nor approval of every on-hold entry.** See paragraphs 41, 56–61 and 83 and the operative ruling. The dispute concerned a supplement, but the interpreted claims regulation covers ordinary foods too. [Full judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A62023CJ0386). [BC02]

Keep the two transition routes separate:

| Route | Statutory conditions to establish | Practical implication |
|---|---|---|
| Article 28(5), referring to Article 13(1)(a) | Body-function claims, operator responsibility, compliance with the regulation and applicable national provisions, subject to safeguard measures; pending-claim provisions in Regulation 432/2012 recitals 10–11 remain relevant. | Cannot casually reclassify a psychological promise as a body-function claim to use this route. |
| Article 28(6)(a) | Qualifying claims used lawfully under national provisions before **19 January 2007**; previous national evaluation/authorisation; Member State communication with supporting assessment by **31 January 2008**; applicable decision and transition status. | Retrieve historical authorisation and notification evidence. |
| Article 28(6)(b) | Qualifying claims used lawfully before **19 January 2007**, not previously nationally evaluated/authorised, and an application under the regulation **before 19 January 2008**; applicable decision and transition status. | A 2026 founder cannot repair missing historic conditions by filing now. Do not infer from this that a founder personally had to trade in 2007: document the claim's eligibility and right to rely on it. |

The latter two routes contain six-month provisions following specified non-authorisation decisions; they are not a fresh six-month marketing window on launch. Primary basis: [Article 28](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02006R1924-20141213); reproduced in paragraph 19 of the judgment. [BC01, BC02]

## What an actual on-hold lookup establishes

The [Commission register explainer](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en) expressly separates the register of decisions from pending function claims. Therefore a missing register result alone establishes neither authorisation nor a definitive refusal. [BC03]

We opened the [EFSA botanical workbook](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2021-06/questions-on-hold-botanical-claims.xlsx), read its records, and cross-checked these IDs against the [Commission pending-ID document](https://ec.europa.eu/food/food-feed-portal/backend/claims/files/claims_pending.pdf), all accessed 17 October 2026. The latter is a supporting working document dated **12 June 2013**, still linked by the Commission at access; its present availability does not make it a fresh authorisation decision. [BC04–BC06]

| ID / EFSA question | Record identity and relationship, paraphrased | What remains unknown for a proposed can |
|---|---|---|
| 2085 / EFSA-Q-2008-2818 | Melissa officinalis; cognition, mood, relaxation | Historic transition evidence, exact preparation/dose and permitted final wording. |
| 2183 / EFSA-Q-2008-2916 | Withania somnifera; mental wellbeing/relaxation | Same, plus ingredient safety/food-use basis. |
| 2348 / EFSA-Q-2008-3081 | Specified valerian root extract; sleep onset | Match extract ratio, solvent and standardisation; national classification; transition proof. |
| 3673 / EFSA-Q-2008-4396 | Panax ginseng; alertness | Preparation/dose, transition proof and source of any claimed effect. |

These are **pending research leads, not approved wording recommendations**. EFSA's workbook identifies itself as informational and points to the underlying Access database. Its Commission-to-EFSA receipt dates in July 2008 do not establish the original applicant's filing date and cannot alone prove either compliance or noncompliance with Article 28(6)(b). No complete transition dossier or final RTD eligibility was established for these examples.

Recommended dossier for each botanical candidate: Latin species and plant part; extract/process/solvent and standardisation; exact claim/ID and proposed Czech translation; current status in both pending list and decisions; proposed claim's Article 13 category; national/historical-use and timely-notification evidence; scientific substantiation; permitted ingredient status for this beverage category; serving and daily exposure; stability/bioavailability; overall label and ad context. The correct outcome until those fields are resolved is **not cleared**, rather than a generic legal/illegal label.

## Czech guidance: useful, dated and subordinate to the law

[SZPI's main guide](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/clanek/voditka-k-problematice-zdravotnich-a-vyzivovych-tvrzeni), first published 21 May 2018 and last updated 3 January 2024, addresses labels, social media, online shop categories and indirect commercial material. It permits equivalent-meaning wording, not stronger effects, and warns against attributing a component's claim to the whole mixture or unapproved constituents. It also describes on-hold claims as usable under conditions, including as support for general claims. This predates the 2025 judgment: read the latter statements subject to the correct Article 28 route. No newer Czech official post-judgment clarification was located in this targeted search; that is a search limitation, not evidence of an enforcement waiver. [BC07]

[SZPI's 24 August 2016 on-hold explanation](https://www.szpi.gov.cz/clanek/vyzivova-a-zdravotni-tvrzeni-zdravotni-tvrzeni-podle-cl-13-odst-1-pism-a-narizeni-c-1924-2006-on-hold-seznam-a-jeho-pravni-vymahatelnost.aspx) calls the list informal, allows equivalent meaning and warns that even listed disease-treatment wording remains prohibited. Its references to older national legislation should not be copied as current law. [BC08]

Two important local nuances:

- **Probiotic/prebiotic:** the [MZe national recommendations, section 2.2](https://www.mze.gov.cz/public/web/file/546391/Narodni_doporuceni_pristupu_k_nekterym_oznacenim_potravin_zejmena_ve_vztahu_k_narizeni__ES__c._19242006.pdf) treat specified “contains probiotics/prebiotics” wording as a nutrition claim under the conditions for “contains [nutrient or other substance]”; they explicitly warn that other Member States may differ. The document is nonbinding, dated 21 November 2013, updated 21 August 2017. This does not approve “improves gut health”, efficacy of arbitrary strains, or EU-wide use. This is not a safe harbour: the [European Ombudsman decision of 20 December 2024, paragraphs 27–30](https://www.ombudsman.europa.eu/mt/decision/en/197581), found the Commission's health-claim interpretation reasonable and explained that inconsistent national toleration would not displace EU law. The case also concerned related terms including prebiotics. Obtain an explicit current EU/Czech legal assessment of the exact wording; do not treat the older national note as authorisation. [BC09, BC15]
- **Natural:** [Czech Decree 417/2016, §2](https://mze.gov.cz/public/portal/mze/legislativa/vap87546-131) flags misleading uses including přírodní. Exceptions include legally defined names and demonstrably justified non-misleading descriptive presentation; unprovable information is excluded. It is not a blanket ban or universal licence. Prefer precise, evidenced ingredient/process information over an unqualified “all natural” halo. “Naturally [nutrition claim]” and “natural flavouring” have their own EU conditions. [BC10]

## Proposed wording: contextual assessment

The following classifications are analyst applications to the **whole proposed presentation**, not an official ruling on isolated words. Dose/authorisation conditions for particular nutrients belong in the integrated claims matrix.

| Proposed context | Classification and risk | More defensible provisional direction |
|---|---|---|
| “FOCUS — a productivity drink; finish more work” with a brain icon and before/after task count | Specific cognitive/behavioural and work-output implications; high. A normal mental-performance nutrient claim does not establish greater output. | “Sparkling tea for your afternoon break”; disclose caffeine quantity accurately if present. Separately use an eligible exact nutrient claim. |
| “Mental clarity in 20 minutes” or “laser concentration” | Specific acute performance promise; high. Both onset and magnitude are additional claims. | Remove onset/superiority; quantify lawful composition without claiming a mental transformation. |
| “Calm — unwind your nervous system” / “mood lift” / “stress relief” | Physiological/psychological claim; high. “Normal psychological function” is not interchangeable with sedation, euphoria or reduction of stress. | Flavour plus “for an evening pause”, avoiding diagrams or testimonials implying treatment. |
| “Unwind with friends” over a dinner scene | Potential occasion language, but context-dependent. Added adaptogen/cortisol/relaxation copy can change the meaning. | “A sparkling drink for evenings with friends.” Test understanding; do not assert that the drink makes people more sociable. |
| “Social tonic — feel the float; social confidence without alcohol” | May imply altered mood/behaviour; high in this context. “Social” is not automatically a health claim, and “tonic” has a narrowly defined exception below. | Adult taste, serving ritual and actual alcohol content. No biochemical social-confidence promise. |
| “SLEEP — treats insomnia” / “for anxiety and ADHD” | Medicinal presentation/disease treatment; unacceptable as ordinary food claims. | Remove disease references; review an eligible specific sleep-onset claim separately from “sleep quality” or “deep sleep”. |
| “Evening citrus”, “serve chilled”, “bitter orange and herbal notes” | Sensory/occasion information, ordinarily lower risk if accurate. A sleeping-brain image or effect testimonial can reintroduce a claim. | Keep factual sensory presentation and avoid an implied pharmacological guarantee. |
| “No crash”, “no jitters”, “no next-day effects” | Potential health/safety and physiological-course claims, not mere taste. Sugar-free or caffeine-free does not establish them. | Truthful caffeine/sugar quantities without guaranteed response or tolerance. |

Health-claim interpretation is broad: **C-544/10, Deutsches Weintor**, 6 September 2012, includes temporary effects and claimed absence/reduction of adverse effects. The wine's alcohol-specific prohibition is not applied to these nonalcoholic drinks; the broader definition helps explain the no-crash risk. [Judgment and official summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/SUM/?uri=CELEX%3A62010CJ0544). [BC12]

## “Social tonic” is not automatically covered by the tonic derogation

[Regulation 2019/343, Article 1 and Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32019R0343) exempts specified generic descriptors from **Article 1(3)** under defined conditions. For tonic, it covers a nonalcoholic carbonated drink containing quinine flavouring FL 14.011, FL 14.152 or FL 14.155, with the term in its descriptive name. Listed forms are English tonic, Bulgarian Tоник, Czech/Slovak tonik, Spanish/Portuguese tónica, Italian tonica and Romanian tonică; the Annex says all Member States. The application recital's initial “except Romania” wording is not the final geographical rule. [BC11]

A new botanical social drink without that identity has no automatic access. Even a qualifying quinine drink gains no permission for added calm, confidence or sleep promises. Assess the entire “social tonic” name and message; do not assume that adding quinine would cure an otherwise misleading proposition.

## Layout, endorsements and advertising review

**C-524/18, Dr. Willmar Schwabe**, 30 January 2020, requires both substantive correspondence and a perceptible visual relationship between general and supporting specific claims. Ordinary rule: proximity. A clear asterisk can exceptionally link different sides when size/length prevents full placement together; it is not a general licence to bury supporting text on the back. An unsupported broad promise cannot be cured by tiny nutrient wording. [Judgment, paragraphs 40–50](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=celex%3A62018CJ0524). [BC13]

[Decision 2013/63/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32013D0063) explains the accompanying-claim requirement and additional Article 10(2) information: varied balanced diet/healthy lifestyle, consumption quantity/pattern, and applicable avoidance/excess-consumption warnings. Place the actual text in the creative review; do not rely on a generic footer or a link to an unrelated research page. [BC14]

Final review should include front/back can or bottle, e-shop, paid ads, influencer scripts, reposted testimonials, search snippets, product/collection names, brain/moon imagery and any linked ingredient explainer. A customer quote such as “this cured my anxiety” becomes a claims issue when selected for commercial use; authenticity is not permission. Scientific citations do not immunise sales copy. Individual doctor/health-professional recommendations also trigger Article 12(c), separate from whether the benefit itself is authorised.

Focused unresolved questions for counsel: exact Article 28 transition basis for each proposed botanical claim; local classification of named focus/calm/sleep copy; final Czech translation and proximity; current Czech probiotic/prebiotic practice; natural-description substantiation; full medicinal presentation; exact nutrient/serving conditions; and whether each distributor's advertising can preserve these limits. No authority or other person was contacted in this research.
