# Low-cost validation and commercial diligence protocol

**Proposed work only, 17 October 2026.** No interviews, supplier quotes, purchases, product trials or buyer commitments were obtained. These are analyst-designed next steps, not validated thresholds or a selected budget/launch market.

Reuse the published [Czech interview guide](../../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/interview-guide.md) and [diary protocol](../../../customers/05-segments-and-occasions/supporting/diary-study.md) for behavioural discovery. The commercial measures below add sales and unit-economics checks; sample ranges are illustrative, not competing validated thresholds.

## First validate an occasion

Compare three candidate jobs separately: a work-break alertness/focus drink; a CBD-free, caffeine-free adult evening/social drink; and, only if interviews reveal a distinct need, a familiar soda/tea replacement or activity-specific hydration drink. All proposed consumer products must be finished liquids in cans/bottles. A bottled RTD shot can be tested if portion/convenience matters; no consumer preparation formats.

Begin with approximately 15–20 adult interviews in a narrowly defined reachable segment. This is a directional learning sample, not a representative Czech survey. Ask about the last actual occasion, drink bought, amount paid, frequency, dissatisfaction and switching trigger before showing concepts. Other habits may inform the interview, but product comparisons use exact qualifying RTDs. Recruit beyond the founder's friends and record negative responses.

Show a small number of plainly labeled concepts at a realistic local price and include an existing-product/no-purchase option. Randomize concept order. Test taste/portion/occasion separately from the ingredient story. Separate demand for less caffeine from demand for no stimulant. For evening drinks, distinguish sensory ritual from an expectation of a noticeable pharmacological effect. Do not promise medical, anxiety-treatment, intoxication-like or unverified cognitive effects.

A Czech pilot is convenient for this founder; compare another EU country only if evidence of channel/customer fit justifies the extra cost. Do not assume US prices, UK CBD permissions or a national energy-growth rate transfer.

## Then qualify an outsourced liquid product

Only after the switch reason survives, prepare one short brief for contract developers/fillers: consumer occasion, can/bottle and portion range, sensory target, shelf-stable versus chilled requirement, restrained claim ambition, ingredient exclusions and caffeine preference. Keep CBD out of the main brief. Request comparable written quotations later; no supplier has been contacted in this research.

Ask for pilot and commercial minimums, setup costs, ingredient/packaging minimums, process compatibility, analytical/stability work, lead times, ownership of formula, quality responsibilities, storage/freight and payment terms. Compare total landed cost at realistic order volumes, not just the filling fee. Prefer an existing suitable production process if it meets the brief; do not assume a cheap MOQ or stable complex blend without evidence.

Before tasting or sale, obtain formula-specific food-use, safety, label and claims review plus appropriate product testing. Ingredient studies in capsules, fresh mixes or oils do not establish potency/effects after commercial processing and storage. Outsourcing does not transfer the brand owner's responsibilities automatically.

## Measure sell-through and paid repeat

An illustrative learning pilot could use 5–10 selected outlets for 8–12 weeks, with matched incumbent controls where possible. Those are proposed design ranges, not success benchmarks. Do not launch nationally to learn whether a product repeats.

| Measure | Definition required | Error to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Units/store/week | Consumer units sold divided by in-stock selling store-weeks, per SKU and channel | Shipments divided by latest authorized doors; comparing multipacks with single cans |
| Unpromoted velocity | Same metric excluding defined promotion/display periods; report both | Treating discounted trial as sustained demand |
| Net revenue/container | Recognized revenue after discounts, rebates and returns divided by comparable containers | Retail shelf price as brand revenue |
| Contribution/container | Net revenue less product, packing, freight, variable fulfilment, channel costs and expected waste | Calling gross product margin profitability |
| Paid repeat | Eligible first-time buyers who make a second paid purchase within a fixed window / all first-time buyers with full follow-up | Survivorship, free samples, automatic subscription renewals or mixed product formats |
| Retention | Paid active cohort at each defined interval, with cancellation, stockouts and channel noted | A one-time reorder percentage without a window |
| Payback | Acquisition cost / observed cumulative customer contribution, including fulfilment and discounts | Revenue LTV or a projected repeat rate as cash return |
| Reorder and retention of outlets | Outlets reordering based on sell-through plus ongoing weekly velocity | An opening order, free fill or stockist logo as consumer demand |

Set go/no-go levels **before** results using the actual net selling price, variable costs, buyer's comparable category velocity, cash available and acceptable payback. No universal 5/10/20 units-per-store hurdle is inferred from the reviewed brands. A small pilot narrows uncertainty; it does not prove a national market.

Reject or revise the proposition if purchase depends on unsubstantiated effects, if taste loses against existing RTDs at price, if unpromoted repeat is weak, or if a feasible small production run cannot support contribution and cash needs. Separate a product problem from an access/stockout problem.

## Evidence to request before relying on a brand blueprint

Ask for dated SKU/channel sales extracts, actual in-stock doors, base/promoted units per store per week, opening shipments and subsequent orders, refunds/returns, retail consumer takeaway versus sell-in, cohort repeat definitions, net/gross revenue bridge, gross/contribution/operating profitability, geography and format splits. For CBD brands additionally require separate CBD-free/CBD/THC lines and beverage-versus-other-format revenue. These are future diligence requests, not claims that such data have been obtained.

For a CBD extension, test whether people pay for CBD independently of the same drink's taste/ritual and generic calm positioning. Keep it at concept research until the intended beverage's authorization, safe specification, lawful supply and viable claims are established. Never use THC growth or supplement approval as the permission or demand case.
