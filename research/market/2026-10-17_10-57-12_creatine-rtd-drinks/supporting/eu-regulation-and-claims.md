# EU and Czech ingredient, classification and claims review

**Research cut-off: 17 October 2026.** This is a source-based issue assessment for a prospective product, not clearance of a recipe or competitor. Source IDs resolve in [sources.csv](../sources.csv). Scientific evidence and legal permission are separate.

## The workable claim is narrower than a general wellness soda

| Route | Verified position | Consequence for a creatine drink |
|---|---|---|
| Short, intense exercise | Regulation 432/2012 permits: “Creatine increases physical performance in successive bursts of short-term, high intensity exercise”. Food must provide a daily intake of 3 g creatine, tell consumers that 3 g daily produces the benefit, and target adults performing high-intensity exercise. [L01] | A possible ordinary-food claim, subject to all conditions. It does not promise an immediate stimulant sensation or establish a general recovery/endurance claim. |
| Strength in older adults | Regulation 2017/672 permits a separate daily-creatine/resistance-training claim for adults **over 55**. It requires 3 g daily alongside progressive resistance training at least three times weekly for several weeks, at at least 65–75% of one-repetition maximum. [L02] | Training requirements and age targeting are material; a general “healthy ageing” soda cannot silently omit them. |
| Cognition | Regulation **2026/1118**, adopted 26 May and published 27 May 2026, refuses the proposed creatine cognitive-function claim following EFSA's 2024 assessment. [L03] | This is an enacted refusal, not merely a pending application or an old scientific debate. Do not position the drink as an authorised creatine focus/brain-health product. |

The 3 g condition is **creatine**, not automatically 3 g monohydrate or 3 g of a multi-ingredient branded premix. A formulation team should reconcile supplier potency, chemical equivalent, label terminology and end-of-life analytical content. It is not an EU-wide statutory maximum intake or a reason to recommend multiple cans. The [technical review](formulation-and-stability.md) explains dose conversion and why a raw-material declaration is insufficient.

Regulation 1924/2006 also requires a varied-diet/healthy-lifestyle statement, the quantity and consumption pattern producing the claimed effect, and appropriate avoidance/excess-consumption information where relevant. General wellbeing claims need an accompanying related authorised claim. Names, imagery, adopted testimonials, websites and advertising can communicate claims; a US label is not a compliant EU template. Claims must not encourage excessive consumption. [L04]

## Exact ingredient identity precedes claims

The Commission's Article 4 consultation published in July 2023 found **chemically synthesised creatine monohydrate of at least 99.9% purity** non-novel because of significant EU consumption before 15 May 1997. A separate August 2025 consultation found the specified micronised monohydrate non-novel; it expressly discusses unchanged composition/structure and absence of nanomaterials. These are positive identity-specific findings, not approval of every encapsulation technology or beverage. [L05, L06]

Conversely, a February 2022 Czech-led consultation concluded that **creatine malate and creatine alpha-ketoglutarate** were novel in foods because the requisite history had not been established. Do not infer legality of a salt from the monohydrate decision. [L07]

For hydrochloride, citrate preparations, ethyl ester, creatyl-L-leucine, novel complexes or nano/encapsulated preparations, this review has **not established a complete lawful EU beverage route**. The absence of a decision in this search is not itself a prohibition. Require the exact chemical specification, process, excipients, particle characteristics, pre-1997 evidence or applicable Union-list authorisation and its use conditions. CreaBev supplier wording such as “EU compliant” or US self-GRAS status is not a substitute for that dossier. The Commission notes that its status catalogue is nonbinding and nonexhaustive; supplement-only use history does not automatically cover ordinary foods. [L08, L09; technical sources]

Check each carrier, sweetener, preservative, colour and flavour under the rules applicable to the final category and concentration. Also check allergens, food-grade impurity specifications and any added vitamin/mineral forms. A claim authorisation establishes neither ingredient safety nor manufacturing legality. Destination-specific rules remain a review item because substances other than vitamins/minerals are not fully harmonised; Germany's BfR explicitly acknowledges this regulatory gap. [L18]

## Beverage versus supplement

A refreshment-sized soda is not automatically a food supplement because it contains creatine. The supplement definition concerns concentrated sources presented for consumption in small measured quantities; liquid ampoules and similar measured-dose formats can qualify. Classification depends on composition, presentation and intended use, not just a chosen label heading. A bottled shot requires its own assessment. [L10, L11]

For an ordinary prepacked beverage, assess Regulation 1169/2011 requirements including legal/descriptive name, descending ingredient list, allergens, net quantity, durability/storage, responsible EU operator, nutrition per 100 ml, language and relevant quantitative ingredient declarations. Creatine is not a mandatory standard nutrition-table row; an amount associated with a claim needs the applicable additional declaration. Do not present a premix mass as a measured amount of active creatine. Food information must not mislead or imply disease treatment. [L04, L12]

Caffeinated variants have an additional practical boundary. Above **150 mg/L**, beverages generally need the prescribed high-caffeine warning and caffeine content in mg/100 ml in the same field of vision as the name; the specified coffee/tea exception is not a blanket exception for caffeine derived from tea. Thus 105 mg in 330 ml is about **318 mg/L**, above that threshold. This arithmetic is not a determination that any reviewed label is compliant. Sweeteners and certain other ingredients can trigger additional Annex III statements. [L12]

## Czech requirements checked separately

The Ministry of Agriculture's published supplement procedure requires submission of the **Czech label text before first placing a supplement on the Czech market**, under Act 110/1997 and Decree 58/2018. Its definition and presentation rules distinguish small measured-dose products from ordinary drinks. The official e-Sbírka index identified a consolidated Decree 58/2018 version effective 1 July 2025; the complete interactive text did not render in this research interface, so no unsupported national creatine maximum is asserted. [L10, L13]

SZPI states that supplements are foods and operators remain responsible for safety; efficacy is not assessed as it is for medicines. Notification must not be marketed as state approval of efficacy or as clearance of a final beverage. Czech mandatory information, consumer-understandable wording and exact claims should be reviewed in Czech, alongside the complete presentation and ordinary-food or supplement classification. [L11]

An EU offer, French production claim, Scandinavian retailer listing, Czech marketplace import or foreign supplement notification does not establish lawful Czech distribution. Nor does a UK or Swiss launch: they are separate jurisdictions. This report has not completed all 27 national notification, other-substance, deposit, packaging, levy or energy-drink age-restriction reviews. These depend on the selected formulation, country and channel and must be costed before entry.

## Questions for qualified review before a consumable pilot or commercial offer

1. Confirm category, exact ingredient identity/process, carrier permissions and country-specific conditions for the chosen recipe.
2. Approve the claimed **creatine-equivalent** amount, serving pattern and adult target population, with retained-content evidence and manufacturing variation allowed for.
3. Assess combined exposure from two or more cans plus powder/capsules, caffeine and all additional actives; decide justified warnings and exclusions. The claim threshold is not a toxicological exposure assessment.
4. Review front/back label, translations, brand name, retailer description and advertising together. Remove unsupported acute energy, cognition, superior absorption, recovery or disease implications.
5. Confirm required Czech notifications/registrations for the actual category and operator; complete other destination checks before expansion.

No authority was contacted and no notification or legal opinion was commissioned. Technical feasibility, health-claim eligibility and safety remain separate release gates.
